# Ultimate Guide - How to Start a Travel Blog

The travel and tourism industry generates annual revenue of around [$7.6 trillion](https://www.wttc.org/-/media/files/reports/economic-impact-research/2017-documents/global-economic-impact-and-issues-2017.pdf), contributing to approximately 10.2% of the world’s gross domestic product (GDP). We all hear stories about how people have funded their travels by sharing their adventures with the blogosphere. But how do you get started?

You need to begin filling up your blog so you can grow it into something worth monetizing. It is too soon for advertising, selling, or marketing. You just want to know how to create a travel blog with no expertise, quickly, and efficiently, with the best chances of future success.

We’re here to help. [Postach.io](https://postach.io/) is the Evernote-powered blogging platform that makes starting a blog and maintaining it fast and simple. What’s more — we optimize all our blog themes using industry standard SEO practices. This means they can be found and indexed by search engines, readers, and potential leads, setting you up to generate significant traffic as you grow your content offering.

If you are thinking about living the travel blogger lifestyle and generating passive income while you explore the world, read on! In this guide, we tell you exactly what you need to do to start a travel blog with [Postach.io](http://postach.io/). Learn how easy it is to start living the life you want to lead.

## 3. Come up With a Clickable Title

Enter a title for your new note. The title you use in Evernote will be the title of the blog post on your site, so make sure you use something catchy that people would actually search for on the web. For example, if I am writing a post about Saigon, Vietnam, the title 11 Coolest Places in Saigon will get more traffic from searches than Alex’s Saigon Experience. The first title addresses something actionable that people visiting Saigon want to know, the second is irrelevant to anyone but me.

![f00db058-a8ab-4a0e-91ee-61ecb8168d56.png.jpg](blog-postach-io--ultimate-guide-how-to-start-a-travel-blog/d8a7a42379aa1783be26750dbcfd40db.jpg)

(Source: Author screenshot)

## 4. Draft Your Content

Populate your new note with some digestible content. The content you craft should provide useful information with actionable takeaways. Sure, you can write whatever you like. But it is a good idea to go through your posts after you have drafted your main content to add some bullet points telling your readers exactly how to do what you advocate.

For example, if you write about your experience of doing a “visa run" in Vietnam, create an accompanying set of bullet points taking your readers through the steps they must take to complete a visa run the right way. Your self-reflective blog post then becomes something that is not only enjoyable to read but provides specific and useful information that addresses particular issues, concerns, or interests people have when they travel to the locations you write about.

## 2. Name Your Site

A great travel blog needs a catchy, memorable, original name. When naming your site, try to keep your options open. For example, if I am writing about Vietnam, I might be tempted to call my blog AsianTravels. If a year later, I decide I want to begin traveling South America, I will have a hard time shoehorning posts written there into my Asia-specific blog. Here are some examples of site names from successful travel bloggers to give you some inspiration:

[Nomadicmatt.com](http://nomadicmatt.com/)

[Expertvagabond.com](http://expertvagabond.com/)

[Ytravel.com](http://ytravel.com/)

[Adventurouskate.com](http://adventurouskate.com/)

[Youngadventuress.com](http://youngadventuress.com/)

[Neverendingfootsteps.com](http://neverendingfootsteps.com/)

We went for [Flipflopsforever.postach.io](http://flipflopsforever.postach.io/) because it is catchy and vague enough for us to be able to write about almost any hot holiday location. An ambiguous name also means that if you create a custom domain and want to sell it in the future, you will not be limited to a specific market.
