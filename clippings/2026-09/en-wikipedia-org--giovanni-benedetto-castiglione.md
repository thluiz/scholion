**Giovanni Benedetto Castiglione** (baptized 23 March 1609[\[2\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJeutter2004-2) – 5 May 1664) was an Italian [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque "Baroque") painter, [printmaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printmaker "Printmaker") and draftsman, of the [Genoese school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_school_\(painting\) "Genoese school (painting)"). He is best known now for his [etchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etching "Etching"), and as the inventor of the [printmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printmaking "Printmaking") technique of [monotyping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotyping "Monotyping"). He was known as _Il Grechetto_ in Italy and in France as _Le Benédette_.

He painted portraits, [history paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_painting "History painting") and landscapes, but came to specialize in rural scenes with more animals than human figures. [Noah's Ark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah's_Ark "Noah's Ark") and the animals entering the Ark was a favourite subject of his,[\[3\]](#cite_note-EB1911-3) and he devised a number of other new subjects from the early parts of the [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament "Old Testament") with the patriarchs and their animals.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/Circe_changing_the_companions_of_Ulysses_into_beasts_MET_DP832221.jpg/250px-Circe_changing_the_companions_of_Ulysses_into_beasts_MET_DP832221.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Circe_changing_the_companions_of_Ulysses_into_beasts_MET_DP832221.jpg)

_[Circe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circe "Circe") changing the companions of [Ulysses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseus "Odysseus") into beasts_, etching, 1650–51

Castiglione was born in Genoa. The biographer of Genoese painters, Raffaele Soprani says his parents had him placed in the studio of [Giovanni Battista Paggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Paggi "Giovanni Battista Paggi"). Wittkower describes him as a "passionate student" of [Anthony van Dyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_van_Dyck "Anthony van Dyck"), who arrived in 1621, and [Peter Paul Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Paul_Rubens "Peter Paul Rubens"), who stayed in the city in the first decade of the 17th century and whose paintings were readily accessible there. He may have trained under the Genoese [Bernardo Strozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Strozzi "Bernardo Strozzi"). He lived in Rome from 1634 to about 1645, then returned to Genoa. He also traveled to [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence "Florence") and [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples,_Italy "Naples, Italy"). He was back in Rome in 1647, before moving in 1651 to be a court artist in Mantua for [Duke Carlo II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II,_Duke_of_Mantua_and_Montferrat "Charles II, Duke of Mantua and Montferrat") and his wife [Isabella Chiara de Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduchess_Isabella_Clara_of_Austria "Archduchess Isabella Clara of Austria"). He died in Mantua.

He had various brushes with the law in his lifetime. The Queen's Gallery in London, where an exhibition of his work was held in 2013, made the following statement: "Giovanni Benedetto Castiglione was also a violent and impetuous man, who was repeatedly in court for assault, allegedly attempted to throw his sister off a roof and was forced to leave Rome, probably after committing murder. The turbulence that characterised his life overshadowed his artistic brilliance, and Castiglione struggled to achieve recognition in his lifetime. Much of what is known about the artist is derived not from fulfilled commissions, but from court documents."[\[4\]](#cite_note-4)

Castiglione was a brilliant draftsman and pioneered the development of the [oil sketch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_sketch "Oil sketch") (often using a mixture of mediums) as a finished work – previously they had been used only for working studies for another finished piece, for example by Rubens. He returned to the same subjects over and over again, as both paintings and etchings, but with significantly different compositions each time.

He also executed some sixty [etchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etching "Etching"), arguably the most famous of which is _The Genius of Castiglione_,[\[5\]](#cite_note-5) published by [Giovanni Giacomo de Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Giacomo_de_Rossi "Giovanni Giacomo de Rossi") in 1648. _Diogenes searching for a Man_ is another of the principal of these; others are about religious themes. Some are moralistic stories such as that of the _blind leading the blind_.[\[6\]](#cite_note-6) The etchings are remarkable for light and shade, and have even earned for Castiglione the name of a second [Rembrandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rembrandt "Rembrandt").[\[3\]](#cite_note-EB1911-3) He was exposed to Rembrandt's etching by 1630.

In about 1648 he invented the [monotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotype "Monotype"), the only printmaking technique to be an Italian invention, making over twenty over the succeeding years. His most popular and influential prints were a series of exotic heads, mostly of vaguely Oriental males, but also of women. These were produced in great numbers. Among the subjects of his etchings are _Diogenes with his lantern_, _Noah leading animals into Arc_, _St Joseph Asleep during Flight from Egypt_, _Circe surrounded by animals_, _Silenus at the fountain_, _Nativity_, _Resurrection of Lazarus_, and _The Genius of Castiglione_.[\[7\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESopraniRatti1768313-7)

_The Adoration of the Shepherds_, Louvre

Castiglione was famous for his ability to paint animals, mostly farm animals, and they were often a dominant motif in his paintings. For example, in the painting of _Jesus clearing the temple of Moneylenders_, the religious event is a minor, background part of the painting, the stampede of animals is far more prominent than the scattering of bankers. Castiglione's attention to detail in the depiction of nature is regarded as proof of the influence of Scorza and the Flemish still life and animal painter [Jan Roos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Roos_\(painter\) "Jan Roos (painter)") who was a long-term resident of Genoa.[\[8\]](#cite_note-cas-8) It has been suggested that it was Jan Roos who convinced Castiglione to enliven his religious and mythological compositions with animal and still-life elements.[\[9\]](#cite_note-9)

In Mantua, he received his name of Grechetto, from the classic air of his pastoral depictions. In his later years, he was severely afflicted by gout.[\[10\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESopraniRatti1768314-10) His brother Salvatore and his son Francesco excelled in the same subjects; and it is thought that many paintings which are ascribed to Benedetto are only copies after him, or perhaps originals by his son or brother.[\[3\]](#cite_note-EB1911-3)[\[11\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESopraniRatti1768315-11) He also influenced [Anton Maria Vassallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Maria_Vassallo "Anton Maria Vassallo") (c1640-60).[\[12\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWittkower1993354-12) Among those who engraved after him were [Giovanni Lorenzo Bertolotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Lorenzo_Bertolotto "Giovanni Lorenzo Bertolotto") in Genoa,[\[13\]](#cite_note-13) the Venetian [Anton Maria Zanetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Maria_Zanetti "Anton Maria Zanetti"), [Michele l'Asne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Corneille_the_Younger "Michel Corneille the Younger"), [Louis de Chatillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Chatillon?action=edit&redlink=1 "Louis de Chatillon (page does not exist)"), and [Coreneille Coemans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coreneille_Coemans?action=edit&redlink=1 "Coreneille Coemans (page does not exist)").[\[14\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESopraniRatti1768314–315-14)

His paintings are to be found in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome"), [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice "Venice"), [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples"), [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence "Florence"), and more especially Genoa and [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua "Mantua"). _The Presepio_ (Nativity of [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus")) for the church of [San Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luca "San Luca"), Genoa, ranks among his most celebrated paintings, and the [Louvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louvre "Louvre") contains eight characteristic examples.[\[3\]](#cite_note-EB1911-3) He painted a _SS. Mary Magdalene and Catharine_ for the church of the Madonna di Castello and _St. James defeats the Moors_ for the [Oratorio di San Giacomo della Marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oratory_of_San_Giacomo_della_Marina "Oratory of San Giacomo della Marina"), both in Genoa.
