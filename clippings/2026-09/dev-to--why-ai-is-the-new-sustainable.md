"Future workshop with AI," "creativity with AI," "blah blah blah with AI". It is everywhere: "artificial intelligence" is already the stupidest and most annoying trend in a long time! Facebook and Google infamously lured customers by offering free services, but social media still involved humans before bots and algorithms took control. Working as a professional web developer for decades, I have seen a lot of profiteering with naive hopes, but AI is one of the worst hypes so far. Above all, I'm still shocked by the extent of the blind enthusiasm for a very questionable technology.

## [](#2026-update-on-the-state-of-ai)2026 Update on the State of AI

_Update:_ In 2026, I updated this article to reflect another aspect of AI: the ethical dilemma of using it as a weapon. Literally, [Claude as a soldier](#claude-as-a-soldier) was drafted to help US-American military and secret service. Even without explicit permission, it is technically possible to misuse AI for dangerous purposes, despit its known tendency to make serious errors.

I am no political expert, but I have researched an experienced enough to be sure that AI in its current form is an immature technology and it's dangerous to rely on it except for tasks that you could have achieved without it. Everything else is blind faith or a digital [Dunning-Kruger bias](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunning-Kruger-Effekt).

Recent studies in 2026 suggest that despite its perceived performance boosts, [AI doesn't reduce work. It intensifies it](https://hbr.org/2026/02/ai-doesnt-reduce-work-it-intensifies-it). A 2025 Model Evaluation & Threat Research [study measuring AI impact on developer productivity](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) concluded that respondants feeling faster by 20% actually worked 19% slower in reality.

However, even if you mangaged to use AI productively for an ethical purpose, you might cause more harm than good on a larger scale.

## [](#unpaid-work-and-a-blatant-waste-of-energy)Unpaid work and a blatant waste of energy

In contrast to nuclear power and genetic engineering, criticism of AI remains weak. Many supposedly alternative, environmentally friendly start-ups and solo self-employed people focusing on the common good allow themselves to be infected by the general enthusiasm for AI and switch off their brains entirely following the motto, "Electricity comes from the socket anyway." Artificial intelligence is not sustainable, neither socially nor ecologically.

AI also represents precarious work and a blatant waste of energy. If we regularly use ChatGPT and similar services, we no longer need to worry about our ecological footprint when using search engines, videos, and series streaming. We can also forget about avoiding waste and preferring organic products.

According to a [2022 study](https://www.epri.com/research/products/000000003002028905), a question to ChatGPT consumed around ten times more energy than a Google search. "To train an AI model, processors of hundreds of graphics cards, each consuming about 1000 watts, ran for several weeks. 1000 watts is as much as a kitchen oven," [Greentech.live quoted Amazon's AI expert Ralf Hebrich](https://www.techfieber.de/green/2023/10/23/greentech-studie-ki-und-chatgpt-sind-energie-monster-so-hpi-studie/) as saying.

## [](#nuclear-power-plants-for-microsofts-ai)Nuclear power plants for Microsoft’s AI

"Microsoft has signed a 20-year purchase commitment for electricity from a decommissioned nuclear reactor. The electricity is needed for AI," as German tech magazine [Golem.de recently reported](https://www.golem.de/news/three-mile-island-microsoft-braucht-atomreaktor-fuer-ki-strombedarf-2409-189181.html).

The business model of so-called social networks such as TikTok and Instagram already relied on the unpaid work of the creative industry and hobby content creators, which in turn was used unsolicited as fodder for the AI systems that are now making the jobs of creative juniors and interns redundant. But the self-exploitation of creatives is just the tip of the iceberg: "Workers in Kenya were asked to read sometimes traumatizing texts to optimize ChatGPT," reported Daniel Leisegang from netzpolitik.org e.V. in the article [Precarious Click Work behind the Scenes of ChatGPT](https://netzpolitik.org/2023/globaler-sueden-prekaere-klickarbeit-hinter-den-kulissen-von-chatgpt/).

## [](#dont-switch-off-your-brain)Don’t switch off your brain!

Worse than calculators, smartphones, and "social" media, artificial "intelligence" also endangers human thinking and coexistence. **"Just ask ChatGPT"** is a call for voluntary disenfranchisement and the abandonment of critical thinking. Search engines also deliver incorrect, outdated, or difficult-to-understand results. Even experts can be wrong or bought by advertising partners. But trusting a technology you don't understand, known to invent alternative facts, and generously provided free of charge by a commercial company abroad is even more wrong than consuming Facebook and the BILD newspaper without criticism.

## [](#unreliable-in-several-respects)Unreliable in several respects

AI is not only unreliable in terms of content. There is also no guarantee that it will still be available next year in the same way, or at all, or only at a high price. The sale of Twitter to Elon Musk and its descent into the dubious hate speech and disinformation network X within a few years should vividly illustrate how little you should trust an online service you don't even pay for.

## [](#twitter-the-underestimated-warning)Twitter: the underestimated warning

"Following the takeover by Elon Musk, a fifth of users in Germany have left the short message service, a third are using it less," and complaints about hate speech and fake news have increased, [Süddeutsche Zeitung summarized a Bitkom study](https://www.sueddeutsche.de/wirtschaft/x-twitter-elon-musk-deutschland-studie-bitkom-1.6301719).

## [](#invented-facts-bans-and-injunctions)Invented "facts," bans, and injunctions

Invented sources from ChatGPT have already [led to embarrassing situations in court](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/05/27/nyregion/avianca-airline-lawsuit-chatgpt.html). Actual facts have led to [injunctions against Microsoft and OpenAI by the New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/12/27/business/media/new-york-times-open-ai-microsoft-lawsuit.html), who were able to prove that their elaborately researched articles had been stolen. Renowned specialist forums such as StackOverflow have since [banned the use of ChatGPT](https://meta.stackoverflow.com/questions/421831/policy-generative-ai-e-g-chatgpt-is-banned), so as not to jeopardize the quality of their content.

## [](#ai-creativity-and-art)AI, creativity and art

More exciting than in science, web development, or the advertising industry, whose influence on the standard writing style of ChatGPT is still unmistakable, AI can also provide artists with new tools that, when used naively, are fascinating even at first glance. A simple example was the interactive installation "Tübingen AI Center" at the Tübingen City Museum last year: a camera filmed the spectators and played back a variation of their footage in various surrealistic styles.

## [](#progress-aberration-or-just-another-tool)Progress, Aberration, or just Another Tool?

I admit that I also use AI and waste energy on things I could do analogous with pen and paper. I haven’t opened a printed bilingual dictionary for years, and without deepL and Grammarly, my English would probably be less eloquent and less easy to understand. I also use completion suggestions when programming, but I don't allow AI to generate extensive code blocks on my behalf. But when I do ask ChatGPT, I've usually already come so far with my other research that I've never gotten a single helpful hint from the stupid chatbot.

I have nothing against progress in principle — quite the opposite! But I oppose dumbing down of the people, and I think some aspects of the current AI trend are dangerous steps backward. Let’s criticize AI, but not for the wrong reasons. AI will not make us all unemployed, any more than photography would have done away with painting or even all art because all you have to do is press the shutter button, or calculators would have made math superfluous.

I have been operated on in hospitals and doctors' surgeries, wear glasses and a hearing aid, use computers, and travel on electric express trains, of which there should be more. I wish we had an ecological turnaround in transportation, social justice, and a fast and reliable Internet. I wish society had set different priorities in recent decades.

Thinking of hospitals, I used to work in one as an assistant nurse when I was young. Performing civilian service was an officially accepted alternative to mandatory military service. And a useful one as well! While our generation was doing dirty work, others were already preparing to technology to replace humans on the battlefield. Autonomous drones, surveillance and secret service intelligence have been assisted by electronic tech long before "AI" became a buzzword.

### [](#claude-as-a-soldier)Claude as a Soldier

Decades later, the United States Department of Defense's demand to drop AI safeguards on Anthropic's artificial intelligence services better known as Claude, ignited debates about AI's military role and ethhics of a digital arms race resembling the dilemma of the nuclear physicists driven to develop a nuclear bomb for the USA before Hitler's army got it first.

[![A cartoon meme about Claude as a robot being drafted by the army to become a soldier.](https://media2.dev.to/dynamic/image/width=800%2Cheight=%2Cfit=scale-down%2Cgravity=auto%2Cformat=auto/https%3A%2F%2Fdev-to-uploads.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fuploads%2Farticles%2F12vl4k9p0uf1fv4k8ycx.png)](https://media2.dev.to/dynamic/image/width=800%2Cheight=%2Cfit=scale-down%2Cgravity=auto%2Cformat=auto/https%3A%2F%2Fdev-to-uploads.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fuploads%2Farticles%2F12vl4k9p0uf1fv4k8ycx.png)

This cartoon showing Claude as a cute tin robot getting drafted for miliary service illustrates a serious issue. Anthropic's safety-first ethos colliding with the Pentagon boosted Claude's popularity even further after it's Sonnet and Opus models already topped the list of the least unrealiable AI services for civilian use.

[Anthropic's official statement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/statement-department-of-war) strives to balance responsible ethics with patriotic compliance:

> However, in a narrow set of cases, we believe AI can undermine, rather than defend, democratic values. Some uses are also simply outside the bounds of what today’s technology can safely and reliably do.

Realistically, it is probably not within their power to prevent the military from using their services. As we experienced with Google dropping their early days' "don't be evil motto" and Elon Musk's hostile takeover of Twitter surrounded by a controversial debate about what is free speech and what isn't, honorable standards often cannot be upheld for long.

The 1980s Hollywood drama [Wargames](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0086567/) about a young computer hacker trying to find a famous computer scientist and prevent artificial intelligence accidentally starting a word war hallucinating a hostile missile attack when it was supposed to play a simulation, the film doesn't seem very unrealistic anymore.

## [](#is-ai-the-new-sustainable)Is AI the new Sustainable?

AI is certainly not the new sustainable. AI is here to stay, but this article's opening thesis was intentionally ironic from the start. Both "AI" and sustainable are overused vague terms misused for advertising purposes unless they lost their meaning.

"Sustainability" often isn't sustainable, but instead so-called [greenwashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwashing), a misleading advertising message. "Artificial intelligence" often isn't intelligent, but instead — you guessed it — a misleading advertising message.

"AI" is the new "sustainable": a euphemistic message that, soon enough, nobody will want to hear anymore when it has become completely meaningless and, above all, a sign of a lack of ideas and stupidity.

Use digital tools, but question them, don’t become dependent on them, and stop adding "AI" to every new product announcement!
