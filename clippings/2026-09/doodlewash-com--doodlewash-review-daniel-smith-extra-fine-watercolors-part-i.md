Daniel Smith Extra Fine Watercolors.  Extra fine- truer words have never been said.  I’m not sure this brand needs much of an introduction. These are artist quality paints. I’ve spent more time looking at [Daniel Smith](http://www.danielsmith.com/Category--DANIEL-SMITH-Paints--m-1461) (DS) watercolor swatches than all other art supplies rolled into one.  The Daniel Smith watercolor line is so extensive, I wasn’t sure how to begin to tackle this post.  I’ve decided to break it into two posts.  Part one, the Extra Fine line, and part two, the PrimaTek and Luminescent lines.

[![Daniel Smith Extra Fine Watercolor tubes with a painted mandala stone by Elspeth Mclean](https://i0.wp.com/doodlewash.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/painttubes-e1526306259296.jpg?resize=1024%2C768&ssl=1)](https://i0.wp.com/doodlewash.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/painttubes.jpg?ssl=1)

Center Stone by [Elspeth McLean](http://www.elspethmclean.com/#!mandala-stones/c3u4)

Mr. Daniel Smith [started the company](http://www.danielsmith.com/content--id-167) in the basement of his Seattle, Washington home in 1976. The company is based out of Seattle, they also have a store in Bellevue, WA.

If I counted correctly- there are 247 watercolors in their line.  This number includes their Extra Fine, PrimaTek, and Luminescent varieties.  They all come in [15ml. tubes](http://www.danielsmith.com/ItemList--DANIEL-SMITH-Watercolor--m-1451) , 88 colors come in [5 ml. tubes](http://www.danielsmith.com/SubCategory--Watercolor-5ml--m-1491), and 51 colors come in [sticks](http://www.danielsmith.com/ItemList--Watercolor-Sticks--m-752).  Very few sets are sold, but there are a handful of starter sets and you can see them [here](http://www.danielsmith.com/ItemList--Watercolor-Paint-Sets--m-750).  I found this [Daniel Smith Extra Fine Essentials Introductory Set of 6](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B00WT5VRF6/ref=as_li_tl?ie=UTF8&camp=1789&creative=9325&creativeASIN=B00WT5VRF6&linkCode=as2&tag=doodlewash-20&linkId=23b1d910ba1a08d6bb8d1e41bba7c76c) on Amazon for about $28.  The Daniel Smith site has a $4.95 flat shipping rate, so does Dick Blick and Jerry’s Artarama.  Many local art stores sell this brand and these sets.

**DOT CARDS!**

Dot Cards are 238 dots of paint- these are like a gateway drug to these paints.  It’s amazing how much paint you can get off of a little dot.  Some art stores give out smaller versions of the cards.  My local art store gave me a small Dot Card of some of the [PrimaTek](http://www.danielsmith.com/content--id-513) line, after that, it was all over.  I went PrimaTek crazy.  Real ground minerals in paints?!  I was hooked. Eventually I got the entire four page Dot Card set.

[![Daniel Smith watercolor Dot Cards](https://i0.wp.com/doodlewash.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/dotcards-e1526306350976.jpg?resize=1024%2C768&ssl=1)](https://i0.wp.com/doodlewash.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/dotcards.jpg?ssl=1)

If you are truly interested in this brand, get the [Dot Cards](http://www.danielsmith.com/Item--i-001-900-482-LIST)– usually around $25 for the entire set.

Amazon has the [238 set](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B00US4V6X8/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=doodlewash-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=B00US4V6X8&linkId=9910a008a2a74d56c09e6044c62847d3) for $22 and the [66 set](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B00US4UZ3A/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=doodlewash-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=B00US4UZ3A&linkId=195bdb4cbe468ba5a37fb318c1d0ea74) for around $8.

Wet Paint has a [66 color](http://www.wetpaintart.com/daniel-smith-try-it-sheet-66-colors.html) try it sheet for $4.49 at the writing of this post. A good deal if you are planning on ordering some other things.

[The MerriArtist](http://www.merriartist.com/Daniel_Smith_Watercolor_s/904.htm?gclid=Cj0KEQjwmKG5BRDv4YaE5t6oqf0BEiQAwqDNfHbtrXPAS2Nof2EmO4TTkqS2IXUwKiYhA15U0bv6ft8aAszt8P8HAQ)– free smaller Dot Card with $15 DS purchase.

I always think- that’s it, I’m done- no more tubes of paint. Insert [Jaws](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZvCI-gNK_y4) theme music.  Then just when I think it’s safe to let my guard down and go back into the water- paint shark!  I find one I must have.  Recently that was [Cascade Green](http://www.danielsmith.com/Item--i-284-600-142-LIST) (PBr7 PB15 these are the pigment numbers that the paint is mixed from).  This is when the Dot Cards come in handy.  Looking at swatches on the screen is fine and all, but it’s just not the same as trying the paint out.  I find the dot of paint is enough to try the color in small paintings or sketches, along with doing a swatch.

Cascade Green

Most of the traditional colors in the DS Extra Fine line are single pigment, but they have quite a few fun mixes that usually have a separation and granulating effect.  I think some of the celestial names got me- Moonglow, Lunar Blue, Lunar Violet.  Who can resist a name like the mysterious Undersea Green?  I can’t.  Many of the multi-pigment paints can be mixed for from individual pigments.

[![Daniel Smith watercolor painting of a mermaid embroidery pattern by Aimee Ray](https://i0.wp.com/doodlewash.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/mermaids.jpg?resize=568%2C426&ssl=1)](https://i0.wp.com/doodlewash.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/mermaids.jpg?ssl=1)

Pattern Drawing by [Aimee Ray](http://aimeeray.com/)

[Undersea Green](http://www.danielsmith.com/item--i-284-600-109)– (PB29 PO49) this is my favorite paint. The lower half of the outside mermaids is Undersea Green.

> “An artisan’s favorite, this exciting medium staining green blends French Ultramarine with Quinacridone Gold. The inorganic, sedimentary French Ultramarine settles and granulates while the organic, transparent Quinacridone Gold floats into a golden halo.”

[Moonglow](http://www.danielsmith.com/Item--i-284-600-057-LIST)– (PG18 PB29 PR177 ) beautiful use of this paint, in a more traditional and an abstract painting [here](http://www.jennygranberry.com/blog/2014/3/10/daniel-smith-watercolor-moonglow).

> “Water frees this amazing three-pigment blend to perform miracles. Watch and wait as Anthraquinoid Red floats, Ultramarine Blue settles and Viridian grays the resulting violet color. Selectively blot and lift a surface wash to expose delicate blue-greens. A description of the fascinating light and dark washes can never match a personal experience!”

One thing missing from this post is pronunciation help! Anthraquinoid, Phthalo, Quinacridone, Indanthrone, Naphthamide. I’m glad I’m writing these to you, and not pronouncing them.  I attempted to put some links to pronunciations, in case you are at the art store and wanting to ask questions.  But, those all pronounce them differently, or they added a weird statement at the end.
