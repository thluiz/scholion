# 3 things you should and shouldn’t tolerate on your team

I think all leaders are passionate about creating a healthy culture on the teams they lead. However, over the past few months I’ve learned an important lesson: a **passion for having** a healthy culture doesn’t always translate into **having** a healthy culture.

Here’s what else I’ve learned: if the team I lead has an unhealthy culture, it doesn’t necessarily mean it’s full of dysfunctional people… but that I’ve **tolerated** dysfunctional behaviors.

A few weeks ago, with a renewed commitment to actually having a healthy culture on our team, I developed six guidelines: three things we will tolerate on our team and three things we will not. A healthy culture begins with clearly defined expectations – so here’s what I shared with our team:

**What we will tolerate**

- **Failing well:** We can and will fall short while giving our best to accomplish something great.
- **Ideological conflict:** We can and will hash out issues, sometimes passionately, in an effort to achieve the best outcome for the organization.
- **Hard conversations:** We will pursue confrontation (rooted in love and respect) as often as necessary to achieve clarity and unity.

**What we will not tolerate**

- **Failing poorly:** We will not allow ourselves to fall short because we were too proud to ask for help or too lazy to give our best.
- **Personal conflict:** We won’t use reactive or insecure statements, passive-aggressive remarks or body language, or anything that represents an effort to achieve a desired outcome for our ego instead of our mission.
- **Side conversations:** We don’t do back-stabbing, side-talking, or information leaking aimed at discrediting anyone else on the team, no matter how “noble” it may appear.

*What are you tolerating right now that you shouldn’t be? What’s your next move to create a healthier culture on your team?*
