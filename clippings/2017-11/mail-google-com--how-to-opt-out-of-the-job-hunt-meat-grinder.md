| |  | | --- | | Hey Thiago Luiz,    Back when I was 21 my promising career as a developer went into a complete tailspin.    At the time I was working for a financial sector startup out in Phoenix. When the Twin Towers came fell on 9-11, my employer went down right along with them.    I had no prospects, so my wife and I packed up what little we had and moved back to Florida, where my parents lived.    My parents didn't really have space for us, so we ended up sleeping on the pullout couch in the living room.    (NOT the ideal situation for a couple of young newlyweds, let me tell ya...)    This was long before Simple Programmer, long before I'd figured out how this whole software developer job hunt thing REALLY works.    So I did what I knew how to do, which was spend hours each day searching Monster, Dice and Indeed, and spamming out my resume at any company that posted a position I seemed even remotely qualified for.    This whole process sucks. But you know what the WORST part is?    Aside from the low-level, feel-it-in-the-pit-of-your-stomach stress and anxiety from being unemployed (that gradually rises toward panic as the weeks go by)...    The most frustrating part is how you NEVER hear back.    From ANYONE.    These companies force you to jump through all their little hoops...    Fill out long application forms...    Write a bunch of cover letters...    And when they eliminate you from consideration, they can't be bothered to send you a lousy email.    I mean COME ON.    You're never sure whether something's about to come through, or whether to just keep applying and applying...    Then one day out of the blue you'll get a call—they want you to come in and interview.    And as you probably know by now, there's nothing like a day-long tech interview gauntlet to crank your stress levels up to 11...    Because they're not really interested in finding out what you can \*really\* do.    Instead the whole process is designed to eliminate you as fast as possible.    They call it, "avoiding false positives."     Me, I call it "torture."     I'd go to these interviews and wind up sweating through my shirt while they made me dance like a monkey with a whiteboard marker, struggling through some obscure algorithm...    Not fun.    I didn't realize it back then, but I was making a VERY common mistake:    I was CHASING opportunities.    And when you chase opportunities (instead of attracting them to come to YOU), usually what happens is you run straight into a meat grinder.    That's what this whole "human resources" hiring game really is—a meat grinder.    It's a machine designed to chew through as many job seekers (read YOU) as possible in as short a time as possible...    Then hire the MOST credentialed developer they can find for the LOWEST salary that developer will accept.    Here's a good way to visualize it:    Imagine you have a funnel.    You and all the other candidates who apply go in the top...    And at the bottom is the ONE job you're all competing for.    This is what happens next:    For every new job that gets posted on the jobs boards, 250 developers apply. (That's an AVERAGE—competitive jobs might see 1,000 applications.)     Then your resume gets run through a keyword filter, and if you're lucky you get quick 6 second scan from some HR flunky.    98% applicants never make it past this point.    98%!    Only the 2% who survive this screening make it through to a hiring manager.    Of those, only 3-4 get even get a shot at interviewing.    Then into the ring you climb for 2-3 rounds of "software developer gladiator death match," because everyone knows that when it comes to the hire:    "There can be only ONE."    If you're fortunate enough to survive all this and get an offer, you're feeling pretty damn lucky that ANYONE would want to hire you EVER.    So when it's time to negotiate your salary, just how much are you going to ask for with 249 people waiting in line to take this same job?    Now...    Contrast all that to an experience I had just a few years later.    At this point I'd been writing my blog, Simple Programmer, for about a year.    And I'd also taken a few other steps toward establishing myself as a respected authority in the local software community in Boise, Idaho.    One day I'm sitting at my desk at work, minding my own business, when my phone rings.    It's the software development manager from a local company that I'd never heard of. They had something to do with graphics and animation—can't remember exactly.    This guy says, "Hey John, we all read your blog over here and we're big fans. I have a job opening up and I'd like to hire you for it."    Now I wasn't terribly interested in the job.    But years of clawing and struggling just to get a foot in the door had conditioned me that you don't just pass up an opportunity like this.    So I said sure, when would you like me to come down for an interview.    "No, no, you don't understand," he replied. "I'm not asking you to APPLY.     "I'm OFFERING you the job—it's yours if you want it."    THAT blew my mind.    After literally spending YEARS chasing jobs the "traditional" way, to get an offer like this out of the blue? And from someone who'd never even met me?    What happened here?    Well, thanks to my blog, and the other strategies I was using to position myself as an expert, without really even realizing it was I'd flipped this whole "job hunt funnel" idea around.    Only now it was working in MY favor.    Now instead of me fighting tooth and nail with hundreds of other candidates for ONE job...    There were dozens of companies who all wanted a piece of ME.    Let me tell you so much better to be in this position, because not only can you relax and let the really great job opportunities come to you...    You also get to pick and choose only the best, most interesting and most promising companies...    Who are solving the most challenging and interesting problems...    And when it comes time to talk salary, you have ALL the leverage—  you can literally ask for the moon and they'll be HAPPY to pay it.    Now that I've experienced both sides of this equation, my recommendation for other developers is:    Start TODAY.     Pick an area to specialize in.    Begin building a name and a reputation for yourself.    Believe me, if I can do this, you can do it too.    And while it does take some upfront effort on your part (although not as much as you might think)...    The payoff is SO far above and beyond what you might expect that it makes the time investment look trivial by comparison.
