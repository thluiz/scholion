**Speculative fiction** is an [umbrella term](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella_term "Umbrella term") encompassing the more fantastical [fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiction "Fiction") [genres](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_genre "Literary genre"), specifically [science fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction"), [fantasy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy "Fantasy"), [horror](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_fiction "Horror fiction"), [weird fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weird_fiction "Weird fiction"), [supernatural fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural_fiction "Supernatural fiction"), [superhero fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superhero_fiction "Superhero fiction"), [utopian and dystopian fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopian_and_dystopian_fiction "Utopian and dystopian fiction"), [apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalyptic_and_post-apocalyptic_fiction "Apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction"), and [alternate history](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternate_history_(fiction) "Alternate history (fiction)") in [literature](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature "Literature") as well as related static, motion, and virtual arts.[[1]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-atwood-1)

## History[[edit source](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Speculative_fiction&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1 "Edit section: History")]

Speculative fiction as a category ranges from [ancient](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient "Ancient") works to both [cutting edge](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_art "State of the art"), [paradigm](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradigm "Paradigm")-changing and neotraditional works of the 21st century.[[2]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-2)[[3]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-3) Speculative fiction can be recognized in works whose[authors](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Author "Author")' [intentions](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authorial_intent "Authorial intent") or the [social contexts](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_context "Social context") of the versions of stories they portrayed is now known, since ancient [Greek](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") [dramatists](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramatist "Dramatist") such as [Euripides](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripides "Euripides") (ca. 480–406 BCE) whose play *[Medea](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medea "Medea")* seems to have offended [Athenian](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian "Athenian") audiences when he fictionally speculated that shamaness [Medea](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medea "Medea") killed her own children instead of their being killed by other [Corinthians](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth "Ancient Corinth") after her departure,[[4]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-4) and whose [Hippolytus](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippolytus_(play) "Hippolytus (play)"), narratively introduced by [Aphrodite](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphrodite "Aphrodite"), Goddess of Love in person, is suspected to have displeased his contemporary audiences because he portrayed [Phaedra](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phaedra_(mythology) "Phaedra (mythology)") as too lusty.[[5]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-5)

In [historiography](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography "Historiography"), what is now called speculative fiction has previously been termed "historical invention",[[6]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-6) "[historical fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_fiction "Historical fiction")", and similar names. It is extensively noted in [literary criticism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_criticism "Literary criticism") of the works of[William Shakespeare](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare "William Shakespeare")[[7]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-7) as when he co-locates [Athenian](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian "Athenian") Duke [Theseus](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theseus "Theseus") and [Amazonian](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazons "Amazons") Queen [Hippolyta](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippolyta "Hippolyta"), [English](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England") fairy [Puck](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puck_(mythology) "Puck (mythology)"), and [Roman](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_mythology "Roman mythology") god [Cupid](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupid "Cupid") across time and space in the [Fairyland](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairy "Fairy") of its[Merovingian](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merovingian "Merovingian") [Germanic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples "Germanic peoples") sovereign [Oberon](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberon "Oberon") in *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream "A Midsummer Night's Dream").*[[8]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-8)

In [mythography](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythography "Mythography") the concept of speculative fiction has been termed "mythopoesis" or [mythopoeia](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythopoeia "Mythopoeia"), "fictional speculation", the creative design and generation of lore, regarding such works as [J.R.R. Tolkien](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien")'s*[The Lord of the Rings](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings "The Lord of the Rings")*.[[9]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-9) Such [supernatural](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural "Supernatural"), [alternate history](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternate_history "Alternate history") and [sexuality](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexuality "Human sexuality") themes continue in works produced within the modern speculative fiction genre.[[10]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-10)

The creation of speculative fiction in its general sense of hypothetical [history](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History "History"), [explanation](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explanation "Explanation"), or [ahistorical](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahistorical "Ahistorical") [storytelling](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storytelling "Storytelling") has also been attributed to authors in ostensibly [non-fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-fiction "Non-fiction") mode since as early as[Herodotus](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus") of [Halicarnassus](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halicarnassus "Halicarnassus"), (fl. 5th century BCE) in his Histories,[[11]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-11)[[12]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-12)[[13]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-13) and was already both practiced and edited out by early encyclopaedic writers like [Sima Qian](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sima_Qian "Sima Qian") (ca. 145 or 135 BCE–86 BCE), author of [Shiji](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiji "Shiji").[[14]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-14)[[15]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-15)

This suggests the [caveat](http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/caveat "wikt:caveat") that while many works now considered to be intentional or unintentional speculative fiction existed before the coining of the [genre](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genre "Genre") term, its concept in its broadest sense captures both a [conscious](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscious "Conscious") and [unconscious](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconscious_mind "Unconscious mind") aspect of human [psychology](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology") in making sense of the [world](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World "World"), reacting to it, and creating [imaginary](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagination "Imagination"), [inventive](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventive "Inventive"), and [artistic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artistic "Artistic") expressions, some of which underlie practical progress through [interpersonal](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpersonal "Interpersonal") influences, [social](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social "Social") and [cultural](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural "Cultural") movements, [scientific](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific "Scientific") research and advances, and [philosophy of science](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science "Philosophy of science").[[16]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-16)[[17]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-17)[[18]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-18)

In its [English language](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") usage in [arts](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts "Arts") and [literature](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature "Literature") since 20th century, "speculative fiction" as a [genre](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genre "Genre") term is often attributed to [Robert A. Heinlein](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Heinlein "Robert A. Heinlein"). In his first known use of the term, in editorial material at the front of the 2/8/1947 issue of *[The Saturday Evening Post](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Saturday_Evening_Post "The Saturday Evening Post")*, [Heinlein](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Heinlein "Robert A. Heinlein") used it specifically as a synonym for "[science fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction")"; in a later piece, he explicitly stated that his use of the term did not include[fantasy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy "Fantasy").

However, though Heinlein may have come up with the term on his own, there are earlier citations: a piece in *[Lippincott's Monthly Magazine](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lippincott%27s_Monthly_Magazine "Lippincott's Monthly Magazine")* in 1889 used the term in reference to [Edward Bellamy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bellamy "Edward Bellamy")'s *[Looking Backward: 2000–1887](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Looking_Backward "Looking Backward")* and other works; and one in the May, 1900 issue of *[The Bookman](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bookman "The Bookman")[*[disambiguation needed](http://toolserver.org/~dispenser/cgi-bin/dab_solver.py?page=Speculative_fiction&amp;editintro=Template:Disambiguation_needed/editintro&amp;client=Template:Dn)*]* said that [John Uri Lloyd](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Uri_Lloyd "John Uri Lloyd")'s *[Etidorhpa, The End of the Earth](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etidorhpa "Etidorhpa")* had "created a great deal of discussion among people interested in speculative fiction".[[19]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-19) A variation on this term is "speculative literature".[[20]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-20)

The use of "speculative fiction" in the sense of expressing dissatisfaction with traditional or [establishment](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Establishment "The Establishment") science fiction was popularized in the 1960s and early 1970s by [Judith Merril](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_Merril "Judith Merril") and other writers and editors, in connection with the [New Wave](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Wave_(science_fiction) "New Wave (science fiction)") movement. It fell into disuse around the mid-1970s.[[21]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-21)

The [Internet Speculative Fiction Database](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Speculative_Fiction_Database "Internet Speculative Fiction Database") contains a broad list of different subtypes.

In the 2000s, the term came into wider use as a convenient collective term for a set of genres.

[Academic journals](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_journals "Academic journals") which publish [essays](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essay "Essay") on speculative fiction include *[Extrapolation](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrapolation_(journal) "Extrapolation (journal)")*, and *[Foundation](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_-_The_International_Review_of_Science_Fiction "Foundation - The International Review of Science Fiction")*.[[22]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-22)

## Distinguishing speculative fiction from science fiction[[edit source](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Speculative_fiction&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2 "Edit section: Distinguishing speculative fiction from science fiction")]

"Speculative fiction" is sometimes abbreviated "spec-fic", "specfic",[[23]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-23) "S-F", "SF", or "sf"[[24]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-24) but these last three abbreviations are ambiguous as they have long been used to refer to [science fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction"), which lies within this general range of literature,[[25]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-25) and in [several other abbreviations](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SF_(disambiguation) "SF (disambiguation)").

The term has been used to express dissatisfaction with what some people consider the limitations of science fiction, or otherwise to designate fiction that falls under readily stereotypical genres so that it can be pigeonholed within such categorical limits as "fantasy" or "mystery".[[26]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-26) For example, in [Harlan Ellison](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlan_Ellison "Harlan Ellison")'s writing, the term may signal a wish not to be [pigeonholed](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeonholing "Pigeonholing") as a science fiction writer, and a desire to break out of science fiction's genre conventions in a [literary](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_fiction "Literary fiction") and [modernist](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_literature "Modernist literature") direction; or to escape the prejudice with which science fiction is often met by mainstream critics.[[27]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-27)[[28]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-28)

The term "suppositional fiction" is sometimes used as a sub-category designating fiction in which characters and stories are constrained by an internally consistent world, but not necessarily one defined by any particular genre.[[29]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-29)[[30]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-30)[[31]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction#cite_note-31)
