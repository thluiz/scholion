## Definition[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=New_Weird&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2 "Edit section: Definition")]

Various definitions have been given of the genre. According to [Jeff VanderMeer](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_VanderMeer "Jeff VanderMeer") and [Ann VanderMeer](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_VanderMeer "Ann VanderMeer"), in their introduction to the anthology *The New Weird*, the genre is "a type of urban, secondary-world fiction that subverts the romanticized ideas about place found in traditional [fantasy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy "Fantasy"), largely by choosing realistic, complex real-world models as the jumping off point for creation of settings that may combine elements of both science fiction and fantasy."[[2]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Weird#cite_note-2)According to [Gardner Dozois](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardner_Dozois "Gardner Dozois"), however, the VanderMeers' anthology "ultimately left me just as confused as to what exactly The New Weird consisted of when I went out as I'd been when I went in."[[3]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Weird#cite_note-3) Robin Anne Reid notes that while the definition of the New Weird is disputed, "a general consensus uses the term" to describe fictions that "subvert cliches of the fantastic in order to put them to discomfiting, rather than consoling ends". [[1]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Weird#cite_note-rar-1) Reid also notes the genre tends to break down the barriers between [fantasy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy "Fantasy"), [science fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction") and [supernatural horror](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_fiction "Horror fiction"). [[1]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Weird#cite_note-rar-1) In comparing The New Weird to [Bizarro fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bizarro_fiction "Bizarro fiction"), Rose O'keefe of [Eraserhead Press](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Eraserhead_Press&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1 "Eraserhead Press (page does not exist)") claims that "People buy New Weird because they want cutting edge speculative fiction with a literary slant. It’s kind of like slipstream with a side of weirdness."[[4]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Weird#cite_note-4)

Part of this genre's roots derive from [pulp](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_magazine "Pulp magazine") horror authors, whose stories were sometimes described as "[weird fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weird_fiction "Weird fiction")". The "weird tale" label also evolved from the magazine *[Weird Tales](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weird_Tales "Weird Tales")*; the stories therein often combined fantasy elements, existential and physical terror, and [science fiction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction") devices.[[5]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Weird#cite_note-5)
