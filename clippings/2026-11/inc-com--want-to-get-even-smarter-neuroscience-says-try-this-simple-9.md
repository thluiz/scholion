Want to Get Even Smarter? Neuroscience Says Try This Simple 9-Minute Routine Twice a Week

Especially if you want to improve memory, attention, and information processing. (And who doesn’t?)

EXPERT OPINION BY JEFF HADEN @JEFF_HADEN

OCT 11, 2023

Ask people which factor contributes the most to success — in whatever way you choose to define “success” — and even though science says you also have to be lucky, most will choose intelligence.

Granted, there are a number of different forms of intelligence. So let’s focus on two. “Crystallized intelligence” is accumulated knowledge: facts, figures. Think “educated.”

Of course, “book smart” doesn’t necessarily mean smart smart. That’s where “fluid intelligence” comes into play: the ability to use retained new information to solve a problem, to learn a new skill, to recall existing memories and modify them with new knowledge. Think “applied intelligence.” (Or what Steve Jobs called the ability to make connections.)

All of which gets tougher over time, since multiple domains of cognitive function naturally decline with age.
