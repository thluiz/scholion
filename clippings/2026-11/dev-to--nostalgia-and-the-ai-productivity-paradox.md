There is something strangely nostalgic about November 2026.

GTA VI has finally arrived after 13 years of waiting. I've returned to Azeroth in World of Warcraft: Forever, a world I have so many great memories of. Meanwhile, my AI coding agent is writing a lot of code that I would have written myself just a few years ago.

Twenty-five years ago, I was fascinated by the virtual worlds that software developers and game designers could create. Eventually, I became a software developer myself.

Now, I wonder whether GTA VI might be one of the last great games of an era of software development that I grew up admiring.

And whether that era is slowly ending for people like me, too.

## [](#the-fun-part-of-software-development)The fun part of software development

We keep discussing how software development isn't going to disappear and how we will still need software developers.

Yeah, I believe that too.

Software development has never been just about coding. But coding was a really fun part of it.

Remember that feeling when you finally ran your application for the first time and it actually worked?

When I was a junior developer, it was more about coding than designing software. I was learning what programming languages could do, exploring their syntax, and simply enjoying coding itself.

When I became a mid-level developer, I started overengineering things, applying design patterns and object-oriented principles wherever I could. The fun was now split between designing and coding.

When I became a senior developer, I started making things simpler. I took pride not only in refactoring complex code into something simple and readable but also in showing junior developers how to achieve the same results with simpler designs or less complex code.

Yeah, pretty much the opposite of my mid-level developer phase. 😆

But at every stage of my career, I enjoyed coding. I took pride in the craft of writing good code, not just designing software.

And now, I feel like we are slowly losing that.

## [](#the-azeroth-paradox)The Azeroth paradox

It's like leveling my Tauren Hunter in Azeroth.

Grinding XP, completing quests, exploring the world, and learning how to play my class are all part of the fun.

Technically, I could automate the entire leveling process. I could wake up tomorrow with a character several levels higher without spending hours playing the game.

But what would be the point?

I would lose a lot of the fun, and perhaps more importantly, I would miss the learning experience that comes with getting to know my character and class.

In software development, we are increasingly skipping the coding part because we can automate it.

We still get the result.

But are we learning the same things along the way? And are we still as proud of the result as we used to be?

## [](#adapting-doesnt-mean-we-cant-miss-the-past)Adapting doesn't mean we can't miss the past

Don't get me wrong. Our industry has changed and will continue to change. We have to adapt, or we risk becoming irrelevant.

I use AI coding agents every day. I understand their value, and I'm not planning to stop using them.

But accepting the future doesn't mean we can't miss something about the past.

Perhaps we spent so many years trying to automate software development that we forgot how much we actually enjoyed doing it.

So, where the hell does all that fun and pride go?

And isn't it somewhat ironic that after spending my working day trying to automate as much as possible, I spend my evening in Azeroth doing things manually because that's exactly what makes them enjoyable?

## [](#the-price-of-progress)The price of progress

Maybe in twenty years, a new generation of software developers will look back at today's AI-assisted development with the same nostalgia I feel about writing code manually. Maybe they will find joy in things that I cannot even imagine today.

And maybe that's just how technological progress works.

Perhaps progress also means losing some of the things that made us enjoy being software developers.

Anyway, my AI agent has probably finished its task by now.

And my Tauren still needs a few more levels...
