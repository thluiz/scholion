Anthropic raised a staggering $81 billion in its IPO Thursday, selling 980,000,000 shares at $83 a piece. The company’s shares are set to hit the public markets above $100 each, with a $2 trillion valuation in sight.

And so, after much debate about whether AI technology could become a real business, Anthropic just turned in the largest initial public offering in history.

The IPO is not the end of the story, though, as Anthropic will now be faced with plenty of challenges ahead. On Anthropic’s IPO day, we look at what got Anthropic here, and what’s next:

## **Speed**

It’s hard to overstate just how fast Anthropic went from an idea to the world’s largest IPO. In the middle of Covid, the company’s co-founders sat outdoors in a San Francisco park, planning the company’s future in chairs they brought from home. Today, it’s become one of the world’s largest companies, a testament to the rapid pace of AI progress and how quickly the new technology is changing the world.

### **A correct bet on enterprise**

Early in the generative AI era, Anthropic correctly bet on enterprise use cases. The idea was that A) Tackling harder enterprise problems would force it to build smarter models, and B) Getting these problems right could be very lucrative. Anthropic made this wager before there was consensus that AI models could become smart enough to handle these types of problems. It hit on the bet.

### **More than just an AI model company**

In early 2025, Anthropic was mostly an AI model company. It sold its AI via an API, and its Claude chatbot was little used. Since then, the company has transformed its business. It is now an applications company with good AI models. Hits like Claude Code have moved Anthropic upstream. The company’s future rides on growing more Claude-Code-style applications for use in industries like manufacturing, life sciences, graphic design, healthcare, and general knowledge work.

### **Intense recent growth**

Since making the applications pivot, Anthropic has grown furiously. It had 1,500 customers spending $100,000 or more in December 2025 and reached 11,900 such customers by September 2026. The company grew revenue by 147% between Q1 and Q2 in 2026 (from $4.7 billion to $11.6 billion) and another 47% in Q3 ($17 billion), demonstrating solid growth even after the tokenmaxxing craze fizzled.

### **A question of sustainability**

Once the initial IPO excitement fades, Anthropic’s ability to sustain its momentum will come into question. The company’s customers are still working to determine AI’s ROI, and once-flowing budgets are starting to tighten. “Cost discipline” is AI’s new buzzword after runaway token spending dominated most of the first half of this year.

### **Challenges everywhere**

Anthropic will also have to fend off challengers coming from all directions: Open weight models, cheap standard models from companies like Meta that competently get the job done, and model routers that switch customers off the frontier. And then there’s the public’s opposition to AI and the data centers that power it. This won’t be easy.

### **A sad trend for retail investors**

SpaceX and Anthropic have both IPO’d at valuations well above $1 trillion. Companies waiting this long to IPO means most of the upside is captured in the private markets, leaving retail investors largely out of the wealth creation picture. It’s a shame, and will likely exacerbate the criticism around AI’s impact on income inequality.

### **Anthropic’s new dilemma**

Anthropic has long claimed safety as a north star, but the public markets will test that commitment. Investors who prioritize quarterly revenue and profits may have less patience than VCs with multi-year commitment to the company. And the safety vs. revenue issue could get especially thorny as models get smarter and the balance becomes harder to maintain.
