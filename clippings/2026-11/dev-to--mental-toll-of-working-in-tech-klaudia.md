Hi folks! 👋 It's been a while since I last wrote here. As some of you may remember, some time ago I published a very personal article about my struggles with burnout:

What was supposed to be a small rant became the most popular article I've ever written. At some point, the number of comments exceeded my expectations. This made me realize how serious the problem of burnout is – I literally got dozens of comments from community members who had gone through, or are currently struggling with the same thing.

That's why I decided to start a series of posts devoted to mental health. In the first article, I'd like to have a closer look at some of the most common **mental threats** we face in the tech industry. Not to scare you and add another brick to your [doomscrolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doomscrolling) list. Rather, to make you aware that all of us are struggling to some extent. And that it's normal.

So, let's start with the one that found me first – right at the beginning of my tech journey.

## [](#impostor-syndrome)Impostor syndrome

I remember my internship as a tech writer. Everything was new to me, as tech wasn't even an industry I considered for my future job. I remember sitting behind my desk, trying to get my head around all the jargon. I questioned literally every task that I did, checking it multiple times before asking anyone for review. _What am I doing here among all these intelligent people? Do I deserve my place here? What if someone discovers that I do not fit?_ - these thoughts were spinning around in my head every day.

At some point, I was even too afraid to ask questions (which is essential in my job!), afraid that I would just look silly. Until I discovered that everyone, even the PM I always looked up to, is just improvising. Everyone is doing their best, but also doubting themselves sometimes. I remember once I went to my PM to ask how a new feature fits into a bigger product architecture. I was very nervous, thinking that it was something I missed, or that I did not understand the product I work on. The PM replied: "You know what... I don't know either. Let's ask the architect." How many more people also didn't know but kept quiet to not make a fool of themselves?

[![Iconographic showing impostor from "Among Us" game sitting stressed behind the desk; on the right side, there are thoughts that are accompanying the impostor syndrome](https://media2.dev.to/dynamic/image/width=800%2Cheight=%2Cfit=scale-down%2Cgravity=auto%2Cformat=auto/https%3A%2F%2Fdev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com%2Fuploads%2Farticles%2Foml60ub8k998dcdrayew.jpeg)](https://media2.dev.to/dynamic/image/width=800%2Cheight=%2Cfit=scale-down%2Cgravity=auto%2Cformat=auto/https%3A%2F%2Fdev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com%2Fuploads%2Farticles%2Foml60ub8k998dcdrayew.jpeg)

I think that now, in the AI era, we face impostor syndrome 2.0. We not only compare ourselves to other people, but also to AI. The internal monologue is similar: _What am I doing here if AI can do this for me? Do I even still deserve my place here? What if someone discovers that they can just use AI instead of paying me?_

Honestly, I don't have a clever answer to this one. It feels like it's an old fear wearing a new mask, and I think we're all still figuring out how to deal with it.

## [](#fomo-fear-of-missing-out)FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)

When writing this article, I remembered that [@konark\_13](https://dev.to/konark_13) wrote an interesting post about different kinds of fears developers face:

FOMO is one of them. In tech, FOMO has a particular flavor. There's always a new tool, a new approach, a new thing you absolutely have to learn this year. Your feed is full of people building impressive projects and mastering something you've never even heard of. So you keep scrolling, keep bookmarking, keep adding courses to a list you already know you'll never finish. Or never even start.

[![Iconographic showing a stressed man; on the right side, there are thoughts accompanying FOMO](https://media2.dev.to/dynamic/image/width=800%2Cheight=%2Cfit=scale-down%2Cgravity=auto%2Cformat=auto/https%3A%2F%2Fdev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com%2Fuploads%2Farticles%2Fll8ek2epq34q16wxret2.jpeg)](https://media2.dev.to/dynamic/image/width=800%2Cheight=%2Cfit=scale-down%2Cgravity=auto%2Cformat=auto/https%3A%2F%2Fdev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com%2Fuploads%2Farticles%2Fll8ek2epq34q16wxret2.jpeg)

This also leads us to **learning paralysis**. We are living in a world where information is more accessible than ever. As much as this is a gift, it can also be paralyzing. I remember spending hours browsing different courses on the Internet, trying to create a learning path and build a skill set that would make me stand out as a tech writer. _Should I learn more about documenting APIs? Or maybe some programming language that would help me automate my work?_ - I spent more time trying to answer these questions than actually doing anything.

## [](#perfectionism)Perfectionism

I had this article sitting in my dashboard as a draft for **months**. Now you know what kept me from publishing it for so long. Yeah, I wanted it to be perfect. I wanted it to be truly helpful, relatable, but also well-written and engaging. I set high standards for myself and became my harshest critic. And the more I postponed writing the article, the more I heard the critic saying: "Well, it took you so long, so now you must come back with something truly great!". A lot of pressure, isn't it?

[![Iconographic showing a person looking at tiny details with a magnifying glass; on the right side, there are thoughts accompanying perfectionism](https://media2.dev.to/dynamic/image/width=800%2Cheight=%2Cfit=scale-down%2Cgravity=auto%2Cformat=auto/https%3A%2F%2Fdev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com%2Fuploads%2Farticles%2F1ehtu6szarddnzj27yte.jpeg)](https://media2.dev.to/dynamic/image/width=800%2Cheight=%2Cfit=scale-down%2Cgravity=auto%2Cformat=auto/https%3A%2F%2Fdev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com%2Fuploads%2Farticles%2F1ehtu6szarddnzj27yte.jpeg)

Think about how many times you kept going down the rabbit hole instead of showing someone your work for initial feedback. Or even worse – how many times did you give up on something because you knew you wouldn't do it perfectly?

For example, recently I took up painting. I've always loved it but haven't done it for years. Because I'll never be a Picasso. It is very sad, actually. Because if I still enjoy the process, does it really make any difference?

And you? What are you postponing or not allowing yourself in fear of not being good enough?

## [](#mental-overload)Mental overload

If learning paralysis is about too many choices, mental overload is about too many open tabs – in your browser and in your head.

You know what a typical day in the tech industry looks like: a code review here, a Slack ping there, three meetings, a production alert, a half-finished task you want to get back to, and a backlog that never stops growing. And after work, there is still a bunch of small daily chores waiting for our attention. This can be extremely draining and make us feel like we are running on a low battery for far too long.

## [](#burnout-and-boreout)Burnout and boreout

By now, we all know burnout: the exhaustion that comes from too much pressure, too much work, and too little rest, for too long.

But it has a lesser-known sibling that can feel just as draining: boreout. Boreout is what happens when work constantly leaves you under-stimulated: bored, unchallenged, stuck doing tasks that feel meaningless. From the outside, burnout and boreout can look alike – the same tiredness, the same disengagement, the same loss of motivation. But their causes are opposite. One comes from too much, and the other from too little.

[![Iconographic comparing burnout and boreout symptoms](https://media2.dev.to/dynamic/image/width=800%2Cheight=%2Cfit=scale-down%2Cgravity=auto%2Cformat=auto/https%3A%2F%2Fdev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com%2Fuploads%2Farticles%2Fdlylakw7rn38sdg3ghlh.jpeg)](https://media2.dev.to/dynamic/image/width=800%2Cheight=%2Cfit=scale-down%2Cgravity=auto%2Cformat=auto/https%3A%2F%2Fdev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com%2Fuploads%2Farticles%2Fdlylakw7rn38sdg3ghlh.jpeg)

Now that I'm thinking about it, it's actually boreout that made me consider staying in Iceland herding sheep that I described in my last article. Because if I delegate all my work to AI, where is the place for me and my expertise? Where are the challenges? Is my job cut down to just managing agents?

## [](#airelated-threats)AI-related threats

Speaking of AI, it has been 0 days since I last worried about my job. Will AI replace me? Will my profession evolve, or will it totally disappear? How can I adapt to the changing industry conditions? How can I stay ahead? I bet all of us have such thoughts at least from time to time nowadays.

Yeah, AI brought a whole new set of mental threats to the table. **AI anxiety** goes hand in hand with an **identity crisis**. Because who am I if not an engineer or a tech writer?

The threat I think is not talked about enough is **AI fatigue**. nd it's simply being tired. Tired of adopting yet another tool, tired of the hype, and the pressure to keep up with all of this. Not to mention all the AI-generated flyers and graphics – packed with too many visuals – that keep flooding us everywhere.

## [](#closing-thoughts)Closing thoughts

By now, you might recognize a few of these in yourself – and if you do, know that it's completely normal. We all have these thoughts from time to time, especially in a field that moves as fast as ours. And we all try to handle them the best we can, even if some days that's really challenging.

And that's really the whole point of this article. I'm not here to add to the doom – I just wanted to name these feelings and make you aware of them. Because once you can name what you're dealing with, it gets a little easier to carry. You're not broken, and you're definitely not the only one – many of us are quietly fighting the same battles.

Of course, naming them isn't always enough. The trouble starts when they stop going away; when the thought becomes the voice you hear every single day, stealing your sleep, and draining the joy out of things you used to love. That's the line worth watching. If you cross it, please reach out to someone you trust. And if it's still not enough, reach out to a professional. Asking for help is not a weakness, and therapy is nothing to be ashamed of. You don't have to hit rock bottom to deserve support.

This was just the first step – naming what we're fighting with. In the next articles of this series, I'd like to dig more into what actually helps us boost our mental health. But for now, I'd love to hear from you: did any of these feel familiar? Or did you fight a completely different sort of demon that sucked your joy? Share it in the comments. And remember – whatever you're going through, you are not alone 💛
