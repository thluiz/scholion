Why You Should Never Use Fabric Softener on Towels, According to a Cleaning Expert

Fabric softener might be doing your towels more harm than good. Here’s why a cleaning expert says to skip it.

By Jolie Kerr Published on November 9, 2026

KEY TAKEAWAYS
Overusing fabric softener on towels reduces absorbency, causes stiffness, and creates malodor from buildup.
Restore towels by washing them with 1 cup of white vinegar and half the usual detergent, and avoid fabric softener.
Use alternatives like vinegar for softness, and wool dryer balls for fluffiness and to reduce static.

Everyone wants soft, fluffy towels, and reaching for fabric softener on laundry day seems like an obvious way to achieve that. Despite its name, however, using fabric softener incorrectly can actually reduce the softness of towels. Over time, using fabric softener on towels and other terry-cloth items, such as bathrobes or bath mats, can lead to problems like malodor and a shortened lifespan.

Ahead, learn more about the effects of fabric softener on towels, how to remove buildup, and alternatives for softer, fluffier towels.

The Effects of Fabric Softener on Towels

Fabric softener coats fibers with electrically charged chemical compounds designed to increase softness and reduce static electricity. However, with towels, fabric softeners can hurt performance by reducing the fabric’s ability to absorb water. If your towels aren’t drying as well as you’d expect and feel slightly waxy, you’ve likely used too much fabric softener.

Fabric softener can also trap odors, causing towels to smell musty even after washing. Using too much detergent can have similar effects. While this buildup is often reversible, persistent stiffness or odors may signal it’s time to replace your towels.

How to Remove Fabric Softener Buildup on Towels

Before you give up on towels that have changed texture or taken on an unpleasant smell, even when clean, try this simple routine that can remove product buildup and restore the towels:

Wash the towels with a half dose of laundry detergent and one cup of white vinegar. Place the vinegar in the fabric softener dispenser tray to ensure it is released at the right point in the wash cycle.
Start a normal wash cycle. White vinegar helps break apart and rinse out lingering excess detergent and fabric softener buildup that leaves towels feeling stiff and smelling musty.
After washing, dry the towels well in the dryer, avoiding fabric softener dryer sheets.

Follow our guide on detergent dosing to determine what a half dose looks like based on load size.

Fabric Softener Substitutes to Use on Towels

Fabric softener alternatives can add softness, fluffiness, reduce static, and add fragrance without leaving a coating or causing product buildup that affects a towel’s performance.

To increase fluffiness: Use dryer balls to separate towels in the dryer, allowing hot air to circulate more evenly, reducing drying time, and making towels fluffier.
To improve softness: Add ¼ to ½ cup of white vinegar to the washer’s rinse cycle to soften towels naturally. Use sparingly, as vinegar’s acidity can damage rubber gaskets over time.
To reduce static: Use wool dryer balls to help minimize static cling.
To add fragrance: Use scented laundry beads in the washer for a fresh scent.

When and How to Use Fabric Softener on Towels

Some people still choose to use fabric softener when washing or drying towels, and that is OK. If you choose to use liquid fabric softener on towels, use a half dose per load.

Medium load: When the drum is half full, use half of the lowest bar on the measuring cup for the correct liquid fabric softener dose.
Large load: When the drum is three-quarters full, use the lowest bar on the measuring cup for the correct liquid fabric softener dose.
Extra-large load: When the drum is full but not packed tightly, use the midpoint between the lowest and medium bars on the measuring cup for the correct liquid fabric softener dose.

If you like to use dryer sheets when drying towels, cut the sheet in half to reduce the amount of fabric softener the towels are exposed to.
