The **_abc_ conjecture** (also known as the **Oesterlé–Masser conjecture**) is a [conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjecture "Conjecture") in [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory") that arose out of a discussion of [Joseph Oesterlé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Oesterl%C3%A9 "Joseph Oesterlé") and [David Masser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Masser "David Masser") in 1985.[\[1\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOesterlé1988-1)[\[2\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasser1985-2) It is stated in terms of three [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer "Positive integer") and (hence the name) that are [relatively prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime_integers "Coprime integers") and satisfy . The conjecture essentially states that the product of the distinct [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor "Prime factor") of cannot often be much smaller than . A number of famous conjectures and theorems in number theory would follow immediately from the _abc_ conjecture or its versions. Mathematician [Dorian Goldfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorian_M._Goldfeld "Dorian M. Goldfeld") described the _abc_ conjecture as "The most important unsolved problem in [Diophantine analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_analysis "Diophantine analysis")".[\[3\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoldfeld1996-3)

The _abc_ conjecture originated as the outcome of attempts by Oesterlé and Masser to understand the [Szpiro conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szpiro_conjecture "Szpiro conjecture") about [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve "Elliptic curve"),[\[4\]](#cite_note-4) which involves more geometric structures in its statement than the _abc_ conjecture. The _abc_ conjecture was shown to be equivalent to the modified Szpiro conjecture.[\[1\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOesterlé1988-1)

Various attempts to prove the _abc_ conjecture have been made, but none have gained broad acceptance. [Shinichi Mochizuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinichi_Mochizuki "Shinichi Mochizuki") claimed to have a proof in 2012, but the conjecture is still regarded as unproven by the mainstream mathematical community.[\[5\]](#cite_note-Ball-5)[\[6\]](#cite_note-nature-2020-6)[\[7\]](#cite_note-7)[\[8\]](#cite_note-8)

A fourth equivalent formulation of the conjecture involves the _quality_ _q_(_a_, _b_, _c_) of the triple (_a_, _b_, _c_), which is defined as

For example:

_q_(4, 127, 131) = log(131) / log(rad(4·127·131)) = log(131) / log(2·127·131) = 0.46820...

A typical triple (_a_, _b_, _c_) of coprime positive integers with _a_ + _b_ = _c_ will have _c_ < rad(_abc_), i.e. _q_(_a_, _b_, _c_) < 1. Triples with _q_ > 1 such as in the second example are rather special; they consist of numbers divisible by high powers of small [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_number "Prime number"). The fourth formulation is:

For every positive real number _ε_, there exist only finitely many triples (_a_, _b_, _c_) of coprime positive integers with _a_ + _b_ = _c_ such that _q_(_a_, _b_, _c_) > 1 + _ε_.

Whereas it is known that there are infinitely many triples (_a_, _b_, _c_) of coprime positive integers with _a_ + _b_ = _c_ such that _q_(_a_, _b_, _c_) > 1, the conjecture predicts that only finitely many of those have _q_ > 1.01 or _q_ > 1.001 or even _q_ > 1.0001, etc. In particular, if the conjecture is true, then there must exist a triple (_a_, _b_, _c_) that achieves the maximal possible quality _q_(_a_, _b_, _c_).

The _abc_ conjecture has a large number of consequences. These include both known results (some of which have been proven separately only since the conjecture has been stated) and conjectures for which it gives a [conditional proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_proof "Conditional proof"). ## Computational results

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Abc_conjecture&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Computational results")\]

In 2006, the Mathematics Department of [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University "Leiden University") in the Netherlands, together with the Dutch Kennislink science institute, launched the [ABC@Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC@Home "ABC@Home") project, a [grid computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_computing "Grid computing") system, which aims to discover additional triples _a_, _b_, _c_ with rad(_abc_) < _c_. Although no finite set of examples or counterexamples can resolve the _abc_ conjecture, it is hoped that patterns in the triples discovered by this project will lead to insights about the conjecture and about number theory more generally.

Since August 2012, [Shinichi Mochizuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinichi_Mochizuki "Shinichi Mochizuki") has claimed a proof of Szpiro's conjecture and therefore the _abc_ conjecture.[\[5\]](#cite_note-Ball-5) He released a series of four preprints developing a new theory he called [inter-universal Teichmüller theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-universal_Teichm%C3%BCller_theory "Inter-universal Teichmüller theory") (IUTT), which is then applied to prove the _abc_ conjecture.[\[30\]](#cite_note-Mochizukiweb-31) The papers have not been widely accepted by the mathematical community as providing a proof of _abc_.[\[31\]](#cite_note-32) This is not only because of their length and the difficulty of understanding them,[\[32\]](#cite_note-33) but also because at least one specific point in the argument has been identified as a gap by some other experts.[\[33\]](#cite_note-stillConj-34) Although a few mathematicians have vouched for the correctness of the proof[\[34\]](#cite_note-35) and have attempted to communicate their understanding via workshops on IUTT, they have failed to convince the number theory community at large.[\[35\]](#cite_note-36)[\[36\]](#cite_note-37)

In March 2018, [Peter Scholze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Scholze "Peter Scholze") and [Jakob Stix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Stix "Jakob Stix") visited [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University") for discussions with Mochizuki.[\[37\]](#cite_note-38)[\[38\]](#cite_note-39) While they did not resolve the differences, they brought them into clearer focus. Scholze and Stix wrote a report asserting and explaining an error in the logic of the proof and claiming that the resulting gap was "so severe that ... small modifications will not rescue the proof strategy";[\[33\]](#cite_note-stillConj-34) Mochizuki claimed that they misunderstood vital aspects of the theory and made invalid simplifications.[\[39\]](#cite_note-40)[\[40\]](#cite_note-41)[\[41\]](#cite_note-42)

On April 3, 2020, two mathematicians from [the Kyoto research institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Institute_for_Mathematical_Sciences "Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences") where Mochizuki works announced that his claimed proof would be published in _Publications of the Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences (RIMS)_, the institute's journal. Mochizuki is chief editor of the journal but recused himself from the review of the paper.[\[6\]](#cite_note-nature-2020-6) The announcement was received with skepticism by [Kiran Kedlaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiran_Kedlaya "Kiran Kedlaya") and [Edward Frenkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Frenkel "Edward Frenkel"), as well as being described by [_Nature_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_\(journal\) "Nature (journal)") as "unlikely to move many researchers over to Mochizuki's camp".[\[6\]](#cite_note-nature-2020-6) In March 2021, Mochizuki's claimed proof was published in RIMS.[\[42\]](#cite_note-43)

So in this case, it does not matter which concept we use.
