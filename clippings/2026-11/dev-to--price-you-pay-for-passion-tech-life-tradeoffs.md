Over the past three weeks, I've written three rather demanding articles. I still want to push a little harder before the end of the year, but this week it's time for something lighter! The Third Cup and its concept of coffee chats fit perfectly here. This is my first post written for any kind of "organization." So grab a coffee and join me! ☺️

In today's coffee chat, I'd like to talk a little about passion. I was inspired by a colleague who recently asked me:

"Sylwia, how do you do it? You write a blog, build demos, speak at conferences, and you've even written a book???" (It was actually a short story 😉 You'll find the link at the end of this article.) "I just sit on the couch and watch TV shows after work!"

I was a little surprised by this because these things don't exhaust me or stress me out. I simply love doing them. Writing, following technology trends, creating things, coding, and so on, and so on. Right now, I'm particularly excited about agentic AI, so I love running models locally, experimenting with stuff, and even digging into emerging standards.

I love technology, and technology loves me back. A lot of things go well for me in this field, especially when it comes to my career and blogging. Of course, don't get me wrong, there are also plenty of things that don't go my way. One of my recent "failures" was having my CFP submission rejected by a conference I really wanted to speak at. Sometimes someone leaves a nasty comment. My list of failures in tech is pretty long, but I've also had quite a few successes, so I can't complain!

Anyone who has a passion knows how wonderful it feels. Having a goal, seeing results, enjoying what you do. But we don't talk nearly as much about the darker side of it. So what does that look like in my case?

First of all, yes, I manage to do a lot in tech. Unfortunately, outside of tech, things aren't quite so rosy. Of course, I still have to take care of the basic household and family responsibilities, but beyond that, pretty much everything falls apart!

I do everything at the last minute and forget a lot of things. Recently, I almost forgot to call my grandma to wish her a happy birthday, even though I'd ordered flowers for her the day before! (Luckily, I remembered just in time.)

I keep putting off tons of things, including non-urgent household chores. A pile of clothes waiting to be sorted can sit there for months because there's always something more interesting or more important to do. An article about MCP? Oh, there's always time for that!

If I absolutely have to buy something, like a new dishwasher because the old one has broken, my research takes about 15 minutes and consists of typing my requirements into ChatGPT. I'd love to do proper research and pick the best option, but I simply don't have the mental bandwidth for that anymore.

I go to the hairdresser once every six months or even less often, and I've never been to a beauty salon. I buy all my clothes online.

I earn pretty well, but I don't have the energy to figure out investing, so my retirement might not look particularly bright. If you're an investor or have one in your family and would like to offer me your services, don't bother — I don't even have the bandwidth to arrange something like that.

When it comes to taxes, of course I pay an accountant because I can't be bothered to deal with them myself, even though my tax situation isn't exactly rocket science. I prefer to outsource even simple things to keep my head clear.

Apart from when I'm exercising, I always wear dresses. Sounds lovely and feminine, right? It's simply because that way I don't have to think about putting an outfit together 😂

I often don't have time to meet up with friends because, for example, I have a series of conferences coming up. Granted, I once took a friend with me to a conference, and we had a great time, but it's not quite the same!

Sometimes I see women my age spending lots of time decorating their homes, especially for the holidays. I absolutely love it and would love to do the same, but when am I supposed to figure all that out when there's a new WebMCP spec to read? 😅 So I just do the bare minimum.

And sometimes I take on too much work and end up getting really annoyed with myself. Because each individual thing is awesome, but put them all together and I end up completely overwhelmed!

And so on, and so on. I could go on forever.

Passion is a beautiful thing, but it often leaves everything else sitting somewhere on the sidelines. Do I regret it, though? Of course not! But it does show that you can't "have it all," because we have limited mental bandwidth, and there are only 24 hours in a day. Want to live a well-organized life and have every aspect of it under control? Unfortunately, having a passion won't help you much there :D

As you can see, my secret is simple: **I do so much in tech because I do surprisingly little in pretty much every other area of my life.** 😅

What about you? Do you have similar struggles? Or is IT just a job for you, and your real passion lies somewhere else?
