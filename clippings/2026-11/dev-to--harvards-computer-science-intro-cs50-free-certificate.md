With over 1.5M enrollments on edX, [CS50, Harvard’s Introduction to Computer Science](https://www.classcentral.com/course/edx-cs50-s-introduction-to-computer-science-442), is one of the world’s most popular MOOCs and an [all-time, top-100 online course on Class Central](https://www.classcentral.com/collection/top-free-online-courses).

Having taken the course myself a few years ago, I can’t say I’m surprised: the course is excellent. It has dedicated instructors, excellent production values, and its content is refreshed annually. Most notably, the course will continue to offer a free certificate of completion in 2020.

## [](#new-curriculum)New Curriculum

Taught by [David J. Malan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_J._Malan) and filmed in Harvard’s Sanders Theater, CS50’s 2020 video lessons bring new content and more demos. Problem sets have been updated to include:

*   [Analyzing a movie database](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2020/psets/7/movies/) (IMBb) using SQL.
*   [Applying “Instagram-like” filters](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2020/psets/4/filter/more/) to bitmap images.
*   [Simulating electoral systems](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2020/psets/3/tideman/) — likely a nod to the upcoming US elections.

In addition, CS50 now offers specialization tracks, allowing students to dedicate the last few weeks of course to one of three subjects:

*   [Web Development](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2020/tracks/web/)
*   [Game Development](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2020/tracks/games/)
*   Mobile Development — either [Android](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2020/tracks/mobile/android/) or [iOS](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2020/tracks/mobile/ios/)

These tracks borrow from some of [CS50’s follow-up courses](https://www.classcentral.com/report/harvard-cs50-new-courses/) introduced in 2018.

Like in previous years, CS50 culminates in an open-ended [software project](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2020/project/) that asks students to put into practice what they learned in the course.

## [](#free-certificate)Free Certificate

Since its launch, CS50 has maintained a firm stance toward openness. In 2020, the course will remain entirely free, including its [certificate of completion](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2020/certificate/). This is how the free certificate looks:

[![Alt Text](https://res.cloudinary.com/practicaldev/image/fetch/s--vGoUxWs---/c_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cfl_progressive%2Cq_auto%2Cw_880/https://thepracticaldev.s3.amazonaws.com/i/2pbngn2wmic0mzimksjc.png)](https://res.cloudinary.com/practicaldev/image/fetch/s--vGoUxWs---/c_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cfl_progressive%2Cq_auto%2Cw_880/https://thepracticaldev.s3.amazonaws.com/i/2pbngn2wmic0mzimksjc.png)  
_CS50 Free Course Certificate_

Note, however, that the free certificate does not include identity verification. Students may instead choose to pay $90 for a [verified certificate](https://www.edx.org/verified-certificate) on edX. Or they may pay $1840 to $2840 to take the course through [Harvard Extension School](https://www.extension.harvard.edu/course-catalog/courses/intensive-introduction-to-computer-science/24107), in which case they’ll have access to a formal transcript and earn academic credit upon completion of the course.
