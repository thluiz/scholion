# 3 Evernote Writing Tips from Best-Selling Author Tim Ferriss

  
    
     
      
 Posted by Kristina Hjelsand on 22 Sep 2014  
    [Comment](http://blog.evernote.com/blog/2014/09/22/3-evernote-writing-tips-best-selling-author-tim-ferriss/#comments)     
         
Hyper-achieving [author and entrepreneur](http://fourhourworkweek.com/blog/) Tim Ferriss is a longtime Evernote enthusiast (and, in the interest of full disclosure, an Evernote advisor). Ferriss is slated to be a keynote speaker at our 4th annual [Evernote Conference](https://evernote.com/ec/) Oct. 2-3, and we can think of few people who better embody this year’s theme of “Achieving Your Life’s Work.”  
      
*“There are very few services that I feel I could not live without. Evernote is one of them…I use it 10 to 20 times a day. Evernote literally saves me hundreds of hours.” — Tim Ferriss*  
      
In advance of the conference, we’re revisiting a few of Ferriss’s top Evernote tips from writing his most recent book, *[The 4-Hour Chef](http://www.amazon.com/The-4-Hour-Chef-Learning-Anything/dp/0547884591)*. He researched and wrote the book entirely in Evernote. Here’s a peek into how he relied on Evernote and indispensable tools like Web Clipper along the way.

- **Simplifying research:** Ferriss used Evernote to collect all of his research for The 4-Hour Chef, from photographs to illustrations and interviews. Using [Web Clipper](https://evernote.com/webclipper/) to gather online research was an integral part of the process.
- **Field work:** He also used Evernote to capture photographs of food-related inspiration found on the go, from relevant book titles to notes on dishes when dining out. These photos were dispatched directly into Evernote, streamlining the organizational process.
- **Paperless writer:** Writing and researching in Evernote allowed Ferriss to avoid amassing the copious stacks of paper from previous book-writing. The only paper he kept on hand were final press checks for the books that were printed for fan giveaways.
