## 1/11

Looking for a job?

Your resume is the first thing employers look at, and often they won't look at anything else unless they're already impressed.

But, on average, they'll only look at it for a few seconds.

So follow these tips to make your resume shine in those few seconds

([tweet](https://x.com/catmcgee/status/1297616747965616133), 2020-08-23 19:28 UTC)

## 2/11

Use a unique design

I always try to make resumes interesting, and the easiest way to do that is to use a unique design. When I was job-hunting I got calls from 75% of my applications, and I attribute that to design.

Be creative. Make it colourful. Use graphics.

Try 
@Canva
!

([tweet](https://x.com/catmcgee/status/1297616749064597504), 2020-08-23 19:28 UTC)

## 3/11

Prioritize information

In those few seconds, your employer will be reading from top to bottom. Put the best information at the top - your most recent experience, your skills, your education, whatever you feel like is the best way to describe you.

Make them want to read more.

([tweet](https://x.com/catmcgee/status/1297616750192873476), 2020-08-23 19:28 UTC)

## 4/11

Introduce yourself

The best way to get the best information at the top is to include a SMALL bio, with an introduction to who you are, what you're looking for, what you can bring, and your proudest accomplishment.

Here's your time to SHINE and show off who you are.

([tweet](https://x.com/catmcgee/status/1297616751442763780), 2020-08-23 19:28 UTC)

## 5/11

Don't include everything

Only include relevant experience and education. If you're applying to a dev job and you've worked in retail before, don't include this. Even if you've only had 2 jobs.

This will give you more space to talk in-depth about your relevant experience.

([tweet](https://x.com/catmcgee/status/1297616752709382144), 2020-08-23 19:28 UTC)

## 6/11

Show off

The person reading your resume does not care what your previous company did. They care about what YOU did, what YOU accomplished, what YOU brought to the team.

Your resume is about YOU. Not people who know you.

([tweet](https://x.com/catmcgee/status/1297616754018000896), 2020-08-23 19:28 UTC)

## 7/11

Give them provable data

Provide numbers. Give exact information of what you've done in your jobs. You didn't 'improve accessibility of their product', you made it meet W3C. You didn't 'make their website better', you improved its conversation rate by 120%.

([tweet](https://x.com/catmcgee/status/1297616755305656330), 2020-08-23 19:28 UTC)

## 8/11

Provide other things

Attach a cover letter to showcase your personality more. Include links to your projects and website. And make these links interesting! Make them want to know what it is.

The more time you can make an employer spend on you, the better your chances.

([tweet](https://x.com/catmcgee/status/1297616756442374144), 2020-08-23 19:28 UTC)

## 9/11

Ask for help

Use other resources to check spelling and grammar (fire it through @Grammarly!)

Ask mentors or friends to look at your resume and tell you what they think. Sometimes, something will make sense to you but not everyone else.

([tweet](https://x.com/catmcgee/status/1297616757843210245), 2020-08-23 19:28 UTC)

## 10/11

Spend TIME on your resume

As tempting as it is to quickly draw up a new resume and start applying for jobs, your resume is the first point of contact you'll make with a potential employer.

It has incredible importance and the time you spend on it should reflect that.

([tweet](https://x.com/catmcgee/status/1297616759172800515), 2020-08-23 19:28 UTC)
