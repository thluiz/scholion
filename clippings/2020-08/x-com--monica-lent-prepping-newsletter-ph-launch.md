I'm going to try to be better at "building in public", so here it goes:

## 2/15

The product I'm launching is my newsletter. As such, the goal of the page is to get an email address!

Before: Bold headline leading directly into long form text.

People told me they subscribed because they liked the personal and direct message.

![imagem](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/Efyz1ZNXkAAy0-x?format=jpg&name=orig)

([tweet](https://x.com/monicalent/status/1296124266799341568), 2020-08-19 16:38 UTC)

## 3/15

But you have to scroll A LOT to reach the first email form.

Plus, people visiting from PH will have seen a description already.

So I opted to create an extremely clear, direct explanation:

1) What the newsletter helps you do
2) The exact audience it's for

Let's zoom in

![imagem](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/Efy0Tn8XkAYPZYX?format=jpg&name=orig)

([tweet](https://x.com/monicalent/status/1296124270301585411), 2020-08-19 16:38 UTC)

## 4/15

About the first form:

Being specific about the audience should help people figure out "Is this for me?" quickly.

Plus, the form is small, inline, looks simple.

I added a subtext to mention "Join over 2,000 developers", as a way for people to know it's kinda legit.

![imagem](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/Efy1B4gWkAE6xnU?format=jpg&name=orig)

([tweet](https://x.com/monicalent/status/1296124273858351104), 2020-08-19 16:38 UTC)

## 5/15

The last thing before *returning* to the long form text is some supportive Tweets from subscribers!

I suspect this will be a good form of social proof because Tweets are public, unprompted statements. It means they're genuine :)

Clearly I didn't have these the first time

([tweet](https://x.com/monicalent/status/1296124283362652162), 2020-08-19 16:38 UTC)
