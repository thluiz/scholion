| Hi friend,    In today's email, I'm going to tell you the single most important step you can take today to grow your blog and make it a success.    It sounds dramatic, but if you read to the end of this email, I think you'll agree.    For some context, I want to quickly recap what we learned from last week's newsletter (in case you missed it):    Last week we learned how Robin Wieruch grew his dev blog to over 300,000 monthly readers and turned it into the cornerstone of his tech career ([click here to read it if you haven't yet](https://el2.convertkit-mail2.com/c/k0u0ke8mqxs6h43dvvbl/25h2hohopp668o/aHR0cHM6Ly9ibG9nZ2luZ2ZvcmRldnMuY29tL3JvYmluLXdpZXJ1Y2gtaW50ZXJ2aWV3Lw==)).    If there's one thing I take away from Robin's story, it's this: Consistency is the primary ingredient for growth.    As developers, we know it's true, don't we? You don't get better at coding by doing it from time to time.    You do it constantly until you can do it without thinking.    Until it's not a struggle anymore.    Until it's a habit.    And building a successful blog is no different.    This was observed by the Roman poet, Ovid:    Dripping water hollows out stone, not through force but through persistence.    When you look at the [graph of people visiting Robin's blog](https://el2.convertkit-mail2.com/c/k0u0ke8mqxs6h43dvvbl/qvh8h7h6mmzz48/aHR0cHM6Ly9ibG9nZ2luZ2ZvcmRldnMuY29tL2ltYWdlcy9ibG9nL3JvYmluLXdpZXJ1Y2gtdHJhZmZpYy5wbmc=), it's not that he became an SEO genius and started getting thousands of visitors all at once.    He began blogging in 2016, built a multi-year habit, and to this day writes something every day.    It's easy to think that if we just figure out "that one cool trick", suddenly our blogs, businesses, or careers will reach our definition of success.    But even looking back on my own blog, the most successful one was a site I started in 2016. Today it has over 120 articles. Most of those are between 2,000 and 8,000 words long. That's more than 300,000 words.    Three BOOKS worth of blog posts.    I have spent years of my life building that website. And that's exactly why it worked. Even if it was hard to see day-to-day.    But for some reason, I have to keep reminding myself of this simple fact:    You have to put in the work. No one else can do it for you.    I've been in a bit of a funk lately. Maybe you noticed (or didn't :)) that I'm taking a break on Twitter. I blocked it in my /etc/hosts file and deleted the app from my phone.    Several friends wrote me to check I wasn't having some kind of meltdown (I mean, I kind of am, but not the kind you should be worried about).    What I realized is that I wasn't putting in the work that I needed to for my projects to succeed.    I was wasting time on Twitter, or websites I discovered on Twitter, consuming more than creating. And it frustrated me. Enough that I felt like I had to do something "dramatic".    Maybe you're in a similar spot. Or you are wondering why your blog hasn't taken off so far. If so, I'm writing this email just for you today.    Here's what I want you to do:    Stop reading this email and go write a blog post.    If you can't do it right now because you're working, put it in your Google Calendar. Then make that event recurring.    Every day or every week, it doesn't matter. It's time to start building that habit TODAY.    Because when you look back one year from now, you are going to be fucking proud of yourself for what you did. And I can guarantee you, that you will see results -- because you were consistent.    I don't have anything else to add for now. I've been grinding away at my projects this week and building a system that lets me put in the work I need to across my different projects. If it's a success or a failure, I'll let you know :)    I've got great stuff in the works for Blogging for Devs, which I am beyond excited to share with you.
