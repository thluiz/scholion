Q3 2025

## Key Takeaways

1. Developers save an average of 7.3 hours per week thanks to AI tools, yet most companies lack a clear AI strategy to harness this productivity boost.
2. Self-learning leads: on-the-job training is the most popular (66%), followed by YouTube (58%).
3. Most developers (76%) say AI makes their work more fulfilling, as it allows them to focus on innovation and creative problem-solving.
4. 65% of developers worry about falling behind on AI skills, and developers are now spending almost 4 hours per week on upskilling.
5. AI and Machine Learning are the top learning priorities for developers in the next 3 months.

BairesDev launched the Dev Barometer, a quarterly look inside the minds of senior software engineers, hearing from 1,129 developers and 50 project managers working across 125 projects in 63 countries, most with 8+ years of experience.

## Developers Are Saving Nearly A Full Day of Coding Work

Across projects, sectors, and regions, developers report a tangible lift in output. On average, developers save 7.3 hours per week by using AI to assist with coding. Nearly half (48%) say 10-50% of their code is now AI-assisted, and the average share sits at 23.5%. The saved time translates into roughly 4 hours a week reinvested in upskilling - as with Jorge García, a senior Ruby developer who transitioned into an AI Engineer role at BairesDev, or Ahmed Somarribas, an integration engineer who says AI itself helped him go from junior-level to almost senior-level in JavaScript within a couple of months.

89% of developers report concrete personal benefits - faster coding, accelerated learning, higher productivity. Industry-wide, 51% of professional developers use AI tools daily, and 84% use or plan to use them in their workflow (Stack Overflow 2025 survey).

## AI and ML Are the Top Learning Priorities

44% of developers named AI and machine learning as their top upskilling priority for the next three months, ahead of every other technical area. 65% worry about falling behind on AI skills. Learning happens mostly through informal channels: on-the-job learning (66%), YouTube tutorials (58%), paid courses (48%). Formal certifications play a smaller role (15%), suggesting agility and tailored learning take precedence over structured programs in a fast-evolving landscape.

New hybrid roles are emerging at the intersection of coding and AI fluency - prompt engineers, AI/ML specialists, AI ethicists, data quality engineers. GitHub CEO Thomas Dohmke is quoted: "embrace AI or leave the profession."

## Organizations Are Still Playing Catch-Up

64% of software development projects are in early AI stages, with an average maturity of 2.2 out of 5; only 3.2% have AI embedded at their core. Among large enterprises, average maturity is 2.6 out of 5, with 65% of enterprise projects citing a lack of AI/ML specialists. Developers point to legacy infrastructure and restrictive policies as barriers; project managers highlight unclear ROI and limited resources. The only barrier both groups agree on is data privacy and security.

87% of developers predict generative AI will disrupt business models within the next year; after GenAI, respondents expect AI agents (75%) and private or fine-tuned AI models (55%) to be the most transformative.

## Remote Work Enables AI Innovation Everywhere

76% of developers agree remote work enabled them to pursue a career in tech. Top-reported benefits: access to global job opportunities (59%), better work-life balance (48%), flexible hours (46%). Melissa Guzman, a full-stack engineer living in the Dominican Republic, notes she doesn't see location as a limitation: "As long as I have internet, I can access and contribute to innovation." Ronny Cortez, an AI tech lead at BairesDev, adds: "The biggest opportunity isn't just access to AI models, it's the mindset. With open-source and lightweight models, we can bring AI to low-resource communities."

## Developers Are Embracing AI. Can Organizations Keep Up?

The Q3 2025 Dev Barometer reveals a rising divide: developers are embracing AI and expanding their skills, while many organizations remain in early adoption stages. Companies that prioritize a clear AI strategy, modern infrastructure, and upskilling initiatives are positioned to pull ahead.
