## [Selected Edition: Great Dictionary of the Japanese Language](https://kotobank.jp/dictionary/nikkokuseisen/2109/) Meaning, reading, example sentences, and synonyms of “序破急 (johakyū, introduction–break–rapid finale)”

### じょ‐は‐きゅう‥キフ【序破急】

1.  **〘 noun 〙**
2.  **1.** The three-part division in the formal structure of **雅楽 (gagaku, court music)** compositions. Corresponds to movements in Western music. *Jo* is the opening section, characterized by not fitting into meter. *Ha* is the middle section of the piece, where the music falls into meter but is played at a slow tempo. *Kyū* is the final section, somewhat faster in tempo than *jo* or *ha*. In **舞楽 (bugaku, court dance)**, *jo* is the entrance music, while *ha* and *kyū* are the accompaniment for the dance. A piece that fully possesses all three—*jo*, *ha*, and *kyū*—is spoken of as ideal, but in reality such pieces are few. *Ha* is performed in [延拍子](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BB%B6%E6%8B%8D%E5%AD%90-112407) (*nobebyōshi*, extended meter), and *kyū* in fast meter. 〔[色葉字類抄 (Iroha Jiruishō, Classified Notes on Characters by Iroha)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%89%B2%E8%91%89%E5%AD%97%E9%A1%9E%E6%8A%84-32580) (1177–81)〕
    1.  \[Earliest example\] “As for music, a complete piece consists of the three sections *jo*, *ha*, and *kyū*.” (Source: *Gakkaroku* (1690), 13)
3.  **2.** The threefold division of tempo in the performing arts. *Jo* is slow, *ha* is neither slow nor fast, and *kyū* is fast. Also used of the tempo of storytelling in **浄瑠璃 (jōruri, narrative shamisen music/drama)**, **講談 (kōdan, historical storytelling)**, etc., and of vocal [緩急](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%B7%A9%E6%80%A5-469466) (*kankyū*, modulation of speed).
    1.  \[Earliest example\] “If one reaches this point, one will surely be led astray regarding the *jo-ha-kyū* of the myriad ways, and the introductory, main, and concluding sections, causes and conditions, and similes of the various sutras and treatises.” (Source: *Sasamegoto* (ca. 1463–64), lower volume)
    2.  “First it is essential to tune the shamisen carefully; then, beginning the singing is crucial—the proper degree of *jo-ha-kyū*, the [浮き沈み](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B5%AE%E3%81%8D%E6%B2%88%E3%81%BF-439150) (*ukishizumi*, rise and fall)...” (Source: *Kayō / Matsu no Ha* (1703), 5, “Uta Onjō”)
4.  **3.** In **能 (Nō, classical nō drama)**, jōruri, etc., the three-part division of script structure. Whether or not it coincides with speed, *jo* is the introductory part, *ha* the development, and *kyū* the conclusion.
    1.  \[Earliest example\] “Writers of old thought about the ending. They also thought about such things as *jo-ha-kyū*.” (Source: *Byōsharon* (1911) by [田山花袋 (Tayama Katai, Tayama Katai)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%94%B0%E5%B1%B1%E8%8A%B1%E8%A2%8B-103456))
5.  **4.** The three-part division in stage direction or production. *Jo* is performed smoothly and plainly, *ha* with fully employed technique and abundant variation, and *kyū* briefly and dynamically. In Nō, this principle is held to apply as the principle of progression from beginning to end—not only to a full day’s program, but to a single Nō play and even to every [一挙手一投足](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%80%E6%8C%99%E6%89%8B%E4%B8%80%E6%8A%95%E8%B6%B3-434035) (*ikkyoshu ittōsoku*, every single movement) in performance.
    1.  \[Earliest example\] “In Nō, how should *jo-ha-kyū* be determined?” (Source: [風姿花伝 (Fūshikaden, The Transmission of the Flower Through Style)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%A2%A8%E5%A7%BF%E8%8A%B1%E4%BC%9D-167872) (ca. 1400–02), 3)
6.  **5.** In **連歌 (renga, linked verse)** and **連句 (renku, linked-verse poetry)**, the three-part progression of a whole sequence. The beginning should be calm and uneventful, the next part should exhaust twists and turns and sustain interest, and the ending should proceed lightly and smoothly.
    1.  \[Earliest example\] “Even music has *jo-ha-kyū*, does it not? In linked verse too, the first session paper should be *jo*, the second session paper should be *ha*, and the third and fourth session papers should be *kyū*.” (Source: *Tsukuba Mondō* (ca. 1357–72))
7.  **6.** The beginning, middle, and end of all things. A figurative expression for the way things unfold.
    1.  \[Earliest example\] “Correct good and evil, gauge the *jo-ha-kyū* of all things, consider cold and heat, hunger and night duty, and serve accordingly.” (Source: [仮名草子 (Kanazōshi, kana booklets / early Edo popular prose)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BB%AE%E5%90%8D%E8%8D%89%E5%AD%90-45747), *Kusaigusa* (1647), middle volume)

## [Revised New Edition: Sekai Daihyakka Encyclopedia](https://kotobank.jp/dictionary/sekaidaihyakka/485/) Meaning of “序破急” and easy explanation

### 序破急 (じょはきゅう)

A Japanese performing-arts term. It is a compound formed from the three words *jo*, *ha*, and *kyū*, which represent the movements of the principal piece (*tōkyoku*) in bugaku of gagaku. Each bugaku item is composed of four parts: prelude, entrance of the dancers, performance of the principal piece with dance, and exit of the dancers. In bugaku of the [左方](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B7%A6%E6%96%B9-511860#E6.94.B9.E8.A8.82.E6.96.B0.E7.89.88.E3.80.80.E4.B8%96%E7%95%8C%E5%A4%A7%E7%99%BE%E7%A7%91%E4%BA%8B%E5%85%B8) (*sahō*, Left Side), the principal-piece portion ordinarily consists of one to four movements, and when there are two or more, they are called by movement names such as *jo*, *ha*, *kyū*, *ei*, and *ten* (*saezuri*, warbling). In particular, although it is rare for all three—*jo*, *ha*, and *kyū*—to be present together, they are often used, and their order never changes.

First, [序](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BA%8F-78748#E6.94.B9.E8.A8.82.E6.96.B0.E7.89.88.E3.80.80.E4.B8%96%E7%95%8C%E5%A4%A7%E7%99%BE%E7%A7%91%E4%BA%8B%E5%85%B8) (*jo*) is notably characterized by the accompaniment in **唐楽 (tōgaku, Tang-style court music)** having a non-metrical rhythm, and the [管楽器](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%AE%A1%E6%A5%BD%E5%99%A8-48519) (*kangakki*, wind instruments), by a style of playing commonly called [序吹き](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BA%8F%E5%90%B9%E3%81%8D-1341611) (*jobuki*, jo-blowing), perform a leisurely undulating melody. As mentioned above, if this *jo* is included in the principal piece, it is always placed at the beginning; however, as part of the bugaku performance as a whole, it is by no means at the very beginning, which requires caution. In contrast to *jo*, *ha* is danced to tōgaku in metrical rhythm in the meters called *nobe* or *tada*, and *kyū* likewise to tōgaku in the meter called *haya*. It cannot necessarily be said that the tempo of *ha* and *kyū* respectively changes from slow to fast, or that *kyū* as a whole is faster in tempo than *ha* as a whole. Even within bugaku, in the case of the [右方](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8F%B3%E6%96%B9-441277#E6.94.B9.E8.A8.82.E6.96%B0.E7.89.88.E3.80.80.E4.B8%96%E7%95%8C%E5%A4%A7%E7%99%BE%E7%A7%91%E4%BA%8B%E5%85%B8) (*uhō*, Right Side), there is no movement named *jo*, and the meters of *ha* and *kyū* also differ from those of the Left Side.

The recognition of the three structural parts *jo*, *ha*, and *kyū* seems to have been fairly widespread in fields other than gagaku during the Muromachi period. For example, [二条良基 (Nijō Yoshimoto, Nijō Yoshimoto)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BA%8C%E6%9D%A1%E8%89%AF%E5%9F%BA-109851) states in *[筑波問答 (Tsukuba Mondō, Tsukuba Dialogues)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%AD%91%E6%B3%A2%E5%95%8F%E7%AD%94-99184)*: “Even music has *jo*, *ha*, and *kyū*, does it not? In linked verse too, the first session paper should be *jo*, the second session paper *ha*, and the third and fourth session papers *kyū*. I am told that in kickball as well it is so, by those advanced in that Way.” In [心敬 (Shinkei, Shinkei)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BF%83%E6%95%AC-82137)’s *Sasamegoto* there is the expression “the *jo*, *ha*, and *kyū* of the myriad Ways.” In the performing arts, [世阿弥 (Zeami, Zeami)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%96%E9%98%BF%E5%BC%A5-85456), who received from [観阿弥 (Kan'ami, Kan'ami)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A6%B3%E9%98%BF%E5%BC%A5-49191) the teaching, “Since everything whatsoever has *jo-ha-kyū*, *sarugaku* too is the same; it should be determined according to the mode of the Nō,” regarded the structure of a single Nō play as follows: the section of the *waki*’s entrance is *jo*; the *shite*’s entrance is the first stage of *ha*; the *shite–waki* exchange is the second stage of *ha*; from the *shite*’s narrative to the [中入り](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%AD%E5%85%A5%E3%82%8A-176907) (*nakairi*, interim exit) is the third stage of *ha*; and the entire latter-*shite* scene is *kyū*. He also said, regarding program organization, that the first item is *jo*, the second item the lingering trace of *jo*, and from the third onward *ha*; eventually this came to be explained as the first item being *jo*, the second, third, and fourth items being the first, second, and third stages of *ha*, and the fifth item being *kyū*. Zeami further expounded *jo-ha-kyū* even within “a single dance and a single note,” “one pointing of the dancing sleeve, one sound of a footstep,” and “a single glance toward the audience.”

However, with regard not only to the *jo-ha-kyū* linked to such aesthetic thought, but even to the *jo-ha-kyū* in the structure of a single Nō play or in program organization, it is most reasonable to think that what was taken from bugaku was only the term itself, and that the structure of the performing arts as such was not directly influenced. It may be said that in bugaku the fixed compound term *johakyū* is not really used, but in other fields it may be used fully as a single word meaning a gradual quickening of tempo; in some cases it also means changes of speed or changes of dynamic intensity. Furthermore, the presence or absence of *johakyū* is sometimes pointed out even in [造形芸術](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%80%A0%E5%BD%A2%E8%8A%B8%E8%A1%93-89251) (*zōkei geijutsu*, plastic arts), which do not possess temporality.

## [Encyclopedia Nipponica](https://kotobank.jp/dictionary/nipponica/748/) Meaning of “序破急” and easy explanation

### 序破急  
じょはきゅう

One of the principles common to the Japanese performing arts, a term referring to the three stages in the structure of a musical or dramatic work. Originally it referred to the three movements composing a single piece of gagaku: *jo* corresponds to a gentle introductory section, *ha* to a development rich in content, and *kyū* to a fast-tempo final movement. This principle entered Nō and came to be applied in two ways: to the unfolding of the song-and-dance in a single play, and to the arrangement of the day’s performance program. For a single play, it is a three-stage development of *jo* dance, *ha* dance, and *kyū* dance, and [世阿弥 (Zeami, Zeami)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%96%E9%98%BF%E5%BC%A5-85456), the great systematizer of Nō, explicitly formulated it in *[風姿花伝 (Fūshikaden, The Transmission of the Flower Through Style)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%A2%A8%E5%A7%BF%E8%8A%B1%E4%BC%9D-167872)* as: “Since everything whatsoever has *jo-ha-kyū*, *sarugaku* too is the same.” Furthermore, at the beginning of *Sandō (Nōsakusho)* it says: “The three forms of *jo*, *ha*, and *kyū* are shaped into five stages, and then words are gathered, melody attached, and they are written in sequence.” That is, *ha* is further divided into *ha*’s *jo*, *ha*’s *ha*, and *ha*’s *kyū*. The day’s program also came, in accordance with this, to be arranged in five-play format. In *Shūdōsho* it is written: “Concerning the *jo-ha-kyū* of Nō, *waki nō* is *jo*; the second, third, and fourth plays are *ha*, exhausting the matter; and the fifth ends as *kyū*,” thus describing a full day of Nō. In *[花鏡 (Kakyō, The Mirror of the Flower)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%8A%B1%E9%8F%A1-45784)* there is an even more concrete and detailed explanation.

This principle of *jo-ha-kyū* later entered **浄瑠璃 (jōruri, narrative shamisen music/drama)** and promoted the development from the early six- or twelve-section jōruri into the dramatic structures of five-act **時代物 (jidaimono, period pieces)** and three-part **世話物 (sewamono, domestic plays)**. In this way, *jo-ha-kyū* may be called a [構成原理](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%A7%8B%E6%88%90%E5%8E%9F%E7%90%86-2783925) (*kōsei genri*, principle of composition) running through Japan’s traditional performing arts. Yet in the West too, just as [Aristotle](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%81%82%E3%82%8A%E3%81%99%E3%81%A8%E3%81%A6%E3%82%8C%E3%81%99-3141907) in the *Poetics* defined the completeness of [Greek tragedy](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%81%8E%E3%82%8A%E3%81%97%E3%81%82%E6%82%B2%E5%8A%87-3149508) as “having a beginning, a middle, and an end,” it is a common principle seen in dramatic structure both East and West. The five-stage development of jōruri may be compared with the five-act form regarded as the golden rule of Roman drama and French classical drama. Moreover, as Zeami said, “Since everything whatsoever has *jo-ha-kyū*,” like the expression “[起承転結](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B5%B7%E6%89%BF%E8%BB%A2%E7%B5%90-12404)” (*kishōtenketsu*, introduction–development–turn–conclusion), it is a basic principle that extends not only to theater and performing arts but to all things, including human life itself.

### じょ‐は‐きゅう‥キフ【序破急】

1.  **〘 名詞 〙**
2.  **①** 雅楽の楽曲構成上の三区分。洋楽の楽章に相当する。「序」は最初の部分で、拍子にはまらないのが特徴。「破」は曲の中間の部分で、音楽は拍子にはまるがゆるやかな速度で奏される。「急」は最後の部分で、序や破にくらべると少し急テンポ。舞楽のときは、序は登場音楽で、破と急は舞の伴奏音楽となる。序破急の三つが完備している曲が理想のようにいわれるが実際には少ない。破は[延拍子](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BB%B6%E6%8B%8D%E5%AD%90-112407)、急は早拍子で奏される。〔[色葉字類抄](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%89%B2%E8%91%89%E5%AD%97%E9%A1%9E%E6%8A%84-32580)（1177‐81）〕
    1.  \[初出の実例\]「夫楽者、以二序破急三曲一為レ具也」(出典：楽家録（1690）一三)
3.  **②** 芸能における速度の三区分。「序」はゆっくり、「破」は遅くもなく早くもなく、「急」は早く。浄瑠璃、講談などの話のテンポや、声音の[緩急](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%B7%A9%E6%80%A5-469466)などにもいう。
    1.  \[初出の実例\]「此二にたどり侍ば、万道の序破急、諸経・諸論の序正流通・因縁・譬喩の所にまどひ侍べしとなり」(出典：ささめごと（1463‐64頃）下)
    2.  「まづ三味線の調子を大切に合はせての上、うたひはじむる事肝要にして、序破急(ジョハキウ)のくらゐ、[浮き沈み](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B5%AE%E3%81%8D%E6%B2%88%E3%81%BF-439150)」(出典：歌謡・松の葉（1703）五・哥音声)
4.  **③** 能や浄瑠璃などで、脚本構成上の三区分。速度と一致する、[しない](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%81%97%E3%81%AA%E3%81%84-2046888)にかかわらず、「序」は導入部、「破」は展開部、「急」は結末部。
    1.  \[初出の実例\]「昔の作家は落ちを考へた。序破急などといふことも考へた」(出典：描写論（1911）〈田山花袋〉四)
5.  **④** 演出上の三区分。「序」はすらすらと平明に、「破」は技巧をつくし変化にとませ、「急」は短く躍動的に演ずる。能では開始から終結までの進行の原理として、一日の番組から、一曲の能、所作の[一挙手一投足](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%80%E6%8C%99%E6%89%8B%E4%B8%80%E6%8A%95%E8%B6%B3-434035)に至るまでこの原理が適用されるべきだとする。
    1.  \[初出の実例\]「能に、序破急(ジョハキフ)をば、何とか定(さだむ)べきや」(出典：[風姿花伝](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%A2%A8%E5%A7%BF%E8%8A%B1%E4%BC%9D-167872)（1400‐02頃）三)
6.  **⑤** 連歌・連句で、一巻の進行の三区分。最初は穏やかに無事に、次に波瀾曲折を尽くし興味があるように、終わりはさらさらと軽く付けて運ぶべきだとするもの。
    1.  \[初出の実例\]「楽にも序破急のあるにや。連歌も一会紙は序、二会紙は破、三・四の会紙は急にてあるべし」(出典：筑波問答（1357‐72頃）)
7.  **⑥** すべて物事の、はじめ・なか・おわり。物事の展開のさまをたとえていう語。
    1.  \[初出の実例\]「善悪を正して、物事に序破急(ジョハキウ)をはかり、寒さあつさ、飢夜づめを思ひやり、つかふべし」(出典：[仮名草子](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BB%AE%E5%90%8D%E8%8D%89%E5%AD%90-45747)・悔草（1647）中)

## [改訂新版　世界大百科事典](https://kotobank.jp/dictionary/sekaidaihyakka/485/) 「序破急」の意味・わかりやすい解説

### 序破急 (じょはきゅう)

日本の芸能用語。雅楽の舞楽における当曲（とうきよく）の楽章を代表する序・破・急の三つの語が合成されて熟語となったもの。舞楽の各演目は，前奏，舞人の登場，当曲の奏舞，舞人の退場という4部分から構成される。そして[左方](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B7%A6%E6%96%B9-511860#E6.94.B9.E8.A8.82.E6.96.B0.E7.89.88.E3.80.80.E4.B8.96.E7.95.8C.E5.A4.A7.E7.99.BE.E7.A7.91.E4.BA.8B.E5.85.B8)（さほう）の舞楽においては，そのうちの当曲の部分は，ふつう一ないし四つの楽章からなり，二つ以上あるときには，序，破，急，詠，囀（てん）／（さえずり）などの楽章名で呼ばれる。とくに，序・破・急は，その三つ全部がそろうことはまれであるにしても，しばしば用いられ，しかも順序はけっして前後しない。まず[序](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BA%8F-78748#E6.94.B9.E8.A8.82.E6.96.B0.E7.89.88.E3.80.80.E4.B8.96.E7.95.8C.E5.A4.A7.E7.99.BE.E7.A7.91.E4.BA.8B.E5.85.B8)は，伴奏の唐楽が非拍節的リズムであるのが大きな特徴で，[管楽器](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%AE%A1%E6%A5%BD%E5%99%A8-48519)は，[序吹き](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BA%8F%E5%90%B9%E3%81%8D-1341611)と通称する吹き方によって，ゆったりとうねるような旋律を奏する。この序が当曲に含まれるとすれば，必ず冒頭に置かれるのは前述のとおりだが，舞楽全体としては，けっして冒頭ではないので注意を要する。序に対して，破は拍節的リズムの延（のべ）あるいは只（ただ）という拍子，急は同じく早（はや）という拍子の唐楽を伴奏に舞われる。破，急それぞれの速度が緩から急に変化し，破全体よりも急全体の方がテンポが速いとは必ずしもいえない。同じ舞楽でも[右方](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8F%B3%E6%96%B9-441277#E6.94.B9.E8.A8.82.E6.96.B0.E7.89.88.E3.80.80.E4.B8.96.E7.95.8C.E5.A4.A7.E7.99.BE.E7.A7.91.E4.BA.8B.E5.85.B8)（うほう）の場合には，序という名の楽章はなく，破，急の拍子も，左方のそれとは異なっている。

　序・破・急という三つの構成部分を認識することは，室町時代においては，雅楽以外の分野においても，かなり広く行われていたようである。たとえば二条良基は，《[筑波問答](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%AD%91%E6%B3%A2%E5%95%8F%E7%AD%94-99184)》において〈楽にも序・破・急のあるにや。連歌も一の会紙は序，二の会紙は破，三，四の会紙は急にてあるべし。鞠にもかやうに侍るとぞ其の道の先達は申されし〉と述べ，心敬の《ささめごと》には〈万道の序・破・急〉という表現がある。芸能の分野では，観阿弥から〈一切（いつさい）の事（じ）に序破急あれば，申楽もこれ同じ。能の風情を以て定むべし〉との教えをうけた[世阿弥](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%96%E9%98%BF%E5%BC%A5-85456)は，能一曲の構成について，ワキ登場の段を序，シテ登場を破一段，シテ・ワキ応対を破二段，シテの物語から[中入り](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%AD%E5%85%A5%E3%82%8A-176907)を破三段，後ジテの場面の全体を急であるとし，また，番組編成についても，初番目を序，二番目を序の名残，三番目からを破とするなどと述べ，やがて，初番目が序で二，三，四番目が破の一，二，三段，五番目が急と説明されるようになる。世阿弥はさらに，〈一舞・一音の内〉や〈舞袖の一指，足踏の一響〉〈見所一見〉などにまで，序破急を説く。

　しかし，これら美学的思考と結びつけられた序破急はもちろん，能1曲の構成や番組編成上の序破急にしても，舞楽から取り入れたのはその言葉だけであり，芸能の構造そのものの直接的な影響を受けたわけではないと考えるのが穏当である。舞楽においては，序破急という熟した用語は用いられないといってよいが，[ほか](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%81%BB%E3%81%8B-2863718)の分野では，これが完全に一つの単語として，速度の漸急の意味で用いられることがあり，緩急変化や強弱変化を意味する場合もある。さらには，時間性を持たない[造形芸術](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%80%A0%E5%BD%A2%E8%8A%B8%E8%A1%93-89251)などまで，序破急の[有無](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9C%89%E7%84%A1-35201)が指摘されることもある。

## [日本大百科全書(ニッポニカ)](https://kotobank.jp/dictionary/nipponica/748/) 「序破急」の意味・わかりやすい解説

### 序破急  
じょはきゅう

日本の芸能に共通する理念の一つで、曲や劇の構成上の3段階をさす語。そのもとは雅楽の一曲を構成する三つの楽章をいい、序は緩やかな導入部、破は内容豊かな展開部分、急は急テンポの終章に相当する。この理念は能に入り、一曲の歌舞の展開と一日の上演曲目の配列との二様に適用されるようになる。一曲については序の舞、破の舞、急の舞の三段展開で、能の大成者世阿弥(ぜあみ)は『風姿花伝』において「一切の事に序破急あれば申楽(さるがく)もこれ同じ」と[明文化](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%98%8E%E6%96%87%E5%8C%96-643189)した。さらに『三道（能作書）』の冒頭には「序・破・急の三体を五段に作りなして、さて詞(ことば)を集めて、曲を附けて書連ぬるなり」とする。破をさらに破の序、破の破、破の急の3段に分けたのである。一日の演目もこれに準じて五番立てとされた。『習道書』に「能の序破急の事、脇能(わきのう)は序なり、二番・三番・四番は破にて、事を尽して、五番目は急にて果てて」一日の能はなると記されている。『花鏡(かきょう)』にはさらに具体的な詳述がある。

　この序破急の理念は下って浄瑠璃(じょうるり)に入り、初め6段または12段だった浄瑠璃の、時代物五段、世話物三段という劇的構成への進展を促した。このように序破急は日本の伝統芸能を貫く[構成原理](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%A7%8B%E6%88%90%E5%8E%9F%E7%90%86-2783925)といえるが、西洋でも[アリストテレス](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%81%82%E3%82%8A%E3%81%99%E3%81%A8%E3%81%A6%E3%82%8C%E3%81%99-3141907)が『詩学』において[ギリシア悲劇](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%81%8E%E3%82%8A%E3%81%97%E3%81%82%E6%82%B2%E5%8A%87-3149508)の完結性を「初めと中と終わりのあること」と定義したように、劇構成には東西を問わずみられる共通の理念である。浄瑠璃の五段展開は、ローマ劇やフランス古典劇の金科玉条とされた五幕形式に比較されよう。しかも世阿弥が「一切の事に序破急あれば」といったように、「[起承転結](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B5%B7%E6%89%BF%E8%BB%A2%E7%B5%90-12404)」の語と同様、演劇・芸能に限らず人生そのものを含めてあらゆる物事に通じる基本的な理念である。

### 【雅楽】より

［リズム］  
　雅楽曲のリズムには非拍節的なものと拍節的なものとがあり，神道系祭式芸能では前者を静（しず）拍子，後者を揚（あげ）拍子ということがある。大陸系の楽舞では，非拍節的リズムは序破急(後述)の〈序〉の部分，および〈音取（ねとり）〉，〈調子〉(〈品玄（ぼんげん）〉〈入調（にゆうぢよう）〉)，〈乱声（らんじよう）〉などにみられるが，リズム型そのものを表す用語はない。拍節的リズムは〈～拍子〉といって区別され，代表的なものに早（はや）拍子，延（のべ）拍子，只（ただ）拍子，八多良（やたら）拍子の4種がある。…
