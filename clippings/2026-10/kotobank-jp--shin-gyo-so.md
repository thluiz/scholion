## [Selected Edition: Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Great Dictionary of the Japanese Language)](https://kotobank.jp/dictionary/nikkokuseisen/2144/) Meaning, Reading, Example Sentences, and Synonyms of “真行草 (shingyōsō, standard-semicursive-cursive)”

### しん‐ぎょう‐そう‥ギャウサウ【真行草】

1. **Noun**
2. **1.** A collective designation for the kanji script styles of 楷書 (kaisho, standard script), 行書 (gyōsho, semi-cursive script), and 草書 (sōsho, cursive script). Also called 真草行 (shinsōgyō, standard-cursive-semicursive).
   1. [Earliest attested example] “況復真行草書勢、絶而不レ継痛哉乎”  
      (Source: 菅家後集 (Kanke Kōshū, Later Collection of Sugawara) [ca. 903], “傷野大夫”)
   2. [Other literature] 〔書史会要 (Shoshi Kaiyō, Essentials of Calligraphic History) - Song〕
3. **2.** By extension, the three kinds of linking in 連歌 (renga, linked verse) and 俳諧 (haikai, comic linked verse), such as 付合 (tsukeai, verse-linking). 真 (shin, “formal/true”) is a way of linking that corresponds exactly to the previous verse in both sense and wording, and also the style of linked verse so produced. 行 (gyō, “semi-formal/running”) is a way of linking in which sense and wording generally conform to the previous verse, and also that verse style. 草 (sō, “informal/cursive”) is a way of linking that relates only loosely or vaguely, and also that verse style. The term is also used for formal styles in 華道 (kadō, the way of flowers / flower arrangement), painting, gardens, and so on. Also called 真草行 (shinsōgyō, standard-cursive-semicursive).
   1. [Earliest attested example] “その身は軍慮に他念なく暫時の隙も机(おしまづき)、真行草のかたからぬ”  
      (Source: 浄瑠璃 (jōruri, narrative puppet theater) ・ 近江源氏先陣館 (Ōmi Genji Senjinkan, The Ōmi Genji Frontline Camp) [1769], book 7)

## [Revised New Edition: Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Encyclopedia of the World)](https://kotobank.jp/dictionary/sekaidaihyakka/493/) Easy-to-understand Explanation of “真行草 (shingyōsō, standard-semicursive-cursive)”

### 真行草 (しんぎょうそう)

A term indicating the typological categories of calligraphic styles in [書道 (shodō, calligraphy)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9B%B8%E9%81%93-535009). Furthermore, it is a term that came to be widely used as an expression of the Japanese aesthetic sense. It is said that [王羲之 (Wáng Xīzhī, Wang Xizhi)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%8E%8B%E7%BE%B2%E4%B9%8B-38607), who laid the foundations of calligraphy in China’s Eastern Jin period, established the three forms of 真 (shin, standard; i.e. 楷書 (kaisho, standard script)), 行 (gyō, semi-cursive), and 草 (sō, cursive) in contrast to the ancient scripts [篆書 (tensho, seal script)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%AF%86%E6%9B%B8-102437) and [隷書 (reisho, clerical script)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%9A%B7%E6%9B%B8-151275). In Japan, in the 《正倉院献物帳 (Shōsōin Kenmotsuchō, Shōsōin inventory of presented objects)》 from the latter part of the Nara period, the characters 真行草 appear as writing attributed to Wang Xizhi. With the spread of calligraphy, 真, the most dignified and well-ordered form, 草, the most unconventional form standing at the opposite extreme, and 行, the middle term between them, came to be used as terminology for a three-stage expression of style in a variety of genres besides calligraphy. For example, the terms 真行草 appear in such things as painting styles, the formats of [表具 (hyōgu, mounting of artworks)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A1%A8%E5%85%B7-613182), plans and [意匠 (ishō, design)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%84%8F%E5%8C%A0-432322) for gardens and architecture, modes of artistic performance in the performing arts, and manners of conduct in [作法 (sahō, etiquette/procedure)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BD%9C%E6%B3%95-509733). Increasingly often, the classification of styles appropriate to time, place, and rank is indicated by means of 真行草.

However, in Chinese calligraphy, within the relationship among 真・行・草, 真 is precisely what is granted the highest value as the most orthodox and proper form, while 行 and 草 are regarded as styles that follow after 真. In Japan, by contrast, especially once the [中世 (chūsei, medieval period)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%AD%E4%B8%96-97328) arrived and distinctively Japanese aesthetic consciousness developed, 真行草 came to be seen as equal in value, or even as though 草 were the most fully [熟達 (jukutatsu, mastered)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%86%9F%E9%81%94-528252) [書風 (shofū, calligraphic style)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9B%B8%E9%A2%A8-535091), giving rise to the idea that upon reaching the extreme of 草 the scale of values reverses. In the world of [芸道 (geidō, artistic way)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%8A%B8%E9%81%93-59071) in particular, the medieval period saw attention directed toward forms of beauty that might be called the beauty of imperfection, such as “わび (wabi, austere simplicity)” and “冷え枯れる (hiekareru, chilled and withered).” A dominant way of thinking emerged that sought to attain the more spiritual beauty of wabi by toning down and breaking up the dignity of 真 through [やつし (yatsushi, intentional reduction/plainification)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%82%84%E3%81%A4%E3%81%97-1212427). For example, in the tea ceremony, tea in the shoin-daisu style is taken as 真, and in formal public ritual one follows that form; however, “〈心の至る所は，草の小座敷にしくことなし〉 (Kokoro no itaru tokoro wa, sō no kozashiki ni shiku koto nashi, ‘Where the heart ultimately arrives, nothing surpasses the small rustic room of 草’)” (《南方録 (Nanpōroku, Record of the Southern Direction)》), and thus tea in the wabi grass-hermitage style is regarded as [茶の湯 (chanoyu, tea practice)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%8C%B6%E3%81%AE%E6%B9%AF-96782) in which one savors the depth of the heart. 真行草 is used not only when indicating such differences in style, but also at times as a term for stages of training. Thoroughgoing practice is the 〈守格 (shukaku, preserving the form)〉 in which one fully masters the formality of 真; but once one becomes a master, it is argued in Noh and other arts, one should break the form and perform freely, aiming at 〈破格 (hakaku, breaking the form)〉. A near-synonymous expression is 〈守破離 (shuhari, keep-break-leave)〉.

## [改訂新版　世界大百科事典](https://kotobank.jp/dictionary/sekaidaihyakka/493/) 「真行草」の意味・わかりやすい解説

### 真行草 (しんぎょうそう)

[書道](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9B%B8%E9%81%93-535009)の書体の[類型](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%A1%9E%E5%9E%8B-150551)を示す言葉。さらに日本人の美意識の表現として広く用いられた言葉である。中国の東晋時代に書道の基礎を作った[王羲之](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%8E%8B%E7%BE%B2%E4%B9%8B-38607)によって，古代の[篆書](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%AF%86%E6%9B%B8-102437)（てんしよ）・[隷書](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%9A%B7%E6%9B%B8-151275)（れいしよ）に対して真（楷書）・行・草の三体が確立したといわれ，日本では奈良時代後期の《正倉院献物帳》に王羲之の書として真行草の文字が見える。書道の普及とともに，最も[格式](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%A0%BC%E5%BC%8F-51708)の高く整った真と，その[対極](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%AF%BE%E6%A5%B5-556753)に位置する最も[破格](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%A0%B4%E6%A0%BC-599765)の草，その中間項の行を，3段階の[様式](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%A7%98%E5%BC%8F-145686)表現の用語として，書道以外のさまざまのジャンルでも用いるようになった。たとえば絵画の画体や[表具](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A1%A8%E5%85%B7-613182)の形式，庭や建築のプランと[意匠](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%84%8F%E5%8C%A0-432322)，芸能における芸態，[作法](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BD%9C%E6%B3%95-509733)におけるふるまい方などに真行草の語があらわれ，時・所・位にふさわしい様式の分類が真行草によって示されることが多くなる。

　ところで中国書道における真行草の関係は，真こそ最も正統なる格式として高い価値が与えられ，行・草は真に追随する様式とされるが，日本では，ことに日本的美意識の発達する[中世](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%AD%E4%B8%96-97328)になると真行草は等価値，ないし草こそ最も[熟達](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%86%9F%E9%81%94-528252)の[書風](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9B%B8%E9%A2%A8-535091)として，草の極に至って価値観が逆転してしまう発想が生じてくる。ことに[芸道](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%8A%B8%E9%81%93-59071)の世界では中世に不完全の美ともいうべき〈わび〉〈冷え枯れる〉といった美しさが注目され，真の格を[やつし](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%82%84%E3%81%A4%E3%81%97-1212427)，くずすことによって，より精神性の高い〈わび〉の美に至ろうとする思想が支配的になる。たとえば茶道では書院台子の茶を真とし，ハレの儀礼ではその形式に従うのであるが，〈心の至る所は，草の小座敷にしくことなし〉（《南方録》）と，わび草庵の茶を心の深味を味わう[茶の湯](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%8C%B6%E3%81%AE%E6%B9%AF-96782)としている。真行草はこうした様式上の違いを示す場合のほかに，稽古の段階を示す語として用いることもある。徹底した稽古は真の格を体得する〈守格〉であるが，名人となれば形式をくずし自由に演じ〈破格〉をめざすべきだ，と能楽などで論じている。同義に近い言葉として〈守破離〉がある。
