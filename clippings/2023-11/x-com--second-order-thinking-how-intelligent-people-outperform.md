𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝘁𝗲𝗰𝗵𝗻𝗶𝗾𝘂𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗹𝗹𝗶𝗴𝗲𝗻𝘁 𝗽𝗲𝗼𝗽𝗹𝗲 𝘂𝘀𝗲 𝘁𝗼 𝗼𝘂𝘁𝗽𝗲𝗿𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗺

Howard Marks defines second-order thinking, or what he refers to as second-level thinking, in his outstanding book "𝘛𝘩𝘦 𝘔𝘰𝘴𝘵 𝘐𝘮𝘱𝘰𝘳𝘵𝘢𝘯𝘵 𝘛𝘩𝘪𝘯𝘨."

𝗙𝗶𝗿𝘀𝘁-𝗼𝗿𝗱𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴: It's the immediate reaction to a situation, often based on surface-level observations. "This decision will lead to X."

𝗦𝗲𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗱-𝗼𝗿𝗱𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴: This delves into the following consequences of that initial decision. "If X happens, then Y might follow."

What are the ways we can put second-order thinking into practice:

𝟭. 𝗦𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘁 𝘄𝗶𝘁𝗵 𝗦𝗲𝗹𝗳-𝗤𝘂𝗲𝘀𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴: After making a decision or judgment, ask yourself: "And then what?" This simple question can uncover the next layer of consequences.

𝟮. 𝗩𝗶𝘀𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗶𝘇𝗲: Create a visual representation of your decision. Start with the primary outcome in the center, then branch out to secondary, tertiary, and further consequences.

𝟯. 𝗖𝗵𝗮𝗹𝗹𝗲𝗻𝗴𝗲 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗮𝘀𝘀𝘂𝗺𝗽𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀. Play devil's advocate.

𝟰. 𝗦𝘁𝘂𝗱𝘆 𝗣𝗮𝘀𝘁 𝗗𝗲𝗰𝗶𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀: What unforeseen consequences arose?

𝟱. 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗮𝗴𝗲 𝗶𝗻 𝗚𝗿𝗼𝘂𝗽 𝗧𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗸 𝗧𝗮𝗻𝗸𝘀: Organize brainstorming sessions where team members explore the potential outcomes of a decision. Collective thinking often reveals consequences an individual might overlook.

𝟲. 𝗨𝘀𝗲 𝗗𝗲𝗰𝗶𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗧𝗿𝗲𝗲𝘀: These tools help you map out every possible decision outcome, visualizing the path of consequences.

𝟳. 𝗥𝗲𝗳𝗹𝗲𝗰𝘁: Set aside time, weekly or monthly, to reflect on your decisions. Consider what went as expected and what unforeseen consequences arose.

The usage of second-order thinking in software engineering could be: Before implementing a new software feature, consider how users will adapt or misuse this feature. How will it impact system performance or security?
