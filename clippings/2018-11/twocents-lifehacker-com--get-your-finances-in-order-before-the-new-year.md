The year is crawling to a close, which means it’s time for year-end lists and New Year’s resolutions. Maybe you’re going old school and waiting until Jan. 1 to get a jump start on your dreams. Or, maybe you’re taking [Aimée’s advice](https://lifehacker.com/make-a-resolution-for-the-last-six-weeks-of-the-year-1830555411#_ga=2.83628629.211797541.1543240974-594046802.1524762060) and working on a new goal in the last few weeks of the year.

Regardless of your New Year’s resolution plan, there are a few things you’ll want to take care of before 2019 kicks off.

Use Your FSA Money

Or roll over $500 to next year, if your employer allows it. Consider the year ahead: Should you contribute the same amount to your FSA (or HSA), or do you need to increase or decrease contributions, based on your family’s medical needs?

Make an Additional 401(k) Contribution

You might consider increasing your 401(k) contributions leading into the new year, if you can afford to do so. You have until April to make IRA contributions for 2018, but 401(k) contributions for the year are typically due by Dec. 31. For 2019, try increasing your contributions by [just one percent](https://twocents.lifehacker.com/you-just-need-to-increase-your-savings-by-1-a-year-1826984496). It’s possible!

Tune Up Your Investments

No, not in reaction to the market’s recent swings, but because it’s good to tune up your portfolio every now and then. Check your risk tolerance, and see if your allocations match up with that and your long-term goals. [Rebalance if necessary](https://twocents.lifehacker.com/when-to-rebalance-your-portfolio-1822973185).

Check Your Credit Report

If you haven’t accessed your [free credit reports](https://twocents.lifehacker.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-credit-how-to-check-1797093506) for the year, it’s a great time to do. Fraud and identity theft are rampant, as are mistakes by credit agencies. Catching things now will only make things easier in the future. And if you’re not planning on applying for credit (or a mortgage, etc.) any time soon, [freeze your credit reports](https://twocents.lifehacker.com/how-to-thaw-your-frozen-credit-reports-1829656046).

Consider Your Guideposts

As I’ve written before, I don’t believe in hard and fast rules about where a person “should” be in their financial lives at certain ages; everything is relative, and everyone’s circumstances differ. That said, if you’re in your 30s and don’t have a grasp on your retirement options or a plan to tackle your debt, that’s ok. You can start now.

A new year is a good time to take stock of where you are along your financial path, and where you’d like to be. If you keep making the same mistakes, it’s worth it, for your stress levels alone, to work out how you can change.

Pick Something to Look Forward To

As I’ve written before, having [something to look forward to](https://twocents.lifehacker.com/pick-something-to-look-forward-to-1827443275) is a great way not only to put your money to better use, but to make yourself happier over all. This could be a far-off vacation, or a good book you’ll treat yourself to after reaching a certain goals. And speaking of...

Make a Goal

...set a [financial goal](https://twocents.lifehacker.com/whats-your-2018-money-goal-1821064068) for 2019. Over the long weekend, I took some time out to reflect on the year and what I wanted to do over the next 12 months. I crossed a few things off of my long-term to-do list in 2018, but there’s always more to be done. Having this in mind is a great way to get motivated for the year ahead, but for me it was more practical than that: Writing down my goals, I started to get a sense of how much money I would need to make things happen.

I also set a few finance-specific goals for myself. I’m flexible with these; having written about money and goal-setting for a few years now, I understand that I can’t control everything and that even the best of intentions can fail. And that’s ok. But I’m also a firm believer in having some sort of idea of what you want and taking steps, [small and large](https://lifehacker.com/focus-on-accomplishing-one-task-today-1830652944#_ga=2.145964407.211797541.1543240974-594046802.1524762060), to attain it.

Consider using a [Personal Inventory Day](https://twocents.lifehacker.com/schedule-a-personal-inventory-day-each-month-1821807681#_ga=2.82942943.87543563.1543241483-3846207152.1521480874) to get everything done, or perform a [life audit](https://twocents.lifehacker.com/perform-a-life-audit-to-set-yourself-up-for-success-in-1821648221) to get a broader sense of what you want. You can start now, or Jan. 1 or in April—the important thing is to consider where you are in your financial life, where you’d like to be, and what steps you can take to get there.
