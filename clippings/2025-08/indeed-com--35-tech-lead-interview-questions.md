## General tech lead interview questions

These general tech lead interview questions help an [employer](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/employer) learn more about your personality and interest in a technical lead position:

-   [Tell me about yourself.](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/interview-question-tell-me-about-yourself)
-   What are your interests outside of work?
-   Why are you interested in this role?
-   [Why do you want to work for our company?](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/why-are-you-interested-in-our-company)
-   What influenced you to get into programming?
-   Are you ready to lead a team?
-   Why do you want a [leadership](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/what-is-leadership) position?
-   What skills help you be an effective technical lead?
-   How would your previous employer describe you?
-   [What is your ideal work environment?](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/ideal-work-environment)
-   Are you open to [mentorship](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/mentorship) programs?
-   What are your goals as a technical lead?

## Questions about tech lead experience and background

These questions help an employer decide if you have the right experience and background to be a qualified technical lead:

-   Which programming languages do you know well?
-   What is your favorite programming language, and why?
-   Tell me about a successful project you led.
-   Are you comfortable giving in-depth presentations?
-   Tell me how you manage priorities.
-   Are you working on any projects in your spare time?
-   Are you able to explain complex technologies in simple terms?
-   What resources do you use to troubleshoot technical issues?
-   Tell me about your [project management](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/what-is-project-management) experience.
-   Explain the steps you take to review a team member's code.
-   What tools do you use to test the quality of code?
-   What IT help desk software are you familiar with?

## In-depth tech lead interview questions

These in-depth questions help an employer gain specific insight into your technical abilities and other skills related to the role:

-   How would you measure your team's performance?
-   What steps would you take to help your team members create goals?
-   What steps would you take to find qualified team members?
-   What would you do if there was a disagreement between your team members?
-   How would you motivate your team to meet deadlines?
-   Tell me about a time things didn't go as planned. What did you do to handle this situation?
-   How would you properly onboard a new team member?
-   If you found a developer who would be more efficient on another team, how would you implement this transfer?
-   How would you respond if a team member suggested using new hardware or software?
-   Explain your research process when designing a new software system.
-   What is the first thing you would do as the technical lead at our company?

## Tech lead interview questions with sample answers

Using some questions from above, here is advice on how to effectively answer common tech lead interview questions. Use these sample answers as inspiration when preparing for your interview.

### Why do you want a leadership position?

As a leadership role, employers ask this question to gauge your motivation for wanting to lead. It helps them assess whether you have the skills to manage a team and prioritize tasks effectively. To prepare, reflect on your [leadership skills](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/leadership-skills) and experience, and consider why a leadership position aligns with your abilities.Example: "I want a leadership position because I have the skills to effectively motivate and inspire a team of developers. In my previous role, I became more confident in my coding abilities, and now I have the expertise to offer support and advice to developers who are still perfecting their coding methods. I have always been someone who meets deadlines and is comfortable talking to clients, so I think I am ready for a leadership position at this point in my career."Read more: [15 Leadership Qualities That Make a Great Leader](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/leadership-qualities-that-make-a-great-leader)

### What skills help you be an effective technical lead?

A technical lead needs both technical and [people skills](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/people-skills) to effectively lead their team. An employer asks this question to see if you have these specific skills. Before your interview, read through the job description and highlight the skills the employer requires for this role. Decide which of these skills you can confidently say you have.Example: "An effective technical lead needs a combination of leadership and [technical skills](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/technical-skills) to efficiently do their job, and I believe I have both of these skills. Throughout my experience as a developer, I have refined my programming and technical abilities. As I grew more comfortable with my work, I challenged myself to help other developers with their projects. I found that I had the skills to explain complicated tasks to my coworkers and help them troubleshoot tasks, which is an important part of being a technical lead."Related: [Technical Lead Skills: Definitions and Examples (With Video)](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/technical-lead-skills)

### Are you comfortable giving in-depth presentations?

A technical lead is often responsible for presenting their team's work to clients or the rest of the company. Employers ask this question to make sure you can be an effective public speaker. In your answer, explain what skills help you feel confident speaking in front of others. Share a previous professional experience you have with public speaking.Example: "Yes, I actually have quite a bit of experience with public speaking. At my last company, I gave monthly company-wide presentations about the work my team was doing. Since we had many people in non-technical roles at our company, I needed to explain complex technical concepts in simple terms that everyone could understand. I have grown used to answering questions about projects and have figured out ways to help everyone be able to understand what developers do."Related: [How To Create an Effective Data Presentation in 6 Steps (With Tips)](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/how-to-create-data-presentation)

### What steps would you take to help your team members create goals?

Employers tend to want technical leads who can motivate their teams and help them create goals. This question helps the employer understand if you have a useful goal-creating process. Prepare for this question by thinking about what you do to create your own goals. The [SMART goal method](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/smart-goals) is one useful way you can help your team make more attainable goals.Example: "I would meet with each team member for an employee performance evaluation to discuss their strengths, weaknesses and what goals they should make moving forward. I find that [SMART goals](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/how-to-write-smart-goals) help people set a clear objective and plan for reaching their goals. SMART stands for specific, measurable, attainable, relevant and time-based. When helping a team member creates a goal, I would go through each part of a SMART goal with them to make sure they are thinking through the goals they are making."

### How would you respond if a team member suggested using new hardware or software?

This question helps employers determine how [open-minded](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/open-minded) you are about new technology solutions. It also shows them your willingness to work with others and embrace their ideas. In your answer, explain what process you would use to determine if a new hardware or software is cost-effective for your team. It could be useful to explain your research process.Example: "In this situation, I would find a time to meet with this team member to further discuss why they think this new hardware or software is useful for our team. I would ask them if they have any experience using it and what features stood out to them. If this new technology seems useful for our team, I would do some research of my own to determine if it is cost-effective for our department. If it is feasible to have, I would figure out a way to fit it into our budget and then create training materials on how to use it."
