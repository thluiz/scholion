The post says aspiring software consultants should think about positioning before anything else. It argues that most developers misread why they get hired, because employers filter for resumes, keywords, experience, geography, and network effects rather than pure coding merit.

## Reading notes

- Positioning is described as how you differentiate yourself from competitors.
- The article treats other software developers as competitors and encourages thinking like a company of one.
- It contrasts the usual developer mindset with the reality that hiring managers often choose among filtered, interchangeable candidates.
- It calls this mistaken self-understanding “expert beginnerism.”
- Positioning strategy is framed as maximizing the number of “why” conversations you can handle well.
- A good why conversation should make it hard to choose anyone else instead of you.
- The “senior software engineer” example shows weak positioning because the role is just a labor gap.
- The stealth consultant example shows stronger positioning when a specialty is needed and others lack it.
- Positioning can be built around a target market, geography, a niche skill, a methodology, or a unique benefit.
- The buyer has to be human and able to understand the value being offered.
- The post closes by urging readers to study and practice position strategy instead of competing in generic skill contests.
