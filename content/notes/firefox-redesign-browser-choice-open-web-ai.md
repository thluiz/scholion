Mozilla says the Firefox 157 redesign is meant to make the browser feel more modern and help it compete for users on product merits, not only on open-web values. Ajit Varma says the team is trying to move faster, support customization, and create more moments where people actively consider which browser they want to use.

## Reading notes

- The redesign is meant to broaden Firefox’s audience beyond privacy-focused users and open-web advocates.
- Varma says Firefox is trying to improve performance, compatibility, and design at the same time.
- Mozilla says faster development has been helped by AI tools.
- Firefox wants to feel modern, match operating-system design language, restore compact mode, and offer more customization.
- Varma says the browser is aimed at a wide group of people, not only users who already care about Gecko or Chromium.
- He says many users do not know the differences between Chromium, Blink, Chrome, Gecko, or Firefox.
- Firefox’s opportunity is to feel special and enjoyable compared with Chromium-based browsers that often look similar.
- Varma says a major barrier is that many people do not actively consider browser choice.
- In Europe, mobile choice screens under the Digital Markets Act have helped Firefox grow.
- Firefox gained users after Chromium browsers dropped Manifest V2 support, while Firefox kept supporting it.
- Mozilla’s AI approach is based on choice, with top-level controls to turn AI off or limit it.
- Varma says Firefox is seeing interest in enterprise and school tools, including manageability and digital loss prevention.
- He says digital sovereignty is another reason some organizations want Firefox.
- Firefox is investing in enterprise tools more heavily than it has publicly emphasized.
- Mozilla says AI use is still a minority of overall browser use.
- The team is focusing on on-device models, open source models, and practical features such as translation and summarization.
- Varma says AI work is less than 10 percent of engineering, while Gecko remains the biggest investment.
- Mozilla says it should have invested in mobile apps earlier.
- For Gecko on iPhone, the biggest obstacle is the cost of maintaining separate iOS builds for different markets.
- Firefox remains dependent on Google search revenue, but Varma says competition and more browser use would strengthen Mozilla’s position.
