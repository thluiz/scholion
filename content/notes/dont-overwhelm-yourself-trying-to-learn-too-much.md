The post says that learning is valuable, but trying to learn everything creates pressure and makes people feel behind. It argues that much of the material studied in advance is forgotten or never used, while learning tied to real problems is retained better.

## Reading notes

- Learning too many technologies at once can make developers feel overwhelmed and unable to keep up.
- The pressure to master everything can feed impostor syndrome and lead to reading books, taking courses, and consuming more material than can be absorbed.
- The author says this kind of forced pace is not very effective, especially for technologies that are not used often.
- The technologies learned best are the ones put into practice.
- He favors just-in-time learning, meaning learning as close as possible to the moment when the information is needed.
- Before starting with a technology, he says it is enough to learn how to get started, what the technology can do, and the basics used most of the time.
- Courses should focus on those three areas and leave details for later, when they become necessary.
- Real learning happens when information is used for a purpose, not when it is collected in advance.
- A developer cannot know everything in software development because new technologies keep appearing and existing ones keep growing in depth.
- The author recommends specializing in one technology in depth instead of spreading attention too thin.
- He also recommends keeping a broad base of general software development skills that apply across technologies.
- Super-programmers are presented as an illusion, since they usually have one or two specialties plus broad general knowledge.
