The article opens by arguing that no language, including English, can hold enough expression to cover the full range of human experience. It then presents examples of words from several languages that name feelings, behaviors, and social situations English lacks a single word for.

## Reading notes

- The article says English has at least 250,000 words, but still cannot express everything a language can convey.
- It frames the examples as cases where other languages have found a precise word and English has not.
- Toska is described as spiritual anguish, longing, restlessness, yearning, nostalgia, love-sickness, or boredom.
- Mamihlapinatapei names the wordless but meaningful look shared by two people who both want to begin something but hesitate.
- Jayus refers to a joke told so badly and so unfunny that people cannot help laughing.
- Iktsuarpok means going outside to check whether someone is coming.
- Litost is presented as agony and torment caused by suddenly seeing one’s own misery.
- Kyoikumama means a mother who pushes her children relentlessly toward academic achievement.
- Tartle is the hesitation that happens when introducing someone because their name has been forgotten.
- Ilunga is defined as a person who forgives a first abuse, tolerates a second, and does not forgive or tolerate a third.
- Prozvonit means calling a mobile phone and letting it ring once so the other person calls back and saves money.
- Cafuné means tenderly running one’s fingers through someone’s hair.
