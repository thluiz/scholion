The article argues that landing page performance depends on psychology more than on button color or font choices. It says strong pages make the offer clear, create curiosity about what comes next, and remind users of a pain the product or service can solve.

## Reading notes

- Effective landing pages are built on buyer psychology, not small visual tweaks.
- The page should be obvious: users should know what the page is about, what the product or service does, and what they are supposed to do.
- Clarity can come from a large headline, direct language, a subheading that explains the headline, and images that reinforce the offer or help users feel its significance.
- The page should create curiosity by giving enough information for users to understand the CTA while withholding enough to make them want to click.
- The article uses examples such as ConversionXL and Pandora to show how curiosity can drive clicks.
- The page should also make users feel some pain by reminding them of discomfort, lack, or loss tied to the absence of the product or service.
- The role of the landing page is to remind users of that pain and present the product or service as the answer.
