The page says people use persuasion all the time in everyday disputes, and that Aristotle identified three core strategies: ethos, logos, and pathos. It connects those strategies to research papers, speeches, and debates, where writers need to build a thesis and argue for it clearly.

## Reading notes

- Persuasion is part of everyday arguments with parents and friends.
- Aristotle named three persuasion strategies: ethos, logos, and pathos.
- Research papers, speeches, and debates also rely on persuasion.
- Logos is appeal to reason based on logic, facts, and statistics.
- Academic arguments rely on logos, and numbers are presented as sound and logical.
- Ethos is the credibility of the person making the argument or stating the facts.
- Credible sources strengthen ethos, and writing should be error-free and concise.
- Pathos appeals to emotion by influencing how the audience feels and imagines a situation.
- Emotional appeals can be effective, but they should be used sparingly in academic papers.
- A paper about the death penalty should rely mainly on logic, with emotion used only when appropriate.
- A paper made only of emotion and opinion is not considered professional and may fail.
