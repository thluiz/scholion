The page gathers 21 ways to reduce online tracking and keep activity harder to trace. It ranges from extensions for webmail and HTTPS to VPNs, Tor, proxy servers, privacy-focused browsers, password managers, and account deletion, while stressing that no single measure guarantees anonymity.

## Reading notes

- browser extensions like Mailvelope can add OpenPGP encryption to webmail services such as Gmail or Yahoo Mail
- private browsing mainly stops the browser from storing cookies and history on the local machine, but it does not hide IP address or identity from the wider web
- social media services collect large amounts of personal data, and deleting accounts is the only sure way to remove it fully
- Ghostery can reveal web trackers and lets the user choose which ones to block
- encrypted email providers such as Hushmail and MyKolab offer more privacy than mainstream services, though they can still be subject to lawful access
- disposable email addresses are useful for avoiding spam and keeping a real address away from websites that require one
- VPNs hide IP address and route data through an encrypted tunnel
- Tor makes tracking harder and can support anonymous access to the hidden web, but it can be slow and restrictive
- proxy servers mask IP address and can make browsing appear to come from another country
- HTTPS Everywhere forces websites to use HTTPS when available
- deleting cookies reduces how much websites can learn about browsing habits
- DuckDuckGo is presented as a search engine that does not track searches
- privacy-focused browsers can help, but the article says they work best alongside other measures
- Edward Snowden recommends Spideroak over Dropbox for more privacy-friendly file storage
- Blackphone is described as a privacy-focused smartphone with built-in protections
- password managers help users keep different strong passwords instead of reusing weak ones
- Whonix is described as a security-focused operating system built around Tor
- Darkcoin is presented as an anonymous cryptocurrency
- virtual machines can isolate risky files from the main system
- browser extensions such as NoScript and ScriptSafe let users control JavaScript more closely
- the final step for complete anonymity is to avoid going online at all and destroy connected devices
