The author argues that motivation for personal projects often fades after the first burst of excitement, and that this is normal. He says many side projects stay unfinished, but a project can still be worth starting and finishing when the scope is clear and the work is organized.

## Reading notes

- Many side projects begin with strong motivation and then stall after a few days.
- He does not finish all of his side projects and says that is fine.
- If an idea still feels worth doing the next day, then start it.
- Use a checklist for recurring project needs like a README, npm publishing, and .gitignore files.
- Keep a generic boilerplate so you do not repeat the same setup work every time.
- Avoid trying to learn more than one new technology at once, because several learning curves can increase demotivation.
- Set aside focused time for the project, without waiting for inspiration.
- Define the project scope and return to it when new ideas appear.
- Share the project on social media, in a blog post, or with friends.
- The author connects side projects with career growth, open source, and improving his English skills.
