The article frames design thinking as a method for tackling complex problems through empathy, definition, ideation, prototyping, and testing. It then focuses on ideation and presents six approaches teams can use to generate new ideas.

## Reading notes

- Design thinking is presented as a solution-based methodology for complex problems.
- The article emphasizes the ideate phase as the part where original solutions are generated.
- Thinking like a designer is described as useful for business because designers focus on people, see problems as changeable, and start working before a plan is fully formed.
- Connecting the dots is described as combining two or more previously unrelated concepts to create a remarkable idea.
- One suggested method is to define categories such as current products, target demographics, and companies admired, then use cards to find new combinations.
- Group brainstorming is described as weak for original ideas because it can produce groupthink and let loud voices dominate.
- Individual brainstorming is presented as more useful when it includes bad ideas, time limits, and clear constraints.
- Brainwriting is described as a better group method, with silent writing, passing cards, and building on one another’s ideas.
- Jumping in and playing is framed as starting with a rough idea and exploring it hands-on before worrying about final outcomes.
- Looking to the web for inspiration is presented as studying existing solutions in order to create an original one.
- Whiteboarding is described as a way to organize ideas, keep a meeting on track, and record thoughts that might otherwise be lost.
- The closing principles say to empathize first, focus on solutions, and use a hands-on approach to ideation.
