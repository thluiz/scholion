The article says the first 30 days as a new manager are a period of uncertainty, and that mistakes are expected. It draws on manager conversations and Know Your Team data to name six common pitfalls and points toward a more careful approach built on listening, trust, and learning.

## Reading notes

- Do not rely only on group communication; schedule one-on-one meetings with each team member to understand their concerns, work style, and where they are stuck.
- Do not start with a “here’s what I think” speech; spend the first month asking questions, listening actively, and learning the context.
- Do not optimize for likability; focus on earning trust through clear intentions and follow-through.
- Do not skip conversations with other managers in similar roles; ask about culture, meetings, best practices, and challenges they did not expect.
- Do not aim to be the best manager in a generic sense; aim to be the best manager for each individual, since people have different preferences and experiences.
- Do not avoid saying “I don’t know”; admit uncertainty, learn what you need to know, and avoid damaging your judgment and credibility.
