The post says reading is part of professional life for software developers and argues that books preserve refined ideas better than videos or workshops. It presents an opinionated list of ten books that shaped the author’s career, centered on coding, design, style, best practices, architecture, agile work, teams, algorithms, and legacy code.

## Reading notes

- The author treats reading as an integral part of software work and says books keep thoughts and ideas more refined than other forms of knowledge exchange.
- The list is personal and not ordered by rank.
- The selection avoids technology tutorial books such as learning a language in 24 hours or mastering Node.js.
- The books emphasized are about coding, design, style, and best practices.
- The Clean Coder presents professionalism in programming through honor, self-respect, honest communication, faithful estimation, and responsibility.
- Clean Code argues that poorly written code can damage an organization and offers advice to improve code quality.
- Clean Architecture is presented as a guide to software structure and design that draws on the author’s experience.
- Head First Design Patterns is recommended for learning design patterns through clear explanations, examples, and practical focus.
- Peopleware discusses management problems in software development and is described as useful both for managers and for job seekers who want to notice warning signs.
- The Algorithm Design Manual is recommended for programmers who want strong algorithm foundations, especially for interviews at large companies.
- Agile Software Development, Principles, Patterns, and Practices goes deeper into object-oriented programming, software architecture, and agile methodology.
- Working Effectively with Legacy Code is described as a needed guide for dealing with legacy systems.
- Head First Object-Oriented Analysis and Design is presented as an introduction to OOP analysis and design.
- The Pragmatic Programmer is described as filling gaps left by the other books and helping readers avoid common pitfalls.
