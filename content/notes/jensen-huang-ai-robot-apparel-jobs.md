Jensen Huang says AI will not simply remove jobs. In his view, work that is more than a set of tasks can survive, while purely task-based roles may be automated. He points to radiologists as an example and says new industries will grow around robots.

## Reading notes

- Huang said jobs need to be more than individual tasks if they are to survive AI.
- He used radiologists as an example, saying their job is to diagnose disease, not just study images.
- He said task-only work, such as chopping vegetables, could be replaced by automation.
- He expects new industries and jobs to appear as robots become mainstream.
- He mentioned technicians and people who manufacture robots as one new area.
- He also described a future robot apparel industry, so robots could look different from one another.
- He said the details of AI’s end point are unknown, and that progress will likely be gradual.
- He said concern about new technology can push AI toward safer, more reliable systems.
- He said AI is already doing more research, reflection, and tool use before answering, which has helped reduce hallucinations.
