The article defines asynchronous communication as sending messages without expecting an immediate response, and contrasts it with synchronous communication, where people respond right away. It argues that constant real-time communication fragments attention, increases stress, and makes meaningful progress harder, while async creates more room for focus, planning, and flexible schedules.

## Reading notes

- Asynchronous communication means sending a message without expecting an immediate response.
- Synchronous communication requires people to be present at the same time or to reply quickly in chats and email.
- Real-time communication creates frequent interruptions, context switching, and less time for deep work.
- Constant availability pushes people to stay online, attend more meetings, and feel more stress.
- Async communication supports better planning, higher-quality replies, and less back-and-forth.
- Writing-centered communication documents decisions and makes information easier to find later.
- Async work helps teams across time zones and gives remote and in-office employees access to the same information.
- The article says synchronous communication is still useful for rapport, sensitive feedback, brainstorming, urgent crises, and some team meetings.
- Moving toward async requires overcommunication, planning ahead, fewer unnecessary meetings, documented discussions, clear response-time expectations, and tools that support transparency.
- The article also says teams need emergency channels and some synchronous contact to maintain personal connection and handle urgent issues.
