Milan Jovanović argues that Clean Architecture works especially well for complex, large‑scale applications. He notes that while it is not revolutionary, it gives clear guidance on how to structure code and offers promises like maintainability, testability, loose coupling, and separation of concerns. He uses it when he wants to apply Domain‑Driven Design, handle complex business logic, build highly testable projects, and enforce design policies through the architecture. He also describes both Clean Architecture and related approaches as domain‑centric, with the Domain at the core and use cases as a shared concept.

He takes a pragmatic stance, allowing himself to "break" Clean Architecture when it simplifies things, accepting that this may not be pure Clean Architecture but still provides most of its benefits. He advises ignoring critics who dismiss Clean Architecture, emphasizing that people operate in their own bubbles, and encourages engineers who see value in it and know how to write good software. He briefly mentions his .NET Weekly newsletter as a resource on .NET and software architecture.
