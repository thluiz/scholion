The article says the question of whether remote workers are more productive is too narrow. Productivity is hard to measure, studies use different methods, and hybrid work complicates any simple office-versus-home comparison. It argues that the return-to-office debate should focus more on flexibility, happiness, accessibility, diversity, retention, and management quality.

It also says employees value flexibility highly, while CEOs often expect a return to office and favor people who come in. Remote work is presented as part of Stack Overflow’s culture, and the article suggests that organizations should improve remote and hybrid work instead of forcing people back to the office.

## Reading notes

- Productivity is not a straightforward metric, and studies on remote work use different definitions and methods.
- Hybrid schedules make the office-versus-home framing too simple for most workplaces.
- Employees, including developers, place high value on flexibility, and many would quit rather than lose remote work.
- CEOs often expect a return to office and tend to reward in-office employees more than remote ones.
- Companies and employees do not agree on the benefits of onsite work, especially when it comes to organizational culture.
- Remote work is linked in the article to happiness, and happiness is presented as connected to performance.
- RTO mandates can hurt diversity, women, and workers with disabilities, including neurodivergent people.
- The article argues that manager training is a major obstacle to effective hybrid work.
- Stack Overflow presents its remote-first approach as part of its culture and employer appeal.
