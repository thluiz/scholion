The article says programming beauty is hard to pin down and asks whether code itself can be called beautiful. It contrasts hate driven development, which can drive productivity, with the idea that developers want work that lets them solve problems intelligently with nice code.

## Reading notes

- Beauty in software is described through integrity, usability, and innovation.
- Integrity means solving one problem well, staying consistent, and avoiding surprise in the interface.
- Usability means guiding beginners, preventing misuse, and still leaving room for advanced users.
- Innovation means finding new ways to solve problems and revealing new opportunities and risks.
- Johanna Rothman’s view reduces beauty to brevity and conciseness, including short stories, short iterations, and short feedback cycles.
- The article links that view to the KISS principle and its many formulations.
- Jeff Atwood is cited for the idea that beauty belongs to the architecture, ideas, algorithms, and strategies that code represents, not the code itself.
- The piece ends by asking what the most beautiful software readers have written and what rules led to it.
