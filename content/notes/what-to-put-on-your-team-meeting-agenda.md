The article says team meetings are most useful when they help the team align around vision. It explains that meetings should connect day-to-day work to company direction, not function mainly as progress updates.

## Reading notes

- Team meetings are framed as dedicated time for alignment around direction and excitement about that direction.
- An effective meeting can cover vision, mission, values, individual contributions, business context, team composition, and high-level updates.
- The main focus should be vision: where the team is headed and what outcomes it is trying to create.
- The article says vision is the most important information to share in a team meeting and the top thing a manager should communicate.
- Suggested agenda topics tied to vision include the why behind actions, current challenges, top projects, retrospectives, and mission, vision, and values with examples.
- The article warns against using all-team meetings only as status updates, since many employees already know what coworkers are working on.
- Example agendas include an icebreaker, big picture, recent wins, challenges, upcoming priorities, and next steps.
- Another example adds positive chat, business update, client update, problem solving or learning, action, and optional hang time.
- The article recommends changing the format when it feels stale and adding more people or guest speakers when useful.
