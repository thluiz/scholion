Leo Babauta describes how even successful self-employed people often avoid important tasks like writing, turning instead to email, messages, news, and other small distractions. The core issue, he argues, is not difficulty but lack of motivation, especially when no external pressure forces immediate action and the online world offers endless alternatives.

He proposes four practices to rebuild motivation for important but non-urgent work: reconnecting with why the task matters, remembering the aspects you actually enjoy, talking with someone to bring the issue into the open, and asking honestly what is really stopping you. Often, time and ability are not the true obstacles, and very little actually blocks meaningful work.

## Reading notes

- The author’s friend, who runs his own business, procrastinates on crucial work tasks such as writing, despite doing well overall.
- The author acknowledges that he shares the same pattern and suggests that this tendency is common.
- People often know there is an important task for their career, personal life, or business but choose minor activities like email and online reading instead.
- Babauta claims the key problem is not that important tasks are very hard but that people do not feel motivated to do them.
- Lack of a boss or external pressure makes it tougher to act, especially with many interesting online distractions and small alternative tasks.
- First suggestion: reconnect with why the task matters and what you love about it, such as teaching others and seeing how they benefit.
- He notes that this deeper meaning is easy to lose sight of when caught up in daily busyness.
- Second suggestion: remind yourself of the enjoyment within the important task, even if parts require heavy concentration or long focus.
- He describes the "beautiful side" of tasks like writing, such as providing a break from chaos, reconnecting with what matters, or serving as self-expression.
- Third suggestion: talk to someone about the procrastination, as explaining the issue and answering questions can help motivation resurface.
- He suggests that exposing the problem to another person weakens the "childish fears and impulses" that usually govern procrastination.
- Fourth suggestion: when you notice procrastination, pause and ask what is truly stopping you from doing the task.
- Common stated obstacles like lack of time may not be accurate, since in reality there is often enough time available.
- Sometimes the blocker is not knowing how to do the task, which can be addressed by asking others or researching how to proceed.
- In other cases, the task simply feels hard or uncomfortable, where the earlier three suggestions can help.
- Babauta concludes that usually nothing substantial prevents you from doing important, joyful, meaningful work and encourages immediate action.
