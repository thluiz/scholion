The article says software candidates at competitive companies face rigorous interviews and should be ready for three kinds of questions: computer science and algorithms, project and puzzle, and behavioral and informational. It also says candidates should speak clearly, explain their reasoning, and show confidence and preparation throughout the process.

## Reading notes

- The article says Google’s hiring process does not automatically exclude applicants without a college degree.
- It says technical candidates may go through trial projects, cognitive tests, and in-person interviews.
- The first category checks knowledge of fundamentals required for the role.
- Interviewers may ask either direct questions or unfamiliar questions with similar solutions to standard technical problems.
- The project and puzzle category asks candidates to talk through their reasoning aloud.
- Candidates should discuss the pros and cons of their algorithm and address space and time complexity in Big-O terms.
- Example technical topics include arrays, strings, linked lists, stacks, queues, trees, and graphs.
- Example problems include merging sorted linked lists, printing prime numbers up to N, and designing a dialer for a feature phone.
- The behavioral and informational category focuses on personality and team contribution.
- For these questions, the article recommends answering first and then explaining, while staying specific and on point.
- It gives examples such as describing yourself, your perfect job, a challenge, what you are proud of, how you resolve team issues, and your problem-solving steps.
- The article says applicants should make an early impression through their resume and interview presence, including clear speech, confidence, posture, smiling, handshakes, and eye contact.
- It recommends practice interviews and cites *Cracking the Coding Interview* and *Programming Interviews Exposed* as resources.
- It also says active participation on Twitter and GitHub can show continued learning and help candidates stand out.
