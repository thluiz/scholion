The post says trust in teams matters because many people do not trust their employer or manager. It argues that understanding trust clearly helps teams focus on the right things and build trust more effectively.

## Reading notes

- Trust is often confused with likability, but being nice or having good intentions is not enough.
- To trust someone, people need to believe both in that person’s intentions and in what that person can do.
- Rousseau’s definition presents trust as accepting vulnerability based on positive expectations about another person’s intentions or behavior.
- Covey’s framework breaks trust into character and capabilities, then into integrity, intent, capabilities, and results.
- Cuddy, Kohut, and Neffinger describe trust in leaders through warmth and competence, with warmth coming first.
- The post connects warmth to positive body language, affirming words, generous actions, and a smile.
- It connects competence to body language, past track record, and future actions.
- Clearer definitions of trust help explain why someone may distrust a leader and how trust can be built in a team.
