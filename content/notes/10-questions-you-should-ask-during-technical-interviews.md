The post argues that technical interviews should qualify candidates without being tied too closely to a specific technology or framework. It favors questions that reveal problem-solving, communication, learning habits, resilience, and fit for the team.

## Reading notes

- Ask about a challenging problem from the last couple of months to see how the candidate solves problems and works with others.
- Ask what resources they use to learn or sharpen their skills to gauge engagement with the wider IT community.
- Ask how they explain X in simple terms to check whether they can simplify technical ideas for stakeholders.
- Ask what qualities they think are required for X to learn how they see the role and what they value in it.
- Ask what words colleagues would use to describe them to learn about traits that may not appear in a resume or social profile.
- Ask about a difficult situation that ended in failure to see how they handled it and what they learned from it.
- Ask about their most and least favourite framework or product to assess enthusiasm and what they notice in tools.
- Ask about the pros and cons of working in an agile environment to understand their view of collaboration, communication, and fast feedback.
- Ask about their last presentation to see whether they can prepare research, build an argument, and persuade stakeholders.
- Ask how they manage work-life balance to understand how they avoid burnout and handle pressure.
- Prefer questions that reveal intelligence, creativity, and learning ability rather than questions whose answers can be memorized.
- Avoid bias based on gender, background, appearance, or other factors when interviewing candidates.
