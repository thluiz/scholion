Buddhism is presented as a religion with about 300 million followers, but also as a philosophy or way of life. The page says its core teaching is the Dhamma, and that the path leads through moral conduct, mindfulness, wisdom, compassion, and personal testing of the teaching.

## Reading notes

- Buddhism is described as a religion that began about 2,500 years ago with Siddhartha Gotama, the Buddha, whose name is linked to awakening.
- The Buddhist path is summed up as living morally, being mindful of thoughts and actions, and developing wisdom and understanding.
- The page says Buddhism can explain life’s purpose, apparent injustice and inequality, and offers a way of practice aimed at true happiness.
- The Buddha is presented as a human teacher, not a god, who taught from his own experience.
- Buddhists are said to show respect to Buddha images as a reminder to cultivate peace and love, not as worship or a request for favors.
- The text says Buddhism is tolerant of other religions and does not seek to convert people.
- It claims Buddhism has a scientific character because its teachings can be tested, and the Buddha asked followers to verify them for themselves.
- The Four Noble Truths are given as the core teaching: life includes suffering, suffering comes from craving and aversion, suffering can end, and the Noble Eightfold Path leads to that end.
- The Noble Eightfold Path is summarized as moral speech, action, and livelihood, training attention on thoughts and actions, and developing wisdom through understanding and compassion.
- The five precepts are listed as not killing, not taking what is not given, avoiding sexual misconduct and sensual overindulgence, avoiding false speech, and avoiding intoxication.
- Karma is defined as cause and effect, with actions producing results and making individuals responsible for past and present actions.
- Wisdom is described as working with compassion, seeing that phenomena are impermanent and incomplete, and understanding truth through direct experience.
- Compassion is defined in terms of sharing, comfort, sympathy, concern, and caring.
- Becoming a Buddhist is framed as testing the teachings personally and taking responsibility for one’s own actions and understanding.
