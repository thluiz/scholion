Babauta contrasts quick weight-loss plans with slower habit change. He says rapid loss often depends on drastic diets and water weight, which do not last when old habits return.

## Reading notes

- Fast weight loss is usually unsustainable because drastic diets produce mostly water-weight changes and encourage a return to old habits.
- The main task is not short-term dieting but changing eating, exercise, and stress-coping habits over time.
- Babauta describes his own progress as gradual: quitting smoking, starting to run, eating more vegetables, becoming vegetarian, then vegan, and adding other activities little by little.
- He says the process included setbacks, travel overeating, and periods of regained fat, followed by resetting with small challenges.
- He recommends beginning with very easy habits, then progressing slowly when they become stable.
- He suggests watching why you eat, looking for healthier replacement habits, and training taste toward foods like vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and dark chocolate.
- He advises making activity social, joining races or challenges, learning to handle social situations and restaurants, and forgiving mistakes while continuing.
