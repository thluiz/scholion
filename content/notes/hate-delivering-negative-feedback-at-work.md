The post argues that difficult feedback is a normal but uncomfortable part of leadership, and that it becomes easier when managers use a few deliberate phrases instead of avoiding the conversation. It frames feedback as something meant to help the other person succeed and to keep the team moving in a better direction.

## Reading notes

- Ask permission before giving feedback, so the other person is not caught off guard and can choose the right time to talk.
- Make your intention clear and explain that the feedback comes from care, belief in the person, or concern for the company’s direction.
- Describe what you observed rather than attacking character, so the feedback stays tied to specific behavior.
- Acknowledge your own fallibility by asking whether you are missing something or misreading the situation.
- Keep the exchange curious by asking what the other person thinks and what both people should do next.
- Treat honest, difficult feedback as part of the job of leadership, even when it is uncomfortable.
