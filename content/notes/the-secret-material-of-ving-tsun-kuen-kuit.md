The post presents Ving Tsun Kuen Kuit as a collection of 51 stone carvings made by Moy Yat with help from Kwong Tse-Nam. It says the work is kept in New York by Moy Yat’s family, remains in good condition, and is described as the only complete stone-carving work that carries the history of Ving Tsun.

## Reading notes

- The post frames the topic as an introduction to Chinese seal stone carving and to the work Ving Tsun Kuen Kuit.
- It identifies Moy Yat as the artist, the creator of the Mey Tung Artists Association, a kung fu master, and an author.
- It says Kwong Tse-Nam assisted in the work as an artist and “Kung Fu cousin.”
- It states that four years were spent finding suitable stones and two more years on the carvings.
- It says the whole job was done by hand and that precision mattered because one mistake could ruin a stone.
- It says there are many stone-cutting styles, each with a different effect and look.
- It says 9 of the 51 pieces are dedicated to Ving Tsun ancestors.
- It says some stones contain sequence names and key points, while others express fighting principles.
- It gives one example of a principle carved in stone: “You walk the bow, I walk the string,” explained as the shortest distance between two points being a straight line.
- It says the work was approved by Ip Man.
- It says the collection is unique, appeals to martial artists and artists in general, and is not planned to be remade because of lack of time and stones.
- It says the value of the set is left unstated because establishing a price would reduce security for displaying it.
