The article says effective presentations come from getting the story right before making slides. It recommends framing the talk around a specific problem, choosing a target audience, reducing visual clutter, and rehearsing delivery so the message stays clear and engaging.

## Reading notes

- Many engineers ignore presentation skills and then struggle to explain their work clearly, which can limit leadership opportunities.
- Start by writing the content as if explaining it aloud, then turn those raw notes into an outline.
- Frame the talk around a specific problem, why it matters now, what the audience should take away, and why they should care.
- Adapt the presentation to the audience, since leadership, engineers, and large groups need different levels of detail.
- Avoid crowded slides, keep a consistent look, and use simple themes that keep attention on the content.
- Give one slide one message, or use animation to present one message at a time.
- Keep delivery engaging with varied tone, simpler sentences, positive body language, humor when appropriate, and slower pacing for complex ideas.
- Rehearse, record practice runs for important talks, cut content instead of rushing, and use speaker-note keywords to stay on track.
- Slides should support the story, not replace it.
