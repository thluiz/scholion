The post frames career growth as something that needs intention rather than passive drift. It argues that progress depends on planning ahead, making your work visible, shaping how you describe your own experience, building a real network, and checking whether you are staying too comfortable or in the wrong place.

## Reading notes

- A career can be ignored, but if someone wants one, they need short, mid, and long term plans and should write them down and revisit them.
- Doing good work is not enough on its own; people often need to hear about achievements, failures, and lessons learned.
- Underselling your role can make others miss your impact, so it helps to prepare a stronger narrative for job searches and interviews.
- A professional network can provide knowledge, guidance, opportunities, and mutual help, especially when built with deliberate effort.
- When things go wrong, the post advises looking at what could have been done differently instead of only blaming others.
- It also warns against blaming yourself for problems caused by toxic or biased environments that you may not be able to fix.
- Becoming too comfortable can leave someone stuck, so it helps to check every year or two whether a new challenge is needed.
