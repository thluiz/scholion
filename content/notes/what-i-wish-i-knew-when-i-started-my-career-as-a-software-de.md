The page collects career advice for software developers starting out. It argues for learning on the job, managing your own progress, understanding workplace politics, and balancing ambition with self-protection.

## Reading notes

- Learn beyond assigned tasks, read papers and books, and build fundamentals one day at a time.
- Take responsibility for your own education and advancement; if promotion does not appear after a few years, changing jobs is often the better move.
- Avoid both under-performance and over-performance, since the second can create extra work and unwanted attention.
- Do not ask for permission unless the risk is real; if the task fits your level and carries limited loss, just do it.
- Do not apologize for using your own time or acting autonomously, because that frames you as needing more supervision.
- Learn the politics of software development, since technical decisions, staffing, and timing are shaped by it.
- Do not try to prove bosses wrong by sacrificing weekends for a project they do not truly support.
- Do not fight other people’s battles, because young employees usually lack power and will be seen as crusaders rather than allies.
- Stop sorting every workplace situation into “good” or “bad”; use the conditions you get instead of reacting emotionally.
- Avoid stepping back on salary unless becoming a founder or making a justified move to a lower-cost or different field.
- Exercise, since it affects health, confidence, sex life, poise, and productivity.
- Long hours can make sense for work that compounds, but they are usually harmful when they just extend grunt work.
- Separate core technical trends from buzzwords, since many fads fade while fundamentals like linear algebra and functional programming remain useful.
- Keep studying as an adult, because great technical growth still takes sustained effort after school.
