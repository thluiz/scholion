The page describes Elixir as a young, open source, functional, general-purpose language that runs on the Erlang Virtual Machine and was released in 2012 by Jose Valim. It says Elixir is used for embedded systems, web development, news services, social media platforms, and scalable applications, and that its design aims at low latency and high concurrency.

## Reading notes

- Elixir is built on the Erlang Virtual Machine and shares Erlang’s abstractions for fault-tolerant and distributed applications.
- The article links Elixir to Erlang, Ruby, and Clojure as part of its design background.
- The main advantages listed are low latency, explicit and readable code, the Erlang VM, Phoenix, concurrency, fault tolerance, speed, and versatility.
- The concurrency model uses message passing between processes.
- The article says Elixir can handle several thousand users at the same time and fits real-time, distributed, scalable, and high-traffic projects.
- It says built-in safety mechanisms and the Erlang VM help isolate faulty modules and keep systems running after failures.
- The drawbacks listed are the learning curve when moving from object-oriented languages, the language’s youth, the need to know Erlang, a limited community, weaker fit for raw CPU-heavy work, and lower demand for developers.
- In the conclusion, the page says Elixir adoption has grown steadily and mentions Stack Overflow, Pinterest, Facebook, and WhatsApp as examples of interest in the language.
