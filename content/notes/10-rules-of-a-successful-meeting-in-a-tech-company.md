The post argues that meetings work better when people know why they are there, who is running the discussion, and what outcome is expected. It also says meetings should begin with something already drafted, stay focused, and end with notes and action points.

## Reading notes

- Send an agenda to everyone days in advance, ideally with the desired outcome.
- Make clear who owns the meeting so the discussion does not drift.
- Start from a draft or prototype instead of asking a group to begin from a blank page.
- Keep people focused, with laptops closed and phones away unless they need to step out.
- Make it clear whether the meeting is for a decision or for discussion.
- Have someone write down action points and notes, and tell them beforehand.
- Prepare audio and video equipment before the meeting starts.
- Do not organize meetings that could have been handled by email.
- Respect other people’s time and do not stretch a short meeting unnecessarily.
