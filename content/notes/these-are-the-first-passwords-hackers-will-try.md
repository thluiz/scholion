F-Secure says hackers usually begin with weak or default passwords when they find a device that looks vulnerable. Its honeypot data shows more traffic tied to scans for devices, with attacks focused on SMB, Telnet, and SSH, which points to ongoing interest in IoT targets.

## Reading notes

- F-Secure uses honeypot servers in multiple countries to track cyberattack patterns.
- The company saw a significant increase in traffic to those honeypots in the second half of last year.
- The rise in attack traffic reflects more threats to IoT devices.
- The most targeted port was SMB port 445, linked to SMB worms and exploits such as Eternal Blue.
- Telnet was also commonly targeted, likely as part of IoT attacks.
- Probes of SSH on port 22 were also common and are associated with administrative access.
- The top password hackers try was “admin.”
- Other common passwords included “12345,” “default,” “password,” and “root.”
- The list also included factory defaults for digital video recorders and embedded devices such as routers.
- F-Secure says brute forcing factory default usernames and passwords remains effective for recruiting IoT devices into botnets used in DDoS attacks.
- The UK recommends that consumer internet-connected device passwords be unique and not resettable to a universal factory setting.
