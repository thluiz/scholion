Eyetracking research revisits the F-shaped reading pattern and says it still appears on desktop and mobile. The article also clarifies that the pattern is only one way people scan pages, and that it often reflects weak formatting and a user trying to move quickly.

## Reading notes

- People often scan webpages and phone screens in an F shape, with fixations concentrated at the top and left side of the content area.
- The pattern usually starts with a horizontal pass across the top, then a shorter second horizontal pass, then a vertical scan down the left side.
- The first lines of text and the first words on each line get more attention than the rest.
- The pattern applies to the content area, not to navigation bars used to decide where to go next.
- Recent eyetracking research finds the pattern on desktop and mobile, and in right-to-left languages it appears in mirrored form.
- Other scanning patterns also occur, including layer-cake, spotted, marking, bypassing, and commitment.
- The F shape appears when text has little web formatting, the user wants to be efficient, and the user is not committed to reading every word.
- People scan this way because they try to minimize effort and quickly get useful information.
- F-shaped scanning can make users miss important content, especially text that appears farther to the right.
- Responsive and liquid layouts can change which words get fixated because text flows differently across viewport sizes.
- Good formatting can reduce F-scanning by putting important points early, using clear headings, bolding key terms, writing informative links, grouping related content, using bullets, and cutting unnecessary text.
- The article says users’ motivation and goals are hard to control, so designers should optimize content and presentation instead.
