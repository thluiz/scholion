The article says managers should not rely only on answers during uncertain times. Asking good questions can reveal how remote employees are coping, what support they need, and whether the manager is helping or creating obstacles.

## Reading notes

- Ask remote employees about their energy level to get a general sense of how they are doing and whether they feel drained.
- Ask what fears or worries they have about the team or company, so those concerns can be named and addressed.
- Ask whether they feel equipped to do their job well, because managers may be blocking performance without realizing it.
- Ask what would help them do their best work while remote, and use follow-up questions to learn how to adjust management, communication, and timing.
- The article recommends using these questions in one-on-ones and says asking at least one question matters more than focusing on reassurance through answers alone.
