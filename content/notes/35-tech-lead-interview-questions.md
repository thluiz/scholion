Indeed's career-advice page compiles 35 tech lead interview questions split into three categories: general fit, experience and background, and in-depth technical and leadership scenarios. It then walks through sample answers for five of them.

The sample answers read as generic templates meant to be adapted by any candidate, but two of them name a concrete technique rather than staying abstract: the SMART framework for goal-setting, and a two-step research process for evaluating a team member's tool suggestion.

## Reading notes

- 35 questions split into three groups: 12 on general fit (motivation, leadership readiness, work style), 12 on experience and background (languages, project management, code review process), and 11 on in-depth scenarios (team performance, onboarding, handling disagreements).
- The sample answer for "why do you want a leadership position" leans on confidence built from mentoring less experienced developers and a track record of meeting deadlines.
- Explaining technical work to non-technical people comes up twice: as a skill ("can you explain complicated tasks to coworkers") and as a habit (monthly company-wide presentations translating the team's work into plain terms).
- The goal-setting sample answer names the SMART framework (specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, time-based) as the method for turning a one-on-one conversation into concrete team goals.
- The new-tool sample answer describes a two-step process: talk to the team member proposing the change, then independently research cost-effectiveness before deciding whether it fits the budget.
