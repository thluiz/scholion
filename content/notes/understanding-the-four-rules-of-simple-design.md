The book uses examples from coderetreat workshops and Conway’s Game of Life to discuss how software can stay flexible as requirements change. Its focus is on the thinking behind refactoring and on the small design choices that help codebases remain adaptable over time.

## Reading notes

- It argues that modern software development faces constant change, so systems need to adapt to expected and unexpected requirements.
- It is based on the author’s experience watching thousands of pairs work on the same problem in coderetreat sessions.
- It presents the feedback the author gives during a typical coderetreat, with emphasis on the thought process behind refactoring.
- It treats Conway’s Game of Life as a backdrop for discussing the four rules of simple design, not as the book’s main subject.
- It aims to help build robust codebases through small decisions made while designing code.
- It includes forewords by Joe Rainsberger and Kent Beck.
- The table of contents lists examples about test names influencing an object’s API, duplication of knowledge, behavior attractors, testing state versus behavior, and pair-programming styles.
