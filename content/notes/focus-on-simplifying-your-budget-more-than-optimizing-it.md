The piece argues that making the optimal choice is not always worth the time it takes. It suggests simplifying your budget instead, so you can spend your attention on the decisions that matter most.

## Reading notes

- Making better choices and making the optimal choice are not always the same thing.
- Ten minutes to tell a bad deal from a good one is time well spent, but ten hours to tell a great deal from a slightly better one is wasted effort.
- Focus less on every small optimization and more on the choices that deserve your mental energy.
- A home loan can be worth several hours of analysis, while clipping coupons to save fifty cents on laundry detergent is not.
- Keeping the basics simple helps preserve mental energy for the places where it matters most.
