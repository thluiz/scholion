The piece argues that the rise of project-based software work and high demand for developers has made representation more useful for some people. It says agents can handle searching, negotiation, and self-promotion, while recruiters can help with salary, benefits, and access to jobs that are not publicly posted.

## Reading notes

- Hiring an agent may help when time is tight, negotiation is hard, or job searching is uncomfortable.
- The agency model is presented as a fit for freelancers who move from one contract to another.
- Some developers prefer representation so they can focus on current work, side projects, or lifestyle choices.
- An agent should still improve the bottom line after fees, not just add another layer.
- A talent agency can raise a developer’s rate and remove tasks the client dislikes.
- Designers and talent managers are described as helping build a worker’s employment brand.
- Recruiters differ from agencies because the hiring company usually pays their fee.
- Recruiters may be useful for salary, vacation, work-from-home days, and other terms beyond base pay.
- Some companies rely on agencies instead of public job boards to fill tech roles.
- Representation can also help when moving to a new city and needing local contacts.
