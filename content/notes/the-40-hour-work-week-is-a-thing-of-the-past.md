The article says the phrase “nine to five” is becoming outdated. Based on an EY survey of 9,699 full-time employees in eight countries, it argues that longer hours, flat pay, and connected devices are making work and life harder to separate.

## Reading notes

- About half of all managers work more than 40 hours a week, and 39% say their hours have increased in the past five years.
- One-third of workers say it is getting harder to balance work and life.
- Employees say responsibilities at work have increased while wages have largely stayed flat.
- Company-provided smartphones and remote-work software give flexibility, but also keep people tied to work seven days a week.
- In the U.S., 58% of managers report working more than 40 hours a week.
- The reported shift in working hours hits parents particularly hard.
- Managers with children are more likely to say their hours have increased in the last five years than managers without children.
- Working women and parents rate managing work and personal life as slightly more difficult than men and those without children.
- Participants blame flat salaries, rising expenses, and increased workload for the difficulty of balancing career and family.
- Some companies offer flexible scheduling, but some U.S. workers say it can bring negative consequences such as being passed over for a promotion or losing a job.
