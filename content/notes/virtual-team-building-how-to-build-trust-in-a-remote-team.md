The page argues that virtual team building should be about building trust in a remote team rather than just creating a pleasant mood. It separates trust into affective trust, based on emotional closeness, and cognitive trust, based on reliability and competence, and says both matter for remote teams.

## Reading notes

- Virtual team building should aim at trust, not only at team members feeling good.
- A team that only avoids conflict and flatters one another can weaken honesty, accountability, culture, and performance.
- Affective trust comes from emotional bond and interpersonal relatedness.
- Cognitive trust comes from reliability and competence.
- To build affective trust, the page recommends stronger onboarding, especially for new hires.
- The onboarding process should include context on the team and business, a clear first project, encouragement in the role, and regular one-on-ones.
- The page lists onboarding questions for new hires about excitement, frustration, clarity, support, and future accomplishment.
- Cliché icebreakers like asking about the weekend are described as tired and unhelpful.
- Better icebreakers are questions that reveal something new or unexpected about the person.
- A buddy system can support affective trust through mentors or random pairings for non-work video chats.
- Dedicated chat channels for casual conversation can replace missing watercooler talk.
- Non-work video chats can help people see expressions and hear laughter, but they should be optional because of video meeting fatigue.
- To build cognitive trust, leaders should show vulnerability, make intentions clear, and follow through on commitments.
- Vulnerability is presented as a way to show empathy and admit shortcomings.
- Clear intentions matter when giving feedback or explaining decisions.
- Following through on commitments is linked to trust and to avoiding an all-talk-no-action culture.
- The page recommends choosing one tactic for affective trust and one for cognitive trust rather than trying everything at once.
