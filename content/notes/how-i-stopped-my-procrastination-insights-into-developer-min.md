The article says procrastination among programmers is tied to how the brain handles demanding work. It points to frontal-cortex overload, decision fatigue, and the way practice shifts some work into more automatic processes. It then argues for focusing on process changes instead of waiting for motivation.

## Reading notes

- Programmers tend to procrastinate because coding tasks require a lot of mental energy and overload the frontal cortex.
- Repeated practice reduces cognitive effort by moving part of the work into more automatic processes.
- Making decisions during development also consumes energy and affects productivity.
- Starting work becomes easier when there is a fixed ritual that avoids the uncertainty of the beginning.
- Dividing the work into very small parts reduces decision-making after the code starts being written.
- Planning before coding helps concentrate decision fatigue in the initial stages.
- Tracking tasks in a personal list, with simple rewards, gives a sense of progress that the Jira board does not offer.
- When a problem drags on for too long, the text recommends measuring the effort, persisting for a defined period and asking for help if necessary.
- The conclusion states that improving habitual actions is more effective than trying to fix only the isolated problem.
