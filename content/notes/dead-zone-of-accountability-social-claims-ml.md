The paper examines how Machine Learning research often includes "social claims" about potential social benefits or technical affordances, which help attract resources and influence. It argues that ML methods frequently fall short of these claims, creating a claim-reality gap, and makes a normative case for mechanisms that hold such claims to account. The authors introduce the notion of a "dead zone of accountability" as a way to locate where accountability is missing, diagnose cognitive and structural resistances around the claim-reality gap, and outline collaborative research agendas to enable social claim accountability.

## Reading notes

- Many ML research works use language that projects possible social benefits or technical capabilities of new methods and technologies, which the authors label "social claims".
- These social claims can help researchers and technology producers secure significant resources and influence.
- There is a recurring gap between social claims and actual outcomes, as ML methods often do not achieve the promised functionality or social impact, which the authors term the claim-reality gap.
- The paper argues normatively that specific accountability mechanisms are needed to address this claim-reality gap.
- First contribution: the authors explain why the lack of accountability for social claims is itself a significant problem.
- Second contribution: they introduce the concept of a "dead zone of accountability" as an analytic lens for scholars and practitioners to identify where new accountability forms could be developed.
- They use this dead zone lens to analyze the claim-reality gap and to identify cognitive and structural factors that resist accountability in this space.
- Third contribution: they propose a prescription in the form of two potential collaborative research agendas aimed at creating conditions that would support accountability for social claims in ML research.
