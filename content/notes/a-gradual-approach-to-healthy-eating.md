The page argues that diets often fail because they demand too many changes at once, ask for abrupt shifts, and feel like a sacrifice. A gradual approach works better because it starts small, feels less overwhelming, and makes healthy eating feel normal over time.

It also says to imagine a broadly healthy diet that still includes foods you enjoy, then add one small change each week and keep it for a week before adding another. Examples include eating more vegetables or fruit, cutting back on sugar or alcohol, switching to whole grains, and eating at home more often.

## Reading notes

- Diets often fail because they require many changes at once, make abrupt shifts, and feel like giving up foods you like.
- Gradual change works better because it starts small, avoids overload, and becomes normal without feeling like a sacrifice.
- The goal is a healthy way of eating that still leaves room for some foods and drinks you enjoy.
- Start with one small change each week and repeat it after a successful week.
- The page gives examples such as adding vegetables or fruit, reducing sugar or alcohol, choosing whole grains, and eating at home more often.
- Small changes build into larger change over a few months and are more likely to last.
