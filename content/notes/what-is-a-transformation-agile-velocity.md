The page says that organizations fail when they use one word, “transformation,” to mean several different things. Leadership may mean technology modernization, workflow change, business model change, or agile ways of working, but each type has different objectives, traps, and measures of success.

It argues that clarity has to come before change. Agile Velocity starts by defining what transformation means for the organization, then assesses capability gaps with Path to Agility®, and only then selects practices tied to the business outcomes that were defined first.

## Reading notes

- “Transformation” can mean digital and technology change, operational and process change, organizational and people change, strategic and business model change, agile ways of working, or AI-native transformation.
- Different functions often define the same word differently, so the CTO, COO, and CEO may all be pursuing different goals under the label of transformation.
- Misalignment leads to conflicting success metrics, with technology teams, operations, and leadership each measuring different results.
- Organizations also pick only one type of change and leave the others untouched, which breaks the connection between the pieces.
- The page says the first step is to define what transformation means for the organization before changing practices.
- Agile Velocity says it aligns leadership on the type of transformation being pursued, surfaces disagreements, and gets everyone pointed at the same outcomes.
- After that, it assesses the organization against 100 capabilities with Path to Agility® to find capability gaps.
- It then chooses practices that close those gaps and connect directly to the defined business outcomes.
- The page presents four offerings: Transformation, Agile Coaching, Training, and Organizational Change Management.
- It also says Path to Agility Navigator supports the approach by assessing 100 capabilities and tracking progress in real time.
