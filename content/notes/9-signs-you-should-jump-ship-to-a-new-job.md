The article argues that, with demand for tech workers rising, it is a good time to judge whether a current employer still fits long-term career goals. It presents nine signs that staying may mean stagnation: new ideas are blocked, respect and recognition are lacking, career paths are limited, leadership chances are missing, training is left to the employee, the IT vision is unclear, teamwork is weak, new trends are treated with skepticism, and there is little room to experiment with new technology.

## Reading notes

- Rising demand for tech workers makes this a moment to assess whether a current employer still fits career goals.
- If new ideas are consistently blocked and failure is treated as unacceptable, the workplace may be hostile to innovation.
- A lack of respect, recognition, and reward for good work is a warning sign.
- When the only visible path is management, the organization may not value different IT career interests.
- If IT staff do not get chances to lead projects from concept through completion, leadership opportunities are limited.
- Training that must be paid for and done on personal time suggests weak investment in employee growth.
- Vague or contradictory goals for IT point to a cloudy view of the department’s role.
- Poor teamwork shows up in weak communication, unanswered messages, and people working in isolation.
- Companies that reject every new trend and keep staff focused only on operations may be limiting career growth.
- A lack of access to new tools and experimentation can lead to tedium and stagnation.
