The piece says that work itself is not the source of stress. Stress, frustration, anger, and overwhelm come from holding on to how things should be, including expectations of other people and the wish to do everything at once. Letting go is presented as the way these problems fade.

## Reading notes

- Stress at work comes from our reaction to work, not from the work itself.
- Interruptions are described as events around us, while frustration comes from holding on to the task we were doing before.
- Coworkers and bosses are not the problem; anger comes from expecting them to behave in a certain way.
- Overwhelm comes from holding on to the idea that we can do everything at once, even though we can only do one thing.
- Letting go is the solution, and the text says that when we let go of these expectations, the problems go away.
- The text says we also hold on to past events, which keeps pain replaying in our minds.
- Mindfulness is presented as the key practice because it helps us see these thought processes and return to the present moment.
- The suggested starting practice is five minutes of meditation, then expanding mindfulness into other tasks.
