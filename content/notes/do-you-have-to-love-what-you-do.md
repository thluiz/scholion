Jason Fried argues that the advice to love what you do is overstated. He says love can grow, but it is not required to start a business, build a career, or do great work. What often drives new companies is frustration with existing options and a desire to make something better.

He uses Uber as an example of a company that began from annoyance at not being able to get a cab, and he describes his own work in the same way. He did not love music collecting or software development at the start; he saw a need, made a tool for it, and later found the same pattern with Basecamp. He says he still does not love every part of running a company, but he values the work, the challenge, and the people.
