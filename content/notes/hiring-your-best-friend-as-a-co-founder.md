The writer describes mixed experience hiring best friends as co-founders at Expojure. Friendship made difficult conversations harder, especially around pay and performance, and one friend left after not meeting expectations. At the same time, another friend brought a useful second perspective and shared motives.

## Reading notes

- The writer warns that emotional closeness can make it awkward to discuss salary, equity, and firing.
- He says a co-founder role should not be assumed for any employee.
- He advises checking whether a person is truly willing to take the leap with the startup.
- He notes that some people join for frustration with a current employer or to learn new technology for their résumé.
- He says work should speak louder than words, especially in the early days when time is limited.
- He argues that motives and vision should be discussed openly once a co-founder is identified.
- He says different perspectives can improve creative output.
- He ends by saying a co-founder can reduce the loneliness of entrepreneurship.
