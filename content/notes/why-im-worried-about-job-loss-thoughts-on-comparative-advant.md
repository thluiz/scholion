The essay argues that AI can leave labor “employed” while still making the labor market worse for workers through wage pressure, pipeline collapse, and concentrated returns to capital. It focuses on distribution, bargaining power, and ownership rather than only on whether jobs continue to exist.

## Reading notes

- Comparative advantage can still be compatible with massive displacement, lower wages, and gains flowing to capital rather than workers.
- The current “cyborg era” helps some jobs, but that does not mean most workers have the tacit knowledge needed to stay protected.
- Young workers appear most exposed, with evidence of lower employment, fewer entry-level postings, and weaker new graduate hiring.
- Organizational bottlenecks slow displacement, but the essay argues those bottlenecks are eroding and may be easier for AI-native firms to bypass.
- The author says AI is already displacing work in illustration, translation, copywriting, design, and related fields even with imperfect models.
- Elastic demand may increase total output, but that does not guarantee the same amount of labor demand, especially in bounded markets like legal review, tax prep, and video production.
- AI video tools are used as an example where one person can replace a much larger production pipeline.
- The essay accepts that new jobs may appear, but says the harder questions are transition paths, income levels, and ownership of the surplus.
- The main concern is who captures the gains from more productive AI systems, since ordinary people could keep jobs while losing share of value.
