Stallman describes a computing setup centered on free software and a free operating system. He uses a Lemote machine, mostly works in a text console, edits in Emacs, avoids nonfree software, and treats DRM as an attack on freedom unless he can break the restriction himself.

## Reading notes

- He uses a Lemote machine with a free initialization program and a free operating system, and notes that Windows has never supported it.
- He says he does not have a preferred GNU/Linux distro and recommends ethical distros that are 100% free software.
- He formerly used an OLPC, but stopped because the project made the machine a platform for Windows.
- He mostly uses a text console because it is more efficient for the editing work he does, and only occasionally uses X11 for graphics tasks.
- He spends most of his time editing in Emacs, and also uses Emacs to read and send mail.
- He edits the pages on this site with Emacs, while volunteer helpers install the political notes and urgent notes.
- He says the site is maintained in a simple way, with pages edited manually as HTML and a cron job rolling over the political notes page every two months.
- He says he chose the GNU system design after not using Unix before deciding to develop a free replacement, and chose it because it was portable and seemed fairly clean.
- He explains why he coined the name POSIX.
- He has several free web browsers on his laptop, but usually does not look at web sites from his own machine and instead fetches pages through a program that mails them back to him.
- He says Lisp is the most powerful programming language, recommends learning Lisp for programming, and suggests the Introduction to Programming in Emacs Lisp and Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs.
- He says the programming languages he uses and likes are Lisp and C, and that he has not had time to learn newer languages because he has mainly worked on free software activism since around 1992.
- He says he finds C++ ugly, and that Python is not fundamentally similar to Lisp because Lisp has read, eval, and print.
- He refuses to install non-free software or tolerate it on computers set up for him, but will briefly use machines that already contain it when he is visiting and was not responsible for the setup.
- He says he does not use Skype because using it encourages other people to use nonfree software.
- He sometimes uses Google and DuckDuckGo search, but only from machines that are not his and that other people also use, and he never identifies himself to the site.
- He does not use social networking sites, has a Twitter account only to log in on other sites to post comments, and says any other account claiming to be him is an impostor.
- He rejects Facebook and Google+ on principle because they require real names, and he is suspicious of Google+ requiring an established identity or ID.
- He says the two ethical issues for an email service are freedom from nonfree software and privacy, but offers no recommendation because he cannot verify privacy.
- He suggests using an email service that is not connected with a search engine.
- He says every product with DRM is an attack on freedom, and one should not buy or tolerate DRM unless one can personally break the handcuffs.
