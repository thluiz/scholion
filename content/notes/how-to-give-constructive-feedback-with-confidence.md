The article says feedback is helpful information shared timely, kindly, and clearly to influence someone’s behavior. It argues that employees want better feedback, not just more of it, and that managers should give it regularly instead of delaying it.

## Reading notes

- Feedback is meant to be timely, kind, and clear, with the goal of influencing behavior.
- Many managers avoid feedback because they fear hurting feelings, causing discouragement, or getting the framing wrong.
- Employees do not only want more feedback; they want feedback that helps them grow and succeed.
- In-the-moment feedback can happen in Slack, docs, PR comments, or a quick 1:1 chat.
- In-the-moment feedback should be specific, helpful, and close to the event, not punitive or nitpicky.
- Behavioral feedback deals with personal behavior and can address either a single incident or a repeated pattern.
- Avoiders soften or sidestep the issue, then grow resentful when the behavior continues.
- Impulsives react quickly and often turn feedback into judgments about character or personality.
- Responders manage their emotions, stay specific about behavior, and invite dialogue.
- The five steps are to check readiness, explain why the feedback is being given, describe the observation, explain the impact, and agree on next steps.
