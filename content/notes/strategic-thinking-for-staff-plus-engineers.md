Shweta Saraf presents strategic thinking as a skill that builds on critical and creative thinking, but goes beyond them toward long-term organizational success. She frames it as a muscle that depends on diagnosis, guiding principles, and cohesive action, then shows how it affects judgment, credibility, and reputation.

## Reading notes

- Strategic thinking should help an organization optimize for long-term success by adapting to situations, innovating, and building the skill continuously.
- Good strategy has three parts: diagnosis, guiding policy, and coherent action.
- Poor strategy often happens when teams focus too much on the how, skip the why, or stop at a high-level plan without execution.
- A strategy can also become too tactical when it solves immediate issues without architectural principles or long-term durability.
- The framework she proposes starts with diagnostics and insights about customers, personas, hotspots, and feedback loops.
- She says she did 50 customer interviews before writing strategy for her org, and involved her tech lead in those interviews.
- Industry peers and build-versus-buy analysis can help avoid working in a silo and sharpen strategic bets.
- Strategy should include guiding principles, a long-term vision, and a small number of tenets that can hold for 12 to 18 months.
- Staff+ engineers can improve roadmap quality, risk mitigation, and funding conversations even when they are not the final owners.
- Metrics, KPIs, leading indicators, and lagging indicators are needed to check whether a strategy is working and to know when to pivot.
- Balancing risk and innovation depends on the organization’s culture, including whether it values execution, data-driven decision making, or storytelling and relationships.
- Staff+ engineers can use rapid prototyping, pre-mortems, peer feedback, and engineering excellence to manage risk.
- The talk separates known knowns, known unknowns, biases with low awareness, and unknown unknowns, and connects the last case to chaos engineering and innovation investment.
- Netflix is presented as an example of long-term strategic betting, from DVDs to subscriptions, original content, global expansion, live streaming, cloud gaming, and ads.
- Real-world examples from distinguished engineers, principal engineers, SREs, and depth engineers stress the value of trust, relationships, business context, delegation, role models, writing, and time management.
- Influencing culture is part of a staff+ engineer’s presence, and leaders should give them business context, a seat at the table, time protection, and strategy pressure-testing.
