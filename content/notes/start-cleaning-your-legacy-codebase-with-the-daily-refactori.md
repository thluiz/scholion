The article argues that teams usually cannot stop development to clean up a codebase, so they need a different strategy. It recommends making technical debt visible, then applying steady pressure through small refactoring habits instead of waiting for a big cleanup window.

## Reading notes

- Large codebases make abrupt changes harder, especially when the team still has to fix bugs and ship features.
- The first step is to make the desired direction visible, for example with a wall of technical debt.
- The suggested practice is to plan 1 hour of refactoring each day for the next 2 weeks.
- Each session should end with a small shipped change, often through a pull request.
- If 1 hour is too much, the article suggests 30 minutes a day or twice a week.
- The author says the habit matters more than the exact amount of time spent.
- The article recommends starting with one person, leading by example, and suggesting the practice to the team later.
- The closing advice is to write down improvements, pick one, and work on it for 2 weeks before reflecting on the result.
