Microverse presents a remote education model built around employment outcomes. It trains students for global software roles, delays tuition until hiring, and supports graduates in finding not only their first job but later ones as well.

## Reading notes

- The article links student debt and underemployment to the appeal of a model where students pay tuition only after they are hired.
- Microverse has trained more than 400 students from 100 countries since 2017 for jobs at companies such as Microsoft and startups in North America and Europe.
- Ariel Camus says the goal is to give people access to a life-changing job regardless of where they live.
- The school was designed to address low completion rates in remote education by building an environment that keeps students showing up.
- Students work with deadlines, tests, peer support, pair programming, and Zoom standups.
- Microverse is selective, admitting 20 to 80 students per cohort from about 10,000 applications for each nine-month session.
- After graduation, the team helps students with their first job and later career moves, including remote jobs, relocations, or local opportunities.
- Microverse measures success by the share of students employed after 12 weeks, which the article says is 78%, and 85% before the pandemic.
- The team is distributed across several countries and is organized into Marketing, Admissions, Learning, Student Success, Career Services, and Partnerships.
- Microverse centers its culture on autonomy, transparency, and trust, and uses Twist because Slack made chats hard to track and information hard to organize.
- Twist supports asynchronous communication, searchable conversations, documented decisions, open channels, and careful use of mentions to reduce noise.
- The team also sets aside time for informal check-ins, including Friday BBBB sessions, to leave room for serendipity.
