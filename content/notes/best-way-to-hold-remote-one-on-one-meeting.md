The article argues that remote one-on-one meetings deserve disproportionate attention because, in remote work, they may be the manager’s main face-to-face interaction with a direct report. It says the meeting should focus on connection, attention, and problem-solving, not on routine status reporting.

## Reading notes

- Remote one-on-ones are presented as especially important in remote leadership because they are often the only face-to-face contact between a manager and a direct report.
- More time should be spent on personal connection and rapport, especially through non-work conversation.
- Body language matters in video meetings, so the manager should avoid distractions, keep the camera on, and prepare to appear calm and attentive.
- Status updates should happen outside the one-on-one, so the meeting can be used to understand how the person is doing and how to help.
- The meeting should be used to uncover potential issues such as unclear goals, communication problems, isolation, or difficulty doing the work remotely.
- Canceling one-on-ones sends the message that the meeting is less important than other tasks, so regularity matters.
- The article gives an agenda template with sections for catching up, issues or concerns, feedback, and team or company outlook.
