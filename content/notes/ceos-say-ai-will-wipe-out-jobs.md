Ford CEO Jim Farley told Walter Isaacson at the Aspen Ideas Festival that AI "is going to replace literally half of all white-collar workers in the U.S.," one of the most direct predictions yet from a large non-Silicon-Valley executive. JPMorgan's Marianne Lake told investors she expects the bank's consumer operations head count to fall 10% as AI tools scale; Amazon's Andy Jassy told employees the company's corporate workforce would likely shrink because of what he called a "once-in-a-lifetime" technology; Anthropic's Dario Amodei went further in an Axios interview, putting half of entry-level jobs at risk within one to five years and U.S. unemployment at 10-20%, and urged executives to stop "sugarcoating" the outlook.

Not every executive agrees on the scale. OpenAI COO Brad Lightcap told the "Hard Fork" podcast he sees no evidence yet of wholesale entry-level replacement, though he still expects displacement and labor-market shifts. IBM's Arvind Krishna offered a concrete accounting either way: AI replaced the work of a couple hundred HR employees, but the company hired more programmers and salespeople in the same period, a pattern AT&T's CFO Pascal Desroches says is genuinely unclear at the aggregate level, even as some companies, Shopify among them, are freezing hiring unless a manager can prove AI can't do the job.

## Fichamento

- Ford CEO Jim Farley's claim, AI replacing "literally half of all white-collar workers," came in a public interview with Walter Isaacson, which the article frames as new for an executive outside Silicon Valley.
- JPMorgan's Marianne Lake told investors in May she expects the bank's consumer operations head count to fall 10% as AI tools take hold.
- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's Axios interview projected half of entry-level jobs disappearing within one to five years and U.S. unemployment reaching 10-20%, explicitly urging leaders to stop "sugarcoating" it.
- Shopify's Tobi Lütke told staff the company won't hire unless a manager can prove AI can't do the job. Some companies are also merging roles, folding product manager and engineer into one position.
- IBM's Arvind Krishna gave a two-sided data point: AI replaced roughly 200 HR jobs, but the company hired more programmers and salespeople over the same period.
- OpenAI COO Brad Lightcap pushed back on the scale, telling "Hard Fork" there's no evidence yet of wholesale entry-level replacement, while still granting that displacement and labor-market shifts are coming.
