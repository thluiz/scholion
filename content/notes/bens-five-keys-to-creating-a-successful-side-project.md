Ben argues that the side project that lasted for him was the one he could keep sustaining over time. He ties that to personal interest, firm limits, no rush, a simple first version, and bringing in help only after the project had already gained traction.

## Reading notes

- The project has to be genuinely his, with enough interest that he can keep working on it for a long time.
- He treats constraints as useful, especially when they keep the work manageable and force better pruning of ideas.
- He argues that the time constraint should be removed from the start so the project can live on without a deadline-driven rush.
- The first version should be basic and not try to do too much, because the point is to launch, learn, and let the project grow over time.
- He recommends starting alone first, then finding help later at the right inflection point, as happened when Jess Lee joined.
- He also says not to feel pressured to make the project commercial, and suggests considering a public API and open sourcing earlier.
