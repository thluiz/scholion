Writing externalizes thinking and lets the author work through more than they could hold in mind alone. The post links this to mind maps, Manuel Blum’s comparison between finite automata and Turing machines, and to cases like reviewing papers or drafting blog posts, where writing reveals gaps and creates new connections.

It also argues that LLMs do not replace this function, because they can produce polished text without doing the writer’s own thinking. The rest of the post turns that claim into advice: draft messily, edit ruthlessly, keep prose simple, write without audience pressure, and schedule writing so it becomes a regular habit.

## Reading notes

- Writing is presented as a tool for processing information, not just storing it.
- Blum’s example is used to say that writing gives people more problem-solving power.
- Writing is compared to external memory and out-of-core execution.
- Drafting a review or blog post can expose missing understanding and surface new connections.
- The post says LLMs know the literature but not the writer’s half-formed intuition.
- The five rules are to make a rough draft, revise it heavily, keep prose plain, write for yourself, and put writing on the calendar.
