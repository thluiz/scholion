Sean Kim describes working from a coffee shop in Toronto while collaborating with teammates in New York, the Netherlands, and Paris as a concrete example of the “future of work.” He argues that remote work, already used by companies like Virgin, 37signals, and IBM, increases productivity and happiness, shortens commutes, and gives employees flexible hours. Citing a Stanford study of call center employees, he notes a 13 percent performance increase for those working from home and points out that office presence does not guarantee focused work, especially with constant interruptions. He contends that companies embracing telecommuting can hire and retain better people by accessing a global talent pool and accommodating workers who prefer remote lifestyles.

He emphasizes that remote work is now easier thanks to laptops, the internet, and numerous collaboration tools, though training, instant feedback, and relationship building can be harder at a distance. To make remote work effective, he proposes focusing on output rather than hours, highlighting ROWE systems where performance is measured by results and reporting that companies like Best Buy and Gap have seen higher productivity, lower turnover, and better customer satisfaction. He recommends setting SMART goals to guide daily work, using examples such as shipping a feature or hitting specific sales targets. Because remote work reduces nonverbal cues, he urges frequent, clear communication through tools like HipChat and more concise writing. He also suggests maintaining a shared project “bulletin board” with tools like Pivotal Tracker, Basecamp, Asana, or Trello so everyone can see priorities and assignments. Finally, he stresses having structured, regular feedback via video chats to support continuous improvement, engagement, and loyalty, framing remote work as a way to tap into a global talent market that is already available.
