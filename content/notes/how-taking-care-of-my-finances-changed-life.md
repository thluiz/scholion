Leo Babauta describes a period of his life when he neglected his health and finances, living with debt, unpaid bills and constant stress while avoiding any engagement with money issues. He explains how a series of small, consistent financial self-care actions over time helped him pay off debt, build savings, begin investing and gain a sense of security and happiness. He frames handling bills and savings as an extension of basic self-care and invites readers to treat financial habits similarly.

## Reading notes

- The author recalls being overweight, in serious debt, smoking, eating junk food, stressed and surrounded by clutter, with basic self-care habits ignored.
- He notes that simple behaviors such as walking, doing a few pushups, meditating, decluttering a bit and flossing were easy but continually postponed.
- His finances were one of the most stressful areas, with unpaid bills hidden away, calls from creditors and collection agencies avoided.
- He describes borrowing from family and even his children’s piggy banks to cover food until the next paycheck, which he says he is not proud of.
- He lived with ongoing stress, feeling desperate about making it to the next paycheck and helpless about escaping the financial hole.
- The turning point came when he began taking small steps of financial self-care instead of avoiding his money situation.
- First he listed all his bills and the amounts owed in a simple, organized way.
- He then prioritized the bills by urgency, making a payoff plan and tackling smaller but equally urgent debts first to shorten the list.
- He canceled nonessential subscriptions and payments, keeping only what he considered essential expenses.
- He began saving modestly, starting with about $20 from each paycheck.
- He set up a weekly review to regularly look over and manage his finances.
- As debts were paid down, he increased his savings and started investing.
- He took extra jobs to accelerate debt repayment.
- He learned to automate his finances so that routine decisions required less attention.
- Over time these actions made his debts manageable and eventually eliminated them completely.
- His savings grew gradually, and he shifted to investing in low-fee index funds.
- With bills under control, he felt he had basic financial safety, which he calls central to his peace of mind in the past decade.
- He emphasizes that the change was slow rather than overnight, but that it led to much greater happiness.
- He encourages readers in similar situations to view financial steps as self-care, much like personal hygiene.
- He suggests that finances do not need constant focus, only regular attention.
- He claims that once someone adopts this kind of financial self-care, the resulting changes can be striking.
- At the end, he promotes his Sea Change Program module on debt elimination and savings/investments, describing its articles, forum, accountability groups and webinar with a guest expert.
- He mentions that the program is free for seven days and then costs $10 per month, providing a link to sign up.
