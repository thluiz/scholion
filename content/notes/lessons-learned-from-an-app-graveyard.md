The article uses the author’s “app graveyard” to show why many mobile apps get downloaded but rarely stay in use. The main point is that an app should solve a real need, work reliably, respect device resources and user data, and make a strong first impression.

## Reading notes

- Many downloaded apps are abandoned, deleted, or ignored, so the author keeps neglected apps on the device as reminders of what caused them to fail.
- An app is not always necessary if a responsive website or web app can serve the content and functionality well enough.
- Apps should work as expected, especially after updates or OS releases, and testing on real devices plus analytics can help find failures.
- Apps should avoid draining memory, bandwidth, battery, or location services, and should disable location updates as soon as possible.
- Mobile design should follow platform conventions and give users guidance when an interaction model is unfamiliar.
- Apps that handle sensitive data need to explain why they ask for information and how they use it, and they should avoid unnecessary registration or sign-in.
- Apps that create or store user data should let people save, back up, or archive it so the data is not lost if the app is discontinued or updated.
- App descriptions should clearly state what is included and what requires in-app purchase, and users should not be forced to rate or share the app to unlock paid features.
- Apps should load quickly and let users start using them immediately, without splash screens or loud launch effects.
- Marketing and incentives can drive downloads, but they do not ensure continued use.
- Platform updates can make third-party apps redundant, so apps need functionality or experience that remains useful after new OS features arrive.
- Developers should watch reviews, support messages, and other feedback, then address the problems that lead users to abandon the app.
