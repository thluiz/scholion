Ownership means acting with the sense that your work is crucial to the success of the project, the team, and the company. The article says this attitude is shaped by actions, feelings, and passion, and argues that it helps engineers grow in several practical ways.

## Reading notes

- Ownership pushes you to learn more because you want to deliver the best quality possible, which makes you read about your deliverables and the technology stack you use.
- A frontend task like building a contact form can lead you to look into a11y, A/B testing, performance budgets, and analytics when you care about quality.
- Ownership makes you a better team player because it pushes you to communicate with teammates and help them get work done.
- Owners do not point fingers; they step in to help because they care about making the work succeed.
- Ownership strengthens debugging and engineering skills because you keep investigating until you find the root cause.
- In the example, the engineer handles a production issue, applies a quick fix to unblock customers, then traces the problem through multiple services until finding the cause.
- Ownership can make you take more responsibilities because you become more knowledgeable, more respected, and sometimes a go-to person.
- More responsibility can bring a bigger role, harder tasks, and maybe a raise.
- The summary says you should act like an owner even for yourself, because that mindset helps you grow and helps people around you grow too.
