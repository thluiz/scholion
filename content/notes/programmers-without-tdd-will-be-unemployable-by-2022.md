The author predicts that routine TDD will become a baseline requirement for professional programming jobs by 2022. He defines TDD broadly to include test-first development and BDD-style practice, with developer-written automated unit tests run regularly. He also says this shift will improve software quality and productivity, even if most TDD practice remains mediocre.

## Reading notes

- By 2022, it will not be possible to get a professional programming job without routinely practicing TDD.
- TDD here means test-driven development, including test-first or design-driven development, and possibly BDD-style practice.
- The key characteristics are developer-written automated unit tests and routine execution of those tests, at least every day.
- The author expects TDD quality to vary, with the best practice excellent and most of it only mediocre.
- He says it will become unacceptable in interviews to question TDD, not know it, fail to use it in an exercise, or dismiss it as useless or unproven.
- Programmers will again be expected to write unit tests for their work.
- Unit testing will be overwhelmingly automated, and manual testing is described as a sin.
- The author argues that TDD reduces bugs, improves maintainability, and increases productivity.
- He expects the industry to be in a skills overhaul as programmers learn TDD.
- TDD is presented as a testable skill, unlike Agile in general, which the author says is harder to verify.
- There will be ongoing demand for TDD training and coaching, shifting from introductory courses to improvement and advanced courses.
- Universities and colleges that do not teach TDD may produce graduates who are not employable.
- Students are advised to avoid colleges that do not teach programming with TDD.
- Colleges are advised to adopt TDD quickly to improve recruitment and graduate employability.
- In business, TDD may reduce day-to-day software problems over time, though companies must address the supply side to see the benefit.
- The author says good use of TDD will reduce the need for debuggers, make IDEs less important, and change the developer tools market.
