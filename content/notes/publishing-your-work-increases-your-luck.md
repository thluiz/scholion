The page says luck is an unexpected good thing, and that publishing your work increases the odds of meeting it. It uses the idea of a “Luck Surface Area” to connect doing things with telling people, since both raise the chance that others notice your work.

## Reading notes

- Luck is defined as something unexpected and good that happens to you.
- Publishing your work increases the number of opportunities for good luck to find you.
- Working in public builds reputation, a track record, and a public body of work that can speak for you.
- The “Luck Surface Area” idea says serendipity grows with the amount of work you do and the number of people you tell.
- If you are already doing things, you may need to reframe work you think is unremarkable because others do not know it yet.
- If you want to start, begin with the smallest thing and get moving instead of waiting for a perfect idea.
- Curiosity can lead you to share projects outside work, and expertise can turn work problems into posts, talks, meetups, podcasts, or open source projects.
- If the exact details of work are not shareable, the concepts, lessons, and takeaways often are.
- Telling people about what you make is part of the process, and publishing can happen on GitHub, a newsletter, a podcast, forums, a blog, YouTube, or anywhere off your hard drive.
- Publishing is a skill that can be learned, and sharing wins, losses, and thought processes along the way helps bring people along.
- After publishing, people may start to recognize you, contact you about work, invite you to speak, ask for your services, or connect with you as peers.
