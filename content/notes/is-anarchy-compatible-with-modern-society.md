The post argues that anarchy should be understood by shape rather than size. It describes anarchist scale as wide and deep, with decentralized networks, social rewards for generosity, and built environments that favor diffusion, repair, and shared use instead of hierarchy and display.

It also argues that anarchist organizing has already existed in large populations, both in prehistorical societies that resisted hierarchy and in modern struggles against state and capital. Examples include prisoner self-organization and the Spanish Civil War, while the main obstacle remains capitalism’s global power.
