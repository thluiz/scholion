The author says writing technical content in a second language gets easier with practice, patience, and simple habits. He advises starting before feeling fluent, planning only the core idea, writing the draft first, and correcting grammar and spelling afterward.

He also recommends searching uncertain phrases in quotes, keeping prose simple to reduce errors, and hiring a proofreader when the text matters for a job or a publication without an edit button.

## Reading notes

- Writing in English can start before fluency feels ready, and unfinished drafts can still help improve composition.
- First attempts will be weak, so repetition and a small daily habit matter.
- Planning should anchor the idea, but not become a barrier to writing.
- Drafting should come before grammar and spell checking.
- If a sentence sounds off, searching it in quotation marks can help confirm whether it is natural.
- Simplicity helps readers understand the point and lowers the chance of grammar mistakes.
- For high-stakes writing, a proofreader is worth getting.
