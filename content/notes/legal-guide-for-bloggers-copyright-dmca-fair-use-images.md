The page lays out the basic legal ideas bloggers need to avoid copyright problems. It defines copyright, public domain, Creative Commons, DMCA takedowns, and fair use, then links each one to how bloggers can use text, images, and other works without unnecessary risk.

## Reading notes

- Copyright belongs to the creator of an original work and limits reproduction, distribution, public display, and derivative uses unless permission is given.
- In the US, copyright for works produced after 1977 lasts until 70 years after the creator’s death, and works can enter the public domain when copyright expires or is waived.
- The public domain lets anyone access, use, share, and adapt works without attribution.
- Creative Commons licenses keep copyright in place but add permissions through conditions such as attribution, share-alike, non-commercial use, and no derivative works.
- The DMCA gives online service providers a process for removing allegedly infringing material and for handling counterclaims.
- Fair use depends on four factors: purpose and character, nature of the work, amount taken, and market effect.
- For bloggers, fair use is most relevant for transformative uses such as reviews, criticism, parody, commentary, news reporting, research, and education.
- The guide recommends public domain directories and image sites such as Pexels, Pixabay, Wikimedia, and Creative Commons search for legally usable images.
- It says full disclosure should cover affiliates, monetization, free products, and sponsorships, with a visible statement when the content could be biased.
- It also argues that using Creative Commons on your own work can increase exposure and lead to more opportunities.
