The article argues that interview performance starts before the meeting and continues after it. It says small talk matters because rapport and likability affect hiring, and it warns that candidates should answer even awkward or detailed questions without sounding dismissive. It also says interviews work best as conversations, not monologues, and that follow-up messages and scheduling interactions shape the final impression.

## Reading notes

- Small talk is part of building rapport, and it can affect whether an interviewer sees you as someone they would enjoy working with.
- Treat every question as meaningful, even if it seems irrelevant, and answer it directly rather than reacting as if it does not matter.
- If you do not know an exact detail, give your best answer and offer to follow up with the precise information.
- If a question is discriminatory or off-limits, steer the conversation away from it without answering or confronting the interviewer.
- A strong interview should feel like a conversation, with room for follow-up questions instead of a long monologue.
- Follow-up after the interview matters because it becomes the most recent impression, so thank-you notes and other interactions should stay polished.
- The process starts before the interview too, since scheduling and voicemail etiquette can shape whether an employer keeps you in consideration.
