The post argues that mindfulness practice can reduce stress and unease by changing how you relate to experience. It first asks you to notice the direct sensations of the present moment, then to meet that experience with warmth and friendliness, and then to see how gain-loss motives are tied to a sense of separate self.

## Reading notes

- Most struggle comes from living inside thoughts, narratives, and storylines about life rather than direct experience.
- The first practice is to return to sensations in the body, breath, touch, sound, light, and shape when the mind drifts.
- This return to present-moment experience is presented as a practice that can be developed over time.
- The second practice adds friendliness, gentleness, kindness, and even love toward whatever appears in direct experience.
- The post compares this to welcoming a person warmly into your house.
- The third practice is to notice that the sense of a separate self is a mental construction rather than something distinct from the world.
- The post links frustration, anger, sadness, anxiety, and stress to actions driven by gain and loss.
- Examples of gain-loss motivation include wanting praise, avoiding anger, seeking validation on social media, and buying things for appearance or reassurance.
- The alternative is to act from love and compassion for others and for oneself as part of an interconnected world.
