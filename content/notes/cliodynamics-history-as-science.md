The page presents cliodynamics as a transdisciplinary field that treats history as a science. It says the goal is to turn verbal theories into mathematical models, derive precise predictions, and test them against empirical material in order to discover general principles of historical change.

## Reading notes

- Empires rise and fall, populations and economies boom and bust, and world religions spread or wither, so the page asks what mechanisms drive these historical processes.
- It says there are many explanations for the fall of the Roman Empire, but no consensus on which are plausible or on the broader mechanisms behind imperial collapse.
- It defines cliodynamics as a field at the intersection of historical macrosociology, economic history, mathematical modeling of long-term social processes, and historical databases.
- It presents mathematical modeling and statistical analysis as key tools in the cliodynamic research program.
- It says the broader aim is to find general principles that explain the functioning and dynamics of real historical societies.
- It notes that the research community has been growing and now has its own journal, Cliodynamics: the Journal of Theoretical and Mathematical History.
- One research focus is the collapse of historical empires, which the page links to waves of political instability in agrarian societies.
- It mentions a theory of secular cycles, empirically tested on England, France, Russia, and the Roman Republic and Empire.
- It also asks whether the modern American Republic may face another period of political instability and social disintegration.
- Another focus is why large-scale states and empires were possible at all, given that they are rare in the historical record.
- It uses multilevel selection theory, agent-based models, and empirical surveys of imperial formation to study the forces that held huge empires together.
- It connects the study of large-scale cooperation to failed states and nation building.
