## Reading notes

- It recommends two beginner books first, Programming Elixir and Elixir in Action, and says they share the same broad structure but differ in depth and accessibility.
- If Elixir is your first programming language, Joy of Elixir is presented as the better fit.
- Elixir’s documentation is described as extensive enough to work as an intro or even replace a book for people who want to start building quickly.
- Elixir School is presented as a deeper online resource with syntax, OTP, Ecto, and translations in many languages.
- Learn With Me: Elixir is suggested as a companion to Joy of Elixir for beginners who want more prose and more explanation.
- The Serokell blog points readers to its Elixir and Elixir Tutorial tags for more material.
- Exercism is recommended alongside reading because it lets learners practice Elixir and includes free mentoring.
- The YouTube section highlights a short syntax overview by Derek Banas, an OTP overview, and a ten-video Introduction to Elixir series.
- The page points readers to r/Elixir and Elixir Forum for community support and extra content.
- For web development, Programming Phoenix is recommended as an introduction to Phoenix and its principles, and Programming Ecto is suggested for deeper coverage of Ecto.
- Designing Elixir Systems With OTP is presented as a practical guide to structuring Elixir applications with OTP.
- Metaprogramming Elixir is recommended for readers who want to understand macros, code generation, quote, and unquote.
- Elixir for Programmers is described as a paid course that gets learners building a Hangman game with OTP and Phoenix.
- Alchemist Camp is listed as a source of free and paid video lessons for beginner and intermediate developers.
- The podcasts Elixir Wizards and Elixir Mix are included as listening material for people who want exposure to topics and guest discussions.
