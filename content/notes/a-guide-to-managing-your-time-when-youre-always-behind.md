Leo Babauta argues that feeling behind is a common human experience and that there is no single fix. Instead, he offers a set of practices: make a task list, focus on one important task at a time, notice procrastination and overwhelm, manage energy, keep structure flexible, and stop judging yourself harshly.

## Reading notes

- Make a task list, then narrow it to the top three most important tasks and focus on one of them at a time.
- Use intentional containers by setting a time period and an intention for one task, then trying to stay with that intention.
- When fear, uncertainty, overwhelm, or the urge to distract yourself comes up, pause and notice the body sensations instead of acting on them.
- Bring curiosity, gentleness, and compassion to the feelings that arise when you want to avoid a task.
- Let go of the attachment to getting everything done, because most deadlines are self-created and the expectation of catching up is unrealistic.
- Turn attention to reality as it is, appreciate what is present, and take the next step from a place of love rather than ideal pressure.
- Manage energy by getting enough sleep, taking breaks away from devices, and setting aside time to replenish during the day and week.
- Build a daily structure, but stay flexible enough to adjust when something truly important comes up.
- Drop self-judgment and replace it with kindness, love, and compassion, especially when you feel behind or fail to meet a goal.
- Put the practices together gradually: structure the day, rest, notice expectations and judgment, and practice mindfulness inside the work session.
