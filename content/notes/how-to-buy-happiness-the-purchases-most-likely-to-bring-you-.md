The article argues that money does not buy lasting happiness, but some kinds of spending can improve well-being. It groups those purchases into financial security, experiences, spending on others, the right material goods, and buying time, while warning against inflated expectations and lifestyle inflation.

## Reading notes

- Paying off debt can reduce stress and improve health, which supports happiness.
- Experiences tend to produce more happiness than material goods, and they keep people present.
- Shared experiences, advance planning, and anticipation can make the benefit stronger.
- Spending on others, including donations and volunteering, is linked to greater happiness.
- Material goods are more satisfying when they fit how people actually spend their time.
- “Experiential” items, like books or games used repeatedly, can bring longer enjoyment.
- Small splurges can feel rewarding, but buying too much reduces appreciation.
- Buying time, such as hiring help or using delivery services, can be worth it if the saved time is well used.
- Expectations should stay modest, and people should buy what they themselves like, not what others like.
- Earning more does not have to mean spending more, since lifestyle inflation can cancel the benefit.
