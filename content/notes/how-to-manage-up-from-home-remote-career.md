The text argues that telecommuting demands new interpersonal skills, especially for those who want visibility with managers or aim for promotion. With fewer informal encounters, workers must actively showcase achievements, cultivate relationships and adopt specific tactics suited to remote work. Research cited indicates remote work is likely to persist and that telecommuters can progress at similar rates to office workers.

Experts suggest that many classic “managing up” habits still apply, such as continuous learning, seeking mentors and accepting more responsibility. Yet remote conditions require workers to communicate their results more explicitly, structure regular contact with managers, lean on virtual networking, help colleagues and managers, connect with leaders beyond their direct boss, and focus on skills and opportunities rather than immediate title or pay increases during crises.
