The post says startup culture treats growth as the main signal of success, but that the idea of infinite growth is an illusion. It argues that real markets flatten, competitors appear, customers lose interest, and companies that chase speed without sustainability often end badly.

## Reading notes

- The text criticizes the expectation of continuous growth and shows that it usually appears in pitch decks, press releases, and market narratives.
- It cites WeWork, Fast, and internet bubble startups as cases in which rapid expansion was not accompanied by fundamentals, cash, or a sustainable business model.
- It argues that growth can come in spurts, as long as it is followed by phases of consolidation, operational improvement, and strengthening the base.
- It points to Amazon as an example of growth in waves, with pauses to consolidate logistics and margins before a new expansion.
- It says vanity metrics, such as downloads, monthly active users, and fundraising rounds, can hide the real health of the business.
- It contrasts these metrics with a focus on unit economics, retention, and operational resilience.
- It reports examples of founders who preferred to grow in a stable way, with profit, training, automation, and less dependence on fundraising.
- It reframes growth as a marathon, in which speed matters, but only works with recovery and market limits.
