The page presents Thomas Edison as someone who criticized sleep as wasted time while relying on brief naps to support long workdays. It also shows how his lightbulb changed the role of artificial light and, in the author’s framing, helped reshape modern sleep habits.

## Reading notes

- Edison reportedly slept only three to four hours a night and treated sleep as a waste of time.
- He spoke as if artificial light freed people from sleep, even making sweeping claims about the effect of light on intelligence.
- In 1921 he wrote that people overeat and oversleep, and said he himself never needed more than four or five hours of sleep.
- The page says that, despite this stance, Edison used power-napping and kept cots in his labs and library.
- Accounts describe him waking from naps refreshed and returning to work immediately.
- His diary complicates his claims, since it records dreams and several comments about sleep.
- The page links his sleep habits to a strong drive for productivity and long hours of focused work.
- Edison is quoted saying success comes from applying mental and physical effort severely and in one direction.
- Friends and biographers describe him reading, observing, and working almost constantly, often for many hours at a stretch.
