The article frames the U.S.-China competition in AI as a Cold War-like struggle with broad consequences for industry, society and geopolitics. It says the race has already boosted tech spending, pushed both governments to support their own industries, and increased fears around safety, espionage and influence.

## Reading notes

- China was frustrated that OpenAI, Google and other American companies were leading generative AI, and it responded with more pressure on tech executives, looser rules, funding and faster buildup of computing power.
- DeepSeek became a turning point in early 2025, giving Chinese leaders confidence that the country had a model it could be proud of and prompting a larger wave of state support.
- The U.S. still leads in the most powerful AI models, advanced chips and private investment, while China relies on engineers, lower costs and a state-led model that can move quickly.
- China is building computing clusters, data-center links and a shared compute pool by 2028, while also pouring hundreds of billions of dollars into its power grid.
- The article compares the AI race to Cold War scientific competition and argues that AI could reshape computing, warfare, labor and daily life if it keeps advancing.
- It says the rivalry is likely to intensify hacking, cyber espionage and mistrust, making cooperation between Washington and Beijing harder.
- China’s AI policy shifted from caution and strict censorship rules to stronger support for developers after U.S. chip export controls tightened and domestic companies pressed for relief.

- The U.S. government’s AI policy and OpenAI’s public warnings both reflect growing concern that Chinese progress could shape global standards and resources.
- China is also pushing domestic semiconductors, with Huawei and local governments working on chip systems, subsidized energy and larger chip capacity to reduce dependence on U.S. technology.
- The article says China may still be years behind the U.S. in high-end chips, but it could compete if AI progress slows or plateaus.
