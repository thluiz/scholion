The article says freelancing can start as extra income and grow into a full-time career. It recommends building a portfolio first, because clients want to see what you can do and how you can help them, then looking for first leads through platforms, social media, friends, nonprofits, and direct outreach.

## Reading notes

- Build a portfolio before anything else, and treat it as proof of work rather than a resume.
- Include information about yourself and, if you want, a photo so clients can get a sense of who you are.
- If you do not have paid work yet, show side projects, redesigns, or small web apps.
- The first clients are usually the hardest to find, and the article lists Upwork, social media, friends and family, nonprofits, and cold emails as possible sources.
- Do not underprice your work just to compete on platforms where rates are pushed down.
- Set rates by considering income goals, expenses, and billable hours, and reduce available billable time to account for administrative work.
- Raise rates over time as projects become more complex.
- Never lower a rate without removing something from the project scope.
- Before starting work, get a signed contract.
- Ask for a down payment, with 50% up front given as a simple starting rule.
- Save the estimate, contract, and key project conversations in one folder.
- Do not promise skills you do not have, do not work for free for exposure, estimate time conservatively, and keep money aside for taxes.
