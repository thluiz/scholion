The essay argues that the question is not whether Maurizio Cattelan’s banana is art, but whether it is any good. The writer says that if something is presented as art, the better response is to judge it as offensive, stupid, facile, or effective, rather than to deny that it belongs to art at all.

## Reading notes

- The writer rejects the claim that art can be settled by saying “that’s not art.”
- Contemporary art often breaks clear categories, and that disruption is part of its force.
- The banana taped to a wall is treated as a critique of art commodification and the spectacle around it.
- The art is located in the sale, the media attention, and the absurdity of the system, not in the banana itself.
- The writer compares this move to changes in rap, stand-up, and Hannah Gadsby’s comedy.
- The claim is that artists should be allowed to decide whether what they are making counts as art.
- Refusing that claim is presented as a way of avoiding judgment rather than making it.
