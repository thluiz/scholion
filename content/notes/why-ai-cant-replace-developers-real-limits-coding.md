AI tools like GitHub Copilot and ChatGPT can save time on boilerplate, documentation and simple bugs, but the text argues that they cannot match developers on complex coding work. The main limit is reasoning in context: when problems depend on project details, AI-generated fixes can create more issues and are harder to debug.

The article uses Apple’s GSM-Symbolic paper to support the claim that large language models rely on pattern recognition rather than true reasoning. It recommends using AI for small, specific and repetitive tasks, giving clear prompts, reviewing the output carefully and stopping when the model is no longer helping.

## Reading notes

- AI already automates parts of development, such as repetitive code, documentation and fixing simple errors.
- The text argues that AI does not write code better than a developer because it does not handle complex problems or the specific context of a project well.
- When AI makes a mistake in a fix, the problem can grow and the generated code is usually harder to debug than code written by a person who knows the system.
- The article says that AI responds to learned patterns, but does not understand context like a human.
- The GSM-Symbolic research is used to show that small changes in problems can significantly reduce the models’ accuracy.
- For programming tasks, the text points out that AI works better on small, specific and repetitive tasks than on challenges that require creativity and complex logic.
- The text recommends treating AI as an assistant or as a junior developer, with human guidance and review.
- Among the common mistakes mentioned are relying too much on AI to solve problems, expecting perfect code, giving too little context and treating it like a senior developer.
