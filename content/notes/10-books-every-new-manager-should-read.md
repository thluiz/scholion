The page recommends starting with ten books for people who are stepping into management for the first time. It says the list is meant to offer practical insights for leading a team and preparing for the challenges of managing people.

## Reading notes

- The selection includes books beyond traditional business titles, such as novels, psychological research, and writing by a Roman emperor.
- Drive argues that money is not the best way to motivate high performance and emphasizes autonomy, mastery, and purpose.
- The One Thing You Need to Know says great managers identify employees' individual strengths and use them, rather than focusing on weaknesses.
- It also recommends setting clear expectations, offering praise and recognition, and showing people that you care.
- Thinking, Fast and Slow explains two systems of thought, fast and intuitive and slow and deliberate, and uses that framework to describe cognitive biases.
- The book is presented as a way to anticipate psychological obstacles for both managers and their teams and to use scientific strategies to deal with them.
