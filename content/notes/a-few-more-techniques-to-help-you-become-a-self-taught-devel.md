The article argues that self-learning is one of the most useful skills for becoming a developer. It focuses on habits that help learners stay consistent, keep going through the difficult middle stage, and build confidence across frontend, backend, or other areas.

## Reading notes

- Set a small daily learning target, with 30 minutes suggested as a practical minimum, including weekends and holidays.
- Use distributed practice instead of long, irregular study sessions, because it makes later review less necessary.
- Track study time in an app like Toggl to see whether learning has been consistent and which topics need more attention.
- Time tracking can also help when motivation drops, because seeing accumulated effort may make quitting feel less acceptable.
- Find a mentor through meetups or Slack communities, but do not expect the mentor to teach everything or build the learning plan.
- A mentor can help untangle difficult concepts after independent study, keep the learner engaged, and share technical and non-technical experience.
- The article also presents Housework Undoer, a free frontend project meant to help junior developers build portfolio work and practice skills for the job market.
