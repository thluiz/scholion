The post argues that web app ideas should move from paper to prototype before becoming an MVP. The prototype is for the maker, helps work out the problem, and makes the project real before it is shared with others.

## Reading notes

- The author says he has moved web app ideas from paper to a fully working product.
- He says many people want to go straight from idea to MVP, but prototyping is a useful part of the process.
- He describes prototyping as a fun and creative way to play with an idea.
- He says not everything has to ship, and that unfinished projects still build skill.
- He compares making web apps to other crafts that improve through practice.
- He says the goal of a prototype is to solve the problem or part of it.
- His process starts with pen, pencil, and paper.
- He uses UI sketches to think through layout, CSS Grid, and flexbox.
- He uses pseudocode to lay out functions and code steps.
- He gives a simple example for getting blog posts, with steps like getting a user ID, calling a blog API, parsing results, and rendering HTML.
- He says the prototype is not for anyone else at first, but it can be used to get feedback and look for people who need the product.
- He says the MVP comes after the prototype.
- At the MVP stage, the product is made presentable and understandable.
- He mentions landing pages, signup, login, and payments as parts of an MVP.
- He says the product building is already done in the prototype stage, and the MVP stage focuses on polish and user flow.
- He says he is currently working on a couple of prototypes and hopes they may become MVPs.
