Remote leadership can push managers toward control, surveillance, and more meetings, especially when they feel unsure whether the team is working. The article says that pressure tends to backfire, because it hurts performance, learning, and motivation, while turning remote work into a performance of being busy.

It recommends shifting from control to trust, making expectations explicit, giving people ways to share progress on their own schedule, and using regular one-on-ones to understand how employees are feeling and where they need support.

## Reading notes

- Remote work can trigger anxious management habits, such as checking on people too often or using tracking software.
- Pressure can reduce performance, weaken outcomes, limit learning, and make the work less enjoyable.
- A remote leader should focus on results and on creating the conditions that help people do the work.
- The team needs clear expectations about communication, working hours, time zones, availability, and response times.
- Managers should give people a way to share progress proactively instead of interrupting them with frequent status checks.
- One-on-one meetings matter, but they should not be treated as status updates.
- One-on-ones are for understanding how employees are feeling, clearing blockers, and supporting better progress.
- The article favors trust over tracking and argues that remote leadership should help people work, not hold them back.
