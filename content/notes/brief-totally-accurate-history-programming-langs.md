One Hundred Percent Inspired by Facts is a tongue‑in‑cheek tour through the history of computing and programming languages. It presents inventors and languages from Jacquard’s loom to Swift as if their motivations, designs and side effects were absurd, petty, or accidental, constantly poking fun at compilation times, bloat, syntax, naming, developer happiness and enterprise culture.

The text follows a timeline, assigning each milestone a humorous origin story: mechanical looms become multi‑threaded processors, language features are blamed for Electron‑era bloat, and famous figures are recast as bored office workers, preachers, or seekers of happiness whose creations accidentally reshape the industry.
