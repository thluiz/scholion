## Reading notes

- Bird wings are presented as specialized arms with feathers, with the same joints as a human arm.
- The bird wing structure includes primary feathers, secondary feathers, greater coverts, lesser coverts, alula, scapulars, mantle, and axillaries.
- The drawing process starts with the wing’s front edge, then adds arm, forearm, primaries, secondaries, coverts, and alula.
- Common bird-wing mistakes include missing anatomy, feathers that do not overlap, and a wing surface that leaves a hole near the body.
- Wing animation follows the difference between downstroke and upstroke, with the wings pushing air down and then folding quickly on the way up.
- The tutorial recommends practice with references, drawing over them, redrawing simplified wings, and then drawing from memory.
- Bat wings are described as elongated fingers with a membrane, connected to the hind limb and stretched through the shoulder and fingers.
- Bat wings are drawn by starting with the edge of the wing and thumb, then building the arm, fingers, membrane, and leg connection.
- Common bat-wing mistakes include missing arm structure, missing membrane, fingers without joints, and membranes cut too deeply.
- Bat flight is shown as faster and more continuous than bird flight, with shallow downstrokes, individually moving phalanges, and a precise folding sequence on the upstroke.
- The final step-by-step section combines angel, demon, bird, dragon, and bat wing poses, starting from a general pose and ending with details.
- The page closes by urging practice with real birds and bats in different sizes, species, and poses before drawing winged creatures from imagination.
