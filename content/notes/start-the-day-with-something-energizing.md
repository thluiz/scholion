The piece says that feeling “not up to” something can mean rest is needed, but it can also reflect emotional reasoning and lead to more avoidance. It recommends moving slowly, keeping breaks, and protecting what matters by working in smaller steps.

## Reading notes

- Feeling unable to do things can come with depression or anxiety, and even enjoyable or meaningful activities can feel difficult.
- Sometimes rest is necessary, especially when stress is triggering symptoms.
- Ongoing avoidance and interference with what is meaningful are signs that avoidance may be making things worse.
- In CBT, emotional reasoning can make strong feelings seem like facts.
- Avoiding activity to conserve energy can reduce momentum over time and push meaningful things out of view.
- Proceed slowly and focus first on what needs attention right away.
- Take extra breaks on low days, including time alone, time outdoors, or a short talk with a friend.
- Plan something to look forward to, even if it is small.
- Spread tasks over several days and break daunting tasks into smaller pieces.
- Start the day with something that gives energy, such as music in bed or a morning walk.
