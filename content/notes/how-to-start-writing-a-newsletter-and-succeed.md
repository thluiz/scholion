The article says newsletter growth depends on having a real reason to keep writing, then choosing a narrow audience and a steady pace. It also stresses clear topics, a usable backlog, readable structure, and active distribution through networks and platforms like Substack.

## Reading notes

- Start with strong reasons for writing, because a whim will not last long.
- Focus the first 20 articles on one audience so the readership does not keep shifting.
- Stay close to your own experience and repeated problems, since that saves research time.
- Decide each article’s problem and reader benefit before writing it.
- Keep a topic backlog, expand vague ideas into core messages, and move favorites onto the calendar.
- Avoid publishing too fast if it hurts work-life balance, quality, or future ideas.
- Use a platform with a network and reasonable metrics; the article recommends Substack if you have no preference.
- Pick a newsletter name, about page, logo, custom domain, and strong intro email to make the publication recognizable.
- Set a deadline and block time for writing sessions instead of sitting too long on one topic.
- Identify the reader, imagine a conversation, take raw notes, build an outline, then draft from that material.
- Write direct, simple openings and keep readability low-friction with short paragraphs, bold text, and bullet points.
- Use images, preferably your own, because the article says they feel more authentic.
- Build real connections with other creators, comment thoughtfully, and send private messages when there is genuine interest.
- Collaborate through joint posts, guest posts, or podcasts when there is mutual benefit.
- Share articles on LinkedIn and Reddit, and use short URLs to track clicks.
- Keep improving by reading, experimenting with style, taking courses, using Grammarly, and studying better creators.
