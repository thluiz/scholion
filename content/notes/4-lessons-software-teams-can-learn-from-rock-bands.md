The article compares rock bands and software teams to argue that both work best when different roles, skills, and viewpoints are present. It also stresses that collaboration still leaves room for individual interpretation, and that teams need patience and perspective when work gets tense.

## Reading notes

- A band needs different roles and skills, and software teams need the same kind of range across experience, technologies, methodologies, and personality types.
- Diversity can create friction, but that friction can become productive when people respect one another.
- When a team lacks a needed skill, it can bring in a consultant or build partnerships outside the organization.
- Rock bands and software teams both spend a lot of time together, so leaders need to build a climate of respect.
- Testers can be left with less respect than developers, and one way to address that is to have developers spend time in QA.
- Collaboration does not mean everyone works the same way; people still need room to interpret tasks and contribute in their own style.
- In programming, as in music, there is more than one way to express an idea within a shared framework.
- Teams also need to keep their cool when deadlines, tradeoffs, and turnover create pressure.
- The article closes by saying success in both fields depends on perspective, the ability to switch off sometimes, and learning from both successes and failures.
