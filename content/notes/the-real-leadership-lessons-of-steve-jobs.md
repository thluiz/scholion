Walter Isaacson argues that the most useful lessons from Steve Jobs come from what he built: Apple as an enduring company, not just individual products. The article presents Jobs as someone whose personality, discipline, and intensity were tied directly to his way of doing business.

## Reading notes

- Jobs’s key leadership lesson is to focus on a few priorities and cut the rest.
- He treated simplicity as something that required deep work, not just minimal design.
- He believed Apple should control hardware, software, and the customer experience end to end.
- When behind, he preferred leapfrogging with integrated products instead of catching up feature by feature.
- He put products ahead of profit and believed strong products would bring profits later.
- He rejected focus groups and trusted intuition about what customers would want before they asked for it.
- He used pressure to push teams past what seemed possible, including shorter boot times and faster glass production.
- He cared about packaging and presentation because people judge products by how they are framed.
- He delayed launches when designs felt unfinished or not right.
- He valued A players and used bluntness to avoid keeping mediocre people around.
- He preferred face-to-face discussion and built spaces to encourage spontaneous encounters.
- He combined strategic vision with attention to small design details.
- He linked humanities and sciences, seeing that mix as central to innovation.
- He kept a countercultural streak alive in Apple’s identity and advertising.
