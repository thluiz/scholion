The post gathers attendee feedback on virtual events and says that good experiences depend on resources and strategies that support discussion and personal connection. It notes that virtual formats can help some people attend more easily, but also points out where they feel weaker than in-person events.

## Reading notes

- Virtual events can make attendance easier for mothers with young children at home.
- Some attendees still want in-person events because they value in-person networking.
- Introverts may find virtual networking and questions less intimidating than in-person rooms with many people.
- Speakers often speak more slowly and seem more comfortable in virtual events.
- The quality expected from virtual presentations is higher.
- Discord works well as a community tool when channels are organized by topic.
- Channels based on sessions or speakers are discouraged because discussion grows more naturally from topics.
- A welcome channel and a Code of Conduct link are recommended in Discord.
- Asynchronous messaging can make it easier to ask speakers questions without interrupting them.
- A strong MC or host can improve the event experience a lot.
- Pre-recorded sessions can feel odd when speaker interaction happens at the same time.
- Live sessions can run over time if technical problems happen.
- A pre-day tech check or a 10-minute buffer is suggested.
- A panel format with questions at the end worked well.
- More broad chat channels can help people feel more welcome and less timid.
- Examples mentioned for positive attendee experience include #WomenOfReact2020 and MagnoliaJS.
