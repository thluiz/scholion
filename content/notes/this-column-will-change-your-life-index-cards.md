The column presents index cards as a cheap, portable tool for personal organization and for writing. They make it easy to capture thoughts and later move, group, and reorder them, unlike notebook pages or handheld devices.

It also suggests that index cards can produce an emergent structure. The writer points to Robert Pirsig’s Lila and Niklas Luhmann’s research notes, where ideas cross-reference each other and unexpected connections appear. The cards are described as more than an archive, since the system can seem to work like a collaborator.
