The article draws on the author’s move into a fast-growing leadership role and turns that experience into five management practices. It focuses on time protection, feedback and development, asynchronous communication, decision-making, and making work feel safe in uncertain developer relations settings.

## Reading notes

- Block off one day each week and keep it free from meetings so there is uninterrupted time for deep work and catch-up.
- The book *First, Break All the Rules* is presented as the most applicable management book the author read, especially because it comes from a large Gallup study.
- The book’s main conclusion is that there is no single consistent rule set for all good managers.
- The same book links productive, happy teams to six conditions, including clear expectations, proper tools, daily use of strengths, recognition, care from a supervisor, and someone who encourages development.
- Use asynchronous 1-1s in shared online documents when time zones and travel make live meetings difficult.
- Keep each async 1-1 document to one page and use it to record impactful accomplishments and what to try next.
- Use the document comments first, then make the live call focus on needs rather than exploration.
- Keep some non-structured 1-1 conversations too, especially about personal goals and life.
- Managerial ambiguity increases in developer relations, so the manager has to create clarity for the team when direction is unclear.
- Making the wrong call is acceptable when the purpose is to add clarity, and the team should be supported if they try and miss.
- The final goal is to create a safe space where the team can work with clarity of role and purpose and feel that someone is looking out for them.
