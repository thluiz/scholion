The author says he has been more creative and has made more progress in the last six weeks, and he attributes most of that to a new habit called the StrikeThru method. He uses it to manage to-do lists and keep track of his thinking in a physical notebook.

## Reading notes

- He describes three main sections: the live list for daily work, the dump for free-form thoughts, and the vault for ongoing project lists.
- He also includes a calendar section for tasks set for specific days.
- Each item can have a tag so it can be moved into the live list by writing the tag number.
- At the end of the day, items can be crossed out if finished, marked with x if no longer a priority, or marked with > if carried over.
- He says the notebook should be carried everywhere to keep the habit going.
- He uses a Moleskine notebook and partitions it himself.
- He skips much of the referencing and tagging from the examples because he wants each item written as a commitment to himself.
- He also uses an online project management system for the team, but keeps personal lists in the notebook.
- He added a section at the bottom of each day for fitness items.
- He says the system works because it is offline, keeps everything in one place, and gives limited space for nine things a day.
- He says the limit pushes specificity and completion.
- He also says crossing things off a physical list feels satisfying and helps creativity.
- He credits Chris Kyle for StrikeThru and Ryder Carroll for Bullet Journal.
