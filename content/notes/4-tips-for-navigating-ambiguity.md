IDEO U frames ambiguity as a condition that makes people uncomfortable and cannot be solved with a finite plan. Instead of planning everything in advance, leaders should create the conditions for people to make progress together, even when the outcome is unclear.

## Reading notes

- Teams can handle ambiguity better when trust is high.
- Ambiguity shows up as friction, both from outside pressures and from internal resistance to forcing a usual process that no longer works.
- Naming unknowns, tensions, and fears gives teams room to work with them and have better conversations.
- Curiosity helps reframe fears as questions and invites learning from colleagues, advisors, and analogous cases.
- Small experiments and prototypes help teams test directions and learn their way forward instead of waiting for the right answer.
