The post argues that corrective feedback should help people improve without turning the conversation into a long buildup or a subtle hint. It says leaders should stay ready to notice and affirm good behavior, choose a timely moment instead of waiting for perfection, and state the issue plainly before explaining its impact.

## Reading notes

- Corrective feedback helps average performers improve and top performers excel.
- Leaders should look for behaviors to affirm and give more positives than negatives.
- Timeliness matters more than finding the perfect moment.
- Waiting too long makes corrective feedback harder to use and more embarrassing.
- Leaders should say what they see, explain the impact, and avoid socializing, shifting topics, or starting with compliments.
