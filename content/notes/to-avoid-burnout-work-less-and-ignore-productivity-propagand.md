Rahaf Harfoush says burnout is not just exhaustion and anxiety but a broader cultural problem tied to the idea that productivity is the main measure of success. She argues that remote work and the pandemic have intensified this pressure, making recovery part of work rather than a break from it.

## Reading notes

- Burnout is worsened by a work culture that treats any time not spent producing as wasted time and ties success to hustle.
- Knowledge workers need space and unstructured time to do their best work.
- Technology, social media, and confinement during the pandemic increase anxiety and shame, pushing people faster toward burnout.
- Recovery has to be built into work because creativity, innovation, and clear thinking depend on rest.
- Working nonstop does not work, and treating a crisis period as normal work-from-home time makes the problem worse.
- People should use some time at home to process emotions instead of filling every hour with activity.
- Practical steps include eating lunch without email or Slack, asking managers for clear expectations, and replacing unwritten rules with written guidelines.
- People who feel burned out should notice discomfort, schedule recovery time, and track how long tasks really take.
- High performers damage their output in the long run if recovery is not an intentional part of their workflow.
