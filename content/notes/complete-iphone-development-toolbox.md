## Reading notes

- To start developing for the iPhone, you need a Mac running OSX and registration on the iPhone Developer Program.
- Free registration lets you experiment and use Apple’s guides and videos, but testing is limited to the simulator.
- The standard program costs $99 a year and is required to test on an iPhone and release apps to the App Store.
- The latest iPhone SDK comes bundled with XCode, and the iPhone Resource Library is presented as the main documentation source.
- Apple’s suggested starting points include an introduction to Objective-C, the iPhone Application Programming Guide, and Getting Started Guides.
- The book list covers Objective-C, Cocoa, interface design, app-driven development, 3D games, and advanced topics such as performance, SQLite, Push Notifications, and networking.
- The screencast section points to Apple tutorials, Stanford and RWTH Aachen lectures, and a YouTube first-app video.
- The tutorial section includes examples for RSS readers, orientation, games, preferences, maps, accelerometer use, music playback, Push Notifications, Store Kit, SQLite, email, Twitter, and GameKit.
- The page recommends iPhone-focused blogs, forums, podcasts, and conferences as ongoing sources of help and updates.
- The open source section lists libraries for Google services, Facebook Connect, Flickr, 2D and 3D games, XML, and cross-platform app frameworks.
- The design and icon sections collect tools for mockups, sketches, stencils, and icon creation.
- The page also lists mobile website tools, app directories, and services for finding iPhone developers or jobs.
