Anthropic executed the largest IPO in history, raising $81 billion by selling 980 million shares at $83 each, with trading expected above $100 per share and a potential $2 trillion valuation. The piece traces Anthropic’s rapid journey from a park-bench idea during Covid to one of the world’s largest companies, crediting a strategic focus on enterprise use cases and a major shift from being primarily a model/API provider to an applications-oriented business. Recent quarters show explosive revenue and customer growth, but the article stresses looming concerns over AI ROI, cost discipline, intensifying competition, the exclusion of retail investors from early gains, and mounting tension between Anthropic’s safety commitments and public-market pressure for revenue and profit.

## Reading notes

- Anthropic raised $81 billion in its IPO by selling 980,000,000 shares at $83 each, with shares expected to trade above $100 and a $2 trillion valuation considered within reach.
- The company’s IPO is described as the largest initial public offering in history, seen as a turning point in the debate over whether AI can become a substantial business.
- Anthropic’s origin story includes co-founders planning the company outdoors in a San Francisco park during Covid, highlighting how quickly it scaled into one of the world’s largest companies.
- The article presents this speed as evidence of rapid AI progress and the pace at which new AI technology is reshaping the world.
- Early in the generative AI era, Anthropic focused on enterprise use cases, betting that solving harder enterprise problems would both improve model intelligence and unlock lucrative opportunities.
- This enterprise bet was made before there was broad consensus that AI models could become capable enough for complex enterprise tasks, and the article says the bet paid off.
- In early 2025, Anthropic is characterized as mostly an AI model company, selling access via API, with its Claude chatbot receiving limited use.
- Since then, Anthropic shifted its strategy to become an applications company that also has strong AI models, rather than just a model provider.
- Products like Claude Code are cited as successful applications that moved Anthropic “upstream” toward higher-value use cases.
- The company’s future is described as depending on expanding Claude-Code-style applications across sectors such as manufacturing, life sciences, graphic design, healthcare, and general knowledge work.
- After the applications pivot, Anthropic’s growth is described as extremely rapid.
- The company went from 1,500 customers spending $100,000 or more in December 2025 to 11,900 such customers by September 2026.
- Revenue grew 147% between Q1 and Q2 of 2026, from $4.7 billion to $11.6 billion, and then another 47% in Q3 to $17 billion.
- This growth is framed as strong even after the earlier “tokenmaxxing” craze had faded.
- The article argues that once the initial IPO excitement passes, questions will arise about whether Anthropic can maintain its current momentum.
- Customers are still working out the return on investment from AI, and budgets that were previously generous are starting to tighten.
- “Cost discipline” is identified as a new buzzword in AI, contrasted with the earlier phase dominated by runaway token spending.
- Anthropic faces competition from multiple directions, including open-weight models that customers can run themselves.
- It also must contend with inexpensive, standard models from companies like Meta, which are described as good enough to accomplish many tasks.
- Model routers that dynamically shift workloads away from the most advanced (frontier) models are highlighted as another competitive threat.
- Beyond technical and market competition, public opposition to AI and to the data centers that power it is flagged as an additional challenge.
- The article notes that both SpaceX and Anthropic have gone public with valuations exceeding $1 trillion.
- It argues that when companies wait so long to IPO, most of the financial upside accrues in private markets, leaving retail investors with limited access to wealth creation.
- This pattern is described as a shame and as something likely to intensify criticism of AI’s role in worsening income inequality.
- Anthropic is portrayed as having long treated safety as a central guiding principle.
- The move into public markets is expected to test that stance, given investors’ stronger focus on quarterly revenue and profit compared with venture capitalists’ multi-year horizons.
- The article suggests that tension between safety and revenue will become especially difficult as models grow more capable and the tradeoffs become sharper.
