The article argues that remote teams often overuse real-time communication and create pressure to reply immediately. That pace interrupts focus, increases stress, and makes it harder to do meaningful work.

It presents asynchronous communication as a better default for remote collaboration. In that model, people reply in hours rather than minutes, plan ahead, document key discussions in writing, and protect uninterrupted time for deep work.

## Reading notes

- Many companies that went remote responded by increasing video calls and chat, with an expectation of fast replies.
- Synchronous communication keeps people tied to team tools and makes it harder to get work done.
- Async communication means sending messages without expecting an immediate response.
- Real-time communication raises collaboration time, interruptions, and context-switching.
- Constant availability encourages people to stay connected instead of being productive.
- Workers react to interruptions with more stress, frustration, time pressure, and effort.
- Immediate replies can lower the quality of discussion because people do not have time to think.
- Mobile technology extends real-time work into nights, weekends, and vacations.
- Async workplaces give employees more control over their schedules and make it easier to fit work around personal responsibilities.
- Writing becomes more central, which improves clarity and leaves a record of important discussions.
- Advanced planning becomes necessary, which reduces last-minute requests and stress.
- Teams can batch messages and protect long periods for uninterrupted work.
- Real-time communication should remain limited to face-to-face connection, video meetings, and informal group chats.
- Managers should set expectations in hours, not minutes, and model the same behavior they want from the team.
