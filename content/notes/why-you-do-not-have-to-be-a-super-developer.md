The post argues that trying to become a “super developer” can lead to burnout rather than success. It says hard work matters, but it is only one part of a larger mix that also includes rest, exercise, healthy eating, sleep, and time off.

- The article begins from tweets telling people in tech they do not need to listen to podcasts all day, code for long hours before and after work, or treat success as constant productivity.
- It criticizes “motivational gurus” who promote working 24/7 and quoting high-output figures as a standard to follow.
- The author says that following that mindset led to the worst burnout and anxiety of his life.
- He describes learning that being the best developer is not the point, and that being a good developer is enough.
- Success is presented as a combination of hard work, time off, exercising, healthy eating, good sleep, and having fun.
- The text warns that beginners are especially vulnerable to this pressure because they may feel far behind everyone else.
