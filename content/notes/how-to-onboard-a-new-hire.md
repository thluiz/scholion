The post describes how Claire Lew approached onboarding a new hire at Know Your Team. She draws on manager advice and the company’s own experience to make day one deliberate, with context, expectations, encouragement, and room for trust.

## Reading notes

- New employees may leave within the first 90 days, so onboarding should be intentional.
- Managers commonly recommend mentorship, weekly one-on-ones, basic setup help, and a clear first project.
- The day-one welcome note shared business context, why the person was hired, and what success should look like.
- Lew also sent documents on company history, purpose, values, how the team works, business context, and upcoming milestones.
- A 23-question survey asked about work style, feedback preferences, conflict, productivity, motivation, strengths, and weaknesses.
- After the survey, she shared the team’s answers and avoided reading the new hire’s responses beforehand.
- The note also listed potential projects for the first six months and Lew’s own onboarding tasks.
- She wanted the new hire to ask questions, notice what felt strange, and offer fresh eyes on the company’s routines.
