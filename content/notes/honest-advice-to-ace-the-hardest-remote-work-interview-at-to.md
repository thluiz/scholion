The post describes Toptal’s screening as exhausting and says it is worth preparing for. It recommends learning what the process looks like for your role, practicing English communication, studying algorithms, and using the final project to show role-specific experience.

## Reading notes

- Toptal is presented as a remote work marketplace with a very selective application process and higher rates than other platforms.
- The first recommendation is to research interview stories for the specific role and, if needed, compare them with similar processes at other big tech companies.
- The language and personality interview filters for clear English communication, so the advice is to speak clearly, not rush, practice speaking regularly, and prepare a friendly interview setup.
- For software developers, the technical screening is framed as an algorithm interview that requires extensive practice.
- The suggested preparation includes reading Cracking the Coding Interview, using LeetCode and Hackerrank, and spending two hours a day for two months solving algorithms.
- The post suggests focusing on easy problems, especially strings, sorting, search, and some recursion, rather than graphs, linked lists, or trees.
- It names Repeated String and Minimum Distances as examples of the kind of problems expected.
- The final stage is a project over two weeks that should match the role, such as UI/UX sketches, a full-stack app, a machine learning project, or a product roadmap.
- The final interview is meant to test the candidate’s expertise through questions about the project.
- The author says the same preparation helped with an Amazon offer and argues that this level of work can qualify someone for other remote marketplaces too.
