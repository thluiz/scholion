Claire Lew says the manager’s role is to create the conditions for people’s intrinsic motivation and talent to show up. She frames leadership as a work in progress and lists habits that help a team work well: clear expectations, enough context, psychological safety, meaningful questions, quick responses, and fair treatment.

## Reading notes

- The role of a manager is not to control people, but to create an environment where their inherent qualities can come to light.
- Clarity means people know what needs to happen, why the work matters, what success looks like, and what quality standard is expected.
- Context includes training, background, tools, and enough understanding of stakeholders for informed decisions.
- Psychological safety depends on leaders going first, showing vulnerability, and responding well when people point out mistakes.
- Asking meaningful questions shows care and can surface information a manager would otherwise miss.
- Responding within 24 hours matters because team engagement is tied to how quickly managers answer ideas, comments, and requests.
- Managers should let go instead of micromanaging and allow others to do work well enough without taking it back.
- Leading from the front means modeling the behavior you want from the team.
- Consistency matters because uneven rules and exceptions create unfairness and set bad precedents.
- Building rapport helps people trust a manager by seeing them as a real person, not only as a boss.
