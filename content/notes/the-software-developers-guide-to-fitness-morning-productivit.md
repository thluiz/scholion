A morning workout is presented as a productivity habit for software developers and other people who sit most of the day. The author says exercising before work helps him arrive awake, fit tasks into a morning window with few meetings, and keep a routine that is easier to maintain than changing workout times from day to day.

## Reading notes

- The author says working 7 hours and fitting in 30 to 40 minutes of exercise can lead to more getting done than working 8 hours without exercise.
- Moving close to work gave him less commuting time and more relaxed mornings with family plus a quick workout.
- He says morning exercise helps him show up awake, ready to work, and make better use of a meeting-free part of the day.
- He links regular exercise with better energy, confidence, stronger core muscles for sitting all day, and the habit of setting and reaching goals over time.
- He says people changing jobs should remember that first impressions matter in interviews.
- He recommends exercising at a time you can keep consistently rather than changing the schedule every day.
- He suggests short, high-intensity workouts, keeping the heart rate in the target zone for 20 to 30 minutes, and using circuits with little rest.
- He prefers exercises that work large muscle groups, such as pushups or burpees.
- His own routine is running 1 to 2 miles and then doing three exercises in a circuit for upper body, lower body, and core.
- He says protein in the morning helped when he started getting tired before lunch after exercise, so he began drinking a fruit and protein shake with yogurt or milk.
- He says protein helps rebuild muscles after a workout and can provide longer-lasting energy than fat or carbs.
