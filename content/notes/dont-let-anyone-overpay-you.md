Basecamp says its pricing and customer choices are tied to the kind of company it wants to be. It built software for small companies like its own, kept prices within set tiers, and refused to let any customer pay more than the top tier, even for large enterprise demands.

The article argues that accepting much larger payments would force Basecamp to build sales, account management, training, and customization around those customers. It says a broad base of small customers is safer than relying on a few large accounts, because it reduces vulnerability and keeps the product focused on small businesses.

## Reading notes

- Basecamp built the product for small companies and kept that focus over time.
- Its original pricing model used three monthly tiers, and it would not accept payments above the top tier.
- The company says its refusal to take oversized deals helped shape the business in a positive way.
- Accepting a customer whose payment is far larger than the rest would require more staff and support infrastructure.
- Basecamp prefers many customers paying small amounts over a few large accounts paying much more.
- A diverse customer base is presented as protection against the demands or loss of big clients.
- The company says it does not want to become a two-headed business serving small and enterprise customers at the same time.
- It focuses on the large number of companies with fewer than 100 employees, especially those with fewer than 20.
