The article compares distributed hiring to “moneyball”: companies can gain an edge by looking beyond local talent pools and treating remote work as a source of high-quality talent. It says the main objections to distributed teams are quality, visibility, and Agile orthodoxy, and argues that better collaboration tools have changed the conditions behind those concerns.

## Reading notes

- Distributed teams let organizations hire remote employees and reach talent that old assumptions would miss.
- Remote workers in the cited survey were happier, felt more valued, and were more productive than on-location peers.
- Companies that allow remote work are associated with lower stress, greater efficiency, and lower turnover.
- The article’s five recommendations are hiring for remote compatibility, creating a sandbox for management, tracking outcomes instead of output, using the right tools, and embracing ceremonies.
- Remote-compatible hires should be proactive, good at prioritizing, and strong writers.
- Management should define clear boundaries, tools, frameworks, and expectations while leaving room for creativity inside them.
- Productivity should be measured by outcomes such as software shipped, stable velocity, and continuous improvement.
- The suggested tools are real-time chat, information radiators, and video conferencing.
- Team ceremonies should be scheduled so people in different locations can participate, and teams should be allowed to define workable times.
- The conclusion says many organizations already depend on distributed development through open-source software and should question the limits of hiring only from local talent pools.
