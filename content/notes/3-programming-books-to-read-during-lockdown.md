## Reading notes

- "Automate the Boring Stuff with Python" is presented as the most practical first book for beginners, especially for learning Python, automation, and basic programming through real day-to-day problems.
- The book is described as moving from basic logic to web scraping, and as a good choice for people who want to pivot into programming from office work.
- "Introduction to Algorithms" by Cormen is presented as a comprehensive computer science reference for data structures and algorithms, useful for exams, interviews, and deeper study.
- The book is said to cover algorithmic complexity, sorting, graphs, hash tables, dynamic programming, greedy algorithms, and divide-and-conquer with mathematical rigor.
- "Cracking the Code Interview" is presented as the most practice-oriented book, aimed at building interview skill through many exercises and problem categories.
- The post says CTCI helps with soft interview aspects too, but mainly values it for hands-on problem solving and practice with coding questions.
- The conclusion groups the three books into beginner, theoretical, and practical recommendations for developers at different stages.
