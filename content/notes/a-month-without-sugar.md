Leo Babauta says life felt just as sweet without sugar, and that giving it up was easier than he expected after months of cutting back. He links the challenge to a broader Year of Living Without and says his resistance is often stronger when he imagines losing something than when he actually goes without it.

## Reading notes

- Giving up sugar in October was fairly easy because he had already been mostly avoiding it for the previous four months.
- He says life was still fun, enjoyable, and lovely without sugar, and not as lifeless as he had expected.
- He thinks resistance shows up more in anticipation than in actual experience.
- Giving up coffee was easier than expected, and going without TV was mostly not missed.
- Not sitting for more than 30 minutes was hard because of physical tiredness, not because he missed sitting.
- Travel in Europe brought some temptation from vegan gelato and other vegan desserts, but most desserts were not vegan.
- Many days brought no sweet temptations because the available sweets were non-vegan.
- Staying home or going out only for exercise or tea reduced temptation.
- Vegan restaurants were a little harder because of vegan desserts, but the no-sugar challenge made it easier to refuse.
- Travel in China was also not very hard because many treats were things he could not eat.
- He says thinking about sugar harming his body makes it less appealing.
- Telling people about the no-sugar challenge helped him stick to it.
- Being busy reduced thoughts about sweets, while tiredness and hunger made temptation harder.
- For November, he plans to avoid computers and the internet before noon, except for writing.
- He will read a novel and write posts or work on his novel instead.
- He missed the first day because he forgot, and on the second day he wrote down things he wanted to look up later.
