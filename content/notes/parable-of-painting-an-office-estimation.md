The author presents a non‑technical parable about estimating work using the example of painting a small office. At first, the reader is asked to estimate painting time based only on room size and desired color. The story then reveals overlooked tasks one by one: buying supplies, moving furniture, taping and covering surfaces, painting the ceiling, and applying and waiting for a second coat. Each missed step forces a revision of the original estimate.

The moral is that people usually estimate work at too coarse a level, ignoring many required steps. Even with careful effort, estimates for individual tasks can be wrong, and omitting entire activities has a much bigger impact on delivery time. If a simple painting job hides so many steps, a large, interconnected software system can hide many more. Breaking work into smaller pieces reduces the chance of missing steps, but "small enough" is far smaller than most assume, and estimation still fails in many ways. For software projects, the author concludes that estimation is such a hazardous game that the safest approach is to avoid playing it at all.
