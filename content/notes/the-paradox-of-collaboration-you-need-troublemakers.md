The post argues that external demands often get handled from inside one silo, which leads teams to protect their own area instead of weighing the company’s wider interests. It says collaboration works better when someone challenges narrow expertise and pushes people to consider the full context.

The author presents troublemakers as people who ask uncomfortable questions, pressure others to explain their decisions, and create productive friction without damaging relationships. The point is that strong collaboration needs people willing to disrupt safe consensus when it blocks better decisions.

## Reading notes

- External stakeholders can place unreasonable demands on a team, and internal groups may defend their own lane instead of the company’s broader interest.
- Asking legal detailed questions can expose missing context and test whether a requested change is actually best for the company.
- People often dislike those questions because they feel challenged, but the goal is to understand options and reach a better decision.
- Collaboration suffers when everyone cares only about a silo, because the work turns into low-value side issues that ignore business impact.
- The article argues that the best collaboration requires troublemakers who create productive friction and challenge the status quo.
- Troublemakers should broaden perspective, not simply provoke conflict, and they should preserve relationships while still pushing for the right long-term outcome.
