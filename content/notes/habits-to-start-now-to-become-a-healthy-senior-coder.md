Kate Gregory’s talk on “The Aging Programmer” lays out habits and attitudes she says can support a healthier old age. She draws on research, survey results, and her own experience as a melanoma survivor to urge exercise, hearing and eye care, sunscreen, moderation with alcohol, and not overworking.

She also frames aging as something shaped by resources, relationships, and mindset. People lose things as they age, but can also gain time, money, confidence, and new friends, and she argues that optimism and treating healthy aging as a learned skill can make a measurable difference.

## Reading notes

- Gregory presents healthy aging as something programmers can start practicing now, not only later in life.
- She recommends exercise, bone-strengthening activity, hearing protection, eye exams, sunscreen, hats, and moderation with alcohol.
- She warns against smoking and overworking, and says there is value in not spending too much time at the office.
- She says money, skills, reliable people, and physical and emotional strength can help when life changes unexpectedly.
- She describes loss as unavoidable, including company closures, friends moving away or dying, and physical limits.
- She says the only cure for loss is gain, and that new experiences and new friends can bring gains.
- She lists some advantages of aging, including more time and money, less fear, and less power held by other people.
- She says age discrimination is real, but consulting can turn age into an advantage because experience becomes a selling point.
- She argues that how people think about aging affects how they age, and that optimism can reduce stress.
- She closes by saying it is not too soon, and also not too late, to start exercising, making friends, and finding purpose.
