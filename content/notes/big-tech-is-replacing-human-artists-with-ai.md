The text says AI-generated art and writing are moving quickly toward human-level output, while the cost of training large models will put the most capable systems under corporate control. It treats this as a coming shift in which Big Tech will own the main tools for creative work.

It also argues that AI creativity lacks consciousness and intentionality, so it represents a loss of meaning as well as a technical advance. The author connects that loss to what he calls extrinsic drift, then extends the concern to regulation, human dignity, and the way companies mediate access to these systems.
