Leo Babauta says distraction often goes unnoticed until it has already taken over. He describes his own scattered day, then lays out a response based on awareness, asking what is driving the urge, and cutting back on sources of interruption so he can return to writing, meditation, and what matters most.

## Reading notes

- Notice distraction by tracking how often you check sites, phone, and screens, so there is a small gap between urge and action.
- Ask what is behind the urge. In his case, it can be fear, fantasy, or fear of missing out.
- Let go of fantasies that require total devotion and are unlikely to fit real life.
- Reduce interruption by closing browser tabs, bookmarking later reading, blocking distracting sites, and setting fixed times for email.
- Use movement, meditation, paper reading, no-wifi spaces, fewer apps, and screen-free meals to create attention.
- Decide what matters most, limit priorities to a small number, and give those things full attention.
- Sitting still and paying attention to the world can restore appreciation for life and weaken the pull of distraction.
