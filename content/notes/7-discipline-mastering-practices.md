Babauta argues that mastering discipline uses the same ingredients as mastering a craft: repeated practice, single-minded devotion to the purpose, continual learning, and care. He then lists practices that train people to act despite discomfort, keep showing up, and find value in the activity itself.

## Reading notes

- Do the task even when not in the mood, instead of waiting for the feeling to change.
- Exercise even when you really do not want to, treating it as basic care for the body.
- Sit with a little hunger to see that discomfort does not have to lead to immediate eating.
- Talk to someone about something uncomfortable in a gentle, compassionate way and look for a solution that works for both people.
- Stick to one small habit for two months, do it five minutes a day, and track it on a calendar or log.
- Turn toward the problem instead of avoiding it, and use the obstacle as the path.
- See the good in the activity itself so the activity becomes the reward.
- Meditate for two minutes by sitting still, focusing on the breath, and noticing urges without acting on them.
