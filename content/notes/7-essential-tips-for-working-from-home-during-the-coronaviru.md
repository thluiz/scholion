The article explains that working from home during the coronavirus pandemic can be harder because it is sudden, lasts longer than a typical day at home, and can blur the line between work and personal life. It recommends routines that help people stay productive and protect their mental well-being.

## Reading notes

- Dress for the day, even if the clothing is less formal than office wear, because changing clothes helps signal that work has started.
- Take care of appearance-related routines such as showering and brushing your hair, since they can support a sense of self-care.
- Set up a specific workspace at home, even if it is only a corner, so work feels separate from the rest of the house.
- Make the workspace comfortable, use natural light if possible, and avoid spreading work into places used for downtime.
- Pack up work at the end of the day, or close job-related tabs and programs, so the workday has a clear finish.
- Keep regular working hours and communicate those boundaries to people you live with so distractions stay lower.
- Add transition routines at the start and end of the day, such as reading, music, a workout, a walk, or time with a pet.
- Limit distractions like news, set timers for breaks, and turn off notifications if constant updates pull attention away from work.
- Communicate often with managers, direct reports, and coworkers, and use phone or video when face-to-face conversation would normally happen.
- Keep up casual social contact with coworkers and friends through chats, calls, video, or shared activities so isolation does not build up.
