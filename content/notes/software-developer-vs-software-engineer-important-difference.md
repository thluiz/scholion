The page compares software developers and software engineers and says the two roles are often confused, even though they differ in scope, responsibilities, work style, and pay. It also says demand for both roles is rising and that the distinction matters for candidates choosing a career path.

## Reading notes

- Software engineers work more broadly, including architecture, construction, testing, and systems work, while software developers build, revise, fix, document, and test applications before release.
- The page says software developers need knowledge of java, ASP.net, Python, and C# to stay relevant.
- Reported median annual base salary is $74,136 for software developers and $89,086 for software engineers.
- The page says salary depends on employer, industry, experience, education, and location.
- Software engineers need a broader skill set, including best-practice engineering principles, communication with stakeholders, analysis, assessment, and scaling.
- The page says software developers often work more independently, while software engineers are expected to collaborate closely with teams and end users.
- It says a Bachelor's degree in computer science, information technology, software engineering, or a similar field is the minimum expected credential.
- Certification programs can add knowledge and may be required for certain jobs, promotions, or projects.
- The page says software developers often follow more varied career paths, while software engineers usually follow a more linear path toward leadership.
- It says software developers may move from junior to senior developer and later into lead developer, systems architect, or management roles.
- It says software engineers may move from entry-level roles to senior engineer, tech lead, engineering manager, VP, and even CTO.
- The page concludes that both roles are in high demand, share some traits and technical overlap, but software engineers carry more responsibility and need a broader, more specific skill set.
