Marc Andreessen says the old claim that software is eating the world has been borne out, with technology’s share of the market capitalization among the ten largest businesses rising from 31.5% to 94.4% over fifteen years. He argues that software is now moving from the speed of human hands to the speed of compute.

He presents Devin as a sign of that shift, saying coding agents barely existed three years ago and now write most code at leading companies. In his view, that raises engineer leverage from 10x to 100x and eventually 1000x, because engineers move from writing code by hand to directing fleets of agents. He cites examples at Mercedes Benz, Rivian, and Itaú, then argues that every increase in leverage expands demand for software and engineers rather than shrinking it. He frames Cognition as part of a broader thesis that small technical teams with powerful tools can transform large markets.
