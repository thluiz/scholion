The article says burnout is common in programming and tries to explain why it happens. It points to physical strain from sitting all day, the mental load of the work, unrewarding tasks, and repeated failure as factors that can push programmers toward burnout.

It then lists habits and changes meant to reduce that risk. The advice includes eating and sleeping well, avoiding overwork, using Pomodoro breaks, staying active, experimenting with technology, meeting other developers, improving the work environment and tools, keeping other interests outside programming, changing jobs or projects when needed, and keeping routine tasks in the daily workflow.

## Reading notes

- Burnout is described as common in programming and in the IT industry.
- Physical inactivity, especially sitting at a desk all day, is presented as one cause.
- The work is described as cognitively intensive and stressful, with mental fatigue as another cause.
- Some burnout comes from work that feels unrewarding and soul-sucking.
- Repeated failure in high-risk work is presented as another source of burnout.
- Basic habits recommended include eating well, sleeping well, not overworking, using Pomodoro cycles, and staying active.
- Programmer-specific advice includes experimenting with technology, learning new things, and dedicating time to playful prototyping.
- The article recommends meetups, conferences, and podcasts to reduce loneliness and stay mindful about the job.
- It advises investing in a good workspace, good tools, and efficient workflows.
- Taking breaks and keeping interests outside programming is presented as a way to avoid monotony and recover motivation.
- Changing jobs, projects, or even fields is suggested when the current work no longer fits interests or values.
- Routine tasks such as testing, comments, and naming improvements are presented as a way to restore a more positive feeling about work.
