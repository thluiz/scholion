The essay argues that remote work has real conveniences, but for many workers it also lowers bargaining power, makes contributions easier to overlook, weakens mentorship, and can slow career growth. It also says large companies may adapt less well when strategy changes, and that remote-first arrangements can benefit tech companies more than workers.

## Reading notes

- Remote work is presented as a change that can expand access to jobs, but also as one that can push American tech workers into global wage competition.
- The author says remote work can make workers easier to outsource and can reduce the value of soft skills, creativity, and collaboration.
- In remote settings, managers may see output without enough context to judge process, teamwork, or effort, which can flatten how work is evaluated.
- Large companies may struggle to change course when markets shift, and remote work can make that harder.
- Remote work is said to limit mentorship, informal learning, and the professional exposure that matters early in a career.
- The essay says many remote-work advocates have incentives tied to tech companies, collaboration tools, or other businesses that profit from dispersion.
- It argues that remote work shifts spending toward companies like Slack and Zoom while reducing support for local businesses and in-person routines.
- The author says advocates often assume workers have the same home conditions, space, and childcare, which is not true for many people.
- The proposed alternative is localism, flexible work instead of remote-only work, and remote work as an option rather than a requirement.
