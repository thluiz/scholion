The post says some problems can be judged in a binary way, but many product-development problems are too hazy for that. In those cases, aiming to solve everything for everyone is often impossible, so treating the issue as a simple yes-or-no question becomes harmful.

It proposes a more practical question for new initiatives: whether they make things better. That shift lowers the pressure to be the one final solution and makes agreement easier, because people can compare one real state with another.

## Reading notes

- Some problems fit a binary frame, but many product-development problems do not.
- Trying to solve hazy problems as if they were binary can be futile and harmful.
- Questions like getting started with Basecamp or the readability of the Active Record query interface are examples of hazy problems.
- A better test is whether an idea makes things better.
- This framing makes new initiatives easier to discuss because they do not have to be The Solution.
- Consensus is easier when people compare whether A is better than B.
