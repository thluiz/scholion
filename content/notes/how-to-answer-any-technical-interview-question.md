The article argues that technical interview answers should show how you think, not just whether you can guess the final result. It recommends honesty when you do not know something, asking clarifying questions, using examples, starting with the simplest workable solution, and then refining it.

It also says to keep iterating if you get stuck, stay calm under pressure, and talk the interviewer through your reasoning. If you do not finish, you can return to the problem after the interview and send a better answer later.

## Reading notes

- Technical interviews often test reasoning, communication, and problem-solving, not only correctness.
- Bluffing is usually a poor choice; it is better to be honest about what you know and do not know.
- If a question is unclear, ask questions until you understand the problem well enough to solve it.
- Use simple examples to find patterns and avoid getting misled by edge cases too early.
- Start with the most obvious solution if it gives you a baseline to build on.
- Look for ways to improve the first solution, including reducing repeated work or using other available information.
- If you are stuck, work through more examples and compare the problem with ones you have solved before.
- Keep going even when you do not have an ideal answer, because calmness and resourcefulness matter.
- Explain your reasoning after reaching a solution and think about how you would verify it in a real job.
- If you do not finish, write the problem down and revisit it after the interview.
