The post argues that many quotas measure the wrong thing. Examples include minimum hours worked, lines of code, and arrest counts, all of which can miss the value the system actually wants.

It says quotas can be useful only when they move a system closer to the desired outcome without creating serious unintended consequences. Otherwise, they reward people for gaming the metric instead of solving the underlying problem.

## Reading notes

- Minimum hours, lines of code, and arrest counts are treated as poor quotas because they track activity or symptoms rather than value.
- The Tesla and Ford anecdote is used to show that a quick act can be worth far more than the time spent doing it.
- The police funding example shows how arrest-based incentives can push attention toward numbers instead of the desired outcome.
- The post leaves open the possibility of a good quota when the gap to the goal is large and the quota creates small, useful gains.
- Quotas have to avoid the law of unintended consequences, or they can leave the system worse than before.
