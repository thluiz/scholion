The essay says that sharing opinions online can bring too much downside, because even true things may be twisted to seem controversial. It argues that this makes successful people especially likely targets, and that keeping useful insights private also keeps them inside insider circles.

It also says that public friction cuts off the discussion ideas might generate. The author hopes social media will move toward a version that lets people speak more openly, because the current cost of speaking can keep the most insightful people quiet.

- Public posts can be risky because people may misread them or twist them on purpose.
- True things can become dangerous to share if they do not match what others want to believe.
- Useful startup insights are sometimes kept private for fear of public backlash.

- Silencing experienced people also keeps valuable information among insiders.

- The author prefers a small set of trusted friends for open discussion.
