Spheres play a central role in contact geometry because contact structures on them can be transferred to other manifolds by cutting and gluing. Recent work by Bowden, Gironella, Moreno and Zhou identifies a new type of contact sphere and uses it to produce infinitely many new contact manifolds.

## Reading notes

- Spheres are useful test cases because results on them can often be carried over to more complex manifolds by sewing the shapes together.
- In contact geometry, a manifold together with a contact structure is called a contact manifold.
- Contact structures are linked to physics, including reformulations of theories about light and water flow.
- When a contact sphere is glued onto another contact manifold, parts of its contact structure can be donated to the combined shape.
- Mathematicians often reduce questions about contact structures on other manifolds to questions about the sphere.
- Bowden, Gironella, Moreno and Zhou found a new type of contact sphere in a recent paper and with it an infinite number of new contact manifolds.
- Contact geometry developed gradually, with early hints in optics and thermodynamics and the phrase “contact manifold” appearing in the 1950s.
- In the late 1960s and 1970s, Gromov, Martinet and Lutz produced new examples of contact manifolds, including a construction that changed the contact structure while keeping the underlying shape the same.
- Eliashberg later described a class of manifolds called overtwisted, whose contact planes rotate more than required, and showed that they are comparatively well understood.
- Tight contact manifolds, which twist less, are described as rarer and much less understood.
