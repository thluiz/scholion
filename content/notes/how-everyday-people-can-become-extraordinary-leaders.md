Jim Kouzes and Barry Posner argue that ordinary people can lead by choosing behaviors that build a shared vision, earn trust, and help others work toward a common purpose. The article presents leadership as something grounded in relationships, not in title or status.

## Reading notes

- Great leaders do not begin by trying to become leaders; they begin by trying to make a difference.
- Focused listening helps create a shared vision by aligning people’s interests, hopes, and fears with a larger purpose.
- Challenging the process means looking for opportunities to improve, experimenting, taking risks, and learning from mistakes.
- Challenging the status quo can show respect when it is framed around helping the team and organization serve its purpose better.
- Trust grows when leaders make the first move, learn about others, share about themselves, and show concern for other people’s interests.
- Accountability in peer leadership depends on personal responsibility and on making clear that each person’s work matters to others.
- After-action reviews help teams learn by examining what was supposed to happen, what happened, what worked, what did not, and how to improve.
- Encouraging the heart means recognizing contributions, showing appreciation, celebrating victories, and reinforcing that people matter.
- The article says the content of leadership has changed little, but the context has changed a lot, especially during Covid-19.
- The authors say leadership is within reach for most people, and that more extraordinary leadership comes from choosing exemplary behavior more often.
