The article describes impostor syndrome as a distorted way of judging your own abilities, where achievements are credited to luck and competence is doubted despite evidence. This mindset leads people to avoid opportunities, hold back ideas, and limit their potential, which also deprives teams of their contributions. It then outlines seven main strategies and several quick tips to recognize impostor thoughts, change self-talk, respond differently to praise and failure, and lean into learning so confidence can grow.

## Reading notes

- Impostor syndrome shows up as an inner voice insisting you don’t belong, you’re a fraud, and others will eventually discover it.
- People experiencing it believe their success comes from luck rather than skill, telling themselves they’re unqualified even when evidence shows they’re capable.
- The article notes that impostor feelings are paradoxically more frequent among people who are highly intelligent and successful.
- Impostor syndrome is described as different from simple self-doubt; it is an inability to judge your own abilities accurately, especially compared with others.
- This skewed self-assessment causes people to limit themselves, avoid projects or roles they could handle, and stay silent in discussions out of fear of exposure.
- By holding back, individuals not only restrict their own growth but also withhold useful perspectives and contributions that teams and businesses need.

- Realizing many others share these doubts can make impostor feelings seem less personal and powerful.
- Recognizing impostor syndrome as something that affects you is presented as a first step to adjusting your mindset.
- The article suggests replacing thoughts that dismiss your accomplishments as luck with an acknowledgment that you struggle to see your own abilities clearly.
- It emphasizes that impostor syndrome reflects difficulty internalizing success, not an actual inability to achieve it.
- Self-talk habits are portrayed as central to impostor syndrome, with the idea that negative inner commentary can be changed like any other habit.
