The piece says promotion to Staff comes from sustained, measurable impact that the company recognizes. It explains that higher-level ICs get leverage through people, writing, and code, and that engineers should choose the kind of leverage that fits their strengths while making their work visible inside the company.

It also stresses collaboration, transparency with managers, and protecting focus time from inefficient meetings. Hard work matters, but the bigger factor is choosing work with more impact, building an internal reputation, and doing work you can sustain for a long time.

## Reading notes

- Focus on measurable impact that benefits the company, and learn what your organization treats as impactful.
- Promotions lag behind performance, so you need to operate at the next level for some time before getting promoted.
- Higher-level ICs rely on leverage through people, writing, or code.
- People leverage comes from technical leadership, including setting direction, reviewing designs and code, and growing others.
- Writing creates leverage by influencing and helping others without your active involvement.
- High-leverage code solves harder problems or helps engineers move faster at scale.
- Tech leads and specialists can get leverage from different places, and both can do excellent work.
- Quality matters, but work also has to be known for it to lead to promotion.
- Internal brand matters more than external social media presence.
- Collaboration, mentorship, and transparency with your manager help you grow.
- Soft skills help engineers stand out and lead.
- Protect focus time and push back on inefficient meetings and processes.
- What you work on matters more than how hard you work.
- Follow curiosity and work on things you enjoy so long hours feel sustainable.
