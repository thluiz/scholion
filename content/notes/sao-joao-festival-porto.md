São João do Porto is described as Porto’s biggest party of the year, held on June 23. The page says it combines Christian roots with older pagan traditions and treats the festival as a citywide celebration centered on the streets, the river, food, music, and fireworks.

## Reading notes

- São João do Porto is presented as the most special event of the year in Porto, with the whole city going out to celebrate.
- The text says the festival has Christian roots and also includes pagan traditions linked to the summer solstice.
- Festivities take place in the streets through small neighborhood parties called festarolas.
- Common food mentioned for the celebration includes sardines, meats, roasted peppers, wine, and barbecue.
- Manjericos are described as decorated basil plants with small messages of love, given as a tradition.
- Paper lanterns are released into the night sky and reflect on the Douro River.
- Martelos de São João are plastic toy hammers used to lightly tap people on the head during the celebration.
- Bonfires are part of the festarolas, and people jump over them for luck and protection.
- Rabelo boats and party boats light up the Douro during the night.
- Fireworks at midnight are described as the main moment of the celebration.
- Cats Porto says it hosts its own São João rooftop party with barbecue, wine, beer, music, and a decorated rooftop.
- After the fireworks, the page says the party continues in the streets and bars until sunrise, with Foz Beach suggested as a place to watch the sun come up.
