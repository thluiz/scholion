The post defines a resume as a short form of a CV and a compact overview of professional or educational history. It argues that a strong resume helps employers judge your skills, experience, and fit for the role, and that the best structure depends on geography and industry.

## Reading notes

- A resume is presented as a summary of a CV, with recent professional highlights in short notes.
- The author uses the T-shirt example to show that employers need direct information, not only a verbal description.
- A strong resume should help an employer make a hiring decision based on skills, experience, academic background, and individual contribution.
- There is no single correct resume format for everyone.
- The right structure depends on target geography, industry, company culture, and other contextual factors.
- Photo use varies by region, with different expectations in South-East Asia, Europe, and the US.
- The same applies to industry choices, since different fields value different kinds of evidence and achievements.
- The post recommends including personal details, an objective or summary, skills, experience, academics, social and professional handles, certifications, projects, and relevant hobbies.
- Experience and academics should be in reverse chronological order, and experience entries should focus on outcomes rather than responsibilities.
- A resume should be short, usually one page, use bullet points, avoid unnecessary photographs, avoid irrelevant hobbies, and avoid grammatical mistakes.
- The post also mentions portfolio sites and professional networks as other ways to present skills and experience.
- It closes with examples of resumes and portfolio sites as references.
