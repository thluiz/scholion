The article argues that inspiration often comes from linking unlikely ideas, not from waiting for a sudden breakthrough. It presents three practical steps: train divergent thinking, capture and revisit ideas, and introduce unexpected experiences to stimulate new connections.

## Reading notes

- Creativity is described as connecting things and seeing something that later becomes obvious.
- Divergent thinking is presented as a way to make associations between unrelated concepts and generate multiple solutions.
- Synectics exercises ask people to compare two unrelated items and list as many connections as possible.
- SCAMPER is offered as a filter for reworking an existing idea through substitution, combination, adaptation, modification, other uses, elimination, and reversal.
- Convergent thinking is used to evaluate and refine options after divergent thinking has produced them.
- Ideas, thoughts, observations, notes, words, drawings, images, and photos should be captured instead of left in memory.
- An “Ideas” notebook in Evernote is suggested for collecting divergent ideas and reviewing them on a weekly basis.
- Tags can organize ideas by keywords such as categories, memories, or locations.
- Unexpected inputs can come from reading history, starting a new hobby, listening to music, or talking to someone new.
- The article says diverse experiences make innovation more likely.
- Team brainstorming can use brainwriting to build shared ideas instead of producing isolated ones.
- Variations mentioned include interactive brainwriting, 6-3-5 brainwriting, and collaborative brainwriting.
- A shared picture of collaborative brainwriting can be saved and reviewed later in Evernote.
