The page presents Creative Market as a source of downloadable textures and explains that textures can make digital art feel less stiff. It says textures may be scanned or photographed materials such as paper, wood, glitter, brick, or canvas, and that artists use them to add character and imperfections.

## Reading notes

- Creative Market offers handcrafted design content from independent creatives, including textures.
- Textures are usually flat photographic images or scans made from materials like noise, glitter, brick, wood, canvas, or paper.
- On Creative Market, textures often come in themed packs with five to 50 items.
- Digital art can look too perfect because software produces clean lines and crisp gradients.
- Adding texture is presented as one way to avoid a stiff look.
- In SketchBook, the texture is imported from the layers window and appears on a new top layer.
- After import, SketchBook switches to the Move Puck, which can be used to scale, skew, move, or pan the image.
- The texture can be moved in the layer stack, either below the artwork or above it.
- With the texture above the artwork, blending modes can change how it affects the layers below.
- The page compares Overlay, Multiply, and Hard Light as examples of blending modes.
- It says no single texture works for every image and encourages experimentation.
- It also mentions free textures through Creative Market’s Free Goods of the Week email and limited-time bundle deals.
