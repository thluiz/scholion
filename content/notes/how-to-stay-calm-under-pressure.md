The article says fear in high-pressure situations can push people into defensive words, premature decisions, or random fixes. It recommends the BAA framework: Breath, Assess, and Act. The goal is to pause, understand the situation, and respond clearly instead of reacting too fast.

## Reading notes

- When someone with authority asks a hard question, the first step is to take a deep breath and let the fear pass.
- In the VP example, the article says to check whether you understand the question, whether there is an assumption behind it, and whether you are hesitating to give a direct response.
- The response should start with a clear answer and a justification based on the previous review or the user study.
- It also suggests asking whether Feature X should be considered again so the other person can add new data points.
- In an outage, the article says to spend a minute understanding symptoms, impact, triggers, and what has already been ruled out.
- It advises saying what you know, what you do not know, and what theories the team will validate, while asking for help from the right team and giving regular updates.
- For critical feedback, the article says not to reject it immediately or accept it without understanding the specifics.
- A useful response is to say the feedback is a surprise and ask for the specific instance so the discussion can continue respectfully.
- The article ends by saying that practicing BAA helps people avoid rash decisions and become better leaders.
