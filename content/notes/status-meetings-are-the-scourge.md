The page argues that status meetings are a costly way to share updates because the time multiplies across everyone in the room, and the same information usually does not need to be heard by everyone at the exact same moment. It says written status reports are better because people can read them when they are ready, keep the conversation in context, and recover time for actual work.

## Reading notes

- Status meetings are presented as the worst kind of meeting because they waste money, time, and attention.
- Meeting time is multiplied by the number of people present, so a short meeting can still consume many collective work hours.
- Updating everyone right now is usually unnecessary because most status information does not depend on the exact moment it is shared.
- Forcing everyone to stop working at the same time is treated as a cost that is rarely justified by what is being said.
- The suggested alternative is to write status reports instead of saying them out loud.
- Basecamp posts these reports in a permanent place so everyone can see them when they are ready.
- Follow-up questions happen below the write-up, so the whole conversation stays together.
- The page says this approach saves dozens of hours a week and works especially well for remote teams and time zones that do not overlap.
- It describes asynchronous communication as the efficient option and real-time communication as the source of distraction.
- Basecamp 3’s Automatic Check-ins replace weekly status meetings and daily updates.
- One check-in asks what people worked on today at 5pm, and another asks what they plan to work on this week on Monday morning.
- Answers are visible to everyone in the company, and each person’s past answers can be reviewed on one page.
- The page recommends starting by eliminating one meeting a month, then moving further once the benefits become clear.
