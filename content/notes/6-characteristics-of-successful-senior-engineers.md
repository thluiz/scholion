The page argues that coding is only one part of software engineering and that successful senior engineers build habits that shape how they work. It groups those habits around understanding the problem, managing attention, documenting decisions, working across teams, asking better questions, and learning continuously.

## Reading notes

- Successful senior engineers first understand the full scope of a problem before looking for a solution.
- They identify business processes, people involved inside and outside the team, and ask project managers and technical BAs for the full picture.
- They prioritize tasks to avoid distractions, define goals, narrow their focus, and make time for the most important work.
- They take notes on the decision-making process, including approach, solution, considerations, roadblocks, dependencies, and follow-up items.
- They communicate that information to project participants and stakeholders so everyone stays aligned.
- They work well across functions by adapting how they communicate with designers and product managers.
- They look beyond the immediate project for opportunities for growth and impact.
- They ask “why” to understand the problem better and to find out what they do not know.
- They keep a growth mindset through constant learning, feedback, learning from failures, and introspection.
- They accept criticism without taking it personally.
- The conclusion says success takes years of hard work, dedication, and continuous learning.
