Robert Lustig argues that sugar should be treated like a controlled substance because people have a limited capacity to metabolize it, average consumption is far above the safe range, and the excess is linked to obesity, diabetes, heart disease, cancer, dementia, and fatty liver disease. He says sugar is addictive, its effects create tolerance and withdrawal, and its social costs justify regulation.

He also argues that sugar meets four criteria for regulation: it is ubiquitous, toxic, abused, and creates externalities. In his view, the practical response is to reduce availability through taxation or restrictions on access, not ban it outright. He adds that public support for such policies depends on tying the tax to visible benefits, like using revenue to improve water supplies or subsidize healthier food.

## Reading notes

- Sugar is now a staple, but human beings have a limited capacity to metabolize it.
- The safe average intake is six to nine teaspoons of added sugar per day, while Americans average 22.
- The excess is linked to obesity, diabetes, lipid problems, heart disease, cancer, dementia, and fatty liver disease.
- Lustig says sugar is addictive and produces tolerance and withdrawal.
- He says sugar meets four criteria for regulation: ubiquity, toxicity, abuse, and externalities.
- He argues that reducing consumption would require taxation or restriction of access.
- He says taxes are more acceptable when the revenue goes back to the community.
- He gives Mexico’s soda tax and water supply improvement as an example.
- He says sugar is a drug because its harmful effects are not just about calories.
- He compares sugar to alcohol and says it is “the alcohol of a child.”
- He says fructose leads to liver fat, insulin resistance, and diabetes.
- He says sugar speeds cellular aging and contributes to addiction.
