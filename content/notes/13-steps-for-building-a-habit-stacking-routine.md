The page explains that habit stacking works best when several small changes are treated as one routine instead of separate tasks. It recommends starting with very little time, using easy actions, and adding more only after the routine feels automatic.

## Reading notes

- Start with a five-minute block and keep the first version small enough to overcome inertia.
- Focus on small wins, with actions that take little effort and are easy to remember.
- Pick a time and location that can serve as a regular anchor for the routine.
- Use a trigger tied to an existing habit, a specific time, or a visual reminder.
- Build a logical checklist so the order, timing, and place of each action are clear.
- Add accountability through people, tools, or public progress updates.
- Give the routine a small reward that does not cancel the benefit of the habit.
- Repeat the routine until it becomes muscle memory.
- Do not break the chain, and avoid missing two days in a row.
- Expect setbacks and treat them as part of staying consistent.
- Schedule some stacks daily, weekly, or monthly depending on the task.
- Scale the routine up gradually, aiming for a longer sequence only after consistency.
- Build one routine at a time and add another only when the first feels permanent.
