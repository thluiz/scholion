Systems thinking is described as a holistic approach to understanding complex problems by examining how parts connect within a larger system. The page says it is both a mindset and a methodology, useful for messy challenges that need multiple solutions and stakeholders. It also argues that systems thinking works best when paired with human-centered design so the analysis stays connected to people, motivations, and unmet needs.

## Reading notes

- Systems thinking looks at how parts of a problem connect inside a larger system.
- It is presented as both a mindset and a methodology for ambiguous and messy challenges.
- The article links systems thinking to product and service design, public policy, urban planning, sustainability, healthcare, education, and organizational change.
- The text says systems thinking alone can stay abstract, so it should be combined with human-centered design.
- Traffic congestion is used to show how broader analysis can shift attention from more roads to public transit, biking infrastructure, traffic lights, and travel behavior.
- Reframing is treated as central because the first question shapes the solutions that seem possible.
- The article recommends starting with a question that is good enough, then refining it through experiments and learning.
- Three mindsets are introduced: zooming in and out, shifting perspective, and being aware of your own lens.
- Zooming in and out helps move between detail and the big picture without staying stuck in one scale.
- Systems mapping is presented as a tool for seeing connections and deciding where to look more closely.
- Shifting perspective helps reveal patterns and overlooked dynamics, and analogies are used as a tool for that practice.
- Being aware of your own lens means noticing how experience, identity, privilege, and role shape what you see and assume.
- Reflective questioning is recommended to surface assumptions and bias.
- In workplace practice, the page advises starting with champions, using small experiments, focusing on the sphere of influence, and leading with outcomes rather than process.
- The map is framed as a learning tool, not the solution itself.
