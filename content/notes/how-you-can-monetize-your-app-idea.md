The page argues that an app idea should be paired with a revenue model before development starts. It defines monetization as turning a product into a source of capital and then surveys the main ways apps earn money, from ads and subscriptions to sponsorship and white label sales.

## Reading notes

- The revenue model should be defined before focusing on engineering.
- Monetization is described as an exchange of goods or services for currency, with profit treated separately.
- Free apps make up most of the store content, and much of app revenue comes from free apps.
- Free apps can earn through ads, subscriptions, online sales, in-app purchases, user data, transaction fees, sponsorship, and white label sales.
- A monetization plan should start from the app idea, the target users, their willingness to pay, and the relation between revenue per user and marketing cost.
- The article recommends testing several hypotheses instead of committing all resources to one strategy.
- In-app advertising is presented as the cheapest and most popular model, especially when the app has many users.
- Ad revenue depends on ad units, views, and click rate, and ad networks can place ads without much developer effort.
- Freemium gives a limited free version and charges for the full package or ad-free use.
- The article says freemium works better than paid download for many apps, but only a small share of users pay for extras.
- Paid downloads need strong marketing, higher product quality, and competitive pricing.
- In-app purchases can cover extra content, features, virtual goods, levels, or media, and they are common in games and gamified apps.
- In-app sponsorship shifts costs to the sponsor, while the app works as a marketing tool for the sponsor.
- White label apps are sold as reusable templates under the buyer’s brand, especially for local businesses in standard sectors.
- Mixed monetization is presented as common, with ads and in-app purchases often combined in free apps.
