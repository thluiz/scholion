The author reflects on publishing 50 posts on Dev.to since February 2020 and sees the site as useful because it gives posts an existing audience. He also argues that the platform rewards recent publishing, so visibility often follows fresh activity more than older work.

## Reading notes

- The author says he started blogging on Dev.to in February 2020 and reached 50 posts in about half a year.
- He lists his metrics as 50 posts, 4,224 followers, 45,852 views, and 1,808 reactions.
- He estimates that each post took about two hours to write, for more than 100 hours of work overall.
- He sees Dev.to as a strong forum for dev writing because it can give a post several hundred or several thousand readers before it fades.
- He describes Dev.to as a churn engine that rewards new content and gives little attention to old posts.
- He says the follower count is mostly the result of frequent posting and of the site suggesting recent writers to new members.
- He says his views did not rise in a steady line and most posts fell between 250 and 1,000 views.
- He says there is basically no correlation between post length and popularity.
- He argues that quality matters more than length, and that weak posts get few readers even when they are short.
- He says he writes mainly for himself and treats the blogs as free self-administered therapy.
- He says he started blogging because, after more than 20 years in his career, he was tired of repeating the same lessons.
- He says collecting his recurring points in blog form helps him avoid repeating the same rants.
- He describes his posts as a personal reference he can send to coworkers when the same issues come up again.
