This guide collects leadership books the author says shaped his own leadership. It treats the books as tools for different needs, from trust and communication to motivation, emotional intelligence, management, and team culture.

## Reading notes

- Dale Carnegie’s book is presented as a classic for improving communication through empathy, listening, avoiding criticism, admitting mistakes, and helping others feel important.
- Stephen R. Covey’s book centers on character, principles, and the seven habits, with an emphasis on proactivity, priorities, synergy, and renewal.
- Atomic Habits is described as a practical framework for habit formation built on cues, cravings, responses, and rewards, with small changes leading to long-term results.
- Radical Candor is about giving honest feedback with care, combining direct challenge with personal concern in private, constructive conversations.
- Goleman’s leadership book argues that emotional intelligence shapes organizational effectiveness, engagement, innovation, and the ability to manage team emotions.
- High Output Management is framed as a practical guide to management, including meetings, feedback, decisions, leverage, and systems that raise team performance.
- Turn the Ship Around! presents servant leadership and distributed leadership, where team members think critically, make decisions, and take ownership.
- Extreme Ownership argues that leaders must take full responsibility, with no excuses, and that failures trace back to leadership.
- Leaders Eat Last links leadership to safety, loyalty, and performance by putting team needs first and creating circles of safety.
- Creativity, Inc. highlights failure, candid feedback, and psychological safety as conditions for creative work and open discussion.
- Emotional Intelligence presents self-awareness, social awareness, self-management, and relationship management as core leadership competencies.
- The Five Dysfunctions of a Team identifies trust, conflict, commitment, accountability, and results as linked problems that can cascade through a team.
- The book on nonviolent communication uses observation, feelings, needs, and requests to improve clarity, empathy, and conflict resolution.
- The First 90 Days and the listed manager books focus on the transition into management, including expectations, feedback, personnel decisions, and team culture.
- Drive argues that autonomy, ownership, purpose, and mastery motivate people more than external rewards alone.
- Good to Great emphasizes disciplined people, disciplined thought, disciplined action, Level 5 leadership, and the Hedgehog Concept.
- The additional recommendations point to leadership through multiplication, idea spread, deep focus, growth mindset, persuasion, human nature, decision-making, cross-cultural communication, and coaching through questions.
