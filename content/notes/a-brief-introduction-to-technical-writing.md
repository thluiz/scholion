Technical writing is presented as a practical kind of writing centered on users. The post says it covers instructional and informational documents, from software documentation to manuals, tutorials, guides, whitepapers, and online help, and that it belongs to the wider field of technical communication.

It also stresses that technical writing differs from marketing copy because it aims for precision and utility. Good documentation helps readers act quickly, while poor documentation slows them down. The writer also points out that technical writers work with document design, research, usability, visual design, instructional design, and editing.

## Reading notes

- Technical writing creates primarily text-based instructional or informational documents for users.
- In software, common forms include product and API documentation, manpages, tutorials, and guides.
- The field also includes manuals for consumer products, manufacturing, aerospace engineering, and medical technology.
- Data visualizations, whitepapers, knowledge bases, and online help articles can also count as technical writing in some contexts.
- Technical writing aims for precision and utility rather than persuasion.
- It should let readers find what they need quickly, especially when they are skimming under pressure.
- Vague, incomplete, or verbose troubleshooting steps make tasks slower and more frustrating.
- Technical documents should support the user and remove barriers to getting something done.
- Technical writing works best when the writer understands the subject matter, the reader, and the context of use.
- Technical writers also pay attention to document design, including layout, formatting, typography, color, and visual aids.
- Their work includes research methods, usability, visual design, instructional design, and editing.
- They need to know users, the scenarios that lead them to documentation, and how to ask subject matter experts for the right information.
