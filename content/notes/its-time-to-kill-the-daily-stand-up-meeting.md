The post argues that daily stand-up meetings often become bloated and unengaging as teams grow. It says the meeting works only when it stays limited to status and blockers, and that other goals should be handled elsewhere.

It also says the interruption cost is high, because it can take 23 minutes and 15 seconds to get back to work after a meeting. It adds that daily stand-ups leave no shared recorded history, which makes it harder to keep everyone aligned or onboard new people.

## Reading notes

- Daily stand-ups are meant to share progress and identify blockers, but many teams add morale, reflection, and connection to the same meeting.
- When a meeting tries to serve too many purposes, it becomes bloated and ineffective.
- A short stand-up can still cost more time than it appears to, because people need time to return to their work.
- The post cites a study from the University of California Irvine saying it takes 23 minutes and 15 seconds to get back to the task at hand.
- With 10 team members, the interruption can add up to 20 hours a week.
- In-person or Zoom stand-ups do not create a shared recorded history of progress.
- That lack of history becomes a problem when someone is sick, remote, or new to the team.
- The alternative suggested is to automate status updates with a tool.
- The suggested check-in asks what people worked on yesterday, what they will work on today, and what blockers they have.
- The post says other goals should move to monthly all-hands or weekly staff meetings.
- It names reflection, recognition, connection, and vision as separate functions worth handling in other meetings.
- The conclusion is that teams should stop keeping daily stand-ups just because they are already in use.
