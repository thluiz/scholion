The article says the hourly rate he offers programmers depends on signs of public work, self-motivation, and technical growth. Open source contribution, Stack Overflow activity, certifications, talks, and focused expertise raise the rate; location, years of experience, previous roles, and formal education matter much less.

## Reading notes

- Open source contribution is the main indicator, because it shows code quality, willingness to accept feedback, passion, and creativity.
- A GitHub account with visible projects can justify a higher rate; an empty account quickly reduces interest.
- Living in an expensive country should not be a reason for a higher rate, because the choice of place to live is personal.
- Stack Overflow activity matters because it shows the ability to ask precise questions and find answers in public.
- If a developer never asks or answers there, the author reads that as stagnation or limited technical depth.
- Years of experience alone mean little, because they may only show time spent in offices.
- Certifications are a positive sign because they show willingness to face difficult exams and invest in the profile.
- Broad skill variety lowers price, while narrow expertise in scarce technologies can raise it.
- Blogs, conference talks, patents, and books increase value because they show that other people have reviewed the work and that the developer can explain ideas clearly.
- Previous employment and job titles matter little compared with what was actually done.
- Education matters mainly when the school is highly selective; otherwise the author gives it little weight.
- The article groups programmers into rate bands from $100+ down to $15, treating the rate as a measure of professional level, not personal worth.
