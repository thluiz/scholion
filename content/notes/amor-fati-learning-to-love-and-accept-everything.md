The post argues that people often expect the world to respond to their will, which makes acceptance feel weak or frustrating. It says this resistance wastes energy and keeps people from working with what is actually happening.

It presents Stoic acceptance as "amor fati," the choice to see events clearly and work with them rather than fight them. The text uses Stoic quotations and metaphors to say that events, other people, weather, and setbacks should be accepted, understood, and met with empathy.

## Reading notes

- Acceptance is hard because people expect events to go their way, especially when they are young or ambitious.
- Albert Ellis’s idea of "musturbation" names the belief that things must happen as wanted or expected.
- Resisting reality is described as unproductive and draining, since it ruins the day and dissipates vital force.
- James Madison is quoted to say that people should prepare themselves to hear truths that inflexibility cannot withstand.
- The Stoic response is to want events to happen as they do, rather than fighting them with large amounts of energy.
- The post names this attitude the Art of Acquiescence and links it to Horace, Lincoln, Musashi, and Schopenhauer.
- Amor fati is described as a love of what happens, paired with seeing reality clearly and accepting it unconditionally.
- A Stoic metaphor compares human life to a dog tied to a moving cart, with the choice between resisting and being dragged or going along and taking freedom where it comes.
- The post closes by saying the world, its events, and the people in life are what they are, and should be accepted, understood, and empathized with.
