Work stress in this study is tied to worse mental health and a higher likelihood of death. The authors also say that jobs with more autonomy can support good health, and that intelligence helps people cope with stress.

## Reading notes

- Researchers at Indiana University’s Kelley School of Business followed 3,148 Wisconsinites for 20 years.
- Heavy workload and lack of autonomy correlated strongly with poor mental health and death.
- When job demands exceed the control a person has, mental health deteriorates and the likelihood of death rises.
- Work stressors were more likely to cause depression and death in jobs where workers had little control.
- Autonomy at work was not a stressor itself, but it helped people cope with other work stresses.
- Intelligence was also described as a coping mechanism for job stress.
- Managers are advised to give employees more control over how and when they do their work, or lower demands if that is not possible.
- The authors say the coronavirus pandemic is likely worsening mental health problems and makes reducing work stress more important.
