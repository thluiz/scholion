## Reading notes

- Dynamic Reteaming is described as a good book that did not impress the author much, because much of its content felt relevant but rarely novel.
- The first part on introducing patterns is considered too long, while chapters 11 to 14 are singled out for explaining the practical side of transformation and reorganization.
- The book is seen as a useful way to put names to common practices in team reorganization and evolution, and as a reference manual more than a conceptual book.
- Team Choice Marketplace, especially Appendix A, is identified as the most interesting part.
- The author recommends the book differently depending on the reader’s situation, saying it is more useful for people new to team reorganization and still valuable as a way to reflect on past reorganizations.
- Entre Deux Mondes is presented as the last Norek book the author had not yet read, and as another work that inspires admiration and respect for the author.
- The book is based on Norek’s time in Calais, in the jungle refugee camp, and with refugees, NGOs, and local police.
- It is described as dark, moving, and a reminder of the conditions faced by people born in places where peace and safety are hard to find.
- La Moustache is described as Carrère’s first fiction book, a short read that was devoured in a couple of days.
- The book is called engaging and disturbing, and the author connects it to the song I’m Going Slightly Mad while reading.
- Its central idea is presented as an apparently minor event opening the abyss of madness, with manipulation and the instability of trust in one’s own mind as major themes.
- The reader follows an inner journey through doubts, convictions, contradictions, and mood swings, and the book is said to show Carrère’s skill at combining narration with introspection.
- The author warns that the book is disturbing and recommends it only to readers who can keep distance from the emotions it provokes.
