The article argues that entrepreneurs need a rare kind of developer for early product work: someone who can turn abstract ideas into a functioning product with limited direction. This developer combines technical skill with the ability to explain technical choices to non-technical teammates, adapt when requirements change, and think through cost and sequence.

## Reading notes

- A "from scratch" developer can turn a blank page into a functioning product under their own direction.
- They are not attached to one technology stack or one way of building things, and they choose tools based on the job.
- They may suggest off-the-shelf software when it helps the product ship faster.
- They are not dependent on a single infrastructure choice and can adapt if constraints change.
- They do not get stuck when new requirements appear and are willing to learn what the product now needs.
- They avoid calling things "impossible" too quickly and keep working through options.
- They can communicate in a structured way about the build cycle, core features, relative costs, milestones, scheduling, scope cuts, and business reasons for technical decisions.
- The article says this skill set comes from practice, repetition, and learning from mistakes.
- It recommends building open source projects or commercial products as a way to develop these abilities.
