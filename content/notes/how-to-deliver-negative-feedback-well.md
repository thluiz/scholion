The article argues that negative feedback is difficult because managers fear demotivating employees or making them feel attacked. It proposes four habits that make the exchange more honest and productive: showing care, sticking to observations, admitting fallibility, and staying curious.

## Reading notes

- Feedback should be framed as coming from care for the person, the project, and the company.
- It should focus on what was observed, not on the person’s character or traits.
- Managers should treat their own feedback as an interpretation, not as infallible truth.
- Feedback should leave room for the other person’s perspective and turn into a conversation.
- The goal is for the other person to feel helped, not judged.
