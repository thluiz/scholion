The article argues that two common reasons for avoiding feedback questions are really excuses. If leaders worry feedback will distract employees or think they should not ask unless they can act right away, they end up preventing the openness they want.

## Reading notes

- The CEO already asked employees questions about what could be better, but hesitated when asked to raise a more direct question about benefits.
- He feared that public discussion of benefits would distract people and create expectations the company might not meet.
- The article says feedback already exists in employees’ minds, whether or not leaders ask about it.
- Leaving unasked feedback to sit and grow makes the distraction worse.
- Asking the question can clear the air and help an employee feel heard.
- The article challenges the idea that leaders should only ask for feedback when they are ready to implement it.
- Acting on feedback can also mean thanking the person, explaining why the feedback will not be used, and giving context for the decision.
- The article says those responses show listening and make employees feel that their feedback matters.
- The closing advice is to stop using those excuses if they want a more open and transparent company culture.
