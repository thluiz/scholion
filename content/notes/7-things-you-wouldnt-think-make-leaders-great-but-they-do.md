The piece argues that great leadership comes from integrating contradictions rather than choosing one side. It says strong leaders are firm in their convictions and vulnerable enough to let weaknesses be seen, compassionate without becoming fixers, and open to others while still able to decide when needed.

## Reading notes

- Great leadership begins with finding strong qualities within oneself.
- Leadership qualities often appear as paradoxes joined with their opposites.
- Great leaders are strong in convictions, relentless in values, and passionate in beliefs, while also being vulnerable enough to show weaknesses and flaws.
- Strength grows when vulnerable selves are allowed to be seen and known.
- Leadership combines toughness and tenderness, and great leaders unite a tough mind with a tender heart.
- A tender heart should not be mistaken for a weakened mind, and a tough mind should not be confused with a heartless soul.
- Great leaders are compassionate and aim to relieve the suffering of others.
- They should also step back and have faith in others, so they do not become enablers or fixers who take away people’s power.
- Great leaders are open-minded, good listeners, and communicators, but they also need to be decisive when the moment comes.
