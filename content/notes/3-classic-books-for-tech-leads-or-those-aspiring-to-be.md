The article says books are a strong way to learn for engineers moving toward leadership, especially when paired with practice. It presents three classic titles that still matter because they address people, influence, and the management costs of software work.

## Reading notes

- Books can prepare software engineers for the Tech Lead role, and the author recommends reading them patiently while practice catches up.
- Peopleware: Productive Projects and Teams is presented as a classic on managing people, with stories that may feel dated but still support a sociological view of projects and teams.
- Peopleware is said to cover turnover, involving the team in hiring, the limits of methodologies, peer review, the ongoing development of soft and hard skills, productivity traps, office effects, and work quality.
- Driving technical change is recommended as an introduction to persuasion and influence, with anecdotes about gaining buy-in, exposing ideas, finding allies, and checking whether proposals are plausible.
- Driving technical change also lists categories of skepticism and is described as useful for understanding how teams react to ideas, though the book is not very practical.
- The Mythical Man-Month is described as a classic on software engineering and management, introducing man-month and the idea that adding people to a late project can make it later.
- The article connects The Mythical Man-Month to coordination costs, saying that larger groups require more meetings and management effort, which can delay software development.
