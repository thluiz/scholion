The page argues that working from home gets easier when the day has a clear structure. It recommends starting actively, preparing meals ahead of time, turning on the camera in work calls, planning breaks, exercising, and setting a fixed end to the workday.

## Reading notes

- Start the day by getting up when the alarm rings, making the bed, and moving before sitting at the computer.
- If mornings feel sluggish, do a few squats, sit-ups, jumping jacks, or simple chores to wake up.
- Plan the next day’s meals in advance and prepare what can be cooked the night before.
- Batch meal prep is presented as faster than deciding what to eat during the workday.
- Turn on the camera during work calls so coworkers can see each other and stay focused together.
- Use video calls to keep a work atmosphere at home, stay dressed, and keep the apartment organized.
- Schedule 3 to 4 breaks instead of sitting continuously for hours.
- Decide in advance what each break will be used for, such as reading, playing with a dog, or exercising.
- Exercise during the day to stay focused and sharp, without needing gym equipment.
- The text suggests doing exercise after the first 3 to 4 hours of work.
- Set both a starting time and an ending time for work.
- When work is over, close the computer and spend time talking with family, roommates, or calling a parent to switch into rest mode.
