The author says he still carries a pocket notebook every day and uses a larger notebook for deep reading because these analog tools help him stay organized, learn better, and keep important ideas from getting lost. He argues that notebooks are useful for capturing thoughts, sketches, class notes, and fragments of ideas in ways digital tools often do not.

## Reading notes

- A notebook helps capture thoughts you want to revisit, especially when a digital tool does not fit the moment.
- Handwritten notes support learning because writing things down by hand encourages deeper processing.
- Paper notebooks help preserve fragments of ideas and make it easier to return to older notes later.
- A notebook reduces distraction because it does only one thing and does not compete for attention.
- Pocket notebooks are practical because they can be carried everywhere and used whenever an idea appears.
- He uses his notebook in several ways: morning reflection, workout records, daily commentary, sketching problems, reading notes, and running lists.
- Notes are processed daily by moving tasks, quotes, and ideas into Evernote, Ulysses, or OmniFocus.
- He rereads finished notebooks and older notebooks to recover forgotten ideas and check what mattered at the time.
- His Evernote system works as a Common Place Book, where he stores quotes, research, and stories for later search and reuse.
- He prefers a Leuchtturm 1917 notebook for larger notes and Field Notes notebooks for pocket use.
