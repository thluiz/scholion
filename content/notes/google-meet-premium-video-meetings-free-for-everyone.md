Google is making Meet, its premium video conferencing product, free for everyone, with access rolling out over the coming weeks. Starting in early May, anyone with an email address can sign up and use features such as scheduling, screen sharing, captions, and adaptive layouts.

The post stresses that Meet is built on a secure foundation, with host controls, encrypted meetings, no anonymous users for individual accounts, no required plugins, and data not used for advertising. It also outlines how free access will work for individuals, teams, businesses, and schools, including a 60-minute limit for the free product and temporary free access to advanced features through Sept. 30.

## Reading notes

- Meet becomes free for everyone, but the rollout happens gradually over several weeks.
- Anyone with an email address can sign up and use core features like scheduling, screen sharing, captions, and expanded tiled view.
- Google presents security as the default approach, with host controls, encryption, complex meeting codes, and no anonymous users for individual accounts.
- Meet does not require plugins on the web and works in Chrome and other modern browsers.
- Meet data is not used for advertising, and Google says it does not sell data to third parties.
- Free individual accounts can schedule, join, or start meetings, with a 60-minute limit on the free product.
- Google says it will not enforce the 60-minute limit until after Sept. 30.
- Organizations using G Suite already have access to Meet, and schools using G Suite for Education can enable it at no additional cost.
- Google also announces G Suite Essentials for teams that need advanced Meet features, including dial-in numbers, larger meetings, and recording.
