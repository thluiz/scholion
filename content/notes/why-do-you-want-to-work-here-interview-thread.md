Oliver Jumpertz’s thread unpacks the common interview question "Why do you want to work here?" He argues that while the question can feel stupid, candidates hurt themselves with shallow answers. Employers use it to gauge motivation, interest in contributing, and whether hiring you will be a good investment. He highlights that companies invest guidance, mentors, education, and budget and expect employees to help them make money. Motivated people contribute more to company goals, while unmotivated ones underperform and leave quickly, which is costly for both sides. Some companies also care about employee well‑being and don’t want to hire someone who will end up unhappy or depressed in a job they dislike.

Jumpertz recommends coming prepared by researching what the company does, what it stands for, and its mission. Since signing an offer is a commitment to show up and contribute to the company’s success, you should know what you expect from them and whether the role is really for you. He suggests writing down what the company does and stands for, then reflecting on what work makes you happy, what you want to achieve and contribute, what culture you need to thrive, and how you want to help the company succeed. Answering these honestly removes the need for a "perfect" fabricated response, which he warns will only hurt you. Instead, he advises crafting a story that explains why you want to work at this specific company, stressing that there is no single right or wrong answer as long as it is honest and believable.

He provides two example answers: one about aligning values and a shared vision of making the cloud easier to use, tying this to the candidate’s content creation and open source work; another about a skillset that fits a SaaS company whose product the candidate already uses and admires, making the role a natural next step. He notes there’s nothing special about these examples beyond their honesty and clear alignment between personal goals and the company’s mission. If a candidate truly believes what they say and only states what is real for them, they will have done well on this question. The thread ends with him hoping readers learned something useful for their next interview and pointing them to a related blog post version of the content.
