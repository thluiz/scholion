A product name can shape how people perceive it, and a weak name can confuse or limit growth. The page says a good name should create the right associations, stay easy to understand, and help people remember the product quickly.

## Reading notes

- The right name can make a product easier to connect with, while a bad one can alienate, confuse, and bore potential users.
- Good names create creative, brand-aligned associations without being hard to understand.
- The article suggests starting by defining the primary user, the problem the product solves, the words that should come to mind, possible metaphors, and the competitors’ names.
- Those answers can be turned into a creative brief that acts as a map for what the name should communicate.
- Name generators can help inspire more ideas, and the process should include checking domain availability, social media usernames, and trademarks.
- The page also recommends considering real words versus made-up words because search engines may affect how people find the product.
- A strong name should sound good, stick in memory, and distinguish the product from competitors.
- Feedback from others and from the target audience can help test whether the name is strong or too safe.
- The conclusion says a good name should be easy for potential users to understand, remember, and love.
