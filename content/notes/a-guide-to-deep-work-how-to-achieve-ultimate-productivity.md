- Shallow work includes tasks that almost anyone can do with minimal training, such as answering emails, responding to messages, and attending meetings.
- The article says shallow work can feel productive, but deep work produces most of the value and improves the quality of valuable output.

- Automating planning and delegating shallow work with project management tools is presented as a way to free time for important work.
- Deep work should be done one task at a time, with focused sessions followed by rest.
- The article recommends building a ritual, with a fixed place, a plan to remove distractions, and some form of sustenance.
- It says the habit should grow gradually, starting with short sessions and building up over time.
- Social media, emails, and messages should be checked only in small scheduled blocks.
- The day should end with a real stop, followed by rest and time away from work.
