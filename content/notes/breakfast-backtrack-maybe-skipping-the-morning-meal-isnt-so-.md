The article says the belief that breakfast is the most important meal rests on tradition, science, and cereal marketing. It notes that newer studies do not show a clear link between breakfast and lower body mass index or better weight loss, and that many adults now skip breakfast or eat later in the morning.

It also argues that what people eat may matter more than when they eat. A breakfast built on refined carbohydrates may raise blood sugar and hunger later, while a protein-rich meal can increase satiety. The article points to eggs and other protein foods as a better morning choice than sugary cereal.
