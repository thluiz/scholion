The article says that books, articles, interviews, and conversations all produce information that is easy to forget. To keep it available, it recommends building a system that helps you revisit what you already learned instead of trying to memorize everything.

## Reading notes

- Take notes while reading so you keep the essence and context of what you are taking in.
- For books, use a commonplace book or an idea index to organize excerpts, thoughts, and notes.
- For conversations, interviews, and speeches, record the important points, using a phone, computer, pen, or pad.
- Digitize notes so they can be searched later, and add tags, context, questions, and related points.
- Collect more information rather than less when a point seems important or close to important, because it is easier to discard notes later than recover details from memory.
- Review notes every few weeks, and set aside regular time to go back over highlights.
- When reviewing, look for actions you can take, share the information with others, and use it in conversation when it fits.
- Use objects and mementos as reminders of lessons and experiences, such as printed quotes on a wall or items tied to a past event.
- Build a sample workflow that includes note-taking, digitizing, tagging, scheduled review, and visible reminders.
- The article concludes that the goal is not perfect memorization but a system for relearning important lessons quickly.
