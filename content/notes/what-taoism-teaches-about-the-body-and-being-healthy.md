Taoism presents the body as part of a larger cosmos, so health is not only an individual concern. Rituals, exercises, meditation, and alchemical practices are described as ways to align the body with natural and social surroundings, cultivate qi, and extend life.

The article also traces how Taoist practice moves from early bodily postures and external alchemy to inner alchemy and priestly ritual. In each case, the body is treated as a place where transformation can affect both the person and the wider world.

## Reading notes

- Taoism understands humans as an integral part of the larger cosmos.
- Rituals and bodily techniques are used to align the individual body with surrounding social and natural environments.
- Taoist texts describe Lao Tzu’s body as a map for the entire cosmos.
- Bringing the body into alignment with the cosmos is said to allow change in the environment through change in the body.
- Early Taoist exercises used body movements and postures to align the body with its environment.
- These exercises were used to cultivate qi, harmonize with nature, nourish health, and increase longevity.
- Early Taoists also used alchemy, mixing rare natural elements into an elixir of health.
- Practitioners consumed these substances in an attempt to attain longevity or immortality.
- Later Taoist masters developed inner alchemy, or neidan, to replicate the landscape within the body.
- Inner alchemy teaches how to refine vital essences from within one’s own body.
- Ritual programs describe an inner journey through temples, grottoes, and divine figures.
- This journey leads the old self to the crown of the head, where a new immortal self emerges.
- Taoist priests use the body as the medium for rituals that reconnect the local community with the Dao.
- Priests report the merits of the local community to the Three Pure Ones.
