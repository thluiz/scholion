The post argues that a Metro style app stands out by defining what it is best at in its category and using that focus to shape content, layout, and navigation. Windows 8 patterns such as the app bar, touch patterns, Share contract, hub-style landing pages, Semantic Zoom, and the Grid template are presented as tools for keeping the app centered on its core scenarios.

The example app, Food with friends, is built around helping users and their friends choose a restaurant to eat at tonight. From that best-at statement, the post derives core scenarios, section order, curated content on the hub, dedicated pages for full lists and item details, and standard navigation patterns that let users move through the app without extra friction.

## Reading notes

- Start with a clear best-at statement for the app’s category.
- Use Windows 8 design patterns and platform features to support that focus.
- Define core scenarios before choosing features.
- Shape the landing page as a hub that shows what the app does best.
- Order hub sections by relevance, freshness, and the app’s main differentiator.
- Customize each section around the scenario it serves.
- Use separate pages for full content and item details.
- Rely on familiar navigation patterns so users can reach content quickly.
