The post argues that one-on-one meetings work better when managers use a small set of questions to build trust, surface concerns, and adjust how they support employees. It frames these questions as part of a manager’s toolkit and recommends using one or two of them in a regular meeting agenda.

## Reading notes

- Ask about life outside work to build trust and make harder conversations easier later.
- Ask what someone is worried about right now to uncover concerns, confusion, or uncertainty before they affect performance or retention.
- Ask what rumors they are hearing so you can spot stress points and dispel misinformation quickly.
- Ask what accomplishment they would be proud of between now and next year to start a conversation about short-term and long-term goals.
- Ask about biggest time wasters, then follow up with concrete ideas to reduce wasted time.
- Ask whether they want more or less direction to adjust your management style and support level.
- Ask whether they want more or less feedback, and what kind, because many employees want more performance feedback.
- Ask about decisions they are stuck on so you can coach them through a difficult choice.
