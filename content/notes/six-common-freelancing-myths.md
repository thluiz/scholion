The article argues that common ideas about freelancing are misleading. It says money usually comes only after time, effort, experience, and a portfolio, and that freelancers still work with clients who shape requests and expectations. It also says freelancing often requires networking, brings its own stresses, and is not just a fallback for people who cannot find other work.

## Reading notes

- Freelance income does not arrive quickly; it depends on skill, experience, negotiation, and portfolio-building.
- Freelancers do not simply act as their own bosses, because clients still make choices that have to be followed.
- Freelancers need some networking and work-social balance, even if the field attracts introverts.
- Low starting pay can be part of building a profile, but value and reputation can later support better-paying projects.
- Freelancing is not stress-free, since searching for jobs, meeting deadlines, and handling remote communication all add pressure.
- Many people freelance by choice, including those who want a different kind of writing, more balance, or who combine freelance work with a full-time job.
