Semaphore presents ten books that influenced its engineering culture and targets senior engineers, while noting that anyone interested in software development can benefit from them. The selection mixes classic and modern titles and spans agile practice, delivery, remote work, project management, data-intensive systems, SRE, microservices, and a practical view of software engineering.

## Reading notes

- _Extreme Programming Explained_ is presented as a book that refined practices now taken for granted, including pair programming, automated testing, TDD, and continuous integration, while also addressing team interaction, management, work ethics, and flat hierarchy.
- _Continuous Delivery_ extends continuous integration and argues for making release and deployment as routine as pushing a button, with attention to delivery pipelines, testing, data handling, and how to evolve the pipeline as a project grows.
- _Remote_ argues that remote work is already the present, not the future, and says face-to-face communication is not irreplaceable, people can be trusted to be productive at home, and offices are interruption factories.
- _The Mythical Man-Month_ warns about the limits of adding people to a late software project and ties complex project work to communication, planning, estimation, and the difficulty of splitting work into parallel tasks.
- _Getting Real_ advocates keeping projects simple, building less and sooner, skipping many design stages, unifying teams, breaking silos, trusting people to work independently, and avoiding meetings.
- _Designing Data-Intensive Apps_ covers data models, SQL and NoSQL databases, message queues, distributed systems, and big data, with a focus on tradeoffs in data-processing technologies.
- _Site Reliability Engineering_ focuses on keeping systems online after they are built, with work on finding problems early, doing post-mortems, handling glue work, scaling platforms reliably, and supporting on-call work.
- _The Phoenix Project_ is described as a fictional company story about a crisis, an urgent project, and a warning against hero figures in a company.
- _Building Microservices_ explains how to architect, build, deploy, secure, and monitor distributed systems, using concrete examples of organizations that scale with microservices.
- _Modern Software Engineering_ argues that software engineering is more than coding and promotes an empirical, scientific, iterative, and practical approach to building software, including abstraction, separation of concerns, and modularity.
