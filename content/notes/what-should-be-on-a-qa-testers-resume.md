The page says a QA résumé should be customized for each employer instead of sent out unchanged. It should present a clear career narrative, use keywords that match the job ad, stay concise, and avoid unnecessary details like a photo or an objective statement.

## Reading notes

- Tailor the résumé to the prospective employer rather than mass-applying with the same version.
- Build a generic base, then adjust it for each job and show a career trajectory.
- Include contact information when applying directly, but remove the phone number for online databases if privacy is a concern.
- Leave out a photograph in North America.
- Skip the statement of objectives, since it is seen as redundant.
- Put a Skills/keywords section near the top, because recruiters and software scan for keywords.
- Use QA-related terms, alternate job titles, programming languages, and tools that match the posting.
- Choose either a chronological or functional résumé, depending on the work history and the job being sought.
- Use the specific job title held, even if the title does not fully describe the work performed.
- Give enough detail about QA accomplishments to show scope and scale.
- Put older experience in the skills or keywords section if it is still relevant, rather than crowding the experience section.
- List education and certifications, but omit dates unless a certification needs to be kept current.
- Include side projects and volunteer work only when they are directly relevant to QA or fit the company culture.
- Keep formatting basic, use a simple font, and limit the résumé to two pages.
- Write in simple, direct, past-tense language and avoid buzzwords.
- Quantify results where possible and focus on how QA work affects the employer’s bottom line.
- Have other people proofread the résumé, because typos can hurt credibility and suggest weak attention to detail.
