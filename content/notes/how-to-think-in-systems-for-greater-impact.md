Human-centered systems thinking combines systems analysis with human-centered design so solutions account for the system, its stakeholders, and what can actually be implemented. The article frames it as a way to move past analysis paralysis, work within constraints, and find leverage points for impact.

## Reading notes

- Systems thinking is presented as a way to understand a whole where the whole is greater than the sum of its parts.
- The human-centered approach combines analytical systems tools with creative design methods.
- The goal is to produce solutions that are holistic and responsive to the needs of stakeholders.
- A common problem is getting stuck in thinking without moving to tangible action.
- Non-obvious stakeholders can matter, as shown by the example of real estate developers in the Innova Schools case.
- Complex problems with many stakeholders, competing incentives, or no obvious solution are a strong fit for systems thinking.
- Constraints are described as useful because they can focus the work and prompt more creative solutions.
- The article emphasizes moving between the big picture and the details.
- Zooming out helps account for wider effects, while zooming in helps evaluate individual needs.
- Systems mapping is presented as a way to deepen understanding, ask new questions, and spot places where impact is possible.
