The post argues that open source and developer products often fail because their design and UX are weak, even when the code is strong. Its main advice is to do less, keep things simple, and build a usable interface with consistent choices across the product.

## Reading notes

- Improve design sense by following Dribbble’s weekly popular shots, saving what you like, and looking for recurring patterns in your favorites.
- Build a logo from two parts, an icon and the logo text, and keep the icon square, symmetrical, and simple enough to read at 32x32 px.
- Use one vibrant color as the main color and keep most of the rest white or black, with a few extra tones for alerts or warnings.
- Limit fonts to one or two, prefer sans-serif for headings, and use the same font for headings and logo text when possible.
- Use only a few icons, keep them stylistically consistent, and choose icons that replace words or reduce cognitive load.
- Use images and illustrations to fill space and reinforce the message, but avoid mixing them too much and apply the same visual treatment as the rest of the site.
- Keep branding consistent by reusing the same color, font, text sizes, padding, and layouts so users do not have to re-learn each section.
- Prefer a class-based, utility-first CSS framework such as Bulma or Tailwind, and avoid locking design into a framework-specific component system.
- Figma is presented as the main design tool to use, with vector editing, prototypes, comments, and collaboration.
- For landing pages, the post recommends a 70vh hero with a More button below it so users know they can scroll.
