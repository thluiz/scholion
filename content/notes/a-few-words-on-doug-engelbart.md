Bret Victor says Engelbart is often misunderstood because writers read his work as a technology story instead of a human one. He argues that labels from today, such as hypertext or video conferencing, distort Engelbart's work because they carry present meanings that do not match his original intent.

Victor stresses that Engelbart's system was meant to support shared intellectual work and collective problem solving. He says the right question is not what Engelbart built, but what world he was trying to create.

## Reading notes

- Engelbart devoted his life to a human problem, with technology as part of the solution.
- The common view of him as the inventor of the mouse misses his larger purpose.
- Calling his system hypertext or video conferencing uses present-day meanings that do not fit his work.
- NLS was intended to augment human intellect and boost collective intelligence.
- His vision was collaborative from the start, with people working in a shared intellectual space.
- Separate pointers in the demo fit that collaborative intent.
- Present-day screen sharing stays tied to a single-user computer model.
- To understand NLS, Victor says to forget today's assumptions about computers and read Engelbart's intent.
- The most important question is what world Engelbart was trying to create.
