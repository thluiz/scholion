The page gathers books on Stoicism and books inspired by it, moving from primary texts to modern introductions, biographies, fiction, and studies of therapy. It explains why the tradition still feels immediate, then groups recommendations by how they can deepen a reader’s understanding of the philosophy.

## Reading notes

- Pierre Hadot’s books are presented as strong guides to Marcus Aurelius and to philosophy as a practiced way of life.
- The Daily Stoic is described as a beginner-friendly daily introduction with 366 Stoic insights, exercises, and short meditations.
- James Stockdale’s book is framed through the Stockdale paradox and as an example of Stoicism under extreme hardship.
- James Romm’s and Emily Wilson’s biographies of Seneca are recommended for readers who want to understand Seneca’s life and political context.
- Donald Robertson’s books connect Stoicism with happiness and with the history of cognitive behavioural therapy.
- Ryan Holiday’s The Obstacle Is the Way and Ego Is the Enemy are presented as Stoicism-inspired books about obstacles and ego.
- William Irvine’s A Guide to the Good Life is described as a well-known modern introduction to Stoicism.
- Alain de Botton, Nassim Nicholas Taleb, and James Miller are included for chapters or books that help place Stoicism alongside other philosophical traditions.
- Nancy Sherman, John Sellars, R. W. Sharples, Andrew Holowchak, F. H. Sandbach, Margaret Graver, Brad Inwood, Ronald Pies, Jules Evans, Lawrence C. Becker, and Massimo Pigliucci are listed as further reading for different levels of depth and focus.
- Tom Wolfe’s A Man in Full is singled out as a fiction novel that features Epictetus and helped revive interest in Stoicism.
