The author draws on over two years of remote work across development and operations to outline practices that make distributed collaboration smoother. They emphasize shared documentation and offer five concrete habits: creating a clear project plan and specific tasks, tracking these tasks and deadlines visibly, scheduling regular check-ins with focused questions, keeping accessible meeting notes, and fostering informal interactions to build rapport. - The article presents five key components that have helped make communication smoother in remote setups.

- For joint projects, the author recommends starting with a clear roadmap that gives an overview of the project.
- At the beginning of a project, teams should clarify goals, scope, top priority features, and the problems the project is meant to solve.
- After defining these elements, teams can break work into specific tasks and decide who will handle each one.
- Once tasks are defined and assigned, they should be organized in a system where progress can be tracked.
- Tools such as Asana, Trello, and Notion are suggested for tracking tasks and organizing work.
- Each task entry should contain clear details and indicate which team member is responsible.
- Deadlines should be realistic and updated when needed rather than remaining rigid.
- Task and deadline information should be visible to all team members to avoid misunderstandings.
- Making task information accessible helps if a task needs to be reassigned later.
- After tasks are set, teams should schedule regular meetings to review progress.
- Check-in meetings should have a defined agenda and set timeframe.
- The author suggests recurring questions for check-ins, including what is working, current roadblocks, whether help is needed, and what the next actionable tasks are.
- Teams should keep a record of each meeting to capture decisions and expectations for the next meeting.
- Accessible meeting notes allow any team member to review decisions and get clarification later.
- The author encourages building casual interactions into remote work routines.
- They propose using a few minutes before meetings to talk about fun or new topics.
- A monthly virtual happy hour is suggested as a way to get to know each other better and build rapport.
