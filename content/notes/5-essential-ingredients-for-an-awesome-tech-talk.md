The post says impostor syndrome can make people fear public speaking or writing, but expertise is not required. What matters is your experience. A good talk turns that experience into a clear story about why you chose a technology, what it does, how you made it work, where others can start, and how to end with a call to action.

## Reading notes

- Fear of speaking often comes from impostor syndrome.
- You do not need to be an expert to give a strong presentation.
- Your own experience is something no one else has.
- Explain why you chose a technology or switched from one option to another, and what problem you were trying to solve.
- Give a short overview of the technology’s highlights, features, and other uses.
- Share the challenges, mistakes, and lessons from making it work.
- Being transparent about mistakes adds credibility.
- If a demo is used, it should be short and support the reason for choosing the technology.
- Do not do live coding unless the point is to demo IDE features.
- Good demos can be screenshots or recorded video.
- Give the audience links and resources so they can start quickly.
- End with a call to action.
