This guide explains how to build an architectural street scene by starting with the mood you want, choosing a style, and then adding scene-specific elements. It also recommends SketchBook Pro’s Perspective Guides and a layer-based workflow to keep proportions, people, and overlapping parts under control.

## Reading notes

- The first step is to decide the mood or atmosphere of the scene and then work from that goal.
- Architectural styles are grouped into Classic, Modern, and Post-Modern.
- Classic architecture can include columns, arched windows, decorative pediments, domes, and moulding.
- Modernist architecture is described as un-ornamented, rigid, and volumetric, with box-like forms.
- Postmodern architecture mixes ideas from classic and modern architecture and often produces strange, eccentric buildings.
- Scene details should reflect the place, such as a cafe with tables, chairs, umbrellas, and plant pots.
- A boardwalk scene can include walkways, bicycles, trees, beach houses, and an ocean view.
- A marketplace can include canopies, food stands, tables, hanging baskets, and busy people.
- The Perspective Guides tool offers 1-point, 2-point, 3-point, and fish-eye modes for straight lines and proportions.
- The layer technique helps organize buildings, landscape, furniture, and people without overwhelming the drawing.
- The tutorial starts by blocking out background building shapes and drawing an eye line slightly above the horizon line.
- Facades are then added using the eye line to keep windows and doors at believable heights.
- Natural elements such as trees, bushes, planters, and flowers are added next.
- People should be aligned at eye level, with size changes used to suggest distance and movement.
- Overlapping lines are erased or traced over once all layers are in place.
- Color, shade, and shadow are added at the end, and the people may be left white so they stand out.
