The post argues that writing gets better when pre-writing is treated as its own workflow. The author compares it to mise en place in cooking, where the useful work is prepared before the actual act of cooking. Writing then becomes the final assembly stage, not the whole process.

## Reading notes

- Writing is only the final stage of a longer process, so ideation, research, peer review, audience testing, reframing, organization, and illustration can happen earlier.
- The main point is to decouple writing from pre-writing so the writing session can stay focused and intensive.
- Pre-writing is described as low-intensity work that can happen whenever an idea appears, then be stored in a searchable note-taking system.
- The author keeps a backlog of possible topics and adds links, facts, quotes, stories, and related thoughts as they come up.
- A good workflow needs to be easy and fast because ideas can be forgotten quickly.
- The note-taking system is treated as a second brain, with computers handling storage and search while the writer handles thinking.
- The backlog makes it possible to prepare many topics at once instead of trying to produce one post from scratch each time.
- When it is time to write, the author chooses the most interesting topic from the backlog and turns the collected material into a draft.
- Improvisation still has a place during writing, even if pre-writing has already been done.
- Editing matters, but the author does not spend much time on it and prefers to publish, then revise later if needed.
- The post closes by noting that the method is shaped by ideas from Building a Second Brain and online writing guides, and that it can still work whether someone writes broadly or focuses on one topic.
