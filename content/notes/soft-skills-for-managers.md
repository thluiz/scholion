The page says strong managers need soft skills that are harder to assess than technical ability. It focuses on initiative, emotional control, and dispassionate empathy, and warns against treating likeability or sociability as proof of leadership ability.

## Reading notes

- Technical skill can often be checked directly, but soft skills are harder to measure and still matter a great deal in management.
- Initiative shows up when managers look for ways to help the business instead of only maintaining process, reacting to problems, or doing busywork.
- A useful interview test for initiative is asking a candidate to explain the why behind decisions on a project they led.
- Emotional control matters because managers are often the first escalation point for conflict, blockers, and HR issues.
- Good managers stay calm under stress, avoid emotional reactions that make situations worse, and can do this repeatedly over time.
- Dispassionate empathy means understanding how different people on a team will react to a decision without letting sympathy cloud judgment.
- Promotions are a key example because one decision can change incentives, expectations, and reactions across the whole team.
- The page suggests testing this by asking about difficult situations involving raises, promotions, cross-team conflict, or underperformance.
- A common trap is confusing a pleasant, outgoing personality with management aptitude.
- The page also warns that confidence and being the loudest person in the room can be mistaken for leadership, so management potential should be judged on the traits above instead.
