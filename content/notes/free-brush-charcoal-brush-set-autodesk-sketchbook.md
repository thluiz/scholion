The post introduces a free charcoal brush set for SketchBook and ties it to the look and control of traditional charcoal drawing. It says charcoal works well for studies, shading, portraits, animals, and reference-based work, while digital tools add layers, erasers, Undo, and smudge options.

## Reading notes

- The set aims to give control over the hardness or softness of digital charcoal without the mess of real charcoal.
- Charcoal drawing is described as a tricky technique that often leaves charcoal on hands, face, and clothes.
- The post says charcoal is often used for sketching, shading, and values rather than fully detailed finished pieces.
- Digital charcoal in SketchBook can use layers, erasers, Undo, and smudge brushes.
- Charcoal can be turned to white for highlights, and the post notes that users can break the rules if they want.
- The brush set includes 10 brushes, among them Standard, Streaky, Smeary Edge, Thick, Broad Side, Itty Bitty Grainy Piece, Large Grainy Piece, Old Conte, Wet Conte, and Melted Conte.
- The Conte brushes are meant to mimic compressed charcoal with wax and a wetter, more permanent look.
- The post says the brush set does not replicate blowing charcoal dust off paper or spraying fixative.
- It recommends using a reference photo if you do not already have a subject in mind.
- It notes that free or stock images should be checked for usage rules.
- For installation, the post says SketchBook Pro members on desktop version 8 can double click the .skbrushes file to install it.
- It also says non-members can download a free trial and unlock Pro membership for 15 days without a credit card.
