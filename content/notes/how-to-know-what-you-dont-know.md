The question is about a harder version of ignorance in academia, when you do not even know that you are missing something important. It uses the example of optimizing a narrow method, like running, while being unaware that a different option, like cars, would solve the problem better. The answers say that the main risk comes from isolation and from working inside a narrow information loop.

## Reading notes

- The problem is presented as one of not noticing important gaps in your own knowledge.
- One answer says the issue is mostly isolation.
- One suggested way to reduce it is to talk to many senior people about the problem.
- Another is to read broadly across books and papers to see what others treat as simple or difficult.
- One answer recommends keeping up with relevant journals, review papers, old references, conferences, and seminars.
- That same answer says experience can help you guess what might exist even before you have seen it.
- One answer suggests asking an AI open-ended questions to surface unfamiliar approaches, then checking the sources carefully.
- The comments stress that there is no perfectly reliable method for finding all unknown unknowns.
