The article presents the memristor as a fourth circuit element that links memory and resistance. It says the device stores charge, keeps data when power is off, and may help move computing beyond silicon-based transistors.

## Reading notes

- Leon Chua proposed the memristor in 1971 as a fourth fundamental element, alongside the resistor, capacitor, and inductor.
- The first memristor was built by Hewlett Packard in 2008, 37 years after the concept was proposed.
- Unlike transistors, memristors do not require a silicon layer.
- Memristors use electrons and ions to store data.
- A memristor can remember the amount of charge that flowed through it, so it retains data when power is turned off.
- This could allow computers to turn on and off instantly and keep RAM from being erased when the machine is off.
- The technology may first be used for super-fast memory chips with higher data density and lower energy use.
- Jennifer Rupp says memristors can have multiple levels, beyond binary 0s and 1s.
- She says this could support computers that work in a way similar to human synapses and may learn and make decisions.
- The article says transistor miniaturization is approaching a physical limit, making a move away from silicon-based computing likely.
- Memristors could be used on different materials and one day be integrated into windows, clothes, or coffee cups.
- Hewlett Packard is developing a memristor-based computer called "The Machine," planned for launch by 2020.
