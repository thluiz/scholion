The article argues that developers improve through a mix of technical habits and social skills. It recommends asking precise questions, contributing in meetings when something useful can be said, learning from other teams, and keeping side projects and career goals in view.

## Reading notes

- Ask specific questions to get more useful answers instead of generic ones.
- Speak in meetings only when there is something useful to add.
- Cross-team collaboration creates chances to learn things not available within one team.
- Passion projects help take the mind off work and can teach skills not used on the job.
- Career goals should be broader than the current company, since jobs and companies change.
- Getting involved in online conversations can help others and make opportunities come.
- Tasks should be prioritized, with urgent work handled first.
- Teammates should be trusted, and work should be delegated when needed.
- Comparing yourself to others is framed as harmful.
- Allies matter because they can help, support, and encourage you.
- New technologies and ideas should be considered if they fit the need.
- Copying and pasting code should only happen after thinking about why it works and whether it is good.
- An inspiring environment gives people encouragement and reduces fear of mistakes.
- Optimism is presented as a way to feel better and see better outcomes.
