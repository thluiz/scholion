The post says programming feels more stressful today because work is organized into repeated sprints instead of larger stretches of time. It argues that this creates continuous pressure, removes natural breaks, and can leave developers without time to recover.

It also says Scrum reduces autonomy, which makes work feel worse even when the amount of effort is similar. The post adds that sprints leave too little room for preparation, and that many Scrum setups still keep a bigger release deadline in the background, which raises stress further.

## Reading notes

- Sprints are described as back-to-back deadlines that never really stop, unlike waterfall-style work with clearer peaks and breaks.
- The post contrasts short-term stress with prolonged stress and says long-term stress is harder on health.
- It argues that Scrum prescribes too much of the process, including duration, meetings, tasks, and roles.
- A mouse study is used to support the idea that forced effort creates stress, fear, and discomfort even when the work done is the same.
- The post says autonomy matters because it supports intrinsic motivation and the ability to adjust, experiment, and improve.
- It argues that sprint planning often leaves too little time for brainstorming, investigation, reading, training, and other preparation work.
- The post says preparation and execution are intertwined, so separating them creates stress.
- It claims many Scrum implementations still contain a hidden waterfall-like release deadline that eventually overwhelms the sprint process.
- The conclusion calls for restoring autonomy and professionalism, with developers controlling both their craft and their process.
