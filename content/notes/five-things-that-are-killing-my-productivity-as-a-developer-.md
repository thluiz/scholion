The author says his productivity varies more with the team and project conditions than with task difficulty or interest. He identifies five recurring problems that affect output: late code review changes, meetings without a clear agenda, broken project setups, frequent priority changes, and micromanagement.

He recommends judging review changes by their cost and deadline impact, keeping meetings short and agenda-driven, warning the team early about dependencies or setup problems, limiting priority switches to real emergencies, and replacing control with honest communication and feedback.

## Reading notes

- productivity changes appear tied to the team and the project rather than to task complexity or enjoyment
- review suggestions should be weighed against implementation time and deadline impact
- if a change is large and the benefit is small, a backlog ticket may be better
- meetings need a clear agenda and should stay short
- conversations that do not need the full group should be moved offline or to 1-1 meetings
- broken setup or dependencies can waste a full day if the team is not warned early
- teams should share dependency and data setup issues so people can work safely
- constant priority switching is presented as a sign of poor planning
- roadmap planning should leave room for unexpected work
- micromanagement is described as control and mistrust, not just status checking
- managers should ask what is happening when output seems off and keep feedback open both ways
