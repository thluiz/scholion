## Reading notes

- The article treats positive thinking cautiously and says fantasizing about a perfect future can reduce motivation.
- It recommends working on one area at a time and choosing the change with the biggest payoff for the least effort.
- The basics come first: sleep, exercise, good food, and close friendships.
- If mental health is a problem, it should become the top priority, with a doctor as the first step.
- For physical health, the page stresses evidence-based advice and warns about back pain and repetitive strain injury.
- It presents positive psychology as one of the best evidence-backed sources of happiness advice.
- Suggested happiness practices include daily happiness ratings, gratitude journaling, using signature strengths, CBT exercises, mindfulness, kindness, active constructive responding, growth mindset, and job crafting.
- Basic social skills matter, and the page recommends practice, Succeed Socially, and CBT-based help for social anxiety.
- Relationships and networks influence jobs, information, norms, and opportunities, so the page advises building genuine connections.
- Where you live can affect career opportunities, pay, innovation, and life satisfaction, so moving to an industry hub may help early in a career.
- Saving enough for six to twelve months of living costs gives security and flexibility, and automatic saving is recommended.
- Productivity advice includes implementation intentions, task tracking, daily and weekly reviews, batching time, Pomodoro, routines, and blocking social media.
- Learning how to learn includes spaced repetition, habit building, and deliberate practice.
- Better job performance comes from identifying what actually drives advancement and focusing on that.
- Better decision-making comes from comparing options, studying bias, forecasting, and using mental models and statistics.
- Other useful work skills include analysis, learning, social skills, management, sales, persuasion, negotiation, and technical skills.
- Mastery takes talent, training, focused practice, and luck, and should be aimed at valuable areas with room for impact.
