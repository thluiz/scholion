The article says a content scorecard helps a blog repeat the qualities of its best posts. It recommends studying top-performing content, finding the common traits behind it, and turning those traits into a short set of publish-or-revise criteria.

It then walks through example criteria: choose a topic that fits the business, pick a keyword with search potential, study the top ranking competitor posts, make the piece actionable, add a content upgrade, keep one clear CTA, and use strong visuals. It also argues that every post should be treated like a homepage entry point.

## Reading notes

- Build a scorecard from the patterns in your strongest posts.
- Use analytics to find the pages that bring the most traffic.
- Study those posts for post type, length, resources, formats, and the kind of value they deliver.
- Set criteria that match your own goals; the example scorecard uses seven checks.
- Keep the topic tied to both audience interest and business relevance.
- Choose a keyword with search potential and use tools like Google Analytics and Moz Keyword Explorer.
- Review the top 10 ranking posts for your keyword and look for gaps.
- Make the article actionable with detailed steps and guidance people can use.
- Add a content upgrade that helps readers apply the advice.
- End with one clear CTA.
- Use visuals to reinforce ideas and improve understanding.
- Treat each post as a possible first impression of the business.
