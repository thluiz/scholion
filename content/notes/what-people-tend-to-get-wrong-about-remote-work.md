The piece argues that remote work can improve when companies stop treating it like an emergency version of office work and instead build remote habits on purpose. Darren Murph says the sudden shift can have a positive impact, but teams need time to develop structure, documentation, and communication norms.

He also says remote work changes the way people think about balance, visibility, and management. GitLab’s practices include public calendars, explicit time blocks, direct check-ins about Zoom fatigue, and tools that make it easier to raise workload concerns. The article emphasizes iteration, short feedback loops, and empathy as core parts of remote culture.

## Reading notes

- Murph thinks the sudden shift to remote work will have a positive impact, even if it is happening under bad conditions.
- He says companies can see savings from not maintaining office desks and real estate.
- Remote work gets easier when teams have time to build handbooks, structure, and healthy remote habits.
- He describes work/life balance as work/life harmony, with different rhythms for different people.
- Managers should ask direct reports whether they feel oversubscribed to Zoom calls or exhausted by Zoom.
- GitLab uses a Slack tool called PTO Ninja to prompt people to think about time off and raise problems in one-on-ones.
- Public calendars and time blocks help people protect focus time, family time, and personal time.
- New hires often get remote work wrong by falling back on old organizational habits.
- GitLab uses values like blameless problem solving, no ego, and short toes to support collaboration.
- Murph says the biggest gap in remote work is empathy, especially when people do not know each other well.
- He highlights tools like Kona, which ask people questions to help them share how they work and how they are doing.
- He advises teams not to expect mastery overnight and to move through remote adaptation in phases.
- Remote work should be treated as a process that improves through iteration and reversible changes.
- He closes by saying companies should not waste the crisis, but use it to build remote infrastructure with intention.
