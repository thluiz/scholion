The piece argues that the best first full-time hire for a solo SaaS founder at about $8K MRR is usually customer success, not engineering. The reason is that churn can suppress growth before new features matter, while support work can consume a large share of the founder’s week. It also says customer success gives the clearest feedback on what users need and improves later hires by stabilizing runway and generating referrals.

## Reading notes

- At around $8K MRR, churn can erase growth fast enough that more feature shipping does not solve the main problem.
- If support is taking about 15 hours a week, handing that work off frees a large part of the founder’s capacity.
- Customer success surfaces qualitative feedback about why users leave, what they use, and what they would pay more for.
- A customer-success hire can make later hires more effective by lowering churn, informing product work, and supporting future marketing or sales.
- The recommendation changes if voluntary monthly churn is under about 3% and support is light.
- In that case, the article says the bottleneck is acquisition, so the first hire should be a marketer.
- The decision depends on measuring voluntary churn and weekly support hours before writing a job description.
