Tunguz scored 7.85 out of 10 on his 2025 predictions and follows up with twelve for 2026, mostly about AI agents reshaping infrastructure, spending, and the web itself.

## Reading notes

- Businesses start paying more for AI agents than for people on rote tasks, following the pattern already seen with Waymo rides costing 31% more than Uber yet growing in demand.
- 2026 becomes a record liquidity year: SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic, Stripe, and Databricks IPO, some among the ten largest offerings ever, while fear of AI disruption drives over $25B in defensive acquisitions.
- Vector databases resurge as multimodal and world/state-space models need new data architectures connecting foundation models to enterprise data.
- Citing METR data on AI task duration doubling every 7 months, frontier models move from completing hour-long tasks to running 8+ hour autonomous workstreams by late 2026.
- AI budgets face scrutiny for the first time, pushing adoption of smaller, specialized, open-source models that get 10x cost reductions.
- Google pulls ahead by breadth rather than any single breakthrough, spanning frontier models, on-device inference, video generation, open-source weights, and search, forcing OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI to specialize.
- Engineering, security, and data observability converge into one discipline, a trend Tunguz says he flagged in his 2025 predictions.
- Stablecoins reach 30% of international payments by December, displacing SWIFT rails for a share of B2B volume as regulatory clarity improves.
- Agent query volume, an order of magnitude above human levels, forces a redesign of transactional and analytical database architecture.
- Data center buildout hits 3.5% of US GDP, a scale Tunguz compares to the historical railroad expansion, with private credit market strain as the main brake on further building.
- The web shifts to agent-first design: documentation and sites built for agentic research first, people second.
- The x402 protocol revives HTTP's dormant 402 status code for real-time agent payments, and Cloudflare's position in web infrastructure makes it the natural chokepoint, a concentration Tunguz expects to draw pushback from larger players.
