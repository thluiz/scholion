The post argues that the first few weeks in a new job matter because they shape how colleagues see you and what chances you have to succeed. It recommends starting with a solid plan, especially if you are changing careers or working remotely.

## Reading notes

- The first day, week, and month leave a lasting impression and shape the mental model others build of your work.
- Aim to come across as authentic, professional, competent, and enjoyable.
- Authenticity here means honesty and transparency at work, not just being the same person in every part of life.
- Professionalism matters because it affects how people treat you and how they judge your ability to handle setbacks.
- Competency means showing the skills needed for the job now and being willing to learn what you do not yet know.
- Enjoyability is about being pleasant to interact with, whether through genuine enthusiasm, stories, or simply being a good human in difficult moments.
- Keep expectations realistic, because major improvements in the first month, quarter, or year may not be welcome or possible.
- Talk with a supervisor about what success should look like and what timing is expected.
- Keep a notepad for questions, unclear tasks, and access issues so you can bring them up and cross them off.
- Draw diagrams of processes and systems to test how well you understand them.
- Explain a system to another person, or even to a pet or dashboard, to expose gaps in your understanding.
- Be patient, because onboarding is often incomplete, outdated, or poorly documented, and coworkers are usually busy.
- The post ends by inviting other people to share what has worked for them during job transitions.
