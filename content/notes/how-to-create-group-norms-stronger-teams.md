The article defines group norms as informal and formal rules that guide how team members interact, shaping predictability, shared expectations, and ultimately team culture. It argues that while every group develops norms, deliberately creating and revisiting them leads to clearer communication, easier conflict resolution, faster onboarding, and healthier work habits. The text then details a practical process: get broad buy-in, reflect on current and past experiences, write specific and measurable norms, secure explicit agreement, gently enforce them, and treat them as living agreements. Examples from companies like Google, Doist, REI, and The Bush School illustrate how intentional norms support engagement, feedback quality, and psychological safety.
