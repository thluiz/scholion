Developers are not opposed to marketing as such. The post says they respond badly to gimmicks, sleazy sales tactics, irrelevant messaging, and approaches that treat them as customers instead of people with real problems. It also notes that developers already use forms of marketing such as blogging, guides, talks, and word of mouth.

## Reading notes

- The strongest developer-led marketing often comes from developers themselves through newsletters, blogs, screencasts, podcasts, events, and books.
- A common mistake is using the broad label “developers” without defining which developers are actually being targeted.
- Marketers need to talk to developers, observe them, and learn their ecosystem instead of making assumptions from a few conversations.
- If a team asks for an email or other action, it should first have earned trust through useful content and easy access to information.
- Messaging should address real problems and the outcomes users want, not only features, speed claims, or specs.
- When everyone in a company describes the product differently, the marketing strategy becomes misaligned and users feel friction.
- Copying competitor tactics without fitting them into a larger strategy can make a company look unfocused.
- Internal teams such as support, technical writing, and developer relations can also help shape and distribute marketing messages.
- The post closes with questions that push marketers to reduce friction, understand audience behavior, and identify what users value and need.
- Its overall claim is that honest, transparent marketing works better when it starts with listening and learning from the target audience.
