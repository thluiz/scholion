The guide frames Todoist as a system for getting work out of your head and into an organized workflow. It walks through adding tasks, setting due dates, grouping work into projects, and using views like Inbox, Today, and Next 7 Days to keep track of what needs attention.

It also covers priority levels, reminders, comments, shared projects, browser and email capture, calendar syncing, labels, filters, favorites, productivity tracking, Karma, themes, and integrations.

## Reading notes

- Todoist is described as a simple place to add and complete tasks, with more features available over time.
- The guide recommends building a habit of capturing tasks as soon as they come up.
- Due dates can be entered in natural language, and recurring due dates reset automatically after completion.
- Inbox, Today, and Next 7 Days are the main task views for sorting and reviewing work.
- Projects are used for bigger goals, team work, trips, and other multi-step work.
- Sub-tasks, sections, and sub-projects help break down and group larger projects.
- Priority levels P1 to P4 sort tasks by importance, with P1 at the top of Today and Next 7 Days.
- Reminders can be automatic or manual, including time-based and location-based reminders.
- Task comments can store notes and files for individual tasks or for entire projects.
- Shared projects let people collaborate, assign tasks, and discuss details in comments.
- Browser extensions let users save web pages as tasks, and email plugins or forwarding let users turn emails into tasks.
- Todoist can sync with calendars, including Google Calendar two-way sync.
- Labels and filters are presented as Premium features for grouping and narrowing down tasks.
- Favorites keep important projects, labels, or filters visible in the navigation pane.
- Productivity view and Karma track completed tasks, goals, and streaks.
- Themes and integrations customize the app and connect it with other tools.
