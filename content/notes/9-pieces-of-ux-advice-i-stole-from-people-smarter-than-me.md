The post gathers nine UX lessons the author says he learned from other designers and researchers. It argues for observing what people create, prioritizing mobile-first design, using empathy, testing early, studying human psychology, and shaping products to make life better.

## Reading notes

- Observe what people create, not only what they say or do, because making reveals deeper insights.
- Design for mobile first, since mobile is central to major services and a growing part of media use.
- Use empathy to see problems from the user’s point of view and notice small changes that can improve an experience.
- Do not claim to design experience itself; design for experience instead.
- Talk to real users often, because without feedback designers become ignorant and blind to their own assumptions.
- Test designs as early as possible so problems can be found before they cost too many resources.
- Study how the human mind works, then apply psychological principles to design in an ethical way.
- Focus on whether a product solves a problem users care about and makes interaction feel simple and relevant.
- Design for scanning, with clear sections, visible click targets, visual hierarchy, grids, short paragraphs, and bullet lists.
