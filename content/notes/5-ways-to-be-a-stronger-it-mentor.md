The article argues that mentoring benefits both sides, and it uses one study plus a manager’s advice to show why mentoring can affect career growth, communication, and access to new skills. It then lays out five habits that make IT mentoring work better.

## Reading notes

- Mentoring can help the mentee advance and can also help the mentor’s career trajectory and pay.
- Mentors may become better communicators and listeners, and they can learn from younger employees’ skill sets.
- A mentor should be realistic about time limits, workload, and the number of mentees taken on.
- The mentor and mentee should talk early about goals, career hopes, availability, and how often they will communicate.
- The mentor should make clear that there is time to ask questions and should not leave the mentee assuming otherwise.
- Trust matters on both sides, including privacy, dedication, and receptiveness to feedback.
- Open communication should include agreed boundaries and preferred channels, while allowing for unexpected situations.
