The article lays out financial priorities for people in their 20s and early 30s. It says to face debt directly, organize repayment by interest rate, automate saving and retirement contributions, keep investment fees low, and negotiate pay early in a career. It also asks readers to decide what they want to save for, including a home, children, and retirement, because those goals require trade-offs.

## Reading notes

- Make a debt repayment plan by listing what you owe, at what rate, and to whom, then pay higher-interest debt first.
- Automate savings and retirement contributions so monthly deposits happen before the money gets spent.
- Watch investment and banking fees, and keep investment expense ratios low.
- Ask for more pay when starting a job, because early salary growth affects lifetime earnings.
- Focus on lifestyle choices that affect spending, including where you live, what you buy, and impulse purchases.
- Invest earlier instead of holding too much cash, and raise retirement contributions gradually if needed.
- Treat saving as a monthly bill, at least up to the employer match if you have a 401(k).
- Open an IRA or Roth IRA if you do not have a good 401(k), and keep investments simple and diversified.
- Build an emergency fund and decide what other goals matter, such as a down payment or a 529 account.
- Consider whether buying a house fits your life, since homeownership may limit relocation and is not always the best investment.
- Take bigger risks only after building a solid financial foundation.
- Plan for the costs of having children, including medical expenses, time off work, childcare, and the long-term effect on earnings.
- Accept money mistakes, fix what can be fixed, and keep going.
