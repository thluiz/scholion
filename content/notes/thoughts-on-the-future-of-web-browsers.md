The post argues for a browser base kept separate from Mozilla, with Firefox forks working together to maintain it. It says AI integrations and other user-hostile features should be removed entirely, not just disabled, and that the current browser trend is moving toward a future built for third-party AI agents rather than people.

## Reading notes

- A thread on Mastodon called for Firefox forks to cooperate on a hardfork isolated from Mozilla.
- The base browser project grew out of that thread and later conversations.
- The author built a patch that removed all AI integration code from Firefox.
- The current base browser work uses patches on Firefox 153 ESR to strip user-hostile features from the code base.
- The author argues that deleting these features is better than soft-forking them out.
- They think the effort may be hard to sustain long term without pooled funding and developer work.
- They describe Mozilla and Firefox as moving toward a browser shaped for AI agents.
- Temporary ways to rely less on Firefox include offline browsing with OpenZIM, standalone applications, and feed readers.
- The author does not want to spend all their effort just resisting browser decline.
- They say web standards have become saturated by commercial interests over the last decade-plus.
- They recall early-2000s browser ideas about feeds, blogging, tagging, and open comments.
- They mention browsers such as Amaya and Flock as examples of a browser meant for creation as well as consumption.
- They have also experimented with small browsers that support Gemini, RSS, and other less polluted web technologies.
- The post ends by saying that maybe this could be one future, and that new noise is needed.
