The article says an unproductive day can come from an unexpected event or from not feeling well, and it treats the problem as a short-term setback when a work system already exists. It recommends getting back to normal by changing the next day’s pace and rebuilding focus before trying to catch up.

## Reading notes

- go to bed earlier after a wasted day, then wake up early the next morning
- begin the morning with something you enjoy instead of going straight into work
- use a “productive mood anchor,” such as coffee, tea, or a personal workweek ritual
- take about twenty minutes to refocus, turn off email and phone, and clear guilt about being unproductive
- make a catch-up list and spread the tasks over about a week instead of trying to do everything at once
- start with a small 15–30 minute task or with the most unpleasant task first
- stay focused by using the Pomodoro Technique, silencing notifications, blocking distracting sites, and working in full-screen mode
