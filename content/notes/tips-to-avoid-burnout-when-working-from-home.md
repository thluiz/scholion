The page argues that people working from home can be more vulnerable to burnout because work and family demands overlap and the workday can stretch into the evening. It presents practical ways to keep boundaries, protect well-being, and keep work from spilling into the rest of life.

## Reading notes

- Remote workers may feel pressure to answer email after hours and let the workday extend into the evening.
- Exercise breaks can help replace caffeine dependence with natural energy and a mental boost.
- Keeping a schedule helps preserve boundaries between work, family, and rest, even if the office routine itself is not realistic at home.
- Swapping chores with a partner can make the mental load of housework more visible and easier to share.
- Turning off devices after work and disabling notifications can reduce distraction and support emotional well-being.
- A longer-term view of a career can help avoid short-term, emotionally driven decisions.
- Workplace stress is described as harmful to health, and the page links burnout to wider health costs.
