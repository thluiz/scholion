The article argues that getting promoted requires a different approach than getting hired. It organizes that approach into three pillars: technical competence, professional communication and relationships, and getting feedback and credit for your work. It also says the focus changes by career stage, from junior developer to principal engineer.

## Reading notes

- Promotion depends on skills, value, and relationships inside the organization, not only on coding ability.
- Technical competence includes understanding the link between tech and business, explaining your work process, and staying invested in learning.
- The technical focus changes by level, from owning part of a codebase as a junior developer to handling big-picture design, writing, and ownership as a principal engineer.
- Professional communication and relationships matter because managers and reviewers want someone who makes their work easier.
- The article values time management, helping others succeed, taking responsibility for product outcomes, and professional communication.
- Mentorship, manager alignment, explicit feedback, and not alienating yourself are presented as ways to strengthen your position for promotion.
- Leadership should be genuine to the person and can take different forms.
- A strategic brag document is recommended to record accomplishments, contributions, mentoring, improvements, and learning.
- The document should use metrics and a problem-solving framing to show what was done, the result, and why it supports promotion.
- The article says quality matters more than quantity and that goals should include more than a job title, such as responsibilities, freedoms, or leadership roles.
