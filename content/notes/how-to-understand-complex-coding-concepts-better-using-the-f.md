The article presents the Feynman Technique as a way to understand difficult coding ideas by reducing them to simple language. It says the method helps you find gaps in your understanding and explain a topic clearly enough for a child to follow.

## Reading notes

- The technique is described as a fast way to turn a complex topic into something you can explain simply to other people.
- Richard Feynman is presented as the creator of the method, and the article says he favored asking hard questions and fully understanding an idea.
- The benefits listed are deeper understanding, faster learning, better recall, improved teaching, and stronger critical thinking.
- The four steps are to choose a topic, explain it to a 12-year-old, review the explanation, and simplify it.
- For the first step, the article uses Javascript loops as the example topic and says to write what you know while you keep learning.
- To practice the second step, it suggests technical writing, online communities, and speaking to an imaginary audience, while noting that a real audience can ask questions.
- The third step focuses on reviewing what was hard to explain and studying again until the missing parts become clear.
- The fourth step asks you to repeat the explanation with the new understanding and simplify the parts that were still unclear.
- The article says the method works best for topics that are hard to understand and is less useful when you already understand the concept.
