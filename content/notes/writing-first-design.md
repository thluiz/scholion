Writing first helps define the project before visual work begins. Downey says it forces a clearer understanding of the client, the audience, the message, and the decisions needed for the final design.

## Reading notes

- Inexperienced designers often begin with tools and layouts, while experienced ones may start with research or paper sketches.
- If a client has no copy yet, writing the site text becomes a way to learn the client’s business, history, audience, and goals.
- If the client already has copy, the designer should read it critically, check tone, length, web suitability, and whether it can be split into coherent pages.
- If the client’s copy is weak, rewriting should be treated as part of design rather than as a separate task.
- For apps and interface elements, writing helps define affordances through prompts, buttons, labels, error messages, and other interface text.
- The article suggests drafting several text variations before moving to visual layout.
- For graphics-heavy posters with little or no text, writing down the goal for five or ten minutes helps clarify what the piece should do.
- The process described is to write first, then sketch, then explore aesthetics in software, then arrange everything, and keep editing in context.
