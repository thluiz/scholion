J.T. O'Donnell says the online job hunt has become degrading and depressing, with employers posting fewer roles, ghost jobs, and waves of AI-driven applications. Her view is that job seekers in 2026 should stop relying on online applications and focus on being recruited.

## Reading notes

- O'Donnell says applying online has become one of the most degrading and depressing parts of job seeking.
- She says employers have pulled back on hiring and that ghost jobs and AI-generated submissions have made the market worse.
- Her recommended approach is to build an online presence and make strategic connections so recruiters notice you.
- She suggests following 20 or 40 target companies on Glassdoor and LinkedIn and commenting thoughtfully on what they post.
- She says engaging with company content can help a candidate appear in recruiting results.
- Another tactic is to post on LinkedIn, connect with people at target companies, and have conversations about work, the industry, and skill sets.
- She describes this as a new form of networking that can lead to direct messages and introductions.
- In one example, a project manager used a daily routine of posting, tagging companies, and reaching out to employees, and a recruiter called him on day 17 with an unposted job.
- O'Donnell says content that works well on LinkedIn includes industry observations, contrarian takes, before-and-after comparisons, listicles, and personal work stories.
