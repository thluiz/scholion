The article argues that software quality depends on both technical decisions and team behavior. It ties long-term success to solution quality, trust in the codebase, realistic commitments, respectful collaboration, constructive code review, and mentoring.

## Reading notes

- The author says that the quality of the solution influences long-term success, because a product should not become slower, unpredictable, or different from what UX proposed.
- He states that code with tests is more reliable and that tests help detect bugs, explain the purpose of the code, and avoid unnecessary code.
- The text argues that the developer needs to learn to say no and to negotiate when deadlines, requirements, or deliverables are unrealistic.
- He considers it unprofessional to make a commitment that cannot be fulfilled or to pretend to deliver something to please the manager.
- The author says that respect among colleagues is part of the developer's duty and that conflicts should be discussed and resolved as soon as possible.
- He advocates support and mentoring when someone on the team faces difficulties, in addition to celebrating wins and sharing failures as a group.
- The text treats code review as a chance to improve the implementation constructively, explaining why a suggestion is better and using examples.
- He states that criticism should not be made in a judgmental or harsh way, because code review is a team exercise.
- The author says that mentoring is also part of the job and helps junior developers grow.
- The conclusion links good software to good teams and says that each person needs to maintain the right attitude to deliver code with the appropriate quality.
