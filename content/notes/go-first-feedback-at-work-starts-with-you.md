The article argues that honest feedback at work depends on leaders being open first. If managers want employees to speak candidly, they need to show that it is safe to do so by taking the first step themselves.

## Reading notes

- The central idea is that people are unlikely to give honest feedback if the leader is not open first.
- The article uses the image of jumping into a swimming pool to show that leaders should be the first to act.
- “Going first” means being forthcoming and candid so employees feel safe speaking up.
- One way to do this is to share what you are struggling with, which models honesty and lowers employee anxiety.
- Another way is to challenge your own opinion in front of the team and invite dissenting views.
- Leaders should also publicly commend vulnerability when employees share frank opinions.
- Recognizing honest disagreement helps set the expectation that direct feedback is welcome.
