The post says managers can support remote employees by asking deeper check-in questions, trusting people to do the work, and noticing where management itself gets in the way. It also stresses psychological safety, quicker ways to share progress, empathy in written communication, mutual support among teammates, and self-care for leaders.

## Reading notes

- Ask questions that go beyond “How are you doing?” so people can answer more honestly about their situation, energy, fears, and workload.
- Give an abundance of trust instead of relying on surveillance, because control can reduce intrinsic motivation and hurt performance.
- Check whether unclear expectations, missing context, unrealistic demands, or frequent follow-up are making it harder for employees to do their work.
- Make it safe to speak up by modeling openness about mistakes and gathering feedback from the team.
- Replace long status update meetings with short asynchronous written updates so everyone stays informed without losing time on Zoom.
- Use empathy in written communication, especially when tone can be misread, and adjust goals and flexibility to current circumstances.
- Create chances for teammates to support one another through social activities or non-work channels.
- Take care of yourself so you have the energy to support the team, and start with one or two changes instead of trying to do everything at once.
