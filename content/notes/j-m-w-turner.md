Joseph Mallord William Turner was an English Romantic painter born in London in 1775 and died there in 1851. The page presents him as one of the greatest British painters of the 19th century, especially for his treatment of light, atmosphere and colour in landscape painting.

He began as an architectural draughtsman and watercolourist, entered the Royal Academy at 14, and became a Royal Academician at 27. His work moved from topographical and classical subjects toward more abstract landscapes, seascapes, and scenes shaped by storms, fog, rivers, the sea, Venice, and the Industrial Revolution. He also worked in printmaking, especially mezzotint, and through the _Liber Studiorum_ tried to raise the prestige of landscape painting. Turner produced about 550 oil paintings and over 2,000 watercolours, left a large bequest to the nation, and inspired early impressionists such as Monet and Pissarro.
