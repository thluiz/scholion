The post argues that aspiring developers should focus on solving real problems rather than building projects without a clear purpose. It also says that getting hired depends on skills, but building a career depends on character, humility, and the ability to work well with others.

It also stresses communication with teammates and clients, especially when explaining technical work or interpreting client needs. The author ends by encouraging developers to enjoy the process, take breaks, and keep other sources of joy outside coding.

## Reading notes

- Developers are hired to solve problems, not only to code, so side projects should be built around a concrete need.
- A project based on a local business or a company of interest can help when applying for a job because it shows relevance to that niche.
- Skills may get someone hired, but character and culture fit matter for navigating the workplace and building a career.
- Good relationships with colleagues support growth because coworkers can offer more experience and resources to learn from.
- Staying humble and open to learning is presented as important, while acting like a know-it-all is presented as harmful.
- Communication matters in meetings, documentation, and everyday work with the dev team.
- Developers also need to explain client needs clearly, especially when the client does not know exactly what they want.
- The post encourages balancing coding with breaks, friends, hobbies, and other sources of joy.
