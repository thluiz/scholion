Remote work is presented as more than a convenience. The author argues that, when it is well supported, it can help with accessibility and inclusion by giving people more space to think, communicate asynchronously, and work at a pace that fits how they function best.

The text links remote work to reduced anxiety, better mental health, and improved productivity, especially for introverts and neurodivergent people. It also stresses the value of good documentation, shared agendas, and advance notice for meetings, since last-minute or surprise formats can be hard for some people.
