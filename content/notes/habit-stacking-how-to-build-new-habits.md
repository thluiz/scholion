The article says habit stacking uses an existing habit as a cue for a new one. Because current habits already have strong neural connections, linking a new behavior to them makes it more likely to stick.

It gives a simple formula, several examples, and advice on choosing a trigger that is specific, actionable, and frequent enough to match the habit you want to build.

## Reading notes

- The brain strengthens connections that are used often and prunes those that are not.
- Current habits can serve as cues for new behaviors.
- Habit stacking is presented as a form of implementation intention.
- The basic formula is: after or before a current habit, do a new habit.
- Small habits can be chained together into larger routines.
- The trigger should fit the time, place, and frequency of the new habit.
- A good trigger is concrete and easy to act on right away.
- Vague goals like “read more” or “eat better” are too unclear to guide action.
