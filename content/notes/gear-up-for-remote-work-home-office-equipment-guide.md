Eric Elliott argues that setting up a remote work space involves many buying decisions, and he gives his current recommendations for remote development. He focuses on gear that supports CPU- and memory-intensive work, clearer video calls, better sound, more screen space, and a more comfortable workstation.

## Reading notes

- Remote software development can strain CPU and memory, especially during video recording, transcoding, screen sharing, video chats, compiling, and browser use.
- He recommends at least 16 GB of RAM, with 32 GB as a better option, and says a current MacBook Pro works well.
- He says newer MacBooks may need a USB-C hub to connect USB 3, HDMI, and memory cards.
- He prefers the Apple Magic Keyboard and Apple Magic Trackpad for productivity, and recommends a real mouse for gaming, such as the Logitech G Pro Wireless.
- For video calls, he prefers an external dynamic microphone over a built-in mic, and names the Shure Beta 58A with a Focusrite Scarlet 2i2 interface and a boom stand.
- He says a larger monitor setup can help coding productivity, and he favors an ultra wide monitor with a desk-mountable stand.
- For noisy environments, he suggests muting when not speaking and using closed-back headphones, noise-canceling headphones, earbuds, or open-back headphones depending on the use case.
- He says good lighting matters for video calls, especially when a bright window is behind you, and recommends adjustable LED panels.
- He recommends a good chair for posture, comfort, and health, naming the Herman Miller Aeron chair.
- He also recommends a desk that fits dual monitors and can be used sitting or standing, plus an anti-fatigue mat for standing work.
