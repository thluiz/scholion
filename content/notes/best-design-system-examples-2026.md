The page surveys design system examples to show what designers can learn from them when building or improving a system. It explains design systems as reusable components plus usage guidelines, then reviews how different systems handle consistency, scaling, accessibility, and documentation.

## Reading notes

- Design systems help teams make interface decisions more efficiently by providing predefined options and guidelines.
- IBM Carbon uses color, not shadows, to show elevation, and documents larger patterns like logins, forms, and filtering.
- Atlassian uses shadows in light mode and shadows plus color in dark mode, with four elevation tokens and clear token naming.
- Adobe Spectrum scales components and typography on mobile, and includes guidance for copywriting, motion, and inclusive design.
- Google Material is flexible and complex, with a strong color and theming system.
- Shopify Polaris offers many visual examples and focuses on e-commerce flows and product pages.
- Github Primer provides strong developer documentation and a CSS framework built for code-centric platforms.
- Apple HIG is text heavy, less detailed, and tightly integrated with Apple hardware and software.
- Gov.uk emphasizes accessibility, inclusion, user testing, and simple patterns.
- Microsoft Fluent 2 keeps guidance organized by platform and covers web, iOS, Android, and Windows.
- GitLab Pajamas includes community collaboration through an issue tracker and gives code examples in Vue.js and Rails.
- Twilio Paste focuses on messaging interfaces and WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility.
- Salesforce Lightning includes motion guidance under the name kinetics.
- Vercel Geist is minimalist and performance-driven, but has limited usage guidance and accessibility issues.
- BBC GEL focuses on accessibility and editorial consistency across a large media product set.
- Mozilla Protocol is concise, open source, and optimized for the web.
