The article argues that resume writing should be selective. Instead of listing every skill, job, or activity, a candidate should present one coherent narrative that matches the role. A clear focus makes the resume easier to remember and more convincing.

## Reading notes

- Overstuffed resumes make it hard for employers to remember anything because they try to cover too much at once.
- A resume should emphasize the one identity or skill set that matters most for the job.
- A clear narrative can help a candidate seem like a stronger fit for a specific role.
- Resumes longer than two pages are presented as unprofessional, especially for technology companies, corporations, and startups.
- It is better to show ability with work samples, portfolios, or real help on a task than to claim excellence in a long resume.
- A lengthy or unfocused resume can make a candidate seem desperate.
- The advice is to tailor the resume to the job so it tells a story that makes the employer want to meet you.
