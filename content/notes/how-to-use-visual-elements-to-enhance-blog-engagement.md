The post argues that blog readers usually scan instead of reading every word, so long blocks of text reduce engagement. To hold attention, it recommends short paragraphs and a steady use of visuals across the post.

It then names four visual formats that can help: graphs and infographics to support claims and improve recall, original photos to add authenticity and distinguish a post, annotated screenshots to explain steps, and videos to raise time on page. Captions are also presented as useful for video views and completion.

## Reading notes

- Readers usually skim blog content rather than reading it word for word.
- People may read only about 20% of a page, and a seven-minute post is roughly 1,600 words.
- Long paragraphs and large text blocks make posts harder to scan.
- Visuals break up content and give readers points to stop and digest key ideas.
- Visuals should appear consistently, not clustered at the start and then absent for long stretches.
- Graphs help validate a message, make posts feel more legitimate, and improve retention.
- Original infographics can be made with free tools like Canva and may bring traffic through reference links.
- Original photos help content feel more unique and more authentic.
- Annotated screenshots with arrows, boxes, circles, or text help explain how-to steps.
- Skitch is mentioned as a free tool for screenshot annotation.
- Videos can increase time spent on a page and can be embedded from older YouTube content.
- Captions are presented as increasing views and making complete viewing more likely.
