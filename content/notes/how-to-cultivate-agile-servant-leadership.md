The page argues that servant leadership gives project managers influence without direct authority and fits Agile values centered on people, collaboration, and value creation. It presents the scrum master as a servant leader who helps teams connect with the organization’s vision and become autonomous and cross-functional.

It then lays out a change framework with three stages. In unfreezing, leaders listen actively, identify needs, heal distress, and communicate a clear vision. In the change stage, they use facts and persuasion to involve people in decisions, build on strengths, and commit teams to action plans. In refreezing, they reinforce accountability, community, and consistent new behaviors.

## Reading notes

- Servant leadership is presented as a way to lead through influence rather than formal authority.
- Agile and servant leadership share an emphasis on people, collaboration, and creating value.
- In Scrum, the scrum master is described as a servant leader who connects teams with organizational vision.
- The framework for change combines servant leadership with Lewin’s unfreeze-change-refreeze model.
- The first stage depends on active listening, empathy, foresight, and emotional support.
- During change, leaders persuade with data and reasoning while involving people in decisions.
- In refreezing, leaders reinforce accountability, community, and the organization’s responsibility for sustaining change.
- The framework is presented as helping project managers reduce resistance to change and support team commitment.
