Addy Osmani advises breaking tasks you tend to procrastinate on into smaller, low‑energy actions to make them easier to start. He lists several reasons tasks invite procrastination and notes that understanding these reasons can help address the problem. He also speculates that task boards like Trello or GitHub Projects might support this with labels, while pointing out that what triggers procrastination varies from person to person.

## Reading notes

- Suggestion to split procrastinated tasks into smaller, low‑energy components to make progress more manageable.
- States that people procrastinate more when a task feels boring, frustrating, difficult, ambiguous or unstructured.
- Adds that lacking personal meaning or lacking reward (such as not being fun) can also make procrastination more likely.
- Mentions that knowing the specific reasons a task feels aversive is helpful, referencing procrastination research by @procrastwitate.
- Says he has not seen a tool dedicated to this, but existing boards like Trello or GitHub Projects might approximate it through labels.
- Notes, citing @jyzg, that procrastination triggers are subjective, since tasks one person delays can seem interesting to another.
