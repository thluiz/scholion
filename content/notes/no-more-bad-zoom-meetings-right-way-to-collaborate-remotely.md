Remote collaboration can use the same problem-solving principles as in-person work if teams bring in the right technology and structure. The text says remote work has shown people can be agile, adaptable, and resilient, even if it is not everyone’s first choice.

## Reading notes

- Good collaboration starts with empathy for the end user and for teammates, including their changing work and care responsibilities.
- A facilitator helps remote sessions by clarifying who speaks, keeping to a shared agenda, and making room for quieter people.
- Starting with individual brainstorming, then moving into small breakout groups, can make remote idea sessions more productive.
- Using different constraints in brainstorming can shift how people approach the problem and the solution.
- Bringing in people outside the core team can reduce bias and add a fresh perspective to the discussion.
