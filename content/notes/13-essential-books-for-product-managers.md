The article says experienced product managers still need guidance and that a strong appetite for learning matters because the way they think affects the way they work. It presents 13 books as useful reading for mid-level product managers, covering product development, data, design, user psychology, leadership, goals, and decision-making.

## Reading notes

- The piece frames career development as something that remains important even after the early years, especially in product management, which changes quickly and spans several disciplines.
- It says the best product managers keep learning and that curiosity is a major asset because thinking shapes work.
- Inspired is presented as a core book for product managers, with observations on product development and examples from companies like Apple, Google, Netflix, and the BBC.
- Naked Statistics is recommended because product managers use data to make decisions, win stakeholder support, and understand what the data really says.
- Beautiful Evidence is included for its treatment of design, using historical illustrations to show what makes design effective.
- Badass: Making Users Awesome argues for a product approach that helps users solve problems and brings product, engineering, sales, marketing, and customer success together.
- Hooked is recommended for its focus on user psychology and behavioral design, with examples from Instagram, Pinterest, and Twitter.
- The Hard Thing About Hard Things is described as a strong overview of startup life and contemporary product development, especially around the difficulty of running a company.
- Measure What Matters centers on OKRs and on setting ambitious goals that separate good companies from great ones.
- Escaping the Build Trap warns against focusing on output instead of outcomes and argues that product managers should not have to choose between user value and business goals.
- Product Leadership distinguishes product management from product leadership and defines the role as staying connected to the why while letting teams shape the how.
- Meaningful uses the Story Strategy to prioritize end users and gives examples from Canva, GoPro, Harry's, Khan Academy, and Nike.
- Sprint presents design sprints as a way to solve big problems and test ideas in five days through rapid iteration and direct user feedback.
- Selling Blue Elephants focuses on rule-developing experimentation and says prototypes and customer research can be used to learn faster and improve workflows.
- The Halo Effect is recommended as a warning about cognitive bias and the need to examine product success with more scrutiny.
