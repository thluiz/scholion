The article links animation timing to how people notice, interpret, and trust interface changes. It says interaction design works in the space between states, so timing affects whether motion feels snappy, sluggish, understandable, or confusing.

## Reading notes

- Interaction design focuses on the change between states, and time is one of its key components.
- People read motion through visual cues such as contrast, scale, movement, and position, and timing helps them judge whether something seems natural.
- Design needs to balance speed, understandability, and confidence so people can complete tasks efficiently.
- Attention happens first, then awareness, and the article places the minimum time to understand stimuli at around 150ms.
- The eye makes repeated fixations of about 350ms, which the article treats as a practical upper limit before focus starts drifting.
- Long waits can make an experience feel broken, so intermediate states like loading screens and skeleton templates can reassure users.
- The article presents 150ms to 350ms as a guideline range for product animations, not as a single fixed number.
