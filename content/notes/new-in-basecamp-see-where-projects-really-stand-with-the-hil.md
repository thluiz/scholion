Basecamp presents the Hill Chart as a new way to track project status when to-do counts are not enough. It is meant to show where work really stands, especially on creative and software projects where progress depends on unknowns, not just completed tasks.

## Reading notes

- Basecamp says to-do lists are useful for avoiding missed work, but they do not always show the bigger picture on large projects.
- The Hill Chart is meant to answer questions about whether a project is on time, whether the team is working on the right tasks, and what should happen now or later.
- The article argues that progress cannot be reduced to a percentage because tasks are not equivalent and new tasks may appear as work continues.
- Basecamp uses a hill metaphor with an uphill phase for figuring out the approach and a downhill phase for execution after the problems are solved.
- The chart is a human judgment of how the work feels, from unknown to known, rather than a computer-generated calculation.
- In the example, Basecamp creates a to-do list for each independently trackable piece of work, turns on Hill Chart tracking, and places each list as a dot on the hill.
- Updating the chart creates a history snapshot, gives managers context, and supports asynchronous commentary, comments, and Boosts on updates.
- The article says the Hill Chart can also reveal when a list should be split into smaller lists because part of the work is solved and part is still uphill.
- The system is flexible per list, so tracked lists can be mixed with regular to-do lists.
- Basecamp describes the Hill Chart as showing work from unknown to known to done, with the goal of making teams more organized and aligned.
