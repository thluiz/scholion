The piece argues that startup culture has narrowed success into domination, unicorns, and venture-backed scale. It pushes back against that atmosphere and says a startup can be built for independence, direct service to customers, long-term relationships, and a life that is not consumed by work.

## Reading notes

- Basecamp is presented as a simple project collaboration tool sold by subscription, built to make work a little better without disrupting everything.
- The author says the current atmosphere of disruption crowds out other motives for starting a company and can be poisonous.
- He criticizes the way startup language turns investors into angels, VCs into a holy step, and liquidity events into a kind of financial salvation.
- Taking money from investors is described as creating more bosses and obligations, not just support.
- He asks founders to reconsider whether chasing a fifty-billion-dollar outcome is really what they want.
- He argues that success has been defined too narrowly around the San Francisco model and the pursuit of unicorn status.
- He lists his own motives for Basecamp: working for himself, making a product people would pay for, putting down roots, seeking financial stability, and having a life beyond work.
- The company’s path is described as a side business at first, with a slow audience build and no plans for domination, IPO, or acquisition.
- He says Basecamp could succeed without eradicating competitors and that business can work as an AND world rather than an OR world.
- The text argues that many traditional markers of startup success are less important than they are made to seem.
- It says people in capital-heavy startup circles act in their own interest and use the language of community and progress to justify that interest.
- The web is described as the greatest entrepreneurial platform ever invented because it has low barriers to entry, broad reach, and no need for permission.
- The closing message is to examine motivations, reject money if needed, and build something useful rather than chase domination.
