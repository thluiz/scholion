The page describes three engineering career paths, individual contributor, management, and executive, then focuses on the individual contributor track. It lists the progression from Engineer I through Engineer II, Engineer III, Staff Engineer, Principal Engineer, and Distinguished Engineer, with increasing responsibility, independence, and technical leadership.

## Reading notes

- A well-defined hierarchy helps engineers understand their career path and what they need to learn to progress.
- The article separates engineering careers into individual contributor, management, and executive paths.
- Engineer I starts as an intern or junior software engineer, focuses on technical skills, resolving code issues, and handling tasks assigned by the Team Lead.
- Engineer II usually has two to five years of experience and handles troubleshooting, code reviews, testing, and more complex tasks with less supervision.
- Engineer III, or senior software engineer, has five or more years of experience, knows multiple coding languages, works independently, and helps junior engineers.
- Staff Engineer is a high-level role with broad software development knowledge, strong coding and debugging skill, ownership of crucial technical components, and technical leadership.
- Principal Engineer makes key product decisions, helps decide who works on specific areas, and provides technical and professional leadership.
- Some companies add a senior principal engineer step before the top technical role.
- Distinguished Engineer is presented as the top technical role, with influence over technical strategy, industry standards, and company leadership.
- A clear hierarchy is said to improve clarity, motivation, engagement, retention, and career progression, even though companies may name or structure levels differently.
