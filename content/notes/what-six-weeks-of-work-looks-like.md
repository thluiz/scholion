Basecamp describes what its core product team of 4 programmers and 4 designers shipped over six weeks for the web and desktop app. The post lists updates to email notifications, automatic check-ins, file sharing, admin tools, navigation, and small interface changes, then notes a set of items that were nearly finished but not yet shipped.

## Reading notes

- The core product team was 4 programmers and 4 designers working in six-week cycles on the web and desktop app.
- Email notifications now batch together pings, to-do assignments, to-do reminders, and file upload notifications into a single digest.
- Automatic check-ins now give customers more control over when they are sent, including daily, every other day, monthly, and exact times.
- Desktop file sharing in Pings and Campfire chats now includes an explicit paperclip button, while drag-and-drop still works.
- Admins can delete comments from comment threads across messages, to-dos, documents, automatic check-ins, files, and schedule items.
- Users can resend invitations or send a direct link to anyone on Basecamp.
- The top area of messages, to-dos, documents, and files was simplified by combining menus and exposing the bookmark icon.
- The draft and publish buttons were rearranged and redesigned to make mistakes harder.
- Users can page through attachments in a thread with arrow keys or on-screen arrows.
- The first message or first to-do in an empty project now takes fewer steps to post.
- Typography updates improved spacing around titles, paragraphs, and lists.
- The admin side now centralizes people management on one screen with single-click permission changes and login links.
- A Jump screen now lets users go to the HQ, teams, projects, assignments, recent activity, bookmarks, and drafts from one place.
- Nearly finished work included a custom emoji picker, lazy-loading images, a quieter Hey! menu for automatic check-in notifications, and consistent dates outside the current year.
- The post says teams are usually two or three people, no dedicated project managers run projects, and work ships when it is ready rather than in a single big release.
