The article presents critical thinking as a disciplined skill that anyone can learn through diligence and practice. It focuses on evaluating a coworker's proposal by first understanding the problem clearly, then checking the evidence, measuring impact, testing whether the solution addresses the real issue, and looking for blind spots.

## Reading notes

- Clarify the problem and the suggestion before judging the proposal.
- Use a concrete example to confirm that you understand who is affected, what is affected, and how the proposal is meant to help.
- Do not accept the problem at face value; check the data and evidence behind it.
- Ask whether the issue is system-wide or limited to a specific case, and whether the metrics support the explanation.
- Separate symptoms from underlying causes, since a different fix may be better than the proposed one.
- Ask “So what?” to understand the impact and business consequences.
- Check whether the proposal addresses the root cause, creates unintended consequences, or only works as a short-term fix.
- Challenge assumptions by asking what could go wrong, whether the solution could backfire, whether it adds hidden costs, and whether bias is influencing your judgment.
- Treat critical thinking as a skill that gets stronger with repeated practice, even though some things will still be missed along the way.
