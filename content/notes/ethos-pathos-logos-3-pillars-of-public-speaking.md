Aristotle’s framework is presented as the basis for persuasive speaking. The article defines ethos as credibility, pathos as emotional connection, and logos as logical argument, then says these are the qualities a speech needs before an audience will accept the message.

It also explains that ethos depends on how the audience sees the speaker, pathos often comes through stories and other devices that trigger emotion, and logos depends on facts, statistics, and evidence. The article closes by saying Aristotle treated all three appeals as necessary, not just one.

## Reading notes

- Ethos is the audience’s perception of the speaker’s credibility, character, trustworthiness, and authority on the topic.
- Pathos is the emotional side of persuasion, created through words, visuals, stories, anecdotes, analogies, similes, and metaphors.
- Logos is logical argument, supported by facts, statistics, evidence, and a call to action that makes sense.
- The article says Aristotle’s *On Rhetoric* is the source of this three-part view of persuasion.
- It presents the three appeals as essential qualities a speech or presentation must have before the audience accepts the message.
- It says Aristotle did not treat logos as sufficient by itself; all three persuasive appeals are necessary.
