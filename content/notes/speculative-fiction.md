Speculative fiction is used as an umbrella term for more fantastical genres, including science fiction, fantasy, horror, weird fiction, supernatural fiction, superhero fiction, utopian and dystopian fiction, apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction, and alternate history. The page also says the term covers related static, motion, and virtual arts.

## Reading notes

- The category is presented as broad enough to include literature and related arts.
- Its history is described as ranging from ancient works to 21st-century works.
- The page links speculative fiction to ancient Greek drama, historiography, Shakespeare, and mythography.
- It says the modern English-language genre term is often attributed to Robert A. Heinlein.
- Heinlein first used the term as a synonym for science fiction, but later said it did not include fantasy.
- Earlier uses of the term are cited from 1889 and 1900.
- The term was popularized in the 1960s and early 1970s by Judith Merril and others in connection with New Wave science fiction.
- In the 2000s, it came into wider use as a collective term for a set of genres.
- The page says the term is sometimes used to express dissatisfaction with the limits of science fiction.
- It also mentions “suppositional fiction” as fiction built around an internally consistent world.
