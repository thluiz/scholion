The article lays out a freelancing workflow from first client contact to final delivery. It stresses agreeing on scope, price, timeline, and budget early, then using wireframes, design reviews, and staged development to keep the project aligned with the client’s expectations.

## Reading notes

- The workflow begins with the initial engagement, where scope of work and price are agreed.
- The initial discussion covers the project objective, expected quality, timeline, budget, and whether the budget matches the expected scope.
- The author says pricing can be hourly, per project, or value-based, and prefers value-based pricing.
- The article recommends taking 50% upfront and 50% on delivery.
- The wireframe and project plan are meant to align both sides on the final direction before detailed design begins.
- The wireframe stage includes example work and a simple high-level mockup of the website or app.
- For custom work, the process may include researching competitors to understand what the client wants.
- The design stage turns the agreed wireframe into a full design.
- The author recommends presenting the design in a meeting and explaining the choices behind colors, layouts, and structure.
- Development starts after design approval and may take about twice as long as the design for small projects.
- Development is split into an initial local build and then publishing a development server version for client access.
- The client should be shown progress through meetings and should also be trained to use the system during development.
- If extra work appears outside the original agreement, the article says it should be added for an added price.
- The final stage includes a last meeting, final training, the final invoice, and setting expectations for future paid work.
