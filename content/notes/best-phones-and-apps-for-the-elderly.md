The post says many seniors feel left behind by the digital revolution and may see smartphones as expensive, hard to trust, and too addictive. It suggests that the problem may be the phone itself, so a simpler device like a Jitterbug may be a better fit than a new Android phone.

If a senior does use a smartphone, the post recommends apps that help with practical needs such as remembering pills, appointments, and events, plus reading help like KNFB Reader for people with vision problems. It also points to social media, especially Facebook, Instagram, and Twitter, as tools for staying connected and engaged.

- Muitos idosos têm dificuldade com smartphones por causa do custo, da desconfiança na tecnologia e da falta de familiaridade com o digital.
