Claude Lorrain was a painter, draftsman, and engraver of the Baroque period, born in the Duchy of Lorraine and active mainly in Italy. The page describes him as one of the first major artists to focus on landscape painting, with works in which small figures, biblical or mythological scenes, and imagined architecture combine with sky, harbor, countryside, and sunlight.

His reputation was established at the end of the 1630s, with many commissions in Italy and abroad. The text also highlights his drawings, open-air studies, and the _Liber Veritatis_, as well as his later reception among English collectors and his influence on the idea of landscape as an autonomous genre.

## Reading notes

- He was born Claude Gellée, in Chamagne, in the Duchy of Lorraine, and spent most of his life in Italy.
- He was a painter, draftsman, and engraver of the Baroque period.
- The page presents him as one of the first major artists, outside seventeenth-century Dutch painting, to dedicate himself consistently to landscape.
- His landscapes often included a few small figures, linked to episodes from the Bible or classical mythology.
- At the end of the 1630s, he was already seen as the leading landscape painter in Italy and received high payments for his works.
- The page states that he was not, in general, innovative in landscape, except for introducing the sun and sunlight into many paintings.
- His patrons were mostly Italian, but after his death he became very popular among English collectors.
- He produced many pen drawings and monochrome wash drawings, as well as studies made outdoors.
- He organized a visual record of his works in the _Liber Veritatis_, with drawings of finished paintings.
- He made more than 40 engravings, generally simplified versions of paintings.
- He painted frescoes at the beginning of his career, now almost all lost.
- His early formation is presented by ancient biographies that differ from one another, and modern scholars prefer Sandrart's version or a combination of the two.
- According to the text, he lived his last years in Rome, on Via Paolina, and died on November 23, 1682.
- Later critical reception describes him as an exemplary landscape painter, and John Constable called him the most perfect landscape painter the world has ever seen.
