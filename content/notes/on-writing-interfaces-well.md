The post argues that interface text is not secondary to visual design. Once styling is stripped away, words remain, so writing needs care, clarity, and repeated editing.

It says designers often undervalue copy because it is not flashy, hard to do well, and assumed to be skimmed. The remedy is to write more clearly rather than more tersely, explain what actions mean, write like you are helping a friend, and keep editing until the wording feels natural.

## Reading notes

- Interface design ends in words once the visual layers are removed.
- Designers often treat writing as a low-priority part of the product.
- Three reasons are given for that neglect: it is not sexy, it is hard, and people assume users do not read.
- The post says interfaces are weakened when important details are cut just to keep copy short.
- It favors clearer explanations over bare minimum text.
- The recurring-event example shows copy improving through several rounds of feedback.
- The best version spells out what changes and uses a friendlier tone.
- The recommended style is casual, positive, and natural.
- Good interface writing comes from relentless editing.
- Strong copy across a product can reduce user confusion and support burden.
