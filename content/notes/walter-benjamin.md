Walter Benjamin was a German-Jewish philosopher, cultural critic, media theorist, and essayist. The page presents him as a thinker whose work combines German idealism, Jewish mysticism, Western Marxism, and post-Kantianism, with interests in literary theory, aesthetics, philosophy of technology, epistemology, philosophy of language, and philosophy of history.

His best-known writings include "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction" and "Theses on the Philosophy of History." The page also emphasizes his unfinished _Arcades Project_, his studies of Goethe, Baudelaire, Kafka, Proust, and translation, and his death in 1940 while fleeing Nazi-controlled Europe.

## Reading notes

- Benjamin was born in Berlin in 1892 and died in Portbou in 1940 by suicide from a morphine overdose while trying to escape the Third Reich.
- He studied at Freiburg, Berlin, Bern, and Frankfurt, and earned a PhD in 1919 on art criticism in German Romanticism.
- His thought is described as combining German idealism, Jewish mysticism, Western Marxism, and post-Kantianism.
- He was associated with the Frankfurt School and kept important ties with Scholem, Adorno, Brecht, Buber, and others.
- "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction" argues that mechanical reproduction changes perception and erodes the aura of artworks.
- "Theses on the Philosophy of History" links historical materialism with Jewish mysticism and the image of the angel of history.
- The _Arcades Project_ is described as his unfinished major work on 19th-century Paris and modern urban culture.
- His habilitation thesis, _The Origin of German Tragic Drama_, was rejected at Frankfurt and later published as a book.
- The page says he gained wider posthumous recognition after his works were published and translated after his death.
