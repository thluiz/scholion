The article distinguishes two different effects of AI at work. Intensive oversight of AI agents can produce mental fatigue, information overload, decision fatigue, errors, and intent to quit, while using AI to replace routine or repetitive tasks can lower burnout.

## Reading notes

- Gas Town is presented as an example of AI making work faster but harder to follow, with users describing stress and difficulty comprehending what is happening.
- The authors define “AI brain fry” as mental fatigue from excessive use or oversight of AI tools beyond a worker’s cognitive capacity.
- In their study of 1,488 full-time U.S.-based workers, high AI oversight predicted more mental effort, more mental fatigue, and more information overload.
- When AI increased workload, cognitive load and mental fatigue also rose.
- Using multiple AI tools at once was associated with higher perceived productivity up to three tools, after which productivity dipped.
- Fourteen percent of AI-using participants reported AI brain fry.
- Reported symptoms included fog, buzzing, difficulty focusing, slower decision-making, headaches, and the need to step away from the computer to reset.
- AI brain fry was linked to 33% more decision fatigue, more minor and major errors, and higher intent to quit.
- Using AI to reduce time spent on repetitive tasks was associated with 15% lower burnout scores.
- Manager support, organized team integration, clear AI strategy, training, and work-life balance signals were associated with lower mental fatigue.
- The article recommends redesigning jobs, setting explicit expectations about AI and workload, shifting metrics from activity to impact, and building worker skills for managing AI workload.
