The page says argumentative writing tries to persuade an audience that your ideas are valid. It presents Aristotle’s three appeals as ethos, pathos, and logos, then explains each one as a different way to persuade readers.

## Reading notes

- Ethos is credibility or ethical appeal, based on the character, trustworthiness, reputation, and style of the writer or speaker.
- Pathos is emotional appeal, aimed at the audience’s sympathies, imagination, identity, self-interest, and feelings.
- Logos is logical appeal, based on reasoning, the clarity of the claim, supporting evidence, premises, and consistency.
- The page says effective argument often depends on giving reasons, using support for a thesis, and avoiding logical fallacies.
- It also says ethos can come from the text itself and from outside reputation, while institutions and public roles can also project credibility.
- For pathos, the page says narrative and vivid description can help create emotional response and move an audience toward decision or action.
- For logos, it lists definition, analogy, cause and consequence, and testimony or authority as common kinds of support.
- The page adds that writers can strengthen ethos through good sense, good moral character, goodwill, clear formatting, and careful revision.
