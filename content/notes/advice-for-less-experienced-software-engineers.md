The article argues that entry-level software engineers face a much harder market than experienced engineers. It says the best response is to widen the search, build proof of skill while applying, and treat the first offer as the priority over waiting for a perfect start.

## Reading notes

- The market is described as especially difficult for less experienced software engineers, with more competition and companies still preferring experienced hires.
- It recommends joining a support group, including college, bootcamp, Discord, or career communities, to compare notes and stay motivated.
- It advises applying widely instead of only to famous or remote companies, and looking at smaller, local, non-tech, onsite, or hybrid roles.
- It suggests also applying to consultancies and developer agencies, while being careful about poor working practices.
- It says entry-level applicants should assume visa sponsorship is unlikely.
- While job hunting, the article recommends tailoring each resume, contributing to open source in meaningful ways, and building experience through side projects or short paid or unpaid projects.
- It lists ways to stand out, including pedigree, depth, breadth, non-trivial projects, papers or blog posts, motivation, and extra effort in applications.
- It says that if you only have one offer, you should take it and focus on getting started rather than waiting for an ideal role.
- It notes that bootcamp graduates should be cautious about programs that promise fast job placement, and mentions longer programs that may prepare people better.
- It also addresses hiring managers, encouraging them to open new grad roles when they have the seniority structure to support them.
