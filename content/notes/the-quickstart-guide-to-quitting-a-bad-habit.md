Babauta argues that quitting a bad habit is possible through small steps that change mindset and environment. He says the motivation should be rooted in self-care, and that change works better when it is prepared instead of improvised.

## Reading notes

- Change starts with a clear reason that matters when temptation appears.
- Set a quit date a few days ahead and make the commitment public.
- Use accountability and support, including friends, online groups, or a partner.
- Track triggers in a Quit Plan and note what need the habit is meeting.
- Match each trigger with a replacement habit that fits the same need.
- Put reminders where the trigger happens.
- Keep a no-exceptions rule and do not trust the idea that one exception is harmless.
- Treat mistakes as information, not as a reason to quit trying.
- Notice urges, delay acting, and use simple actions like breathing, water, walking, or calling someone.
- Stay positive and answer negative self-talk with realistic confidence.
