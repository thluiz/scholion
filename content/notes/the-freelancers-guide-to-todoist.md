The guide argues that freelancers need a productivity system that covers both business development and delivery work. It uses Todoist as the center of that system, from finding leads and saving pitches to organizing client work, invoices, and personal time.

## Reading notes

- Freelancers often need to build a business skillset in addition to their craft, including pitching, proposals, contracts, meetings, invoices, payments, portfolios, and taxes.
- A task manager like Todoist can help keep those responsibilities in one system while leaving space to do contracted work.
- Promising gigs can be saved in a dedicated project, and browser extensions or mobile browsing can turn links into tasks.
- Pitch ideas and dream clients can be collected in separate projects so outreach stays intentional.
- Personal branding can be supported with recurring portfolio updates, follow-ups for testimonials, regular social posts, and saved articles for sharing.
- Labels can separate self-promotion, useful resources, and content from peers.
- A freelancer pipeline project with sections like Pitched, Accepted, and Rejected can show each client’s stage at a glance.
- Projects can be created for each client, with sub-projects used when one client has different kinds of work.
- Tasks, subtasks, milestones, and due dates help break large deliverables into manageable steps and avoid last-minute work.
- Emails, priority levels, and a custom filter can pull important tasks into one view and keep daily work focused.
- Calendar sync and shared projects help manage meetings and project communication with clients.
- Paperwork, invoices, and follow-ups can be treated as tasks with due dates and reminders, including reminders to collect payment and request testimonials.
- The guide also recommends scheduling deep work, using recurring tasks for it, and keeping some time free from meetings and email.
- Todoist can also hold personal projects and recurring habits so work-life balance is part of the same system.
