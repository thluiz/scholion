Effective software risk management is presented as a way to identify and reduce risks in software projects before they cause financial losses, downtime, or project failure. The page breaks risk work into categories, process steps, monitoring, and team coordination, and it links QA and cybersecurity to lower risk and better project outcomes.

## Reading notes

- Software risk management helps identify and mitigate risks in software projects.
- Poor risk management can lead to financial losses, project failures, system downtimes, and reputational harm.
- The page groups risks into technical and business risks.
- Business risks include schedule risks, budget risks, and operational risks.
- Technical risks are tied to software functionality, performance, complexity, and changing requirements.
- Programmatic risks come from external factors such as market changes and regulatory shifts.
- The main process steps are risk identification, analysis, planning, and monitoring.
- Risk identification can use expert consultation, audits, and brainstorming.
- Key Risk Indicators signal growing risk exposure.
- Risk analysis uses quantitative and qualitative methods to assess likelihood and impact.
- A risk matrix helps prioritize risks by severity.
- Risk response strategies include acceptance, avoidance, mitigation, and transfer.
- Risk owners, responsibilities, and risk appetite should be assigned in the mitigation plan.
- QA helps identify vulnerabilities early and supports compliance with regulations.
- Cybersecurity and development teams should collaborate to address security vulnerabilities and incident response.
- Continuous monitoring and real-time reporting help teams adjust strategies as risks change.
- Risk management software can automate repetitive tasks and improve efficiency.
- The page cites project examples showing the value of governance, training, and standards.
