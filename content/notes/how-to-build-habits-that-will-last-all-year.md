The article says new habits are hard to keep, but they become more likely when you give them momentum. It recommends starting with very small actions, adding milestones, making the goal measurable, and focusing on one habit at a time.

It also points to practical ways to keep a behavior going: identify what is stopping you, plan for situations that could disrupt the habit, stack it onto an existing routine, make the start easier by preparing what you need, and reward yourself after you do it.

## Reading notes

- Begin with a tiny version of the habit, then increase it gradually once it starts to feel routine.
- Use milestones so the habit feels more achievable and progress can be marked along the way.
- Make the goal specific and measurable so you can tell whether you did it in a given day, week, or month.
- Identify what keeps breaking the habit and adjust for that obstacle.
- Plan for situations that could interrupt the routine so you do not have to improvise later.
- Attach the new habit to an existing routine that already happens regularly.
- Make the habit easier to start by keeping the needed tools or materials close at hand.
- Reward the habit after completing it, but choose a reward that does not undermine the habit itself.
