The post presents blogging as useful for career growth, lead generation, and learning, and says it has helped the author try different stacks and frameworks. It argues that blogging works best when it is treated as a steady practice, with a clear topic focus and a habit of writing and publishing over time.

## Reading notes

- Blogging can help generate leads, support a developer career, and serve as a place to revisit information later.
- The author says blogging gave freedom to experiment with different stacks and frameworks, which helped them understand their use cases better.
- Consistency is presented as necessary for building an audience and for keeping up with self-imposed publishing deadlines.
- The author recommends managing time with a schedule that leaves room for research, demos, and writing.
- Topic consistency matters too, since the blog should stay focused on what the author does and knows.
- Drafting posts in advance helps keep a publishing schedule even during vacations or time off.
- The author writes posts in Markdown and stores drafts locally, then uses Markdown to Medium when publishing on Medium.
- Keeping a list of ideas helps with planning and with deciding what to publish in the coming weeks or months.
- Ideas do not have to be highly technical; they can also come from work-related topics or simple subjects that help less experienced readers.
- The author says it is better to have a blog on your own domain and linked to your portfolio from the start.
- When posting on other platforms, the author advises checking whether republication on your own site is allowed.
- The post says a personal platform matters more than posting only on publications.
- The author recommends learning from other content creators as well as bloggers, using Twitter, Medium, Dev.to, podcasts, official documentation, and books.
- Blogging is said to teach how to separate constructive criticism from unhelpful criticism.
- Sharing posts across many platforms is presented as a way to help more people and support the community.
- The author lists Medium, Dev.to, Twitter, Reddit, Hacker News, LinkedIn, Instagram, and major publications such as freeCodeCamp as sharing channels.
