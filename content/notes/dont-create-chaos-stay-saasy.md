Good leadership should leave a room with more certainty than it had before. The post says ineffective leaders often add chaos by reopening settled decisions, pushing their own priorities, or ending meetings without clear next steps, while good leaders remove distractions and get the right decision made.

It recommends measurable goals, meeting agendas, written confirmation of complicated decisions, documents for complex choices, and extra communication to avoid duplicated work. It also says people should not use authority to bypass team processes, and should ask clarifying questions before asking for changes.

## Reading notes

- Effective leaders reduce confusion rather than add to it.
- A room you enter should leave with clearer goals and a firmer plan.
- Chaos can come from missing context, weak planning habits, or someone forcing a preferred topic or change.
- Measurable outcomes should be reinforced often, even when it feels repetitive.
- Meetings should have a simple agenda.
- Complicated, non-obvious, or contentious decisions should be confirmed in writing.
- Complex decisions should live in a document or ticket.
- Over-communicate what you will do next, and also what you will not do.
- Do not bypass team prioritization processes with authority.
- If a plan seems strange, ask why before asking for a change.
- The more senior the role, the more important it is to avoid creating chaos.
