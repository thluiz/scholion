Mo Gawdat says AI will remove a share of jobs in some sectors, but he argues that people can still adapt by learning how to use the tools well and by focusing on work that stays centered on human judgment and relationships. He points to nursing, counseling, and similar roles as examples of work that still connects directly to people.

- He says people should learn the tool and focus on human-centric jobs.
- He names nursing and counseling as work that can still make a living through human connection.
- He says communication, relationship-building, and good judgement remain hard to automate.
- He argues that taste matters because AI can create quickly, but judgment decides what to keep.
- He says AI is not the enemy and can be used to create opportunities.
- He advises people to learn how to interact with AI and work in a hybrid world with it.
