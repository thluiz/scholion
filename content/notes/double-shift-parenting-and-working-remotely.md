The piece argues that remote work with children at home is manageable when expectations are clear and routines are adapted. It focuses on the overlap between work and caregiving, and on practical ways to reduce friction for parents working remotely.

## Reading notes

- Do not panic when childcare interruptions break into meetings or calls; many people understand these disruptions more than expected.
- Set up a dedicated work area and clear boundaries so children know when work is happening.
- Keep some regular routines at home, including space for play, art, blocks, and quiet time.
- Talk with your manager and team about schedule expectations, since kids can change plans on short notice.
- Accept that productive work time may shift to early mornings and evenings.
- Make time for self care and for activities that are not tied to work or children.
- Share household duties with a partner or other support person, including laundry and meals.
- Reach out to other parents through work, school, daycare, or online communities for mutual support.
- Treat the situation as temporary and look for small opportunities that may come from working with children at home.
