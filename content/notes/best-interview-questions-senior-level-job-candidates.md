The article says senior-level hiring takes longer and costs more, so interviewers need questions that reveal more than résumé highlights. It stresses scenario-based and behavior-based prompts, consistent evaluation tools, and questions that stay aligned with current business strategy and the open role.

It also says strong questions can surface hard and soft skills, culture fit, accountability, collaboration, and whether the candidate understands the company and can explain the value they would bring. The article lists seven questions to use, including asking about interest in the company, career expectations, conflict resolution, business impact, management style, support needs, and whether the candidate has questions.

## Reading notes

- Senior roles can take weeks or months to hire, and the process can cost companies several times a candidate’s salary.
- Interviewers are advised to use scenario-based and behavioral questions, plus interview guides and scorecards.
- Hiring teams should keep the job profile aligned with business strategy and check that the role is still relevant.
- A candidate who knows little about the company can be a red flag, while expressed interest and a willingness to learn can be a green flag.
- Responses about career goals should fit what the company can realistically offer in the role and in its progression path.
- Conflict questions are meant to show how candidates handle disagreement and whether they take accountability.
- Candidates should be able to explain the impact of their work with concrete, numbers-based examples.
- The article says recruiters should listen for signs of collaboration, such as balanced use of “we” and “I.”
- Answers about an ideal manager should fit the company culture and leadership style.
- Questions about support and shared workload help show whether the candidate will fit a lean or highly collaborative environment.
- The article recommends leaving time for the candidate’s own questions and values thoughtful questions that show preparation.
