Chelsea Handler reflects on starting out as an "academic reject" who barely finished high school and never went to college, yet later discovering a deep love of learning at her own pace. She argues that education is necessary for becoming a better person, succeeding, and even surviving, and that it continues beyond formal schooling through the "classroom of life." For her, being educated means having a strong drive to expand the mind, especially by exploring subjects that do not naturally attract us. She connects this view to her career shift, intending her new Netflix talk show to be more educational than her earlier, more frivolous TV work.

Handler describes her self-education through voracious reading: books on history, civilizations, civil rights, politics, and various cultures, as well as newspapers to follow world affairs and dictionaries to build a rich vocabulary. She sees a broad vocabulary as both mental adornment and a kind of informal degree. Throughout, she stresses that asking questions is often more valuable than knowing answers, suggesting that teaching oneself and one’s children to ask good questions signals real intellect. She closes by sharing physicist Isidor Rabi’s story about his mother, who always asked whether he had asked a good question that day, presenting this habit of questioning as a model for turning innate ignorance into insight.
