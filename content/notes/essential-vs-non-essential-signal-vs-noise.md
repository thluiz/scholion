The post says the hardest part of launching a simple product is deciding what to include and what to leave out. Its method is to separate essential from non-essential features, and keep non-essential work out of version 1.0.

## Reading notes

- Features that start with “wouldn’t it be nice if...” or “It would be cool if...” are usually non-essential.
- During Campfire development, the team used a “v1.1” label for ideas they wanted but did not want to ship in 1.0.
- The post lists several deferred features, including room locking, room caps, last-message markers, private chats, off-the-record messages, clearing backlog, and an API.
- The team says Campfire had already been used for over a month without those features and worked fine.
- Non-essential features slow release, dilute focus, consume resources, and increase the chance of bugs at launch.
