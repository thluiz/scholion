Anemari Fiser says she assumed technical skill alone would carry her as a Tech Lead, then found daily conflicts kept demanding something else. Her first case: two developers stuck in an hours-long fight over which JSON parsing library to use. Her instinct was to step in and pick the technical answer herself, until she learned to ask questions first and discovered the library wasn't the real issue at all. The two developers had a standing personal conflict, and it was surfacing through whatever technical decision happened to be in front of them.

Her second case is a team that had the knowledge, time, and product buy-in to address technical debt but still wasn't moving on it. The missing piece wasn't capability, it was nobody taking ownership of driving the initiative forward. She could have stepped in and taken charge herself, but frames that as unsustainable; the fix she went with was enabling a senior team member to own the process instead.

## Fichamento

- Her core claim, stated directly: most tech problems are people problems, and solving them requires soft skills rather than more technical depth.
- Case 1 (library dispute): what looked like a technical disagreement was a proxy for an unrelated ongoing personal conflict between the two developers; resolving the library question wouldn't have resolved anything.
- Case 2 (stalled tech debt): the team had every resource needed except someone willing to own driving the initiative; she treats stepping in personally as a short-term fix, not a sustainable one, and instead empowered a senior team member to own it.
- Her list of 7 soft skills to develop as a Tech Lead: listening, building relationships, giving feedback, delegation, facilitation, conflict resolution, and mentoring/coaching.
- On conflict specifically: she frames disagreement itself as a sign of a healthy team, with the skill being how to navigate it rather than how to prevent it.
- Closing framing: soft skills don't go obsolete the way technical stacks do, because the work of leading always runs through people regardless of what the technology underneath changes to.
