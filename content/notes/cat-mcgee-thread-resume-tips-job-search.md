Cat McGee presents a series of resume tips aimed at job seekers, emphasizing that employers often only glance at a resume for a few seconds. She argues that design, clear prioritization of information, focus on relevant experience, concrete achievements and careful review all increase the chances of getting noticed and called back.

## Reading notes

- The resume is often the first and sometimes only thing employers review, and they may only look for a few seconds, so it needs to stand out quickly.
- Using a unique, visually interesting design can help a resume stand out; McGee attributes getting calls from about 75% of her applications to creative design and suggests tools like Canva.
- Information should be prioritized from top to bottom, with the strongest elements such as recent experience, key skills or education placed at the top to entice further reading.
- A small introductory bio at the top can summarize who you are, what you want, what you offer and your proudest accomplishment.
- Only relevant experience and education should be included; unrelated roles, like retail for a dev application, can be left out even if that means listing only a couple of jobs.
- The resume should highlight what the candidate did, achieved and contributed, rather than describing what previous companies did.
- Achievements should be backed by specific, provable data, such as meeting particular standards (like W3C) or quantifying improvements (for example, increasing conversion rate by 120%).
- Additional materials like a cover letter and links to projects or a personal website can further show personality and work, and links should be engaging enough to spark curiosity.
- External help is encouraged: use tools to check spelling and grammar and ask mentors or friends to review the resume for clarity.
- Time investment in crafting the resume should match its importance as the first point of contact with a potential employer.
- McGee closes by offering to give feedback on completed resumes, reinforcing her willingness to help readers apply these suggestions.
