Kenneth Reitz presents burnout in software development as a gradual process, especially in open source work with large user bases and heavy maintenance demands. He says success brought more pressure, less drive to start new projects, and more strain from community attention.

He describes a turning point in which he stepped back from Twitter, tech news, and other noise, then shared responsibility through delegation and co-maintainers. He also says hobbies outside coding helped him keep a healthier balance.

## Reading notes

- Burnout in software development is described as real and gradual, not sudden.
- Open source projects with many users create pressure through support and maintenance.
- As Requests grew, his desire to build new projects fell while the burden of maintaining existing ones increased.
- He nearly considered removing his public work from the internet because the pressure felt overwhelming.
- He reduced stress by unfollowing everyone on Twitter and ignoring tech news.
- Publish-only mode is presented as a way to recover from open source stress.
- Delegating work to co-maintainers reduced the burden on him.
- Hobbies like music production and photography are part of his balance today.
- Open source collaboration works best when people share responsibility and do what they do best.
