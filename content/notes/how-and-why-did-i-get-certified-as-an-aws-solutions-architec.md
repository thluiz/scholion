The article describes the AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate exam, its main domains, and the study path the author used to get certified. It also lists the reasons he pursued the certification, including learning, credibility, and career visibility.

## Reading notes

- The post focuses on AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate (SAA-C02), while noting that SAA-C03 is the latest version.
- The certification validates knowledge of architecting and deploying secure and robust AWS applications.
- The exam is split into four domains: secure architectures, resilient architectures, high-performing architectures, and cost-optimized architectures.
- The author recommends a Stephane Maarek Udemy course because it covers the services in the exam and includes practice tests and slides.
- He suggests filling knowledge gaps before moving on, especially in areas like networking and VPC.
- He recommends practicing directly in AWS, using free tier resources when possible, and deleting services afterward to avoid charges.
- He advises reading user guides, developer guides, and FAQs for core services such as IAM, S3, CloudFront, EC2, Lambda, ELB, RDS, VPC, API Gateway, and DynamoDB.
- He also suggests reviewing FAQs for other exam services like Route 53, EBS, WAF, VPN CloudHub, Kinesis, CloudWatch, CloudTrail, Direct Connect, KMS, Cognito, Organizations, and Glue.
- He recommends studying the AWS Well-Architected Framework, including its six pillars, plus the Disaster Recovery whitepaper and other AWS whitepapers.
- He advises taking practice exams on Whizlabs, repeating the study cycle after each exam, and aiming for 80% to 90% before taking the real test.
- He notes that non-English speakers may request an extra 30 minutes for the exam.
- He says the certification may help with job search and visibility, learning software architecture and AWS, credibility in consulting, and filling knowledge gaps.
- He stresses that hands-on experience matters most, but certification can add value when combined with practice.
- He says he timeboxed the learning and practice to keep it focused.
