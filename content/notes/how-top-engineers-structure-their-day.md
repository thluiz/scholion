The page argues that engineers work better when they manage energy rather than time. It says many people waste their best hours on meetings and Slack, then struggle with focus later, while stronger performance comes from matching high-effort work to natural energy peaks and grouping low-effort tasks together.

It also says deep work should be protected as a fixed block each day, shallow work should be batched, and some of that shallow work can be reduced with automation. The page adds that chronotypes, lighting, noise control, and location can support this rhythm.

## Reading notes

- Engineers can ship more code without burnout by aligning work with energy rhythms instead of fighting them.
- High-energy tasks include deep coding, debugging, and system design, while code reviews, emails, and documentation fit lower-energy periods.
- Chronotype matters, with Lions peaking early, Wolves late, Bears on standard office hours, and Dolphins showing irregular bursts.
- Batching communication reduces context switching and keeps conversations from dragging on through repeated back-and-forth.
- Deep work needs a protected focus block each day so the day does not become fragmented.
- Shallow work is better handled in batches, such as after deep work or while waiting for a deployment.
- Automation can reduce shallow work through CI/CD pipelines, Slack bots, and keyboard shortcuts.
- Environmental tweaks like blue-light filters, morning sunlight, and noise-canceling headphones can support the chosen work rhythm.
