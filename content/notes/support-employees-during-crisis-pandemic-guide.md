The text describes how the pandemic has disrupted work and why acting as if everything is normal increases stress, burnout, and distrust. It argues that organizations are more likely to endure a crisis when leaders adjust expectations, prioritize employee well‑being, and communicate clearly and empathetically, even about bad news. Through examples from companies like Magoosh, Tettra, Doist, Buffer, Carta, Airbnb, Emplify, Starbucks, and others, it outlines concrete practices: acknowledging uncertainty, being transparent about layoffs and changing goals, expecting less output, affirming family responsibilities, enabling flexible schedules through asynchronous communication, helping employees disconnect, and offering mental health resources.

- The pandemic created historic economic disruption and forced many into sudden remote work while some organizations still expect uninterrupted productivity.
- Leaders trying to replicate office control with constant video calls, chat, and surveillance add pressure instead of easing it.
- Treating work as normal despite childcare demands and anxiety increases stress, burnout, distrust, and lowers morale.
- The article argues organizations do better in crises when they adapt expectations and focus on employee well‑being, not only financial results.

- Leaders should explicitly acknowledge that things are not normal and create space for employees to share how they are feeling.
