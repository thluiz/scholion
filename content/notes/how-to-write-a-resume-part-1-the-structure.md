The article introduces the author’s goal of helping people build or edit resumes, especially job seekers and people changing careers. It argues that resume structure matters and that the right format depends on experience, the role, and the kind of history you want to show.

## Reading notes

- The article presents three resume structures: chronological, functional, and combination.
- Chronological resumes list work history in reverse order and fit people with several previous jobs.
- Functional resumes focus on skills and actual experience and can suit career changers or people with gaps in employment.
- Combination resumes mix skills focus with work history and are presented as a good option for middle-level developers.
- The resume should include required and optional sections, and the required ones are contact info, education, and work history.
- Contact info should include name and e-mail, while phone number, location, and social networks are optional.
- The objective or short description section is optional, and the author advises skipping it if there is nothing useful to add.
- Education should list institution, specialization, degree, and dates, with entries in reverse order.
- Additional education can include courses, books, conferences, and progress on platforms like Hackerrank or LeetCode, especially for trainee and junior developers.
- Work history should be in reverse order and include company name, position, dates, and a few main tasks or achievements.
- Skills should be grouped into categories and limited to skills relevant to the job being applied for.
- Achievements and recommendations are optional sections that can help if they are notable and relevant.
- The author advises against adding an interests section and against turning the resume into a portfolio page with too many links and descriptions.
