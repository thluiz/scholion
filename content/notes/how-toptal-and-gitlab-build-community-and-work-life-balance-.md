Toptal and GitLab use Slack to support remote work in different ways, but both treat it as central to team communication and culture. Toptal relies on channels for local groups, skills, social interests, working parents, and hiring, while GitLab uses status features and channel-based updates to protect time and reduce unnecessary meetings.

## Reading notes

- Toptal describes Slack as its digital headquarters and says remote work requires more communication and the right tools.
- The company organizes internal communication mainly through Slack channels instead of email.
- Toptal uses country-specific, skills-based, social, parenting, and hiring channels to help people connect and share information.
- Toptal also uses custom apps and integrations such as PTO Ninja, Polly, Donut, Chronos, and Toptal Slackbot to handle approvals, surveys, introductions, meeting feedback, and hiring messages.
- Michelle Labbe advises patience and flexibility when teams are shifting to remote work.
- GitLab encourages employees to block out “do not disturb” time and time to unwind after work.
- Bryan Wise says this helps teammates respect availability across time zones and reconsider whether a meeting is necessary.
- GitLab asks people to keep meetings purposeful, start and end them on time, and use Slack channels for important updates instead of email or a wiki.
- The page argues that clear communication helps preserve company culture and keep work from blending into personal life.
