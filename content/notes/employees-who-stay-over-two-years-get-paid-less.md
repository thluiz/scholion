The article argues that employees who stay at the same company for more than two years can end up earning much less over a career. It says annual raises are often around 3% or less than inflation, while leaving for a new job often brings a 10% to 20% salary increase and sometimes more. It also says companies are more willing to pay higher base salaries and titles when hiring from outside.

It adds that job changes are not risk-free. Some employers dislike frequent transitions, and money is only one part of career decisions. The article’s conclusion is that workers should negotiate more aggressively, consider moving when it helps their pay, and think of themselves as responsible for maximizing their own earnings.
