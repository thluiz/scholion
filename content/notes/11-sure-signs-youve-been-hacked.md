The article says antimalware tools miss plenty of attacks, especially new ones, so users should watch for system behavior that points to compromise. It recommends a full restore as the safest response, because a compromised computer cannot be fully trusted again.

## Reading notes

- Fake antivirus warnings usually appear after the system has already been exploited, often through unpatched software.
- Unwanted browser toolbars, redirected searches, and frequent random pop-ups are common signs of malicious software.
- If friends receive fake email from your account, the article says it may still be worth scanning the computer and checking for unwanted software.
- Sudden password changes often mean an account was phished, so contacts should be notified and the service should be contacted.
- Unexpected software installs can come from other programs or from malware that installs itself like a legitimate program.
- If the mouse moves on its own and makes correct selections, the article treats that as a serious compromise and advises calling professionals.
- Disabled antimalware software, Task Manager, or Registry Editor is described as a strong sign of malicious compromise.
- Missing bank money and calls from stores about unpaid shipped goods are treated as high-impact fraud cases that require law enforcement and credit monitoring.
- The article says most malicious hacking starts from unpatched software, Trojan horse programs, or fake phishing emails.
