The study says the brain regulates its resource use while reading and tries to identify the most essential information. It uses less cognitive effort when processing text and gives more attention to words that add the most value to understanding the message.

## Reading notes

- The study, by researchers in computer science and psychology at the University of Helsinki, was published in _Scientific Reports_.
- It extends earlier work on word length, word frequency, and syntactic and semantic errors to the discourse level.
- The experiment used six-sentence paragraphs and Wikipedia sentences while recording EEG.
- The model, based on information theory, linked word informativeness to brain activity.
- High-value and low-value words produced different selective electric brain potentials.
- Brain measurements for individual words could be used to predict whether the information gain is low or high.
- The researchers say this may support future brain-information interfaces.
- They also note possible uses in healthcare and in modeling consumer tastes, values, and opinions.
- The study is at a basic stage, and practical applications still face ethical and technical challenges.
