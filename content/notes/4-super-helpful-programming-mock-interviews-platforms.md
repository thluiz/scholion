The post says practice is what keeps interview knowledge in long-term memory. It describes the author’s experience with mock interviews, especially Pramp, and then compares four platforms for improving programming, frontend, design, and soft skills.

## Reading notes

- Practice is presented as the main way to make interview knowledge last beyond reading videos, books, and articles.
- The author says failed foreign job interviews helped reveal weaknesses in data structures, algorithms, frontend topics, and confidence.
- Pramp is described as free, peer-based, and useful for data structures, algorithms, frontend, product management, system design, and data science.
- Pramp pairs users with a peer, uses video chat and a collaborative code editor, and lets them alternate between interviewer and interviewee roles.
- TechMockInterview is paid per session and uses seasoned software engineers from companies such as Amazon, Google, and Facebook as interviewers.
- TechMockInterview gives hiring results and written feedback so users can see where they need to improve.
- Interviewing.io focuses on software engineering technical interviews, is free, and uses anonymous audio-only interviews.
- Interviewing.io lets users practice with real engineers from top companies and may lead to invitations from top companies without a resume.
- InterviewBuddy offers online face-to-face mock interviews for HR, technical, and specialized roles.
- InterviewBuddy provides booking options and detailed scorecards with feedback on strengths and weaknesses.
- The conclusion says practicing with strategy or one-to-one help is more effective than practicing alone.
