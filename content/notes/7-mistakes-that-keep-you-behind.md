The article argues that progress in JavaScript depends on better learning habits, not on more material. It says learners improve by coding as they go, practicing concepts on their own, and focusing on one step at a time instead of trying to collect everything first.

## Reading notes

- Rushing through concepts leaves people forgetting what they learned and searching old material later, so the article recommends hands-on practice before moving on.
- Depending too much on other people's code or libraries can block independent problem solving, especially when interviews expect you to apply concepts yourself.
- Spending too much time gathering tutorials, ebooks, and bookmarks can feel productive, but the article says direct coding helps ideas stick longer.
- Consuming too much information without coding in between makes it easier to forget and harder to apply what was learned later.
- Comparing yourself to more advanced developers ignores the learning path that got them there and distracts from present progress.
- Learning tools and frameworks before JavaScript itself can lead to memorized usage without understanding why the tools exist or what problems they solve.
- Breaking concepts into smaller steps makes difficult topics easier to understand and easier to ask for help about.
