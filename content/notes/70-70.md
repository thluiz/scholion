The author says many founders treat a startup partnership as a matter of respect and trust, while avoiding concrete agreements. He argues that this leaves important issues unresolved and makes later conflict likely.

## Reading notes

- The author describes a friend who wanted a partner for a startup, but was offended when asked practical questions about shares, control, exits, and future financing.
- He says many people react as if such questions were a prenuptial agreement, while they would rather base the partnership on love and trust.
- He argues that equal business relationships are not truly equal, and that unresolved conflicts of interest can damage or destroy a startup.
- He calls this pattern “70/70,” meaning each founder expects 70% for themselves and sees the split as a sign of respect rather than a working formula.
- He says younger founders often do not think through future conflicts, ownership of assets, or how profits and control will work.
- He notes that trust matters, but trust alone is not enough when the terms were never discussed.
- He gives examples of the kind of terms that should be settled in advance, including what happens on quitting, who controls accounts and domains, who approves expenses, and how new funding is handled.
- He says he now asks these questions upfront and walks away if the other side wants a partnership based only on “love.”
- He ends by saying that founders who are afraid of a prenuptial style agreement are likely to create an ugly breakup later.
