The article argues that imposter syndrome is common in software development because the field changes quickly and constantly exposes people to what they do not know. It says the feeling can affect both beginners and experienced developers, so coping with it is part of the work.

## Reading notes

- Imposter syndrome is described as chronic self-doubt and feelings of inadequacy even when evidence points the other way.
- Software development is presented as fast-paced and always changing, which keeps developers facing unfamiliar problems.
- A mentor can give perspective and guidance on early progress, even if they do not code alongside you.
- A peer pair helps people share frustrations, failures, victories, and daily experience with more openness.
- Being around smarter people and harder problems can create better learning opportunities.
- Curiosity matters, and asking direct questions is presented as a way to expose ignorance and learn faster.
- Picking one thing and practicing it regularly can deepen understanding and change how a developer talks about the topic.
- When coding, it is important to understand why code works and why it is structured the way it is, including when copy-pasting.
- Awareness of imposter syndrome helps interrupt the cycle of self-doubt, stress, and delay.
- The article recommends reading Chapter 1 of The Pragmatic Programmer for its approach to pragmatic programming.
