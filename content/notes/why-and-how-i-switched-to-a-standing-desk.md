The author changes his desk to standing height after spending long hours sitting for computer work and wanting a small, practical way to burn more calories while losing weight. He says standing helps his posture, opens his chest, and lets him move, pace, dance, and fidget more naturally during the day.

He describes the transition as hard at first, with three painful days and some exhaustion, then easier by the fourth and fifth day. He converts an Ikea Jerker desk to standing height, adds a monitor riser, later uses a mat and a footrest, wears supportive running shoes, and still sits for lunch, calls, meetings, and short breaks.

## Reading notes

- He works about 45 to 50 hours a week on his computer and had been doing that work while sitting.
- He says the sedentary workday is part of why he is more than 20 pounds overweight.
- He had long been interested in alternative desk setups after writing about a treadmill desk in 2006.
- He says a standing desk is cheaper and easier to manage than building or buying a treadmill desk.
- RescueTime reports, weight loss goals, and a guide that recommends moving from sitting to standing to walking pushed him to make the change.
- He says standing helps him burn more calories and keeps him from slumping with curved back and forward shoulders.
- He says standing with forearms at a 90 degree angle improves posture and opens the chest.
- The first three days were painful and distracting, and he needed to sit down every hour or so.
- By the fourth day it felt better, and by the fifth he could work for two hours without thinking about standing.
- He used an Ikea Jerker desk that can be assembled at different heights and made the change hard to reverse.
- If a desk cannot be adjusted, he suggests temporary setups with shelves, cans, printer paper, or buying a standing desk.
- He wears ordinary running shoes and sometimes goes barefoot, though barefoot standing does not last long.
- He orders a mat, then says it helps barefoot work and flat shoes, and adds a footrest for shifting weight.
- He also buys a monitor riser so the screen sits at eye level.
- He still takes sit-down breaks at lunch, on phone calls, in meetings, and sometimes for short rests.
- He notes that standing too much can also have health risks, so his routine now mixes sitting, standing, pacing, and stretching.
