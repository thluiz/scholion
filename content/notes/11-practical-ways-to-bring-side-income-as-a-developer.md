The post argues that developers should not depend on a single income source and presents practical ways to build side revenue. It groups options that can produce direct payments, recurring income, portfolio value, or long-term credibility, while noting that some methods are easier to start than others.

## Reading notes

- Open source bounties can pay from $200 to $10k for a single pull request and also help build credibility and a portfolio.
- Guest technical writing can pay well, be done remotely, and grow a developer’s reach if they can explain technical concepts clearly.
- Hackathons may not guarantee money, but they can lead to cash prizes and strengthen a portfolio.
- Digital products such as guides, templates, plugins and courses can create passive income with little maintenance if they solve a real market need.
- Building a SaaS as an indie hacker is presented as a strong long-term path, though it requires doing everything independently.
- User testing pays developers for short testing sessions and feedback, with tech knowledge helping them work faster.
- Freelancing is described as common but saturated and unstable, so reputation and client outreach matter.
- Newsletters can grow a subscriber base that supports ads or paid subscriptions.
- Grants and open source internships can fund living expenses for short periods while a developer works on a project.
- API monetization lets developers publish and sell APIs as a service.
- Paid communities can become a strong revenue source, especially when they allow recurring subscriptions and peer interaction.
