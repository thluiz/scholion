The article presents the Feynman Technique as a way to understand complex coding concepts by breaking them into simple terms. It uses JavaScript loops as the main example and shows how explaining a topic plainly can reveal what you still do not understand.

## Reading notes

- The Feynman approach is described as a quick way to turn a complex topic into one you can explain simply to others.
- The article says the method is based on asking hard questions and fully understanding an idea.
- Its stated benefits include deeper understanding, faster learning, better recall, improved teaching, and stronger critical thinking.
- The four steps are to choose a topic, explain it to a 12-year-old, review the explanation, and simplify it.
- The loop example explains that loops repeat a code block until a stop condition is met.
- It lists for loop, for-of loop, for-in loop, while loop, and do-while loop as types of loops.
- The for loop runs while a condition remains true and stops when it becomes false.
- The while loop keeps running as long as the stop condition is true and is useful when the number of iterations is unknown.
- The do-while loop runs the block at least once before checking whether the stop condition is true or false.
- To make an explanation clearer, the article suggests writing, posting in technical communities, joining online groups, or teaching an imaginary audience.
- It also mentions the rubber duck technique as another way to explain code clearly.
- Step 3 is to review the explanation and return to the material where the explanation was weak.
- Step 4 is to explain the topic again with the improved understanding and simplify the parts that were still unclear.
