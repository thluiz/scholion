The author says he has made real progress on ippy.io and sees that as a success, after many earlier side projects fizzled out or were abandoned. He then reflects on what helped him stick with this one and turn those lessons into a simple approach for choosing and sustaining side projects.

## Reading notes

- He recommends looking for products in interesting spaces, choosing one that already works and seems buildable, and then making your own version with improvements.
- He says the goal should be to replicate functionality, not design, code, or copy, and then improve the UI, niche, speed, or price.
- He argues that originality is overrated and that the better target is being the best version of a useful idea.
- He advises moving slowly, accepting that progress comes in waves, and taking breaks instead of forcing work when motivation is low.
- He warns that overwork can kill excitement and push people toward chasing new ideas before an old project is done.
- He suggests redefining finishing as long-term work on a project that may generate income over time, rather than treating completion as a hard endpoint.
