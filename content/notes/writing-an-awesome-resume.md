The post says a resume should be tailored to the people who will read it in sequence: first non-technical screeners, then hiring managers, then technical interviewers. It should be short, easy to scan, and focused on the points that help a candidate pass each stage.

It recommends required sections such as contact information, either an objective or a summary, education, technical skills, and experience. It also stresses one page for a resume, specificity in skills, honest claims that can be discussed in detail, careful grammar, and cover letters only when they add value or are requested.

## Reading notes

- A resume should support the hiring pipeline and help the reader reach a follow-up decision quickly.
- One page is treated as a resume; multiple pages are treated as a CV.
- The objective statement is useful only when it gives concrete details about role, employment type, or location.
- Summary statements should stay to one or two sentences, not a paragraph.
- Contact information should be current and professional, and should not use an employer-associated email or phone number.
- Grammar mistakes weaken the application, so the resume should be reviewed by several people.
- Technical skills should be specific, relevant, and free of filler items.
- Meta skills such as being a fast learner or team player are described as poor use of space.
- Education details matter more for new graduates and can be shortened later, while honors and continuing education should still be included.
- Experience should be written with verbs, minimal redundancy, and concrete technologies or responsibilities.
- Publications and patents can be mentioned briefly on the resume and listed in detail in a CV.
- Artistic formatting can help differentiate a resume if it stays clear and purposeful.
- Cover letters are optional in portal applications but useful when contacting managers or recruiters directly.
- Applications should be sent as PDFs, with a plaintext version ready for systems that convert files.
- Applying to mismatched roles just to get into a system is discouraged; direct contact is presented as the better option.
