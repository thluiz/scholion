The post explains three CMS models: coupled, decoupled, and headless. It says a headless CMS removes the front end, uses an API to make content available through any channel, and gives developers more freedom, better security, and more control.

It also argues that businesses need this model because the digital world now includes websites, apps, AR, VR, and wearable devices. A headless CMS is presented as useful for reaching customers across channels, improving agility, supporting scalability, and letting design and content move forward at the same time.

## Reading notes

- A coupled CMS keeps back end and front end together in one application, which is easier to set up and maintain, but brings custom-code, security, and performance problems.
- A decoupled CMS separates back end and front end, sends changes through an API, and offers better security and scalability, though it still requires managing both sides and works best for web-focused channels.
- A headless CMS removes the front end and the presentation layer, exposes content through an API, and is best for multiple channels and platforms.
- The post links headless CMS use to current digital behavior, citing connected devices and the spread of websites, apps, AR, VR, and wearables.
- It says a headless CMS can help businesses respond faster, reuse components, and work on design and content at the same time.
