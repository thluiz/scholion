The page argues that a year-end one-on-one should be used to reflect with a direct report on what went well, what did not, and how the working relationship should move forward. It says this conversation can clarify whether the relationship is healthy and set the tone for the next year.

## Reading notes

- Use the year-end one-on-one to reflect together on progress, discouragement, support, and growth.
- The meeting can establish a shared narrative that the relationship is working, or show that it may not be a good fit.
- Treat the conversation as a way to acknowledge how far you have come and to set the tone for the next year.
- It is not presented as a formal performance review with self-ratings or a 1-to-10 scale.
- The questions should differ from standard performance review questions because the meeting also shapes the working relationship.
- The template starts with catching up on the holiday season and the year-end workflow.
- The reflection section asks about personal progress, team progress, pace of work, pride, discouragement, support, growth, the manager’s growth, and changes in the work relationship.
- The looking-ahead section asks what the manager can do better, how the employee wants to grow, what the manager should pay attention to, and how both people can challenge and support each other.
- The takeaway section asks what both people are taking away from the conversation and what next steps each needs to take.
- The page says not every question will fit into a single 1 to 1.5 hour meeting, and that is fine.
- The goal is to make the conversation less about evaluation and more about shared progress moving forward.
