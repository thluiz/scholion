The author says they work best with less context switching, but sometimes priorities are out of their control. The post gathers tactics for handling task transitions, keeping work organized, and making it easier to return to a task after interruptions.

## Reading notes

- Start the day by setting goals and estimating time for each task, and move lower-priority goals to another day if the list is too full.
- Group browser tabs or use separate windows so small references are easier to find later.
- Prioritize time-sensitive tasks to reduce urgency and avoid being pulled away.
- Turn off or limit notifications so messages are checked when they are less disruptive to the flow.
- Block calendar time for specific tasks to stay accountable and avoid reacting to requests as they come up.
- Use a Pomodoro timer to work in short bursts with breaks for messages or water.
- Set aside the beginning and end of the day for smaller tasks such as email and PR review.
- Write notes while working, including research, investigation, collaboration, and other details that help when switching tasks.
- Use draft PRs to leave detailed notes on remaining work and how to test it.
- Leave broken tests as TODOs so part of the work is done and the next step is clear.
- Multitasking is presented as slower and less effective than focusing on one task at a time.
- Skipping breaks is treated as a bad habit because people still need nourishment, hydration, and periodic rest.
- The post also says it is worth taking time to enjoy the day, such as sunshine, snacks, or time with a pet.
