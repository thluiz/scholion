The author says remote work began by accident and has continued for nearly 10 years. He presents practical habits that help him work better from home, including a dedicated workspace, protected focus time, attention to health, and a routine that keeps work and family life separate.

## Reading notes

- A workspace used only for work helps the mind switch into work mode and makes it easier to leave work behind at the end of the day.
- Natural light, fresh air, and a tidy room are presented as ways to make the workspace calmer and less mentally tiring.
- Flow is described as the state where creative work feels natural and time seems to pass differently.
- To reach flow, the author suggests changing environment, blocking uninterrupted time, using music or white-noise apps, wearing noise-canceling headphones, and taking breaks.
- He says starting the day with physical activity helps, even when gym access is limited, and that the goal is simply to get the blood pumping.
- Drinking water is useful because it helps him stay energized and forces him to get up and stretch.
- Good sleep depends on stopping work at a consistent time and avoiding late-night work.
- Work-life balance is treated as one of the hardest parts of remote work because a laptop at home makes it hard to disconnect.
- A routine gives predictability for both the worker and the people around them, and it does not need to fill every hour of the day.
- Weekly planning can be done with a planner or Google Calendar, and the author recommends placing the important tasks first.
- The article urges people to plan time for important relationships and events, including date nights and anniversaries.
- The author advises against keeping work apps on the phone, since removing them reduced the urge to check work during mornings, meals, and family time.
- For people suddenly working remotely during a pandemic, the author says the situation is harder than usual and asks readers to be kind to themselves.
