Nick Cosentino says engineering management is about enabling people to do their best work, not just making technical contributions. He describes a path from startups to Microsoft, where his role in Substrate includes team leadership across deployment, routing, and DDOS protection, and where he tries to balance business needs with individual growth.

## Reading notes

- He frames management as a people-first role that depends on situational leadership, transparency, and trust.
- He says every engineer needs a different approach, so he tries to understand each person’s feedback style, goals, challenges, and interests.
- He treats one-on-ones as meetings for the employee, with the employee’s agenda driving the conversation.
- He recommends using one-on-ones for career alignment, clear growth expectations, and discussion of rubrics and level expectations.
- He describes Microsoft’s culture as supporting diversity and inclusion, blameless post-incident reviews, growth mindset, safe-to-fail behavior, and Fix Hack Learn weeks.
- He highlights safe deployment practices, flighting, and a One Microsoft mindset as important parts of Substrate’s work.
- He sees AI as a tool that will replace some tasks and strengthen others, and he mentions Copilot for meeting summaries, code help, and incident debugging.
- He emphasizes flexibility, servant leadership, time blocking, learning from other leaders, and Radical Candor as practical supports for the role.
