Rhetoric is presented as the use of language to persuade, and Aristotle’s account is used to divide persuasion into three modes: ethos, pathos, and logos. The page also places rhetoric within the broader medieval trivium and quadrivium framework.

## Reading notes

- Rhetoric is one of the arts of using language as a means to persuade.
- Aristotle says persuasion by spoken word has three kinds.
- Ethos is appeal to the audience’s sense of honesty or authority.
- Pathos is appeal to the audience’s sense of emotions.
- Logos is appeal to the audience’s sense of logic.
- Ethos persuades through the speaker’s personal character and credibility.
- Pathos works when the speech stirs the hearers’ emotions.
- Logos works through persuasive arguments that prove a truth or an apparent truth.
- The page links rhetoric to grammar and logic as part of the trivium.
- The trivium is presented as preparatory for the quadrivium and higher study.
