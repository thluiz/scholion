The image was captured in infrared by the Gemini North Telescope in Hawaii and is among the sharpest ground-based observations of Jupiter. It uses lucky imaging to reduce the blurring caused by Earth's turbulent atmosphere and combine the clearest exposures into a sharper mosaic.

## Reading notes

- The picture traces glowing regions of warmth beneath Jupiter's cloud tops.
- Infrared lets scientists see past haze and thin clouds at the top of Jupiter's atmosphere.
- Researchers want to understand what makes and sustains the gas giant's weather systems and its great storms.
