The page argues that the daily stand up is simple in form but hard to master. It supports coordination, accountability, quick decisions, and flow, and it helps the team inspect progress toward the sprint goal and deliver a shippable increment within the sprint.

## Reading notes

- The daily stand up is a time-boxed event used in Scrum, Kanban, and similar frameworks to create regularity and reduce waste.
- For shu-level teams, these events are prescribed and not optional.
- A good daily stand up improves communication, reduces interruptions and context switches, and helps remove impediments.
- It supports quick decision-making, productivity, creativity, ownership, visibility, and autonomy.
- The meeting should start on time, happen in the same place and at the same time, last 15 minutes or less, and use a visible, up-to-date task board.
- The agenda is commitment-based and uses four questions about yesterday, today, impediments, and anything else that needs discussion.
- The daily is meant to synchronize the team for the next 24 hours, not to become a status meeting.
- The Scrum Master or Sprint Lead ensures the daily happens, but the whole team is responsible for running it and keeping to the format.
- If discussion goes off format or becomes too detailed, the team should call it out and move unresolved issues to a sidebar or later discussion.
- Teams should hold each other accountable when commitments are repeated without progress, and should help unstick blocked work.
- Common pitfalls include turning it into a status meeting, discussing irrelevant topics, ignoring commitments, ignoring impediments, overrunning the timebox, and missing team members or outside participants.
- A strong daily stand up shows strict timeboxing, shared focus on priorities and risks, progress monitoring, quick adaptation, fine-grained coordination, impediment removal, mutual accountability, and peer pressure when needed.
