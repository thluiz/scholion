The post argues that silent disagreements often stay hidden until they cause wasted effort, lower ratings, blocked promotions, or project trouble. It says the goal is not to remove disagreement, but to surface it early by creating a safe space for open concerns.

## Reading notes

- Low engagement can signal disagreement, especially when several stakeholders just reply “okay,” ask no questions, and stay disengaged.
- If people are silent, the post suggests smaller discussion groups, 1:1 conversations, specific questions, and attention to who is unusually quiet.
- A teammate may seem aligned during planning but still act against the proposal during execution, which can point to disagreement about timing or priority.
- The post recommends discussing execution timelines, comparing the proposal with other work, and getting sign-off on milestones.
- Repeated new concerns before a decision can signal deeper disagreement, caution from past failures, or discomfort with broader team and political issues.
- The post recommends multiple brainstorming sessions, a structured list of issues, a deadline for new concerns, and space for sensitive topics.
- When people seem less supportive to others than they do in front of you, the cause may be power dynamics or fear of confrontation.
- The post suggests making disagreement safe, addressing concerns publicly, and building trust through follow-through on feedback.
- When discussions keep ending with “Let’s take it offline,” decision-making may be getting deferred because the right decision-makers are not present, the issue feels low priority, or people have not made their disagreement into a clear position.
- The post recommends agreeing on timing, checking whether the right decision-makers are in the room, and setting deadlines for follow-up.
