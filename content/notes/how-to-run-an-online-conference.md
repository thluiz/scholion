The post describes how All Day Hey! Live was moved online during the pandemic and how the author handled the technical side of the broadcast. It focuses on preserving the social aspects of an in-person conference through Zoom, Slack, ticketing, a live portal, and pre-recorded talks, while also noting the limits of the setup and the choices made around tools and workflow.

## Reading notes

- The conference was taken online because the in-person event was unlikely to go ahead as planned during COVID-19, and the online format was also seen as a way to experiment and reach more people.
- The author wanted the online version to keep the social side of the event, including questions for speakers during talks and a sense of live interaction through Slack.
- Eventbrite was chosen for ticketing because it worked well for attendees and for back-office management, even if it was not the cheapest option.
- The live portal at alldayhey.com gathered the conference information in one place, linked people to the Zoom broadcast and Slack, and later served as a library for rewatching talks.
- Talks were pre-recorded so speakers could be available live during the event, the videos could be edited more professionally, and recordings could be ready before the day.
- The production pack for speakers included a GoPro Hero, an SD card, a Gorillapod, and a USB-C lapel microphone.
- Wirecast was chosen over OBS Studio because it supported a virtual camera and microphone on Mac, Twitter feeds, cleaner layer management, and Wirecast Rendezvous for remote audio and video.
- Wirecast layers were organized for audio, talks, hosts, overlays, and holding screens, and a Stream Deck was used to trigger scenes quickly during the broadcast.
- Loopback was used to route music from Music.app into Wirecast without playing it locally, which made monitoring easier.
- The broadcast machine and the monitoring machine were kept separate so the broadcast computer could focus only on streaming and recording.
- Zoom was used for the conference broadcast, with Webinar and Cloud Recording add-ons, and the author adjusted audio and video settings to work with Wirecast virtual inputs.
- Practice mode, cloud recording, disabled chat, and disabled raising hand were used to reduce disruption and provide a backup.
- The author notes that Zoom briefly broke virtual camera inputs on Mac OS Catalina during preparation, which required a workaround.
- On the day, the event ran from a green room in Wirecast Rendezvous, with holding screens used while attendance filled up before the hosts went live.
- Contingency planning included a backup 4G router and a prepared update to the live portal so attendees could still reach the uploaded videos if the live stream failed.
- Closed captioning was explored for accessibility, but the cost was too high for the budget, and the author treats accessibility as an ongoing task.
- Support handling covered access problems, late ticket purchases, and code of conduct concerns throughout the day.
