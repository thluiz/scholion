The article addresses the interview question "Why do you want to work here?", arguing that flippant answers hurt candidates even if the question feels stupid. The author explains that employers ask it to understand motivation, cultural fit, and whether hiring you will be a good investment, since they spend time and money on guidance, mentoring, education, and budget. Motivated people tend to contribute more and stay longer, while unmotivated ones deliver less, are more likely to leave, and may harm their own well‑being if they stay in a job they dislike.

To answer well, candidates should prepare by researching what the company does, what it stands for, and its mission, then reflect on what work makes them happy, what they want to achieve and contribute, what culture they need, and how they want to support the company’s success. With these answers, they can build a genuine story about why this specific company fits them. The article gives two sample answers that connect the candidate’s values, skills, and interests with the company’s mission and product, stressing that honesty and belief in what you say matter more than crafting a supposedly perfect response.
