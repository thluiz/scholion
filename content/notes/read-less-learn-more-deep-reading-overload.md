The article argues that in a world flooded with TV, books, websites, and social media, trying to consume more and faster leaves people exhausted and less able to learn. The author describes a personal year of extreme consumption using audiobooks at high speed and speed‑reading apps, which led to shallow recall and almost no real understanding. Drawing on research about willpower, decision fatigue, and differences between screen and paper reading, the piece distinguishes short‑term remembering from deeper knowing, and claims that fast, distracted reading treats books as disposable data. Slow, focused reading, by contrast, supports deeper comprehension, integration of ideas, and original thought. The author suggests practical habit changes: turning off notifications, dropping book‑count goals, rereading, taking notes, and treating reading as a way to grow rather than a metric to optimize.
