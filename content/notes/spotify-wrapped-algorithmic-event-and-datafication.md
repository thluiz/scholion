Spotify Wrapped is described as an annual algorithmic event that turns listening habits into shareable data stories. The post argues that it is also a marketing campaign, but users do not only consume it passively. In workshops, they reflect on what Spotify claims to know about them, how its categories flatten listening behavior, and how much trust they place in the platform’s data.

## Reading notes

- Wrapped is framed as a yearly moment when users are collectively oriented toward Spotify’s data capture and the inferences it makes about identity, taste, and lifestyle.
- The feature is presented as a marketing tool designed to drive subscriptions and social media sharing through personalised cards and prompts.
- Spotify keeps building anticipation with new labels and formats, including Audio Aura, Listening Personality, Sound Town, and the latest music phases in Wrapped 2024.
- The 2024 version also included a personalised AI podcast built with NotebookLM, and users responded with lukewarm or critical reviews.
- The authors’ workshop series with more than 200 participants explores Wrapped through decoding listening characters, a walkthrough of the interface, and a Spotify data selfie collage.
- Participants use the workshops to question Spotify’s claim to know them through data and to examine the purpose and impact of algorithmic categories.
- Users describe a mismatch between Spotify’s assumptions and their actual listening, especially because Spotify only counts activity inside its own ecosystem.
- Some participants point to glitches and odd classifications, such as brown noise or accidental loops becoming top tracks, which raises doubts about accuracy.
- Users still find value in Wrapped, sometimes changing their listening beforehand to shape the result, while also feeling discomfort with being reduced to data points.
- The post concludes that Wrapped invites both celebration and critique, and that its visual storytelling continues to shape how users relate to music listening.
