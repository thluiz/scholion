Grant argues that the feedback sandwich should be abandoned because it weakens the message in two ways. People may fixate on the criticism and forget the praise, or they may remember the praise and miss the criticism. He says the opening matters most when the goal is to help someone improve.

## Reading notes

- The feedback sandwich places criticism between two positive comments to make it easier to hear.
- Grant says the method does not work and should be dropped.
- He says recipients may focus on the criticism and dismiss the praise.
- People who have received feedback sandwiches before may start expecting criticism as soon as praise begins, which can make the praise seem insincere.
- Genuine praise can also drown out the criticism because people remember the beginning and end of a conversation more than the middle.
- Grant recommends prefacing criticism with a sentence like, “I’m giving you these comments because I have very high expectations and I know that you can reach them.”
- In the Stanford study he cites, students who heard that sentence were more likely to implement the suggested changes.
- Grant says the key message should come before the content of the feedback and should show that the intent is to help, not attack.
