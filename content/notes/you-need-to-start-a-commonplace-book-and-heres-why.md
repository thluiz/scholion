Starting a commonplace book only needs a journal, a pen or pencil, and things you want to record. The page says the practice should stay informal, flexible, and easy to begin.

It recommends ignoring perfection, not organizing entries too early, using more than quotes, and making the process feel like an event. It also says inspiration can come from unusual details, nature, books, songs, films, and conversations.

## Reading notes

- A commonplace book can start with a journal, a pen or pencil, and material worth recording.
- The book does not need to look perfect, because a messy one is better than none.
- Entries can be added as they are found, without making a table of contents or dividing them into subcategories.
- The format can include drawings, doodles, photographs, magazine cut-outs, pressed flowers, or pasted pages.
- The act of writing can be slowed down and turned into a calm moment, such as with tea, sunlight, or a blanket and candle.
- Inspiration can come from unusual observations and from nature, books, song lyrics, movie dialogue, and a best friend’s words.
- The author uses a small journal for portability and recommends Rhodia journals and Stabilo Fineliners.
