The article says effective technical presentations start with a clear purpose, shaped by what the speaker wants the audience to think, feel, or do after the talk. It also stresses adapting to the audience, using stories instead of flat facts, and keeping slides minimal so the speaker can connect directly with listeners.

It recommends shorter talks, deliberate practice of delivery, and avoiding live Q&A in favor of a clearer way for people to follow up after the presentation.

## Reading notes

- Define the talk around a specific outcome for the audience, because a presentation without a goal has no clear path to success.
- Adjust the content to the audience by considering beginners and experts, their assumptions, what they have already seen, the right pace, and how the talk can stand out.
- Present information as a story with place, time, and people, since that is more memorable and emotionally engaging than abstract facts.
- Do not read slides verbatim, avoid speaker notes, and use fewer words on slides so they work as prompts rather than scripts.
- Prefer talks of 20 minutes or less, because they are easier to structure and easier to keep engaging.
- Practice delivery by reading aloud from a children’s book and varying pace, volume, pitch, and pauses to expand vocal range.
- Skip live Q&A and instead tell people how to contact you or continue the conversation after the talk.
