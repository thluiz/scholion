The post argues that ending an email with “thanks in advance” can sound offensive because gratitude normally follows action or agreement, not the request itself. It says the phrase also lets the sender skip thanking the recipient afterward and can make the recipient feel cut out of the exchange.

The author recommends other closings such as asking for help directly, thanking the reader for considering the request, or saying the request may not be possible. The post also says people should thank recipients after they act, or even after they simply consider the request and decline it.

## Reading notes

- “Thanks” is presented as something that usually follows the action you are grateful for.
- In email, the recipient has not agreed or acted by the time they reach the end of the message.
- “Thanks in advance” is described as presuming compliance before the other person responds.
- The phrase is also said to let the sender avoid thanking the person later.
- The post offers several alternative closings that stay polite without assuming the outcome.
- It ends by urging writers to drop “in advance” and to thank people after the request is handled or considered.
