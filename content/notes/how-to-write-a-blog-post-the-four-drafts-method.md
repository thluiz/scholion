The video argues that writing a blog post is a step-by-step process rather than a matter of waiting for inspiration. It adapts a four-draft method from a novel-writing process and applies it to blogging, moving from raw ideas to audience-focused structure, then revision and review.

## Reading notes

- The method starts with a blank page and a Throwaway Draft, where the writer dumps everything they know about the subject without editing.
- The first draft is for the writer alone, and the goal is to free-write for 25 minutes without worrying about grammar, punctuation, or sentence structure.
- After that, the writer shifts attention to the reader and does audience analysis to identify who the audience is, what brought them to the post, and whether there are primary, secondary, or gatekeeper readers.
- The audience analysis helps decide the most effective structure, such as a listicle, an opinion piece, or a comparison.
- The Throwaway Draft is then reshaped into that structure to create the Rough Draft.
- The Rough Draft is edited by reading through it and making changes, then checking tone and grammar with Grammarly, reading it aloud with text-to-speech, and consulting a style guide when needed.
- These edits produce a Review Draft that is ready for technical, peer, editorial, and sometimes sensitivity review.
- After incorporating feedback, the writer arrives at the Final Draft and publishes it, including on a personal blog or on dev.to.
